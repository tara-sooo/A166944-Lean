## 1. Mark the actual source population, before any accounting reduction

Use canonical MIN, odd sources M in [X,2X), N=X/2 for dyadic X divisible by4, B=ceil(sqrt(7X)), t0=M+2, C0=2M-2, individual K(M)=floor((7M-1)/4). Write h=2t-C>=0 at attained states, r(C) for the least eligible odd prime <=B when any exists. For an executed event of label p and wait w, h'=h+2w-(p-1), w<=2r(C), r(C)<=p. All C,t lie in windows of length O(X), C+2<=7X+2. On a no-twin window, any original natural terminal requires elapsed time >=K(M)-t0-2B=Omega(X), by the existing quiet-tail/twin certificate.

Run ALL source paths, without any artificial stopping, or alternatively the earlier cell/full-inverse stopping scheme. Process increasing C. At each horizon distinguish actual arrival group V(t,C) and actual next-event group A(n,p,C) (only tags with that next event within their own cutoff). At a cell stop examine V groups before stopping; at a projected prime stop examine A groups before stopping. Use the existing first-saturation marking rule from research/2026-11-24-min-first-saturation-clock-mergers.md: permanently mark a tag at the first V or A group of mass >=kappa it encounters, inspecting V before A. A new mark at V comes from an earlier executed A class of mass <kappa, or from an initial singleton. A new mark at A comes from a V group of mass <kappa. Previously marked witnesses need not have small mass. The original cutoff can truncate the path, and does not affect the first-mark capacity argument. No new absorption or change of MIN choices is made by marking.

For an integer H>6, also record a tag that first attains h>=H before or at its first V mark (if simultaneous, give height priority). A tag whose first mark is at A has been examined for height at its actual pre-state V. A proposed event that stops without execution does NOT itself count as an attained height. Denote by Z_pre(H) the number of distinct source tags first hitting H before any earlier mark. For these prefixes every prior attained pre-state and every executed event group has mass <kappa (the final hitting event also has such an A group, since A is inspected before execution). This statement uses the whole active source population, not a selected terminal subpopulation, and does not require a non-splitting forest after the mark.

## 2. Unconditional deletion of every sufficiently large label at low premark height

This is the main new elementary estimate. Suppose an event of label p>2H+2 executes while its source is still unmarked and has not attained H. Nonnegativity of post-height gives
    4r(C) >= 2w >= p-1-h > p-1-H > p/2,
so r(C)>p/8. This holds equally for reset and nonreset events, without any upper bound on the post-height.

At a fixed eligible projected key (p,C), its actual possible clock n is in a single residue class mod2p. All relevant prior t have 0<=2t-C<H, and 1<=n-t<=2r(C)<=2p. Hence there are O(1+H/p) possible n, in particular O(1) for p>2H+2. Each event with unmarked tags has A group mass <kappa. Use the established uniform per-prime sieve cost S_p^cost=sum_(even C eligible for p) r(C)=O(X/log^2 p), for p>=11 and p<=B. It implies at most O(X/(p log^2 p)) eligible horizons at which r(C)>p/8. Summing their possible clocks and multiplying by kappa bounds the total incidence count, and therefore the number of distinct tags with ANY such large event before first mark/height, by
    E_large <= O(kappa X sum_(p>2H+2 prime)1/(p log^2 p))
             = O(kappa X/log^2 H).
Here H>=12, all constants absolute as in the preceding uniform sieve. This is an unconditional all-depth SOURCE bound. It does not estimate the number of later large events by already marked tags. It improves the older low-height nonreset-only arguments by not needing to exclude resets or require a large post-height. In particular, every tag outside this exceptional set uses labels <=2H+2 on its entire premark, preheight prefix, including the event that first attains H.

Let L_H^*=2 product_(3<=p<=2H+2 prime)p, or use the product restricted to p<=B. The exact even-multiple barrier implies that for such tags the entire premark low-height path stays in its initial cell (jL_H^*,(j+1)L_H^*], including the arrival at its first V mark or the pre-horizon of its first A mark, if it has not hit H. Its horizon gain is <L_H^*. Chebyshev gives log L_H^*=O(H). For H=floor((log B)^1/4), L_H^*=B^o(1)=o(X); the executed elapsed time in the prefix is <=3 times the gain. This is a local origin bound for the newly marked unexceptional tags, NOT a bound on the origins of previously marked witnesses in their groups.

## 3. Lower-height first passage with no BL restriction

Use the finite-time averaged clipped potential from research/2026-11-25-min-ghost-finite-time-averaged-clipped-potential.md. Choose integers Y>=P>=11, 2P<=S, 0<=K<=S, L=2 product_(3<=p<Y prime)p, and H>=max(12,L,2(K+6)), H<=B (sufficient, not necessary). In particular Y<=H. Let D_{Y,T}(C,t) be the maximum nonnegative signed height gain at attained events within time budget T of the <Y ghost. Define F=(1/(S+1)) sum_(T=S..2S) min(K,D_{Y,T}), and Phi=2t-C+F. For actual LOW p<Y, the previous exact finite-budget recursion and monotone boundary estimate give positive Phi increment <=6r(C) in general, and for p<P
    [delta Phi]_+ <= 4P 1{D_{Y,2S}>K}+2P K/(S+1).
For p>=Y, positive increment <=4r(C)+(K-p+1)_+. Write
    mu=Pr_uniform phase rectangle(D_{Y,2S}>K),
    eta=P(mu+K/(S+1))+1/log^2 P
              +sum_(q>=Y prime)(K-q+1)_+/q^2.
The high excess sum is O(K/(Y log Y)) and zero for K<=Y-1.

Exclude the hitting tags charged in section2. On the remaining first-passage prefixes ALL labels are <=2H+2. This matters because the old H>=BL (in particular H>=B) restriction is no longer necessary. For a fixed projected key p,C, count the clock n in the interval determined by pre-height <H and wait<=2p. It has O(1+H/p)=O(H/p) possible values for p<=2H+2. There is no need to bound the final post-height by H+4B when counting clocks: use the pre-height band and the wait bound directly. Each counted clock has at most kappa unmarked incidences. The previous sum of r-cost for p>=P is then O(kappa X H/log^2 P); the excess high term is O(kappa X H sum_(q>=Y)(K-q+1)_+/q^2). For p<P, count ALL attained physical pre-states with h<H in each phase pair modulo L. Because H>=L, X>>L, there are O(XH/L^2) per phase pair, and each has at most kappa unmarked incidences. Summing gives O(kappa X H P(mu+K/(S+1))). No equidistribution of actual source phases is assumed. Every hitting tag has Phi gain >=H-(6+K)>=H/2. Therefore
    Z_pre(H) <= O(kappa X [eta+1/log^2 H]) = O(kappa X eta),
where the large-event deletion was added only once and 1/log^2 H<=1/log^2 P. This is an unconditional inequality; it does not assert eta->0. It applies both to the original full path population and to first-passage before a first mark in the stopped population. In particular it does NOT need the preceding small-terminal-physical-class assumption, the global prefix cost cap, or any H>=BL hypothesis. The new restriction is first passage before saturation, not first passage along every arbitrary heavy path.

As before, the finite-time infinite-CRT condition D_infty(2S_j)/S_j->0 in probability along a diverging sequence would imply a choice of S,K,Y,P and growing kappa with kappa eta->0. The common CRT coupling error O(S/(Y log Y)) permits Y>=S^2, K<=Y-1; choose K/S->0, P->infinity sufficiently slowly, then kappa->infinity slowly with kappa<=ceil(log^(1/4)Y). Choose Y(B) along that sequence sufficiently slowly to have L<=H, 2(K+6)<=H, and Y<=c log log B. In fact we may demand L<=log^(1/8) B. The illustrative H=floor((log B)^1/4) meets all conditions eventually. These parameter choices can all be made in order by the diagonal argument, but the probability convergence is not proved.

## 4. Unconditional mixed-label first-saturation deletions

The following bounds are valid independently of the unproved eta limit. Count ONLY new marks whose height has never reached H before the mark (thus own height at the mark is <H). For A marks, this refers to their actual prior V height, not the hypothetical post-height of the current event. Allow previously marked or previously high witnesses.

An arrival via label p at V(t,C) has the parity-dependent exact congruences:
    even t: p|C, t==0 mod2p;
    odd t: p|(C+2), t==p+2 mod2p.
With b(t)=0 for even t and 6 for odd t, 2p divides h-b(t). For p>=7, nonnegative h at a nonreset state implies h-b(t)>=2p. Reset means h=b(t). A root injection is an odd reset of height6. At a fixed V, each incoming label p has just one preceding A class, and any newly marked tags in that class number <kappa; if it were already >=kappa, all those tags would already have been marked. At A, every newly marked parent V has mass <kappa, and all its parent times are in an interval of length <=2r(C).

(i) Mixed top-witness V marks, including reset/root and arbitrary small labels.
Choose Q>H, Q<=B. If V at h<H has ANY incoming label p>=Q, it must be a reset. For two distinct incoming labels p,q, the common parity forces both to divide the SAME C or C+2. Count reset states in the O(X) horizon window by O(X/(pq)) (since pq<=B^2=O(X)); reset time is fixed by C and parity. For each such pair the two incoming classes together contain at most 2(kappa-1) newly marked tags. Count ALL pairs with p>=Q, q>=3 distinct; for each newly marked donor class one can choose a pair including it and a top witness, or if it is the top class itself choose any other incoming label. If no other label exists, a new mark requires a root injection, since otherwise the whole V group is its one earlier A class. Root/top cases are bounded by O(kappa X sum_(p>=Q)1/p). Root tags at a V with both top and other labels can also be charged by the root/top count. Thus the number of newly marked tags in ALL such top-witness V groups is
    O(kappa X (1+sum_(q<=B prime)1/q) sum_(Q<=p<=B prime)1/p).
No bound on the total mass of the top witness class is assumed. Unlike the earlier top/top first-saturation estimate this includes a single top label merging with arbitrarily small incoming labels.

(ii) ALL long-clock A marks at low own pre-height.
Choose R0 between 11 and B. If r(C)>R0, both C-1 and C+1 lack an odd prime divisor <=R0. The same two-form upper-bound sieve used in the preceding notes gives O(X/log^2 R0) candidate C (for the subpower choice below this is well within the available sieve range). For each C, there are O(H+1) possible own parent times with h<H, each has <kappa newly marked tags and can feed only one actual A under deterministic earliest selection. Thus
    E_A,long = O(kappa X (H+1)/log^2 R0).
This excludes such new marks without any top-witness requirement.

(iii) Mixed top-witness A marks at short/intermediate r(C).
Suppose r(C)<=R0 and H+4R0<2Q. Any parent time of A differs from a newly marked own parent time by <2r(C); hence every top witness in that A has attained height <H+4R0<2Q. It must be a reset, so C or C+2 is divisible by some p>=Q. Count O(X sum_(Q<=p<=B)1/p) such horizons. Again at most O(H+1) possible low-height own parent times with <kappa new tags each. The count is
    E_A,top = O(kappa X (H+1) sum_(Q<=p<=B prime)1/p).
This works with ANY other incoming labels, including small or previously marked witnesses, and does not assume the whole A has small mass.

(iv) Nonreset V with a medium-or-larger witness, and individual nonreset arrivals.
Let J>=7, J<=H. A nonreset V at h<H with an incoming p>=J has no root injection. A new mark requires at least two distinct incoming labels (otherwise the whole V was the previous A). For p>=J and any distinct q>=3, both labels divide C or C+2 on the same side and 2pq divides h-b(t), which is positive (p>=7 excludes negative nonzero multiple at h>=0). For each parity the number of (C,h) possibilities is O(X/(pq) * H/(pq)); the nonzero height multiple removes a possible +1. Charge each new donor to a pair with medium-or-larger witness as in (i). Summing gives
    E_V,nonreset,J = O(kappa X H sum_(p>=J)1/p^2 sum_(q>=3)1/q^2)
                  = O(kappa X H/(J log J)).
Additionally, count every premark executed event p>=J whose post-state is nonreset of height <H, even if there is no merger. There are O(X/p) eligible landing horizons per side and O(H/p) nonzero possible height multiples. Each physical A supports <kappa unmarked tags. Its total original-source count has the same upper bound O(kappa X H/(J log J)). Both bounds are independent of how often a witness was selected earlier. They are not bounds for all marked donor mass arriving at these states.

Concrete simultaneous parameters (roundings harmless):
    H=floor((log B)^1/4),
    Q=ceil(B exp(-sqrt(log B))),
    R0=ceil(exp((log B)^(1/3))),
    J=max(7,floor(H/sqrt(log H))),
    kappa->infinity, kappa<=ceil(log^(1/4)Y), Y<=c log log B.
Eventually H+4R0<2Q, R0<B, and kappa=o(sqrt(log H)). Prime counting/partial summation gives sum_(Q<=p<=B)1/p=O(1/sqrt(log B)), and sum_(q<=B)1/q=O(log log(3B)). Consequently (i), (ii), (iii), (iv) are ALL o(X), as is section2. For example (iii) is O(kappa X/(log B)^1/4); (ii) is O(kappa X/(log B)^(5/12)); (iv) is O(kappa X/sqrt(log H)). These are unconditional asymptotic deletions for their explicitly restricted newly marked/pre-mark populations. The other first marks, especially low-label clock merges and reset mergers without a top witness, are not bounded by these results. In particular this is not an upper bound on the entire weighted physical far-merge proxy P_phys,far of the original stopped forest.

## 5. Joint necessary inequality on the original full source paths

There is a simpler joint deficit route at the level of source counts. Use the ORIGINAL full paths with no accounting stops, and mark them as above. Let M_res count newly marked sources whose first mark precedes the first attained height H (or who never attain H), excluding the exceptional sets of sections2 and4. More explicitly, their premark path contains no label >2H+2 and no nonreset arrival at h<H via p>=J; the first V mark has no top witness and if nonreset has no witness >=J; a first A mark has r(C)<=R0 and no top witness. By section2 these unexceptional newly marked sources start in the same L_H^* barrier cell as the horizon of their first mark. Their previously marked witnesses need not do so.

Assume a no-twin window, i.e. none of the original paths can end at a twin terminal. A tag with neither height hit before mark nor any mark and with no large-label exception has its ENTIRE executed path confined to the L_H^* cell, with elapsed <=3L_H^*=o(X). This contradicts the natural non-twin terminal elapsed lower bound Omega(X). Hence every original tag is either a height hit before or at first mark, a lower-height first mark, or in the large-label exception. Delete the other first-mark exceptions using section4. We have the unconditional necessary inequality on no-twin windows
    Z_pre(H)+M_res >= N-o(X) = X/2-o(X).
Combining with section3, any bound
    C*kappa*eta + M_res/X <= 1/2 - epsilon
with fixed epsilon>0 eventually (using an actual uniform constant C in the first-passage estimate) would suffice for arbitrarily large windows, and in particular Z_pre=o(X) together with M_res=o(X) suffices. No global R_pre cap, cell stop, terminal clock moment, or weighted variance argument is needed for this ALTERNATIVE implication. The old weighted far-proxy inequality remains a different sufficient route, and is not asserted to follow from this count.

The substantive joint feature is that every height hit is charged only while its physical mass is small, and every remaining new source merger has an own prefix localized by the same height restriction. Every newly marked tag has a witness source separated by >=kappa-1 in origin, since kappa distinct odd origins span >=2(kappa-1). At an actual A merge they share the same executed event in the full path model; at V they share the same state. However, M_res also counts local physical saturation, and cannot simply be identified with the weighted far-proxy P_phys,far. It may be a more demanding residual, since local coalescence can be common even when twins are plentiful. No claim that M_res=o(X) follows from the barrier alone: L_H^* is much larger than kappa. Previously marked witnesses may already carry large mass. A possible next concrete attack is to count transport of these local groups between barrier cells, rather than trying to eliminate all small local clusters; that would require a new multiscale mass/height estimate. Alternatively retain the existing weighted forest target and use the premark local bounds as a decomposition of first saturation only. The present inequality does not prove a global anti-concentration upper bound.

For reference, the stopped-population version also yields the same necessary inequality restricted to stopped tags. By the earlier full inverse/cell budget S_c+S_p=N-o(X) on no-twin windows. A stopped tag with no mark or height hit has physical terminal group <kappa. For prime stops at (p,C), possible n for its pre-height <H are O(1+H/p), so unmarked low-height prime mass <=O(kappa J_p(1+H/Y))=o(X) using J_p<=N/B. For cells, root injections total O(J_c L)=o(X), and each cell has at most two crossing gateways per p; h<H gives O(1+H/p) arrival clocks. Hence low-height unmarked cell mass <=O(kappa J_c(pi(B)+H log log(3B)))=o(X) since J_c=O(log^2 Y). This smaller H permits the direct terminal count without the high landing-divisor deletion. This stopped variant is not needed for the full-path implication but preserves contact with the previous stopping framework.

## 7. Status and next concrete objectives

This turn proves an asymptotically vanishing bound for all large labels before low-height saturation, extends the finite-time first-passage theorem down to polylogarithmic H, and obtains new mixed-label/long-clock/nonreset witness deletions. It also gives a joint source-count necessary condition without the artificial stopping rules. The low-phase convergence eta->0 and the remaining low-label/reset first-saturation deficit are not solved. In the original weighted-forest route the full P_phys,far upper bound remains unresolved. Do not interpret the new count inequality as a solution of either. Further work should quantify mass transport between local saturation groups or find a direct analytic upper bound for the signed finite-time LOW leakage, rather than merely continuing to reformulate the infinite-CRT lifetime condition. No new branch, no main edit, no Lean compilation.
