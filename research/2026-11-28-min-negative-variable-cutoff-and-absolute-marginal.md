## 2. Variable cutoff covers the whole plateau, not a fixed rectangle

Put a0=.160, b in [.313,.350], and F0(x)=log((d−x)/x)/d. Ordinary lower at a0 has 2<d/a0<4 and gives S(R^a0)/M_R>=F0(a0) minus an arbitrarily small fixed error. For prime q in [R^a0,R^b), t=log_R q, set
  a(t)=(d−t)/3+.0002.
It satisfies 0<a(t)<t, 1<(d−t)/a(t)<3 throughout this band. Exact Buchstab:
  S(R^b)=S(R^a0)−sum_q S_q(q),
  S_q(q)=S_q(R^a(t))−sum_{R^a(t)<=r<q, r prime} S_qr(r).
Let C_R(b) be the sum of the last nonnegative pair correction over ALL q in the stated band. Distinct q,r; a single integer can contribute more than once, and C_R counts those incidences. It is not a distinct-root count.

For the upper of S_q(R^a(t)), append q in Q1 (t<=.35<.3748), split ordinary upper components of residual level R^(d−t−small slack) at the two remaining capacities, use the earlier squarefree-mask transfer. Sifting primes below q guarantee unique grouping of q in the first coefficient. The normalization is 1/(d−t), because sF(s)=2 exp(gamma) on 1<=s<=3. To implement varying exponents rigorously, partition [a0,b] into finitely many fixed narrow exponent bins, and use the larger cutoff a(t_min) for the analytic upper with the smaller allowed level d−t_max. For an upper of the actual S_q(R^a(t)) instead use the SMALLER cutoff a(t_max), which is still on the plateau for sufficiently small mesh (the .0002 slack); then S_q(R^a(t))<=S_q(R^a(t_max)). Split weights on that bin with level d−t_max minus arbitrarily small slack. All moduli obey the original fixed power inequalities. Fine multiplicative boxes separate product constraints. Let fixed mesh and lower/upper sieve approximation errors be sufficiently small, then R large. The upper summed coefficient approaches integral_a0^b dt/[t(d−t)]. This gives the exact analytic bridge (in liminf/limsup notation, or with arbitrarily small fixed error for all sufficiently large R):
  S(R^b)/M_R >= F0(b)+C_R(b)/M_R−epsilon.                 (1)
The identity of the ordinary coefficients follows by integrating 1/[t(d−t)]. The lower and upper do not depend on an unproved asymptotic for C_R. This includes far more positive terms than the rectangles of 4708dfc and 2d5e94f.

A broader surplus version: for a fixed cutoff c>0 below every a(t) in a selected compact q band, replace the upper on S_q(R^c) by its general linear-sieve upper with residual A=d−t. In M_R units define U_A(u)=F(A/u)/(2 exp(gamma) u). For r exponent u, the ordinary pair lower coefficient is L_A(u)=f((A−u)/u)/(2 exp(gamma) u). These are the standard linear sieve functions. The differential identity (sF(s))'=f(s−1) on s>3, and the constant plateau below3, give
  U_A(c)−1/A = integral_c^(A/3) L_A(u) du/u
when c<=A/3. Thus any extra positive lower beyond the ordinary pair lower, integrated over a compact sector, transfers directly into (1), up to fixed approximation errors. For u<A/3, q can be appended in Q1 and r in Q2 (u<=.155<.2498), the two remaining capacities have sum d−t−u, and the usual lower transfer is valid. For u>=A/3 the ordinary pair lower is zero, and no packing or lower estimate on that region is presumed. One may apply this statement to a finite partition of the q band with cutoffs tending to A/3; no uniformity for infinitely many bins is needed. This is an algebraic consequence of standard sieve differential equations, not an independent solution of the surplus estimate. Simply taking a smaller cutoff and retaining the ordinary lower terms below the plateau does not improve (1): those terms pay exactly for the increased upper.

## 4. Absolute marginals before imposing the quotient's primality

NEW elementary estimate for the exact real source, independent of the Pascadi endpoint issue. Let Qset be any set of squarefree moduli composed of primes in [R^.08,R^.35], with 1<k<=R^.49. Then
  sum_{k in Qset} | # {n in source: n=2 mod k} − B_R/phi(k) |
     << R^(1−delta)                                      (2)
for some absolute delta>0 (e.g. .0004 for sufficiently large R). This is on moduli composed only of polynomial-sized primes, not a full Bombieri-Vinogradov bound for arbitrary k. In particular it applies to k=qr on D_b^*. The arbitrary subset need not be rectangular.

Proof: first take a product box of p and v intervals, where P V comparable to R, P exponent near .375, V<=R^.6252 up to constants. Let alpha,beta be their prime indicators. They are automatically coprime to every prime in the modulus range. For primitive characters modulo e in [Q,2Q], the standard multiplicative large sieve gives
  sum_e (e/phi(e)) sum_chi^* |A(chi)|² << (Q²+P) P,
  and analogously for V,
using trivial L² norms, where A=sum_p alpha_p chi(p), B=sum_v beta_v chi(v). By Cauchy the primitive discrepancy contribution with weight 1/phi(e) is
  << sqrt[(Q²+P)(Q²+V)PV]/Q
  << sqrt R [Q+sqrt(P+V)+sqrt(PV)/Q].
For R^.08<=Q<=R^.49, this is O(R^.99) up to harmless absolute constants (other exponents <=.8126 and .92). For imprimitive nonprincipal characters modulo k, the conductor e>1 divides k and contains at least one polynomial prime. Since alpha,beta are coprime to all k, induced sums equal their primitive sums. For fixed e, sum_{k:e|k} 1/phi(k) over permitted squarefree k is <=(1/phi(e)) sum_{f} 1/phi(f) <<1/phi(e); f uses at most floor(.49/.08)=6 distinct polynomial primes, and the prime reciprocal sum in the whole exponent band is bounded by Mertens. Therefore summing the dyadic conductor estimates costs O(L) only. Principal term is exactly box mass/phi(k). This proves the bound for one product box.

For the exact slab restriction n∈[R+1,2R+1], partition the p,v ranges into multiplicative intervals with ratio 1+R^(-.001). There are O(R^.002 L²) product boxes; keep those entirely contained in slab. Boxes intersecting boundary give n in O(R^.999) length union of two endpoint intervals. Unique semiprime representation implies <=O(R^.999) boundary source elements, no short-interval PNT required. Each n−2 has at most 12 polynomial prime factors counted with multiplicity, so its contribution to total divisor discrepancies over k in Qset is bounded by a constant; sum_k 1/phi(k) is also bounded by the same harmonic argument. Boundary error O(R^.999). Interior error O(R^.992 L³), proving (2), with slack for interval and dyadic constants. No use of a short prime interval theorem.

The same proof for PRIME moduli q with exponent near .375, e.g. [.37,.38], gives absolute power saving as well: when q could equal p or v, simply exclude q-dividing coefficients in the principal term, while nonprincipal primitive characters already vanish at such factors. The large sieve estimate uses the original coefficients. The summed correction to B_R/phi(q) is O(B_R/R^.37) since each n has at most two source factors. In particular for any fixed multiplicative window [R^.375,C R^.375] with C>1,
  sum_q # {source n: q|n−2} ~ B_R log C/(.375 L).
This uses PNT/Mertens over the fixed multiplicative window, with the absolute error negligible versus B_R/L. Thus even BEFORE roughness conditioning, the previous padded countermodel violates the prime-divisor marginals by a factor of order L: every positive-weight model j has a factor in its fixed padding window, whereas the actual source mass of those with such a factor is O(B_R/L). This strengthens the earlier anti-padding estimate in a different direction; it does not prove a stability/classification theorem for all extremal sequences.

## 5. Use the correction to force isolated actual first-minus events in the complementary case

At b=.313, let a=.310,c=.312. For any small fixed approximation error, ordinary lower S(R^a)/M_R >=F0(a)−epsilon; first-prime upper for q∈[R^c,R^b) yields S(R^c)/M_R <=S(R^b)/M_R+F0(c)−F0(b)+epsilon, by the same plateau argument. Equation (1) bounds C_R(b)/M_R <= S(R^b)/M_R−F0(b)+epsilon.

If n−2 is R^a-rough with least prime r<R^c, and has two DISTINCT primes below R^b, take q>r to be another such prime. Then q in original q-band, a(t)<a for t>=a, so this n contributes to C_R(b). If its least factor repeats instead, remove all square divisibilities q²|n−2 for q>=R^a; crude integer count O(R sum_{q>=R^a}1/q²+R^(.5))=o(M_R), no primality assumption. Thus there are at least
  M_R [F0(a)−F0(c)+2 F0(b)] −2 S(R^b)−3epsilon M_R−o(M_R)
real n with exactly ONE prime factor below R^b, this factor r in [R^a,R^c). Certified rational log series gives F0(a)−F0(c)+2F0(b) > .0061522732628427. Consequently for every sufficiently large R, either
  (A) S(R^.313)>=.001 M_R, giving density-scale stationary first height floor(R^sigma) for all fixed sigma<.313, including .3129>5/16; OR
