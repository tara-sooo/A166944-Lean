## 1. Obstruction: the infinite candidate terminal-offset support is not small

Keep the common independent CRT coordinates c_p,b_p mod p and common clock parity. At time s before a hypothetical fresh q>=z, let g be the actual relative pre-horizon. The initial horizon of its LOW-only (<z) suffix is g+q-1. Let M_s(v) be the size of the support of total terminal gains of all unused q>=z whose LOW suffix terminates by elapsed v, as in the preceding note. That support ranges over infinitely many candidate primes, not only over the probable actual selections. We prove a lower bound in the previous fixed-power parameter regime.

Fix theta in (2/3,1), z=ceil(U^theta), and v=floor(U^(3theta-2)(log U)^(3/2)), as in the preceding proposed parameters. In particular v grows by a positive power and v=o(z^alpha) for a fixed alpha strictly between (3theta-2)/theta and 1. Use target LOW primes p in [z^alpha,z) with p=1 mod 15. At an arbitrary pre-horizon g and time s, write C3=c_3+g mod 3. Construct the hypothetical LOW suffix after q as follows:
* If C3 !=1, prescribe the new horizon residue 0 mod 3. This is possible with a NONZERO q residue because q=1-C3 mod 3.
* If C3=1, prescribe the new horizon residue 1 mod 3 (q=1 mod 3). Start with a single even-side p=3 event, waiting ell<=6 for its next clock, and increasing horizon by 2. It brings the horizon residue to 0 mod 3.
In the first case let ell=0 and d0=0; in the second let d0=2. After this optional prefix require exactly one p event, on the odd side (pre-horizon -1 mod p). The required initial p residue is -1 if no prefix, -3 if prefix. If prefix, -3 is not eligible at p>=7, so it cannot compete with p=3. The one p event leaves residue -2 mod p, which is not eligible. Since p=1 mod 15, its increment p-1 does not reactivate prime 3 or 5. Require the initial prescribed p residue not equal c_p+g-1 mod p, so the resulting q residue is nonzero. Also require that the next odd-side p clock after time s+ell occurs by s+v.

For every other prime l<z, choose the hypothetical new horizon residue x_l so that:
(i) x_l != c_l+g-1 (mod l), ensuring q is nonzero mod l;
(ii) l is ineligible at all visited horizons of the intended suffix. There are at most three such horizons (initial, optional +2, and final +d0+p-1).
For l>=7 the exclusions are at most six: with the prefix, the two forbidden pairs before and after +2 overlap at -1, so their union has size three, and the final horizon adds at most two, plus one nonzero-q exclusion. Without prefix there are at most five. Thus an allowed residue exists. At l=5, with prefix the choices 0 and 3 remain noneligible also after +2, and the final p-1 increment is 0 mod 5; at most one of those two choices is excluded by (i). Without prefix there are three noneligible residues and at most one extra exclusion. The prime 3 and the target p were handled above. These choices ensure that the actual LOW-only path consists of precisely the optional 3 and the single p, then terminates, not just an admissible itinerary that may lose to another clock. All q residues are units; by CRT and Dirichlet's theorem infinitely many prime q realize them. Thus excluding any finite set of already used labels makes no difference. Each qualified target p gives a distinct terminal gain d0+p-1 for the fixed prehistory. No actual large q of modest size is promised by Dirichlet's theorem, and this is why the lower bound concerns infinite candidate support only.

Uniform high-probability count of qualified p: fix arbitrary even g in [0,U^2], integer s in [1,U], common parity, optional-prefix choice and ell in [0,6] with ell=0 for no prefix. For each target p the two coordinate conditions have probability
    (1-1/p) * (# odd ticks in (s+ell,s+v])/p >= c v/p
for large U, since v=o(z^alpha)<=p and distinct odd ticks in that interval have distinct residues mod p. These conditions are independent across target primes for the fixed parameters, conditional on parity. The fixed-modulus Mertens theorem in progressions gives
    sum_(z^alpha<=p<z, p=1 mod 15) 1/p
       -> (1/phi(15)) log(1/alpha)>0.
Hence expected qualified count >=c_theta v. Chernoff's inequality for independent Bernoulli variables gives probability exp(-c'_theta v) of having fewer than half that lower mean. Union over the O(U^3) pairs (g,s) and finitely many prefix and parity choices still costs o(1). We do not condition on adaptive prehistories here: the bound holds SIMULTANEOUSLY for every such g,s. By the earlier first-selection union bound, probability of selecting any p>=U through U is O(1/log U); otherwise g<=U^2 since at most U events, each gain<U. It follows that with probability 1-o(1) every actual fresh high event by T<=U satisfies M_s(v)>=c_theta v for the infinite support.

This lower bound obstructs the PREVIOUS specific choice M=ceil(U^(3theta-2) sqrt(log U)), since M/v->0. More precisely take T=floor(Y sqrt(log Y)), U=T+2Y, and v,z,M as above. The previous BadTime includes the event tau_z>v for the initial LOW ghost. Conditional on its low coordinates and tau_z<=v, absence of any fresh high selection by T implies that no p>=z matches on the frozen LOW terminal during the T-v ticks at the end. By the exact cutoff insertion formula the probability of this is at most
    product_(z<=p<=(T-v)/2 prime) (1-2/p) = theta^2+o(1)
by Mertens (log z/log T->theta). Any divergence from the initial low trace is a fresh high selection. Thus probability of some fresh high through T is at least (1-theta^2-o(1))*Pr(tau_z<=v). With the simultaneous support lower bound, BadSpread (M_s(v)>M at some fresh event) has at least this probability minus o(1). Consequently
    Pr(BadTime)+Pr(BadSpread) >= 1-theta^2-o(1).
This proves the two previous sufficient hypotheses cannot BOTH be o(1) with the specific proposed v,M. It does not invalidate the previous general upper bound, or prove the original tail criterion false. Finite V=8Y support samples could not detect arbitrary enormous CRT-realizing q. A larger M and/or a weighted candidate count is needed. We now replace the infinite unweighted support completely.

## 2. Uniform inverse branching degree in a polynomial horizon window

Truncate the analysis to fresh q<V=U (round V if needed); probability any selected p>=V through U is O(1/log U) by first-selection union, hence discard this event. This does NOT replace the infinite process by a finite one when applying conditional quiet-gap probabilities; only large selected labels in the finite observed prefix are deleted. All actual relative horizons through U then <=U^2. For arbitrary even relative horizon h in [0,3U^2] let
    B(h)=#{LOW prime l<z : c_l+h = 0 or -2 (mod l)}.
An executed LOW event ending at h must have its label in this set, since both divisibility sides land at 0 or -2. The predecessor relative horizon is then uniquely h-(l-1). This reverse graph ignores clocks and includes all potential eligible reverse steps, so it is an upper bound on actual clock paths. At fixed h the count is a sum of independent Bernoulli(2/l), mean O(log log U). By Chernoff or the standard exponential-moment bound P(B(h)>=b)<=(e mean/b)^b with b=ceil(log U), a union over at most 3U^2+1 horizons shows
    P(max B(h)>b)=o(1).
In fact the bound is smaller than any fixed inverse power of U. It is a property of the LOW horizon coordinates only, measurable at time zero if those are revealed. No random assumption about actual attained horizons is required. Call its failure BadReverse.

On the good event, for any terminal horizon in the window, the number of distinct predecessor horizons reachable by at most k LOW events is <=(b+1)^k (tree bound including zero steps, or induction with a stay choice). This counts distinct starts, not labels or possible clocks. If a hypothetical q<V at prehistory (s,g) has a LOW suffix of at most k events and terminates, its start g+q-1 is among these reverse predecessors, and q is uniquely determined by that start. Its gain is at most k(z-1). Restrict to k=O(log U/log log U) so all such suffixes from g<=U^2 and q<V remain inside [0,3U^2] for large U.

## 3. Weighted terminal reactivation of previously selected high labels

Condition on the prehistory before tick s, revealing all p<z initially, as in the prior notes. Work at histories with g<=U^2 and good reverse degree; both are measurable. For a previously selected high p>=z, consider candidate unused fresh q in a dyadic band [Q,2Q) subset of [z,2V), with q<V. Only consider candidates whose full LOW-only suffix has at most k events, regardless of its elapsed time. Its terminal horizon lies in [g+Q-1,g+2Q-1+kz]. The number of possible even terminal horizons there at which p is eligible is
    O(1+(Q+kz)/p) <= O((k+1) Q/z)
because Q>=z,p>=z. For each such horizon the reverse fiber bound gives at most (b+1)^k candidate q. Thus the sum of q^-2 over these reactivating candidates in this band is at most
    O((k+1)(b+1)^k/(z Q)).
Sum geometric bands starting at Q=z. The resulting bound for a single previously selected p is O((k+1)(b+1)^k/z^2), independent of terminal offset SUPPORT size. The same q may be counted for more than one p, which is harmless. By the exact conditional-history hazard for unused q, at most 2/q^2 at s<=U (z^2>=2U), and E K_s=O(s/(z log z)), the probability of any such fresh event through U reactivating a PREVIOUS high label at a <=k-step LOW terminal, on the good reverse/window states and q<V, is at most
    O((k+1)(b+1)^k U^2/(z^3 log z)).
For k=floor(kappa log U/log log U), 0<kappa<3theta-2, (b+1)^k=U^(kappa+o(1)); the bound is o(1). No condition on the infinite endpoint offset support remains. This handles old high labels, not the fresh q itself. It is also independent of elapsed suffix length, as long as the <=k-step hypothesis holds.

## 4. Exclude own q reactivation by a medium-prime clock guard

Take r=floor(z/(2k)), for large U r>=11, r^2>=4U and log r asymp log z. Now use the separate conditional history filtration revealing only p<r initially. This is legitimate for estimating interference probabilities; section3 used a different filtration to count endpoints, and only unconditional bounds are combined. Call a fresh HIGH event still the FIRST selection of q>=z; its conditional hazard is O(1/(z log z)) in this filtration. Previously selected medium or high p>=r have known time coordinates; unused p>=r have conditional selection hazard O(1/(r log r)) as a population, by the exact law and r^2>=4U.

Choose v=floor(U^rho), 0<rho<3theta-2. Then v<z-2 and U+v<=2U. After each fresh high q selected by U, forbid any event with p>=r during the next v ticks. The new q has no possible clock (own spacing q-2>v). A previously selected p>=r may have a possible clock in that interval; for any realized p time phase at most O(v(1+U/p)) candidate start ticks s<=U have this property. The previous selection is history-measurable before s, and P(p seen by U)<=U/p^2. Multiply by fresh high hazard and sum as in the clock-guard note:
    P(any such known-medium-or-high clock guard violation)
     <= O(v U/(z r log z log r) + v U^2/(z r^2 log z log r)).
An unused medium or high label may be selected after the fresh q; condition at the fresh high selection time, then sum the conditional hazard over v future ticks and over fresh high starts (expected count O(U/(z log z))). This gives
    O(v U/(z r log z log r)).
These estimates do not assume that medium labels' actual coordinates are fresh upon jumping. Both are o(1) for the stated power choices since r=z/polylog U, rho<3theta-2<2theta-1. In addition, the probability of any high selection in the first v ticks is O(v/(z log z)) by the fresh hazard until the first high. The horizon/time prefix after each fresh q, on the complement of guard failures, agrees with the <z ghost through elapsed v and uses only labels <r: every actual event in the interval has label <r, hence is also the least matching LOW label; at every quiet actual tick there is no matching LOW label either. This agreement is valid even if the LOW-only suffix does not finish. If it finishes within v and uses <=k events, its total gain satisfies d<=k(r-1)<z-1<=q-1. Section1 of the fresh landing note then ensures that the fresh q is not eligible at that terminal (q>=5). Known high labels were controlled by section3. Medium labels <z, selected or not, are ineligible by LOW termination itself.

This replaces the prior own-divisor-support counting by a medium-clock guard plus actual depth restriction. It does not prove that actual suffix depths are <=k. Note that v can exceed r in some choices not used here; our chosen parameters ensure v=o(r). No condition of bounded gain is imposed separately.

## 5. Updated sufficient criterion

Let T=floor(Y sqrt(log Y)), w=2Y, U=T+w, z=ceil(U^theta) with 2/3<theta<1, and choose 0<rho,kappa<3theta-2, v=floor(U^rho), k=floor(kappa log U/log log U), r=floor(z/(2k)). Define BadTime as before: the initial LOW-only (<z) lifetime exceeds v OR some actual fresh high event by T has a counterfactual LOW-only suffix exceeding v. Define BadDepth as existence of such a fresh high event by T with LOW suffix finishing within v but taking more than k LOW events. No initial depth cap is needed. All other exceptional events have o(1) total probability: actual selection >=U through U, BadReverse, old high terminal reactivation for <=k depth, known/fresh medium guard failures, and initial high interruption before v. On the complement, by induction the initial LOW path finishes by v; every following high event is fresh, the subsequent LOW suffix finishes by v in <=k events, and at each LOW terminal no low or known high label is eligible. The conditional quiet-gap opportunity test from earlier notes therefore applies without conditioning on future good events, with success probability c_theta>0 under actual revealed history (all <z coordinates plus selected labels). Each failed test reaches a next opportunity within w+v on the good event and sigma_Y>T. At least m=floor((T-v)/(w+v)) tests start by T and fail there. Thus
    Pr(sigma_Y>T) <= Pr(BadTime)+Pr(BadDepth)+o(1)+(1-c_theta)^m
                  = Pr(BadTime)+Pr(BadDepth)+o(1).
The remaining two conditions concern ACTUAL low continuations after realized fresh jumps, not all candidate q. If both were o(1) along a diverging sequence, finite-time cutoff coupling would prove the clipped-height tail condition and remove the small physical cell-stop residual. This is a sufficient criterion, not proof of either bound. In particular actual continuation depths can be large; the finite sample below is far from satisfying the small depth condition. No assertion of improvement over the original tail criterion as a matter of logical strength (other than removal of the contradictory unweighted support condition at its previous parameters).

## 7. Residual

The weighted reverse-depth bound instead removes known-high endpoint reactivation without any unweighted support condition; medium guards address the own fresh label. Remaining: control actual LOW suffix time and depth after adaptive high jumps, or find a weaker averaged criterion allowing some long/deep suffixes.
