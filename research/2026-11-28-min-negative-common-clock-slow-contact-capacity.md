## 1. Universe of actual keys

Let L=log R, T=ceil(8 sqrt(2R+1)), and use canonical roots A in [R,2R] with 6|A. Permit actual histories through any original age <=C T, where C is a fixed positive constant (e.g. C=2 to cover pre-observation plus observation). All centers are even in [R-2CT-6,2R] and heights h=x/2+n in [0,CT+3] by checked canonical height/gain. A firing key is its source position a, its posttick absolute clock n, winning prime p, and sign s=+1 for even n, -1 for odd n. Its destination is x=a-p+1. The calendar and height pins are
    p | x-(1-s),  p | h-b_s, b_+=0, b_-=3,
    n=0 mod2p (plus), n=p+2 mod2p (minus),
    p<=2CT+7.
A slow arrival means that at least one actual history realizing this key waited at its source for a FULL w>=U=R^alpha ticks from birth or the preceding firing, where 1/4<alpha<1/2 is fixed. The full wait w<=2 qmin(a) (positive first wait <= the calendar period of the least neighbor factor), hence source neighbors are U/2-rough and p>=U/2. A source is large enough that the fired neighbor p*v has v>1, also U/2-rough. These facts do not rely on a root having been rough at birth or on any label restrictions earlier in its history. A physical key may be slow for one history and fast for another, depending on arrival time; the arithmetic universe below only requires existence of a slow realization. We count distinct physical keys/pairs, not all root-pair incidences with multiplicity.

## 2. Two slow arrivals at the same physical state

Two different moving predecessors of the same state (x,n) have the same sign by clock parity, and distinct primes p,q; equal prime would give the same predecessor position. Their common destination obeys
    x=(1-s)+pqt,  t>=1,
and h-b_s divisible by pq. Since pq>=U²/4>>T, the height strip and |h-b_s|<=CT+6 imply EXACTLY h=b_s for sufficiently large R. Consequently for fixed x,p,q,s there is at most one clock n, namely b_s-x/2. Also the number of distinct slow moving predecessors at any such state is bounded by floor(log(2R+1)/log(U/2)), at most 3 eventually since 4 alpha>1; this follows from their distinct primes dividing x-(1-s)>0. In particular a nonreset-height state cannot have two slow moving predecessors.

We now apply the spatial double-sieve count from e51533d to arbitrary slow firing SOURCES, not necessarily canonical initial roots. The argument there never used source divisibility by 6 for the same-sign pair count. For fixed q,t,s, the four forms in p
    p, tp+1, (qt+1)p-2s, qt p+q-2s
are U/2-rough, with all pairwise determinants nonzero (q>=5,t>=1), coefficients/determinants bounded by R^O(1). The source cofactors and opposite neighbors give the last three forms. The uniform four-form Selberg upper (e51533d section2) with X~R/(qt)>=U/2 bounds possible p by O(X(log L)^4/L^4). Independently q and qt+1 must be U/2-rough; for fixed t, their two-form upper on dyadic q intervals bounds the reciprocal sum by O((log L)^2/L), uniformly. Summing t<=O(R/U²) harmonically gives at most
    E_ss <<_alpha,C R(log L)^6/L^4 = o(R/L²)                 (1)
unordered pairs of different slow moving predecessor keys at the same physical state. The clock adds NO extra factor because h=b_s. The proof works for p,q arbitrarily close to the square-root cutoff, even when pq~R, by sieving p and q rather than a CRT parameter of length R/pq. The estimate is over the whole slab and all allowed absolute clocks. It does not count two roots that have already merged at the same predecessor as two separate incoming keys.

## 3. Slow arrivals into or near another rough center

Let z=U/2 and 0<=D<=L^B0 for any fixed B0. Say a slow arrival at x is near-rough if there is an even offset d with 0<=d<=D such that y=x-d satisfies qmin(y)>=z. In particular d=0 includes an arrival into a destination where another root has a long stationary phase (a full wait or residual/elapsed idle of length >=U suffices for roughness). Allow y to be twin; no assumption of finite twins.

For fixed p,s,d, write c=1-s and x=c+p v. The four integer affine forms in v are
    v+1, p v+p-2s, p v+c-d-1, p v+c-d+1.              (2)
The first two are the incoming source's fired cofactor and opposite neighbor, the last two are the target y±1. All are z-rough. Their distinct undivided shifts as forms in x are p,p-2s,-d-1,-d+1, with the sign correction in the first shift: more directly in x, the first form is (x+p-c)/p=(x+p+s-1)/p, and the second is x+p-1-s. Thus the four shifts are p+s-1,p-1-s,-d-1,-d+1. Their differences, other than +/-2, are among p+d-1 and its +/-2. Since p>=z>>D, all shifts distinct. In v all coefficients/determinants have polynomial magnitude, and all bad primes >=11 divide p or (p+d-1)(p+d-3)(p+d+1). This last fact follows directly by cross multiplication of the slopes and constants in (2).

Use the refined four-form upper of 54a7bf6 sections2-3, preserving the actual bad-prime factor F4(p+d-1) = prod_{ell>=11 prime dividing (p+d-1)((p+d-1)^2-4)} (1-4/ell)^(-1). The factor from p is uniformly bounded. The parameter interval has length X~R/p >= c R^.5, so the uniform spatial count per p,d,s is
    O(R F4(p+d-1)/(p L^4)).
For each such position, the clock n must satisfy one residue modulo 2p and lie in an interval of length CT+3 by the height strip. Since p<=2CT+7, there are O_C(T/p) clocks. Hence for each fixed d,s the total weight by p-1 of all near-rough slow keys is bounded by
    O(R T/L^4 * sum_{z<=p<=2CT+7} F4(p+d-1)/p)
    = O(R T/L^4)                                         (3)
using the uniform shifted-prime reciprocal average in 54a7bf6 section2 (shift d-1 fixed while summing p). In particular the FULL preceding wait w of any history realizing such a key satisfies w<=2p, so the same bound up to constant holds for its maximum possible full wait weight. Summing d and signs gives
    sum_{near-rough slow keys} (p-1) << R T(D+1)/L^4,
    sum_{near-rough slow keys} sup full wait << R T(D+1)/L^4.   (4)
These are physical-key capacities for arbitrary histories, not a bound on uncapped root multiplicities.

For a top slow arrival, impose additionally p>=Y=T/L^B with fixed B>0. This is above z for large R. For each dyadic p band [P,2P] above Y, (54a7bf6 (1)) gives sum F4(p+d-1)/p² << 1/(P log P). Geometric summation bounds the total reciprocal-square sum by O(1/(Y L)). Thus the NUMBER of top slow keys near rough centers satisfies
    E_top,near(D) << R T(D+1)/(Y L^5)
                   = R(D+1)L^(B-5).                     (5)
Choose B=2 and D=0: there are O(R/L³)=o(R/L²) distinct top slow arrival keys at rough destinations throughout the slab, regardless of how many preceding events their histories contain. This includes asynchronous contact with a stationary occupant at the destination, not merely two simultaneous moving predecessors. Taking D=L^eta with eta<1 still gives o(R/L²). Formula (4) remains useful for all slow labels and larger D, where a count bound would be too weak.

## 4. Exact common-clock merger-loss accounting

At one absolute tick n, consider a finite set of distinct occupied positions at n-1, with no new births in that tick. The actual deterministic map sends each to one destination at n. For each destination bin let i=0 or1 indicate an idle predecessor, let k be the number of slow moving predecessors (full wait>=U along the retained actual histories), and f the number of other moving predecessors. Classify a predecessor as slow if its selected representative has such a full wait; if multiple histories at the same position choose a representative, which is an actual path. If the bin is nonempty, its contribution to the reduction of distinct occupied states is exactly i+k+f-1. It is bounded by the following branch charges:
- if i=0 and k>=1, keep one slow predecessor; loss <=f+binom(k,2); if k=0 loss<=f;
- if i=1, keep the idle predecessor and charge loss f+k exactly. Split the slow charges into top slow arrivals at rough destinations, non-top slow arrivals at rough destinations, and slow arrivals at nonrough destinations (qmin<z). The last case has a short full stationary phase (w< U) for the incumbent if its outgoing event completes, and a short residual idle if the horizon ends sooner. An incumbent may have arrived after the preceding calendar firing, but its full wait is still <=2qmin. The distinction concerns the arithmetic roughness of the destination, not a known future realization.

For a synchronized observation interval after all selected roots have been born, with no new births or unrelated deletions, summing this exact loss identity gives the difference between initial and final number of occupied states. For multiple standard spatial windows of width comparable to T, apply the identity within each window and sum. A physical key or pair can occur in at most O_C(1) windows, since the original root A lies between x and x+2CT+6; standard window IDs are disjoint intervals of width comparable to T. Thus the global charges to slow-slow moving pairs and top-slow arrivals at rough destinations have total capacity
    O_C(E_ss + E_top,near(0)) = O(R/L³ + R(log L)^6/L⁴)
    = o(R/L²).                                            (6)
If a collection starts with total N0 >>R/L² DISTINCT occupied states across windows and loses a fixed positive fraction by common-window retirement, then all but o(R/L²) of that loss must be charged to (a) fast moving predecessors, (b) non-top slow arrivals into a rough occupied destination, or (c) slow arrivals into nonrough occupied destinations. This is an exact reduction of the remaining density-scale first-contact problem, not a proof that these remaining charges are small. A bin may also admit several slow and fast predecessors at once; the branch selection above prevents double-counting them as independent first contacts. The hypothesis of a large starting distinct population at synchronized square-root observation windows is NOT supplied by (1)-(6). It cannot be inferred from the earlier sparse envelope packing.

## 5. Apply the capacities to capped life/occupation objectives

For a cap M on live roots at each physical bin within each standard window, each distinct moving predecessor carries at most M roots. At a merger with d distinct incoming predecessors, retaining the preferred predecessor's roots first and then filling unused capacity loses at most M roots per other predecessor. This is a feasible local choice for the cap at the new state; each removed root is retired permanently and never resurrected. For any such cap clipping during an observation interval of length T, each lost root forfeits at most T future live ticks. Therefore the total future-life charge of losses of the two types bounded in (6) is at most
    O(M T [R/L³ + R(log L)^6/L⁴]).
For the original full canonical population N~R/6 and M~L^2.1, normalized by NT this is
    O(L^(-.9)+L^(-1.9)(log L)^6)=o(L^(-.25)).              (7)
Thus these types of common-clock merging are negligible even at the original occupation target scale. For a sparse input population of size >>R/L² the analogous conclusion holds in relative count for cap1, not necessarily for cap L^2.1. No cap policy is claimed to preserve all roots; fast/non-top/short-idle losses and initial collisions may dominate.

The arbitrary-label near-rough wait/gain capacity (4) has another use. With M~L^2.1 and D=floor(L^1.5), the sum of completed waiting times or gains at near-rough slow keys over capped root incidences, divided by NT for N~R, is O(M(D+1)/L^4)=O(L^(-.4))=o(L^(-.25)). For waits, a full source wait is charged only once at the completed event; within an observation window its observed part is no larger. Multiplicity is bounded at the immediately preceding bin, which must be included in the FullCapped condition. The few possible window copies have the same O(1) overhead. No lifetime-loss bound for arbitrary p follows from this gain-weighted estimate alone.

Every other completed slow event has no z-rough even site within backward distance D of its landing. Therefore any subsequent stationary phase of full wait>=U (including an incomplete phase already idle for U) must start more than D farther to the left, by monotonicity. If there is no subsequent long phase before retirement, this is the other alternative. If the path is continued, every phase inside that interval has next wait <U and a decrease >=2; it leaves the interval in at most U(floor(D/2)+1) additional ticks. This uses (4) to extend the earlier first-exit neighborhood exclusion to all slow physical arrivals and all capped histories. It does NOT bound gain/time from above or imply that the short intermediate phases have long elapsed time; a fast large jump can cross the entire interval. The near-square weighted good field upper from earlier notes remains a separate statement with its own unreviewed multi-form components.

## 6. Remaining obstacle and next use

The two new bounds distinguish slow arrival branches from already merged multiplicities. They provide a common-ABSOLUTE-clock capacity over entire arbitrary-length histories, unlike e51533d which concerned first exits only. They do not establish a density-scale lower for distinct occupied states at common square-root retirement. In particular a fast moving predecessor can land on a long idle trajectory, and two paths can merge during short phases. Neither is excluded by height product alone (only one moving prime may be present). It would be incorrect to estimate all collisions by (6), or claim the former R/L² source population remains separated through square-root time. No .40 low-gain witness or 4.85 claim. A next concrete question is to bound losses charged to fast arrivals by a renewal of distinct representatives at later slow phases, accounting for their preceding short-phase ancestry, rather than counting all short keys as if they had long duration. Alternatively use (4) to make a compressed graph of long phases separated by short intervals, but a genuine lower on its number of occupied nodes is still required.
