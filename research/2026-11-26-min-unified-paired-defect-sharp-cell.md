## 2. Both sides satisfy the radical restriction for the chosen label

For any original tagged event f at prehorizon E, and any odd prime p>y0 (largest factor of L0), define G_p^rad(f) by scanning backward on that SAME tag's completed events, excluding f. Start radical L0; stop before an event with label p or an event whose inclusion makes the lcm of periods exceed 4H. G is E minus earliest accepted prehorizon, zero if none. Here f's own label need NOT be p.

In an actual first-defect pair the chosen label p has 2p not dividing the unchanged lag Delta. Every selected label from the source crossing inclusive up to E exclusive has period dividing Delta on BOTH original histories. In particular neither member can have selected p in that interval, irrespective of the other member's label q. Therefore both contributing tagged events satisfy the same backward scan test with excluded label p. At a source atom K each original involved tag has at most 2P of energy capacity counting both ordered roles; this remains true even when the tag is not the chosen destination, by the old row and column bound. In both histories K is in the interval [E-G_p^rad(f), E] intersect [E-L_s-b_s,E], with b_l=P/a and b_h=B (relax both to B if desired). Count grid points in the intersection. For fixed grid, sector, distinct p,q define
    U_(P,s)(E,r|p)=2 sum_(original tag events f at E,r)
                         [min(G_p^rad(f),L_s+b_s)+P].
Then exact total pair charge satisfies
    D_(P,s)(E;p,q) <= min(U_(P,s)(E,p|p), U_(P,s)(E,q|p)).
The source atom involvement bound applies separately to the p and q side, since each contributing pair has exactly one member in each set of tags (p!=q). Orientation chosen by the old rule is retained; the two possible ordered focal roles are already covered by the factor two, not added again. The same conclusion holds after previous masks. For each sector average over the original m grids with zero on unusable grids:
    Dbar_s(E;p,q) <= mean_P min(U_p,U_q)
                  <= min(mean_P U_p, mean_P U_q).      (1)
The first bound is sharper. The discrete source-atom and modular candidate refinements from d16b4e0 may also be used bilaterally: on the q side candidate lag must be divisible by its actual prefix radical and not divisible by 2p, not 2q. No claim that the sum over all pair-key capacities is bounded by the old chosen-label cap summed over single keys, because the same tag can occur in multiple relaxed keys. Exact charge reindexing, unlike summing relaxed caps, conserves the old D.

At a late defect (prefix radical >=H/ell^.25) the two source physical populations already satisfy b_v,b_v'>n_c/sqrt(ell) after the previous mask. Formula (1) bounds these same charges using their actual destination-side populations as well. We do not infer that a large source group remains large at the destination without applying a separate stability condition; the bilateral bound itself needs no such inference. It applies to early and late sectors without new assumptions.

## 3. Two-prime spatial support and payments

If p!=q, both are eligible at E, so E congruent to either 1 or -1 modulo each prime. For fixed ordered distinct p,q there are at most 4 CRT residues mod pq. The global E interval has length O(X) and pq<=B²=O(X), so #E=O(X/(pq)), with the +1 absorbed. This remains valid if one of the two selected periods divides Delta. No prime-tuple lower estimate. Let Q=B/u, nu=number of the two labels >=Q. For each sector and each exact remaining distinct-label pair key pay its entire charge if
    Dbar_s(E;p,q)<= H u^nu/(u ell³).                  (2)
Use disjoint nu sectors. Sum using sum_p 1/p=O(ell), sum_{Q<=p<=B}1/p=O(ell/u). The total energy cost per sector is O(XH/(u ell))=O(NT/ell); fixed two sectors and clock coefficient c_a=O(1) preserve o(NT). This new mask is applied after the old single-label thin key payments and late masks; the old charge need not have been small at the single key. The new pair-key residual D_pair,rem<=D_old, with difference O(NT/ell). In particular in the high-high distinct-label sector the absolute energy cutoff is H u/ell³. By (1), if E has few original tags on E,q, the charge can be paid even with enormous E,p population. For high focal pursuit C_h=L_h+B+P_max=Theta(B exp(1/tau)), all keys with either side's projected population <=H u/(2 C_h ell³) are paid. This cutoff is order B u^(3/4)/ell^4 for the default tau=1/log ell. The same formula with C_l=(1/m)sum_usable(L_l+P/a+P) applies to low. The more precise bilateral radical-suffix sums can improve these sufficient checks. We have not paid all distinct-label keys.

For same label p=q, E fixes its sign, hence both departures obey the same residue modulo 2p. With signed unchanged input lag Delta and waits w_x,w_y, let h=w_y-w_x. A same-label defect requires
    h!=0, h even, |h|<2r(E), Delta+h=0 mod 2p.
Also 2p does not divide Delta; otherwise both candidates periodic and the waits equal. At a prefix radical R not containing p, Delta=kR, 0<|k|<=4H/R. Hence for fixed R,p and actual r(E) the eligible signed k are in at most O(r(E)) residue classes modulo p (R even and p coprime to R), with O(1+H/(Rp)) representatives per class. This bounds possible nonzero lag multipliers by O(r(E)(1+H/(Rp))), also trivially O(H/R). When R is large it is a finite short congruence test, not an independence-based saving on all source slots. We keep the same-label sector unchanged in the sufficient objective. No sum over all possible R with an unjustified small budget. Note h uses actual waits at the two arrivals, not a difference of selected prime sizes.

## 4. Reintegrate the local excess lemma: sharper common source capacity

For any contiguous completed event segment, write g_i=p_i-1, E_i=(2w_i-4g_i)_+. The earlier weighted-boundary-flux lemma proves
    sum_i E_i <= sum_i g_i+4.
For completeness the proof uses E_i<=4, E_i<=g_i for non-3, and the following forbidden or restricted words. Consecutive 3,3 has second wait exactly 1, so its excess zero. q,3,q with q>=5 is impossible since its q prehorizon shift q+1 changes residue +/-1 to 0 or 2 mod q. 5,3,7,3,5 impossible since shift14 changes +/-1 to 0 or3 mod5. Pair each positive-excess 3 with its preceding non-3, except a possible first event. Blocks (q,3) have excess<=8, gain q+1. Only (5,3) can have surplus<=2; the next block cannot start with5 and either supplies >=2 slack or is (7,3), after which a further block cannot start with5 or7 and supplies >=2 slack. At most a first isolated 3 and a final incomplete compensating group leave surplus2 each. Thus the lemma holds for any contiguous segment. It follows that
    sum_i w_i <= (5/2) sum_i g_i+2.                  (3)
This improves the raw w<=3g bound on every contiguous segment with long total gain, not a claim that individual events satisfy w<=2.5g.

At a good right crossing k with label q and n_k crossed atoms, either q<=P/a and the whole right cell (noncrossing events since previous crossing plus k) has gain G_cell<=P+q-1 and n_k=1, or q>=aP and only the crossing itself can be a good assigned focal event. Other crossing labels have zero good weights. In the small case all retained assigned good clock is a subset of this contiguous right cell, so by (3)
    W_l+W_h <= (5/2)(P+q-1)+2
             <= [(5/2)(1+1/a)+2/P] P.
In the large case W_l+W_h<=w_k<=2q=2g_k+2. As g_k<=(n_k+1)P and n_k>=a-2, we obtain
    W_l+W_h <= [2+2/(a-2)+2/((a-2)P)] P n_k.
Uniformly in the original analytic grids, write c_small=5/2+o(1), c_large=2+o(1), using a->infinity and P_min->infinity. These are sharper capacities for both sectors together, per original source tag-atom. No alteration of the EXACT old energy normalization c_a, which continues to convert exact forward charge to clock. In particular it would be wrong simply to replace 3 D_old by (5/2) D_old in the previous sufficient objective: if one changed its denominator, the energy itself would increase. We use the new constants ONLY for direct upper envelopes on clock units, or weighted raw boundary occupation.

## 5. A constant improvement of the joint raw-cut sufficient test

Keep the exact joint substochastic kernel of 0bc6ab1. At an atom, for a stable focal tag i and sector s let beta_i^s be the sum of partner coefficients of the desired admissible backward and forward cut (e.g. use reverse first-meeting tree, forward residue-exact prefix tree, enlarged allowed sets including all old and new residuals). The kernel before masks is shared by both sectors as proved in 0bc6ab1, and beta_i^s<=1 even if both types of destinations accessible. Define beta_i=max_s beta_i^s, taking beta_i^s=0 if the sector unavailable at this source. Can keep beta_i positive also for zero-weight rows to obtain a relaxed bound, but take it zero on rows without any relevant good source eligibility. These fractions can be computed by grouped tree sums with unit focal weights or relaxed by the union mask B_i or D_i of 0bc6ab1. No need to duplicate low and high source occupancy. The desired exact clock at this source is bounded by
    sum_i sum_s w_i^s beta_i^s <= sum_i (w_i^l+w_i^h) beta_i.
Classify each source by small or large crossing q and use section 4. Define R_small,R_large as the grid averages of sum P beta_i in each type over all original source atoms/classes/tags. If using binary union occupancy, beta_i can instead be 1 for any accessible allowed target, 0 otherwise; the same bound holds. Both are nonnegative, total <=N(H+P_max). Then
    B_rem+c_a D_pair,rem
      <= (5/2+o(1)) R_small+(2+o(1)) R_large.        (4)
The exact forward component includes same-label charges unchanged. This is a direct upper bound on the exact residual clock after all payments, including (2); no new source weight normalization. The exact row-specific weighted tree bound may still be smaller; take the minimum.

Thus on the same admissible cut masks and same frozen populations a sufficient condition is
    liminf [(5/2) R_small+2 R_large]/(NT)<1/2.        (5)
In particular liminf (R_small+R_large)/(NT)<1/5 suffices, compared with the previous crude raw occupation sufficient threshold 1/6 from 0bc6ab1. If R_small=o(NT) on a subsequence, then liminf R_large/(NT)<1/4 on that subsequence suffices. This is a genuine constant change in the sufficient raw-occupancy threshold (not just a little-o deletion), and uses the earlier independent local excess packing result. It is logically weaker than the old 1/6 test when the SAME binary union or row-fraction targets are used. It is not logically weaker than the old exact residual test or exact weighted tree test, which can be smaller than the raw envelope. No assertion that R_small=o(NT), or that (5) actually holds on the original ensembles; those remain the target of a global occupation estimate. Formula (1) and (2) give tighter admissible destination supersets/cuts for that evaluation, with no double counting. Main and independent branches not modified. No Lean compilation.
