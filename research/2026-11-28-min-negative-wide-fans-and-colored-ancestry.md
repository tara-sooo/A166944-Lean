## 1. Wide direct fans without nested congruences

Let P grow, fix a sufficiently small absolute epsilon>0. There are >>P/log^2 P primes p in [P,2P) with p=1 mod 12 and p-2 free of primes <=P^epsilon. This is the classical lower sieve on primes: for squarefree d of primes >=5 and d<=P^{1/3}, the conditions p=1 mod12,p=2 mod d give one reduced class mod12d. Apply Bombieri-Vinogradov to the von Mangoldt weighted interval, X=P/phi(12), g(d)=1/phi(d). The sum of absolute remainder terms is O_A(P/log^A P) since moduli 12d are below the BV level. The dimension-one fundamental lemma with s=log(P^{1/3})/log(P^epsilon) sufficiently large gives weighted mass >>P/log P by Mertens. Prime powers contribute O(sqrt(P)log^2 P), so the desired prime count follows. The shift -2 instead of +2 and the fixed mod12 condition preserve reduced classes and do not change sieve dimension.

Choose any r distinct such labels with 2r+2<=P^epsilon. Require x=0 mod12, x divisible by each selected p, x+-1 rough at Z>=2P, and x+p and x+p-2 free of primes <p for each selected p. This ensures precisely the previous verified shortReset_merge_birth hypothesis for A=x+p-1.

For q<=2r+2 force x=0 mod q (use 12 for q=2,3). All selected p are primes >q; p-2 is q-rough, so the source exclusions hold. For a selected q, force x=0 mod q. For any selected p>q, p mod q !=0 and p-2 mod q !=0: since P<=q<p<2P, the only possible positive multiple of q for p-2 is q itself, ruled out by p,q=1 mod12. Thus all selected-prime constraints hold without any nesting or mutual congruence 1 mod each other. For every other q<=2P, at most 2(r+1)<q forbidden classes (including the target). This establishes CRT compatibility. Let B=12 prod_{5<=q<=2P}q. Sieve remaining q>2P up to Z=R^delta exactly as in the previous note, summing over allowed CRT classes; uniform dimension 2, level R^{1/2}. A lower bound for the density rho of those classes is exp(-O(r log P)): forced small primes cost <=O(r log r) in log density, selected primes <=r log(2P), and for the other q>2r+2, if q<=4(r+1) use one allowed residue (cost O(r log r) even without prime counting), otherwise excluded fraction <=1/2 and cost O(r sum_{q<=2P}1/q)=O(r loglog P). This handles the primes just above 2r+2 where the excluded fraction may be close to one. Thus for B=R^{o(1)}, r log P=o(L), L=log R,
    # centers >> R L^-2 exp(-O(r log P)) - O(B sqrt(R) log R).
Remove O((R/T+1)P) integers at window/slab edges with T=floor((Z-8)/3). This is negligible in the parameter regimes below. Every remaining center receives at least r+1 actual canonical births by its own birth, all in the same window, and remains stationary throughout the common observation. This is a physical-state, least-prime result via the existing checked local lemma, not relaxed spatial ancestry.

Two useful growing regimes:
(a) P=floor(L^{1/2}), r=floor(P^epsilon/4). Then log B=O(P log P)=o(L), r log P=o(L). At least R^{1-o(1)} centers have r+1>=c L^{epsilon/2} stationary actual roots each at polynomial time T~R^delta/3. More precise lower density is L^-2 exp(-O(L^{epsilon/2} log L)); it is NOT a negative-log-power mass lower.
(b) r=floor(log L/(loglog L)^2), P=ceil(r^{2/epsilon}). Then r log P=o(log L), log B=(log L)^{O(1)}=o(L); at least R L^{-2-o(1)} centers have r+1=(log L)^{1-o(1)} stationary roots each. This improves the previous Linnik nested construction in the joint density/multiplicity regime, while giving depth one rather than a hierarchical firing word.

For M~L^2.1 the constructed fans fit the cap. Counting one fan per center gives the corresponding lower bound on terminal clipped mass; no claim at near-square-root time. All one-step reset parents (including both signs) satisfy the verified spatial divisor pins p|x or p|(x-2), hence at most 1+omega(x)+omega(x-2)=O(L/log L) roots in a direct fan, already too small by the original ordinary twin upper sieve O(R/L^2) to meet R/L^.25 even if all such centers were available near sqrt(R). Deep noncanonical histories remain essential.

## 2. Contract the base walls and color the genuinely intermediate jumps

Return to the original T~sqrt(R) exp(-sqrt(L)), M~L^2.1. Choose positive even Q=2 product of odd primes <=z, with z->infinity slowly enough that log Q=o(loglog L), e.g. z=floor(sqrt(loglog L)). All asymptotics refer to sufficiently large R. Partition the containing spatial interval I=[R-3T-6,2R] into Q-cells, including partial end cells. The verified wall lemma implies that a firing p|Q never crosses a cell boundary. Every firing decreases x and every quiet tick keeps x fixed. In one visited cell there are at most Q/2 distinct even positions (partial cells no larger); hence if a prefix crosses k cell boundaries in the sense of k firing events that change cells (not the number of geometrical boundaries skipped), it visits at most k+1 cells and at most (k+1)Q/2 stationary phases. This is valid for arbitrary repeats, not a restriction to bounded total event depth.

Fix 0<a<1 and set Y=T/(log L)^a. Color a cross-cell transition HIGH if p>=Y, MID otherwise. All HIGH events along any canonical prefix up to common retirement have count h<=ceil((3T+6)/(Y-1))=O((log L)^a), by actual canonical gain bound. This includes pre-observation. Every MID crossing has p not dividing Q, thus p>z. Crossings of either color strictly decrease cell number.

Form a relaxed directed two-colored graph on cells: a colored edge exists when any admissible spatial edge of the specified color joins the two different cells. Multiple spatial edges of the same color between the same cells may be collapsed; retain both colors if necessary. Actual histories map to paths; do not infer actual reachability in the reverse direction. For large R, Y^3>2R+1. Thus any of x+-1, x, x-2 has at most two distinct prime factors >=Y. Verified spatial source and destination pins show both high out- and indegree per cell <=2Q (two forms, at most two factors each per even site). Define E(v)=max(1,max over even sites x of cell of 1+omega(x-2)+omega(x-1)+omega(x)+omega(x+1)). Both MID in-/out-degree <=Q E(v). Empty partial cells may be omitted. No assumption about independence of degrees.

## 3. Colored maximum-degree path inequality and asymptotics

For a set S of terminal cells let P_{d,h}(S) count all colored paths with at most d MID edges and h HIGH edges ending in S, allowing every starting cell and every length including zero. Let D(v)>=1 be max MID degree, H0>=1 a global HIGH degree bound, K>=1. Then
    P_{d,h}(S) <= 2^{d+h+1} H0^h [ |S| K^d + (d+h+1) sum_{D(v)>K} D(v)^d ].    (1)
For each of the at most 2^{d+h+1} binary color words of length <=d+h, a path all of whose vertices have MID degree <=K has <=K^d H0^h choices per fixed endpoint. Otherwise fix a maximum MID degree vertex v and its index; inside the induced subgraph of degree <=D(v) both the incoming and outgoing segment counts multiply to <=D(v)^d H0^h. Summation over all such v and indices proves (1). Equivalently use E(v) and threshold A log L in this argument, with D bounds Q E(v), K=Q A log L and the bad term Q^d sum_{E(v)>A log L} E(v)^d.

Let d=floor(c log L/loglog L), fixed c>0, h as in section 2. Elementary omega tails (factorial moment, as in prior reverse-depth note) imply #cells E>=k <=C R e^{-k} for k>=A log L, with A a sufficiently large fixed constant. Indeed a cell with E>=k contains an even site with the four-factor-count sum >=k-1. A large-degree site bound follows by selecting j distinct prime divisors of an integer <=2R+1 and using sum_{p<=2R+1}1/p=O(log L); union over the four shifts. Each exceptional cell has an exceptional site; no Q loss in this tail. Summation of k^d e^{-k} for k>=A log L is O((A log L+1)^d e^{-A log L}) because d/k=o(1). All good path factors have
    (Q A log L)^d=L^{c+o(1)},  (2Q)^h=L^{o(1)}, 2^{d+h+1}=L^{o(1)}.
Therefore for arbitrary fixed C0, increasing A if necessary,
    P_{d,h}(S) <= |S| L^{c+o(1)} + O(R L^{-C0}).                  (2)
A path determines its starting cell, which has <=Q possible canonical roots. Hence multiplying by Q=L^{o(1)} retains (2)'s exponents. This extends previous reverse-depth estimate to arbitrary in-cell event histories and every very high crossing allowed by gain. Not a bound on actual unrestricted MID depth.

## 4. Unconditional mandatory MID crossings at the original horizon

At age floor(T/2) any root with <=d MID crossings has <=h HIGH crossings. Its at most (d+h+1)Q/2 distinct stationary phases contain a run of >=H=floor(T/[4Q(d+h+1)]) consecutive quiet transitions, for all sufficiently large R; the number of firing transitions is at most the number of phases minus one, negligible. By the exact calendar wait bound, all odd factors of the two neighbors of that run's center exceed H/2. Thus that center is in the near-square-root rough set S_H (and the root supplies a colored prefix path to its cell). The previous non-twin upper sieve applies, since log(T/H)=O(loglog L+log Q)=o(sqrt(L)), so
    |S_H| <= twinCount + O(R L^-2.5).
For completeness the semiprime upper is uniform by the dimension-two upper fundamental lemma from Tao Notes 4, on r and q*r+-2 for fixed q in (H/2,sqrt(2R+1)], with sifting power small, distinct forbidden classes for small odd primes not equal to q, and remainders O(2^omega(d)); summing q by Chebyshev dyadic bound contributes O(L^-.5). The usual twin upper sieve is O(R/L^2). Taking the set of cells of these sites (cardinality no greater), (2) gives
    # {A: MID crossings by age floor(T/2) <=d}
      <= L^{c+o(1)} (twinCount + O(R L^-2.5)) + O(R L^-C0).
Consequently, unconditionally, for every fixed c<2, all but o(R) canonical roots already have more than c log L/loglog L MID crossings by age floor(T/2). This is a genuine asymptotic lower on actual crossing depth at the required near-square-root time, not a static or numerical statement. Under finite twins the stronger c<2.5 version holds. Every such counted event has z<p<Y and crosses a Q-cell. This demonstrates that neither low-prime runs nor the unrestricted top band can account for typical actual histories at this time. It does not bound gain from above.

For occupation, restrict a root prefix to <=d MID crossings and all visited cells satisfying E<=A log L. Since this is prefix-closed, for any physical bin its possible starting roots are bounded by Q times the good-path count for that endpoint cell, L^{c+o(1)}. Thus for c<2.1 this restriction is cap-safe incl pre-observation. For the raw shallow MID cohort (allow exceptional cells), divide phases into observed length <=H'=T L^{-b}, b>.25, versus longer. Short total normalized mass O((d+h+1)Q L^-b)=L^{-b+o(1)}. Long phases map to rough centers, and distinct (root,site) pairs are at most Q^2 times (2), since starting and ending cells have <=Q sites each. Each pair contributes <=T ticks. Hence
    eta_mid(d) <= L^{c+o(1)} twinCount/N
                  + O(L^{c-2.5+o(1)}+L^{-b+o(1)}+L^-C0).
Under finite twins it is o(L^-.25) for c<2.25, even with unrestricted low runs and all high events. With the unconditional twin upper, c<1.75 cannot furnish the desired L^-.25 lower even if twins are infinite. Exceptional paths alone similarly contribute arbitrarily small normalized mass by the bad term in (1) for all endpoint cells. The next quantitative goal is positive lower mass for actual MID-rich histories, not merely another wall decomposition.
