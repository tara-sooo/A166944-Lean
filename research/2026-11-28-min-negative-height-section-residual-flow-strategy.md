## 2. Exact graph and discount

L=log R. Use coordinates (x,h), h=x/2+n. Actual deterministic tick: idle (x,h+1), or with prime p, (x-p+1,h+1-(p-1)/2). Work on the finite even-x slab [R-4H,2R], 0<=h<=H, with canonical injection b(x,h)=1 when R<=x<=2R, 6|x, h=3, otherwise 0. Stop at the FIRST height H (no outgoing edge there), and remove edges exiting the slab or height domain. Upward height change at most 1 means first upward crossing reaches exactly H. This graph is acyclic: x strictly decreases on moving edges, h increases on idle edges. At height H only idle edges enter from h=H-1. Every vertex has at most one outgoing edge. For every edge u->v use weight w_uv=exp[-(log L)(x_u-x_v)/H] in (0,1]. Idle weights are 1.

Define C(v) recursively in topological order by
    C(v)=min(1,b(v)+sum_{u->v} w_uv C(u)).
It is a capped, discounted, nonnegative flow; no new root mass is introduced except b. It is not equal to uncapped reverse multiplicity, but is <=1 and <= the actual sum of discounted root ancestors on paths stopped at first H, by induction. For a root A at sink x, weight telescopes to L^(-(A-x)/H). Each root reaches at most one first-height sink. Age there equals H-3+(A-x)/2. Therefore roots with A-x>4H contribute total O(R/L^4) to the uncapped discounted sink sum, hence also bound the contribution of sinks with no root of gain<=4H. If sum_{h=H} C(v) >> R/L², then >>R/L² distinct sinks have a canonical ancestor of gain<=4H and age<=3H. Such paths never left the spatial slab before first H, because x decreases. This is an actual common-height result, not a common elapsed or signed clock result. No twin or low-gain assertion follows just from it.

## 3. One-sided residual stability (new exact paper lemma)

For any trial array F(v) in [0,1], let
    r(v)=[F(v)-min(1,b(v)+sum_{u->v} w_uv F(u))]_+ .
Then
    sum_{h=H} C(v) >= sum_{h=H} F(v) - sum_all_vertices r(v).    (*)
Proof: d(v)=[F(v)-C(v)]_+ <= r(v)+sum_{u->v} w_uv d(u), by monotonicity and 1-Lipschitz clipping. Iterate in the finite DAG. Each r(u) can propagate to at most one sink along the unique outgoing path, with weight <=1. Sum at sinks. This proves (*) without assuming any bound on actual ancestor multiplicities or independence of orbit residues. In particular exact local subsolutions with r=0 certify a lower, and approximate subsolutions can lose only their integrated positive defect.

If F is the clipped discounted flow of a trial deterministic graph with the same vertices, b, stopping rule and edge weights (weights determined by displacement), compare the graphs. At vertices with unchanged outgoing edge the contribution cancels in the incoming comparison. At a changed trial edge u->v, the positive residual contribution at v is at most w_uv F(u)<=F(u). A new true edge contributes nonnegative mass and cannot increase positive residual. Thus
    sum_{h=H} C(v) >= sum_{h=H} F(v)-sum_{u with changed trial edge} F(u).
This statement applies to any truncated-label or coarse graph with outdegree<=1 and the same DAG orientation, provided F is its actual clipped discounted flow. No assumption of trial reachability being canonical in the true graph. It converts true occupation lower into a trial-flow lower plus a weighted defect upper involving ONLY the trial flow. This is the proposed main tool.

## 4. A concrete obstacle and the first computational/analytic test

For the prime-cutoff trial graph using only odd primes <=z, its outgoing edge differs from the actual one only when the trial was idle and actual winner >z. At preheight h, eligible p divides 2h+3 (even clock) or 2h-3 (odd clock) and the corresponding x+s. For each p, spatial/height constraints give O(RH/p²) physical states in the rectangle, p<=2H+3, since h<=H and H=o(R). Elementary prime reciprocal-square bound gives O(RH/(z log z)) changed states. Even with z comparable H, the crude cap1 error O(R/L) does NOT meet an R/L² target. A proof requires an actual trial-weighted defect upper, a stronger trial sink mass, or a hybrid approximation. It is not enough to count candidate large keys unweighted. Proposed pilot: compute trial sink mass and actual changed-edge trial mass at H across several powers of R and cutoffs; measure certified margin (*) before attempting an asymptotic proof. One warning about uniform-state drift: in the finite model with all odd primes <=z and uniform independent x-even, n over complete CRT periods, conditional on clock parity each prime's eligibility has probability 1/p² and the events are independent across primes. Thus minimum winner p has probability p^-2 prod_{3<=r<p prime}(1-r^-2). Hence mean height increment 1-EG/2 eventually becomes negative in this uniform modular model. This is NOT a claim about the actual canonical occupation law. It indicates why simply postulating uniform state residues and positive drift is not a convincing replacement for an actual path proof. The residual method does not require that uniformity or a positive drift for every trajectory.
