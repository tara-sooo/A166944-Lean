# Arbitrary-wait macro-to-small connectors and unconditional terminal-anchor exclusion

2026-11-28 JST, Issue #10. Continuation of 07503a495c962aeb854ba29e7a20a73e7f6eefe5. Main target unchanged: H=floor(R^.4), >>R/(log R)^2 distinct first-H canonical sinks with total gain<=4H. The last checkpoint's relative defect theorem applies only to specific arithmetic source families or the whole local lattice, not a conditional occupation law along many sections. Here use exact small-alphabet walls to remove the elapsed-time restriction of earlier top-anchor separation, and use the fact that terminal sinks are counted without clock multiplicity to eliminate a much wider terminal label range. These are unconditional arithmetic upper/reduction theorems for actual histories, not an asymptotic occupation lower. No new external literature, Lean compilation or independent review; core c9b2d47/614 unchanged. 

## 1. Exact small-alphabet connectors

Let L=log R, H=floor(R^.4) (the following estimates also work for H of fixed polynomial order up to O(sqrt R)). Consider all canonical histories from A in [R,2R], 6|A, stopped at first height H or first gain>4H. Include only anchors before budget exit. All states considered lie in an O(R) spatial slab of positions comparable to R, and 0<=h<=H. Winner p<=2H+3 by the pre-height divisibility. At a firing poststate (y,n), sign s=+/-1, write b_s=0/3 and c_s=1-s. Pins are y=c_s mod p, h=y/2+n=m p+b_s with m>=0, n=0 mod2p for plus, n=p+2 mod2p for minus. Height bound implies m=O(1+H/p). We count physical event keys (y,n,p,s), not multiplicities of roots already merged.

Choose z=floor(c log L) with sufficiently small fixed c>0 so Q=2 prod_{3<=r<=z prime} r <= L^(1/4) for large R, using Chebyshev. All moves with labels<=z stay in one Q-cell: if y is a multiple of Q, a move crossing strictly below y from source a>=y would have even 0<=a-y<=p-2; eligibility requires a-y congruent to +/-1 modp, impossible (1 odd, p-1 outside). Monotonic x and repeated application give total gain <Q throughout any consecutive run of such moves, however many ticks elapse. This is the existing checked wall result (0e9b47b). Use the universal complete-event block bound W<=17G/8+395/12 from c0e45b0, independently computationally certified there, not newly kernel checked.

Define the small connector from an actual event poststate to first H or to the state just before the first event with label>z, whichever comes first. If budget exit by a small label occurs earlier, stop before that move. Include the poststate and idle states through the stopping state, not the next nonsmall landing. For any retained portion the complete small waits are a contiguous actual block with gain<Q, and the incomplete final wait is <2qmin(current center) by the calendar. If all even centers y-d, 0<=d<=Q, satisfy qmin<K, every connector has elapsed length <= C0=ceil(17Q/8+395/12+2K). This bound also establishes finite termination if a connector could otherwise continue indefinitely. It is valid without slow full wait at the anchor, any word restrictions, or no-twin assumptions. It avoids using a speculative uniform distribution of the occupied cohort.

## 2. Macro anchors with arbitrary full waits: remove all rough neighborhoods

Set Y=H/L^(1/4), K=H/L^5 (round harmlessly). A macro anchor is any actual p>=Y. Call it rough-bad if some even center x=y-d, 0<=d<=Q, has qmin(x)>=K. For fixed p,s,d, the spatial pin fixes x=c_s-d mod p. Write x=c_s-d+p t. Both x-1 and x+1 are K-rough. Their two affine forms have distinct roots modulo every odd prime except p (determinant 2p); p>Q+3 so neither constant vanishes modp. Parameter interval length O(R/p) polynomially large. The elementary two-form upper of earlier checkpoints, at level a small fixed power of R below K, bounds the number of possible x by O(R/(p L^2)), uniformly in d,s,p, since log K~L and the omitted factor p is bounded. This does NOT require either fired source neighbor/cofactor to be rough. For each position, clock height strip gives O(H/p+1)=O(H/p) possible event clocks. Therefore the total number of distinct rough-bad macro keys, over the entire graph, is
    E_rough << R H (Q+1)/L² * sum_{Y<=p<=2H+3 prime} 1/p²
             << R H (Q+1)/(Y L³)
             = O(R/L^(5/2)).                             (1)
Elementary dyadic Chebyshev gives the reciprocal-square bound. In particular it is o(R/L²). This removes possible very long final idles even for fast macro arrivals. Unlike previous four-form slow-arrival estimates, this two-form count needs the label threshold close to H. It does not handle all smaller arrivals.

## 3. Isolate entire small connectors following nonreset macro anchors

Every remaining macro key has its entire small connector inside displacement Q and elapsed C0=O(K+Q). Suppose two distinct remaining anchors are within Q in position and C0 in signed clock. Their heights satisfy
    |mp-m'q| <= C0+Q/2+3 < Y.
If one has m=0 and the other m'>0, impossible. If p=q, then m=m'; the spatial pins force position difference c_t-c_s, by Q+2<Y. Equal sign gives identical keys, opposite sign forces an even clock difference +/-2 in contradiction to opposite clock parity. Thus p!=q. For both m,m'>0, there are O((H/Y)^2) coefficient pairs, O(H) candidate integer p (primality not needed for this upper), O(C0+Q+1) integer q candidates by the height relation, and O(Q+1) spatial offsets. For distinct actual prime labels CRT fixes one spatial class modulo pq; number of positions O(1+R/(pq))=O(R/Y²) here. Height and position determine clocks. Hence the total count of potentially close nonreset pairs in the entire strip is
    E_pair << H (H/Y)^2 (C0+Q+1)(Q+1) R/Y²
            << R (Q+1)(K+Q+1)/H * (H/Y)^4
            = O(R/L^(15/4)).                            (2)
Indeed Q<=L^.25, H/Y=L^.25, K/H=L^-5. Constants fixed. Count all pinned pairs, not assumed independent. Delete the two endpoints of each such pair, plus (1). All remaining NONRESET macro anchors have mutually disjoint full actual small-connector state sets. Moreover none can intersect any remaining RESET macro anchor's small connector, since any common state would force the above near conditions. No pairwise disjointness among reset-reset connectors is asserted.

This extends 7e90c0f (fixed short rectangular connectors after slow top arrivals) in two ways: the preceding wait may be arbitrarily short, and the connector lasts until the next nonsmall move or first-H/budget stop, not a prescribed short time cutoff. Its absolute time is now controlled internally by rough-neighborhood removal and walls. C0~2H/L^5 is much larger than the prior R^.25/L^7 cutoff for H=.4. Actual individual connectors can be short; no lower bound on their durations follows. Neither (1) nor (2) bounds how many good anchors are canonically reached.

Apply to fixed-budget min-plus transport from arbitrary selected first-height sources. At a bad key retire its representative on its incoming moving edge (cap1 at predecessor). Each distinct key occurs once in the acyclic physical graph, hence total lost representatives O(R/L^2.5)=o(R/L²), even across all heights or overlapping observation stages. Replacing the minimum ancestor by a different representative is not needed; take minima of remaining actual histories. At a merger after deleting those keys, if two representatives have distinct recent retained macro anchors and only small-label moves since their anchors, and at least one anchor is nonreset, their connectors cannot intersect. Thus those merger classes are eliminated globally at negligible cost. Same-anchor multiplicities are not new branches; unanchored, mesoscopic, and reset-reset contacts, source injections and budget exits remain. This is not a total loss bound.

## 4. Unconditional terminal exclusion over a much wider label range

Now allow H=R^theta with fixed theta>0, up to O(sqrt R), Y'=H exp(-L^gamma) for any fixed 0<gamma<3/4, K'=Y'/L^4. Keep Q<=L^.25. Count DISTINCT first-H sinks x with ANY witness that has some firing p>=Y' followed by total subsequent gain d<=Q, no restriction on order or size of those intervening labels. In particular this includes a history whose last label>z is >=Y', since every following small run stays in its Q-cell. Include slow and fast anchors, reset and nonreset, rough and nonrough sinks. No premise of finite twins.

Split by terminal qmin:
(A) If qmin(x)<K', then from the anchor postheight h_a to H, complete waits <=17d/8+395/12 and final idle <2K', so
    H-(13/8)Q-395/12-2K' <= h_a < H.
Here h_a-b_s positive for large R. For each of O(K'+Q+1) possible integer h_a and each sign, its nonzero h_a-b_s has O_theta(1) prime divisors >=Y', since log Y'~log H~L. Each fixes y=c_s modp at O(R/p) positions, and clock is fixed by height. Each actual anchor determines at most one first-H sink regardless of previous history (if H reached before, the witness is not a first-H path). Therefore
    N_A << R (K'+Q+1)/Y'=O(R/L^4).                       (3)
This is the earlier terminal-alignment argument with expanded threshold range; no new history count.

(B) If qmin(x)>=K', the anchor's spatial pin is x=c_s-d modp with even 0<=d<=Q. Use the same two-form upper as (1), but now count x only, not event clocks. Uniformly per p,d,s the count is O(R/(p L²)), because log K'~L. Chebyshev dyadic reciprocal-prime sum over Y'<=p<=2H+3 is O((1+log(H/Y'))/L)=O(L^(gamma-1)). Thus
    N_B << R (Q+1) L^(gamma-3)
         = O(R/L^(11/4-gamma)).                        (4)
Since gamma<3/4, this is o(R/L²). Combining gives the unconditional global upper
    N_terminal << R/L^4 + R/L^(11/4-gamma)=o(R/L²).      (5)
For example gamma=1/2 gives O(R/L^2.25), with Y'=H exp(-sqrt L). This counts sinks with EVEN ONE witness of the indicated type, not just chosen minimum-gain ancestors. It requires only a small terminal displacement, not all subsequent labels small, and removes the terminal roughness hypothesis in 0c4cd9e. It does not need a slow anchor, prime-distribution lower theorem, or an assumed twin shortage. All constants may depend on fixed theta,gamma,c. The statement with Y' much lower does not extend the whole-connector pair isolation (2) to that lower threshold; those proofs have different clock costs.

## 5. Consequence for global height transport

For a first-H path with all labels<=z, total gain <Q by the wall theorem, and final qmin >=(H-3-13Q/8-395/12)/2, by the canonical ledger. Thus an intrinsically active first-H sink with qmin<H/16 cannot have an all-small witness at large R. Combining with (5), except o(R/L²) sinks, EVERY witness of an active first-H sink must have a final nonsmall event with label in
    z < p < H exp(-sqrt L)
(take gamma=1/2), followed by the small connector. In particular a lower of order R/L² for such active sinks is equivalent, up to o(R/L²) exceptional sinks, to a lower of that order for this intermediate-terminal class. This is a necessary reduction, not an equivalence to the full 4.85 target unless the target is restricted to active sinks. At H=.4 the all-small rough population may still be of order R/L²; it cannot be removed unconditionally by the existing rough upper. At H comparable sqrt R/L^B with fixed B, under finitely many twins, those all-small sinks number O(R log L/L³) by the old non-twin rough-frontier estimate; consequently a density-scale near-square first-H proof under that assumption must produce the intermediate-terminal class. No twin infinitude follows from this necessary condition alone.

Use (1)-(2) to contract the disjoint connectors after retained nonreset macro arrivals in the global min-plus transport. They do not produce repeated small-label merger charges against other retained macro connectors. Exits must be handled at the next nonsmall transition, or contacts with other categories. In combination with (5), the natural next lower-bound problem is occupation of the intermediate terminal gateways and their earlier multiscale ancestry, rather than more top-anchor supply or local uniform-lattice transfer. This is a new obstruction/reduction of the remaining proof obligation, not the desired density-scale lower. No proof that the unoccupied intermediate gateways are reached. 4.85 remains unmet.
