## 1. Original source units and missing-clock measure

Keep XVII dW, alpha, phi, chi, mu=(1-chi)dW and original frozen kernels, grids, stable slot and class denominators. Under finite twins XVII gives int(1-phi)dmu=o(NT) and int alpha dmu=o(NT). Reuse exactly the original good right-witness atom assignment. Split by sector s=l,h, using each sector's actual pursuit L_s, rather than the common longer word for coding. Positive high rows have the same compatible set with their high word as with the common word by original singleton theorem. For an assigned focal event unit v at an original atom/class/sector let m_v be its missing clock divided among those atoms in precisely the same proportions as original assigned clock. It is nonnegative, and sum m_v for a single original tag across units/sectors at the atom <=c_a P. Full partner denominator n remains the original class size; every positive assigned unit is stable. For two such units v,z, the forward row survival factor theta(v,tag(z)) in [0,1] is the original exact fraction surviving all masks for that partner, including zero for same word. More generally define theta(v,j) for every full original class partner j, zero for unstable. At the finer atom and focal-event unit scale the row coefficient is sum_j theta(v,j)/n. Averaging with missing fractions recovers int phi dmu because XVII's eventwise pulled-back phi is the original atom-weighted coefficient and missing clock is split in the same proportions. If eventwise phi is merely an admissible upper, use the exact residual theta for this result, as allowed in XVII. No new partner distribution in the definition of the original charge.

Let M be averaged total assigned missing clock. Unassigned missing clock contributes to int(1-phi)dmu, so under finite twins M=(W_all-V_h)+o(NT). At this stage M>=epsilon NT on an unbounded dyadic subsequence is an additional hypothesis, NOT yet a theorem. The result below gives a stronger restriction conditional on that branch.

## 2. Fixed finite high-discovery code

Fix constants delta>0 (take 0<delta<1), integer D>=1, integer J>=2. Set Y=ceil(B^delta). At each source class, same source label q, common posthorizon J_src and phase wheel L0, for every stable sector word maintain R=lcm(L0,2q, periods of all previously selected word labels), before each next event. Stop before the first event whose PREVIOUS radical is >4H. Also stop at the original sector word end. If the event label p>=Y and p does not divide R, record:
  (a) rank of p among the eligible prime divisors >=Y of BOTH C-1 and C+1, in increasing order, at that actual prehorizon C;
  (b) floor(J (C-J_src)/L_s);
  (c) floor(J w/p);
  (d) w modulo D+1.
Then update R by lcm(R,2p). Include a new high record even if its multiplication makes R>4H; there is no subsequent record. At most K=ceil(log(4H)/log Y)+1=O_delta(1) records, since all recorded primes distinct and all except possibly the last divide a radical <=4H. At most d=ceil(2 log(7X+1)/log Y)=O_delta(1) eligible high labels at each horizon, by products of distinct prime divisors of neighbors. The gain bin lies in {0,...,J-1} because word stops on first gain crossing L_s; wait bin lies in {0,...,2J} because w<=2p. The padded code has at most
    Ccode=(1+d J(2J+1)(D+1))^K
possibilities, bounded by an absolute constant for fixed delta,D,J as X grows. Use the finite list itself, or pad with distinguished stop symbol. All tags in a physical slot have same code, but MANY words may have a code. At square-root floor Y=ceil(eta sqrt X), eta fixed positive, eligible label count <=4 and at most TWO high records (product of two such primes exceeds 4H since H=o(X); allow the overflow record). Thus constant Ccode <=(1+4J(2J+1)(D+1))² in this top range. If the source q itself is top, at most ONE further high record. These are counts of code possibilities, not counts of words or physical slots.

## 3. Arithmetic restriction on same-code first defects

Compare two distinct stable sector words with identical code. Before their first actual defect at common prehorizon E their labels and waits are identical, same prefix radical R divides the nonzero initial lag Delta_t, |Delta_t|<=4H. In particular R<=4H and the number of high records already made is the same for both. Let x,y be their next actual selected events. At least one selected period fails to divide Delta_t by two-way earliest-hit/tie MIN. Call a selected label NEW-HIGH if >=Y and not dividing R (a weaker condition than nonperiodicity under the particular lag).

If both next labels are NEW-HIGH, both make the next record at E, whose rank is the same by code equality. Hence both labels are the SAME p. Different waits are necessary for a first defect; common residue and ratio bin imply
    D < |w_x-w_y| < p/J.
Also their actual departure times both obey the same residue mod2p, so dist(Delta_t,2p Z)<p/J. This excludes ALL two-sided new-high DISTINCT-label first defects and same-label bounded wait differences from same-code pairs. It also excludes relative wait differences >=p/J, without needing a bound on absolute deficit. It does not imply the defect event has a large absolute wait, or that its exit lag is restored.

If exactly one next label is NEW-HIGH, that strand makes a record at E. The other must make its next high record at a strictly later actual prehorizon E' in the SAME gain bin, since the lists agree, with
    0<E'-E<L_s/J.
The actual selected label at E' has same recorded rank (at its OWN prehorizon), and its wait ratio bin and wait residue agree with the record at E. It is NOT necessarily the same prime. Its previous radical is <=4H, or it would not appear in code. The later event can be after the first defect and on a different projected path. This is a required actual compensating discovery, not a claim of unchanged lag after divergence. With neither label NEW-HIGH at E, a chosen actual nonperiodic label must be <Y, since every label dividing R is periodic under Delta_t. The three cases exhaust actual first defects.

For top Y, there can be no two distinct top labels already in a translated prefix; source q top included. Thus in exactly-one case if the other next label is top, it is the sole earlier top label; later compensating top discovery is genuinely new on that ray. No claim the interposed gain is o(B) because L_s/B may diverge. This distinction matters for direct close-transition sieve.

Call a forward comparison STANDARD if it has two NEW-HIGH labels that are distinct, or two NEW-HIGH same label with either wait difference <=D or >=p/J. Every standard comparison has DIFFERENT codes. In fact we can instead use the larger complement of the three same-code necessary conditions above, which also excludes one-new comparisons without a compensating record in the correct bin. For the subsequent estimate use ANY designated type of pair that is impossible within a code (standard suffices). No assumption that existing target masks enforce that type.

## 4. Quantitative same-code collision and supply tradeoff

At one atom/class/sector let M_c=sum of missing unit weights of code c, and n_c^code the number of original stable partner tags with that code in this sector (not original phase denominator n). Then M_c<=c_a P n_c^code. Original partner selection weights same-code partners by n_c^code/n, including self/same word. Thus
    sum_c M_c n_c^code/n >= sum_c M_c²/(c_a P n).
Sum/average over original grids, sectors, classes, atoms. Cauchy and <=Ccode codes per class, at most two sectors, sum P n<=N(H+Pmax) per grid give
    Kcode := mean sum_(atoms,classes,sectors,c) M_c²/(c_a P n)
        >= M²/[2 Ccode c_a N(H+Pmax)].                (1)
The factor 2 is conservative, permits counting both sector denominators. Same-code partners either do NOT survive as forward comparisons or survive and are of the complementary (nonstandard) type. Consequently, if F_other^mu is the original retained forward clock with the missing focal weight AND complementary type (all original masks, no new destination assignment),
    int(1-phi)dmu + F_other^mu >= Kcode.              (2)
Unassigned missing gives only nonnegative extra loss. Unlike XVII's fixed-word-count estimate, (1)-(2) hold with no bound on the total number of words or phases. If M>=epsilon NT and finite twins, F_other^mu>= [epsilon²/(6 Ccode)-o(1)] NT, using c_a->3, H/T->1. Thus the high/high distinct new-label route (even adding the indicated same-label types) cannot exhaust the residual missing clock. This is a constant positive necessary supply of one-new delayed discovery, same-label near-periodic intermediate waits, or small-label nonperiodic defects. It does NOT bound their supply from above. Can use only one sector and its missing total in (1) with factor 1, and its own code constant; the same loss term remains an upper on sector rejection. An arbitrary fixed delta,D,J is permitted; no rate for growing code cardinality. If all complementary types were payable o(NT), a macroscopic M would contradict XVII. No unconditional lower for M yet.

## 5. Arbitrary fixed-size mutually admissible actual strands

This strengthens pair supply without assuming independent trajectories. At each nonempty (atom,class,sector,code) group put measure mass M_c²/(c_a P n). Conditional on the group draw two independent ORIGINAL focal units v,z with probabilities m_v/M_c, m_z/M_c. The averaged expected rejection in the direction v to tag(z), integrated against this group measure, is
    mean sum_group sum_(v,z) m_v m_z (1-theta(v,tag(z)))/(c_a P n)
       <= mean sum_(atoms,classes,sectors,v) (m_v/n) sum_(full partner tags j) (1-theta(v,j))
       <= int(1-phi)dmu.                              (3)
The first inequality uses sum m_z over units of one original tag <=c_a P. The second uses the original missing redistribution, and unassigned loss nonnegative. This is a capacity domination, not an equality of the two measures. No assumption of symmetry of the original directed masks. The same estimate applies to the reversed direction by exchangeability of sampling. Now if M>=epsilon NT on an unbounded subsequence, the total group measure Kcode>=c(epsilon,delta,D,J) NT by (1). Under finite twins, normalized expected rejection tends to zero. For every FIXED integer k>=2, sample k independent units from the same selected group. Union bound/Markov shows that for any fixed gamma>0, the fraction of this sampling measure with some ordered distinct-index pair theta<1-gamma is o(1) (<=k(k-1) int(1-phi)dmu/(gamma Kcode)). Thus there exist k original actual stable strands at ONE original atom,class,sector and with ONE code, for which ALL ordered pair survival fractions >=1-gamma. They are distinct words and physical slots because theta=0 on same word, including diagonal; choose gamma<1. In the special case binary masks all ordered pairs actually survive. If theta is fractional, this is a statement of positive, near-full surviving fractions, not a new simultaneous selection of actual defect destinations. All unordered pairs satisfy the three arithmetic cases in Section3. Since the cases define symmetric colors, finite Ramsey gives arbitrary fixed m mutually admissible strands with every pair in the SAME case by taking k sufficiently large (independent of X); no rate or bound for m growing with X. This follows from the fixed-size construction, not from treating unrelated pair samples as a common source. This is a new multi-strand obstruction with precise capacity, but it remains conditional on macroscopic uncovered clock and finite twins. The fixed-color cases still permit arbitrarily large abstract families absent further MIN arithmetic; no finite Ramsey contradiction by itself.

## 6. Direct deficit-coordinate supply on the correct lifetime scale

Direct 6f7af67: on actual selected top-label (p>=eta sqrt X) positive-multiplier events with adjusted half-deficit x<=K X^beta, beta fixed in [1/2,1), each x has at most one such prime factor. Close consecutive selected events with outside relay E<=B_cut and |ordinary postdeficit difference|<=A_cut (and direct small-shift conditions) have p!=r, c=x2-x!=0. At fixed x,c,E,side pair the primes p,r unique and the two horizon congruences determine prehorizon modulo pr=Theta(X), hence O(1) candidate horizons globally. Its deficit interval sieve gives close ENDING gain O(B W (A_cut+1)(B_cut+1)/u^nu), W=max(ceil sqrt X, actual x range+1), nu=2 for beta<3/4 or nu=1 for beta<1. This uses neither expensive clock nor bounded multiplier cap. We import the stated proof for fixed beta,eta,K and original prehorizons in [2X-2,7X], not a new uniform beta->1 sieve. The proof's padding/CRT remains valid for this fixed multiplicative horizon window.

For any contiguous actual tag block of gain comparable to H with selected total F>=rho H, set z=B U_out/H, y=B V_d/H, r=B R_x/X with U_out outside gain, V_d total ordinary deficit variation and R_x selected half-deficit range. Direct (5) divided by H and BW/H=O(u^.25(1+r)) gives, by choosing B_cut=ceil(Crho z), A_cut=ceil(Crho y) whenever product is below u^(nu-.25),
    (1+z)(1+y)(1+r) >>_{rho,beta,eta,K} u^(nu-1/4).       (4)
If product already larger there is nothing to show. Otherwise cutoffs polylog and satisfy direct close hypotheses. Since R_x<=V_d/2+3, r=O(y/u^.25+1), hence also (1+z)(1+y)^2 >> u^(nu-1/4). Stronger range factor in (4) is retained when actual range small. This is only a polylogarithmic obstruction on B-scale relay/variation, not macro gain. For uncapped selected list of F>=rho H with product o(u^(nu-.25)), choose a slowly growing factor multiplying both cutoffs with squared factor times product still o(u^(nu-.25)). Direct high-deficit alternative then forces all but o(H) selected gain into x>K X^beta-o(X^beta) for any FIXED applicable beta. For beta>1/2, low product implies V_d=O((H/B) polylog)=o(X^beta); actual block then has uniformly high ordinary deficit (>=2K X^beta-o(X^beta)). Original x<=3+5H/2=o(X) is compatible with every fixed beta<1; does not rule out this high-baseline alternative. No polynomial deficit bound follows from our short original lifetime alone. Apply once per tag lifetime or disjoint long blocks with explicit total budget, not repeatedly summing a global O(X) error on arbitrary short intervals. In Section3 one-new delayed-discovery case, the intervening gain bound L_s/J is generally far larger than allowed B_cut; do not apply (4) to these paired events without an actual same-ray close transition and deficit cap. This transfer is a separate available supply estimate, not the missing proof of the three cases.
