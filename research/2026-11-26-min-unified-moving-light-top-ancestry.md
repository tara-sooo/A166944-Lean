## 2. Top-source weighted support

For p>Q, any expensive high event at C requires p|C+sigma and r(C)>=tau(p-1)/2. The previously established two-form upper sieve at C=p j-sigma, on j and p j-2sigma, with cutoff p^(1/40), excluding 2,p, gives #C=O(X/[p log² p])=O(X/[p u²]) uniformly for p>Q. Error O(p^.2) absorbed since p<=B=Theta(sqrt X); cutoff below tau(p-1)/2. Ordinary prime upper bounds and partial summation give sum_(Q<p<=B)1/p=O(delta+1/u)=O(delta): integrate O(1/(t log t)) from Q to B, giving O(log(1/(1-3delta))) plus O(1/log Q). This is an upper bound only; no assumption of tag distribution.

Let n_cut(p)=H u/(p sqrt(delta)). Pay all surviving forward energy from original expensive top focal waits at keys with n(C,p)<=n_cut(p). The ACTUAL waits of all such tags at one key sum <=2p n_cut(p). Hence summed cost in actual clock units is
    O(sum_(Q<p<=B) X/(p u²) * H u/sqrt(delta))
      = O(XH sqrt(delta)/u)=O(sqrt(delta) NT).         (1)
The same holds for their exact forward fractions since assignment never reuses one original wait within a grid. This is a projected key condition, not a class-size cap divided by the wheel.

At fixed C,p, the actual completed exit time v is in a single residue class mod 2p, determined by the unique sign sigma, and spans O(H) from the original deficit slab. Consequently there are O(H/p) possible physical exit slots (H>>B). Pay all surviving top forward fractions from slots with b(C,p,v)<=u/sqrt(delta). The summed actual waits are
    O(sum_(Q<p<=B) X/(p u²) * H/p * 2p * u/sqrt(delta))
      = O(sqrt(delta) NT).                            (2)
This proof does not require each expensive tag to be in a slot of exclusively expensive tags. Both (1),(2) can be applied together with other source masks; no double counting if applied sequentially. The result excludes actual focal waits, not every high partner incidence. Since p>Q>P_max, any retained top focal wait has a self right witness. The old actual phase-class n_c and b at source remain unchanged; surviving top focal keys must have n(C,p)>H u/(p sqrt(delta)), and its own actual source slot b(C,p,v)>u/sqrt(delta). In particular if p is near B the projected cutoff is order B u^.75/sqrt(delta). This is much larger than the earlier generic thin cutoff H/(p u ell²). The physical cutoff is separate and may be redundant on some already massive sources; no claim that every tag removed by (1),(2) was still charged.

## 3. Moving-light ancestry in the long prime-root ensemble

Use the same wheel L0, with LOW labels its odd prime factors p<=y (if empty take y=2). Every contiguous LOW-only gain is <L0, as in the independent ancestry-Kraft branch: at a multiple of L0 no such prime is eligible; a LOW jump starting below cannot cross that multiple, only possibly reach it. Reverse at projected posthorizon J: candidate nonLOW p divides J or J+2, and its preceding prehorizon is J-p+1. No time information is required for an upper count of possible histories.

Uniformly over actual horizons J=O(X), d(J)=#distinct candidate nonLOW primes <=B dividing J(J+2) is O(u/ell). Indeed the product of d distinct integers >=2 is >=d!, while J(J+2)=O(X²), so d log d=O(u). Take integer d_* sufficiently large of order u/ell bounding this maximum, and g0=max(2,L0(d_*+1))=O(u). Every compressed reverse history with j nonLOW jumps consists of j LOW offsets, each even in [0,L0), each followed by one candidate nonLOW reverse jump, and a final LOW offset. At most L0 g0^j such words from any C. An invalid word is simply discarded. The bound also applies to histories whose earlier endpoint is an arbitrary visited recorded horizon, including one inside a longer LOW block; the partial block still has gain <L0.

Set
    m0=floor(B^.75), k=floor(u/(8 ell)).
Call a recorded incidence an eligible START if it is its own original root or its visited projected horizon A(C_s)<=m0. At any fixed C_s at most m0+1 tags have eligible starts (the root injection adds at most one). Call a current recorded incidence MOVING if some earlier or current eligible start on that SAME history is within k+1 completed nonLOW jumps, with LOW jumps uncounted. Both endpoints may be terminal snapshots where appropriate. The original A(C) and eligible starts are frozen even if charges have been paid.

For any current C, reverse the actual suffix from a witnessing start. There are at most L0 sum_(j=0)^(k+1) g0^j possible compressed words, each determines its starting horizon. Each start contributes at most m0+1 tags. Multiple possible witnesses only overcount. Hence the uniform MOVING population bound is
    A_mov(C) <= M_* := (m0+1)L0 sum_(j=0)^(k+1) g0^j
                     = B^(7/8+o(1)).                 (3)
The asymptotic follows from log g0<=ell+O(1), k=u/(8ell)+O(1), and log L0=o(u). This works at arbitrary absolute depth, without the earlier short-clock exceptional-degree pruning. We use the polynomial scale permitted by the present long gain window, not the independent branch's cubic-log threshold. A physical state (t,C) is a subset of recorded horizon visitors, so the same bound applies to the corresponding moving histories at true ticks.

In fact pay ALL remaining expensive high forward fractions from original focal events that are MOVING, not just top. For every selected p>=Y, same two-form sieve gives O(X/(p u²)) eligible C since log p>=u/10, r(C)>=tau(p-1)/2. Each such key has <=M_* moving tags, and w<=2p. Summing over p>=Y gives
    W_mov,high <= O(X M_* pi(B)/u²)
                = O(X B M_*/u³)
                = o(NT),                             (4)
using pi(B)=O(B/u), H~T=Theta(B²/u^.25) and M_*=B^(7/8+o(1)). No factor number of grids because exact per-grid energy is at most the original wait/c_a and the masks are tag predicates independent of grid. No assertion about MOVING low waits, since their local support may be much denser.

Thus every remaining expensive high focal event has >k+1 nonLOW jumps in its history (root is an eligible start) and EVERY visited horizon from which the depth distance to that event is <=k+1 has A(C_s)>m0. In particular a recent return to a light projected horizon makes the event payable even if its current n(C,p) is huge. This is an actual ancestor-history restriction, not a lower bound on every physical slot along that history.

## 4. First-good prefix and expiry identity for long histories

For each tag let i_heavy be the FIRST recorded horizon index with A(C)>m0, if any. Define a monotone GOOD prefix: before i_heavy, and from i_heavy through at most k further completed nonLOW jumps. If no first heavy horizon occurs, the whole history is GOOD. The prefix includes all occupied true ticks at each qualifying recorded waiting horizon, and its qualifying terminal snapshot if any. Stop at the last such physical vertex (expiry). It is nonempty: at least root is good. If first heavy occurs at root, use root as eligible start; otherwise the preceding recorded horizon is light and supplies an eligible start with depth distance <=k+1 through this fresh-heavy prefix. Before first heavy, the current recorded horizon itself is eligible. Thus every GOOD state is MOVING, so the full GOOD population at any physical vertex is <=M_* by (3). This proof allows arbitrary depth before first heavy.

For each physical vertex V let g_V be the number of original tags present while GOOD, e_V the number whose GOOD prefix expires at V. Let F_gg be the number of unordered distinct original-tag pairs whose first true meeting occurs while both GOOD. The independent branch expiry identity transfers unchanged:
    F_gg = sum_V [e_V(g_V-e_V)+binom(e_V,2)]
         <= N(M_*-1).                                (5)
For completeness, two tags meeting while both good share deterministic suffix until the earlier of their expiries; because GOOD is a prefix property, both are still good at the earlier expiry. If expiries tie in time the same shared vertex is reached. Charge the pair to this vertex exactly once. Conversely a pair counted at an expiry met no later than that state while both good, hence its first true meeting was within both good prefixes. Finite terminal snapshots and unequal individual truncations are included. Sum e_V=N. This proof does not require that the full physical visiting population be monotone along all histories, or that the first meeting be the first time A(C) crosses m0 for either member.

## 5. Entire backward compatible suffix payment

Return to the frozen 590c137 same-slot resampled compatible clock and every subsequent true birth reindexing. Each remaining positive atom of an ordered pair has charge <=c_a P/b_min, with b_min=B^(1-o(1)) as in section 1. A fixed unordered pair can contribute at most 2 orientations times one source occurrence per tag and strict grid atom. Across its entire common future suffix, #atoms for either tag <=H/P+1, since gain intervals on that original path are disjoint. Hence the TOTAL grid clock of this unordered pair over all source atoms is <=2 c_a(H+P)/b_min, independent of the later size of its physical group. Both sectors already share the row cap c_a P. This bound applies after earlier birth masks as well.

Pay every remaining backward fraction whose pair first met within both GOOD prefixes. By (5) its full grid-averaged cost is
    B_gg <= 2 c_a (H+P_max) F_gg / b_min
         <= 2 c_a (H+P_max) N(M_*-1)/b_min
         = O(NT B^(-1/8+o(1)))=o(NT).                (6)
No recomputation of b, normalized pair weights or actual first meeting. The payment covers the entire backward compatible clock from such first meetings, even if both original histories became nonGOOD long before the eventual source. This is the new joint use of the independent branch's expiry estimate and the present branch's polynomial source-slot lower bound. It does not cover pairs with one GOOD and one already nonGOOD at their first meeting. If both meet before either first-heavy threshold the old small-parent mask may already pay them; (6) additionally allows meetings in the fresh-heavy prefix with arbitrary other nonGOOD population at the same vertex. No assertion about the positive size of the new deletion.

## 6. Updated sufficient route

Apply (1),(2),(4) to exact old surviving high forward fractions, and (6) to exact old surviving backward compatible fractions. They are disjoint between forward/backward; within each direction apply sequentially. Total new payment o(NT). Denote the residuals B_new and D_h,new. Low forward D_l,rem unchanged. Under finitely many twins the existing lower certificate remains
    NT/2-o(NT) <= B_new+(3+o(1))(D_l,rem+D_h,new).
Thus liminf [B_new+3(D_l,rem+D_h,new)]/(NT)<1/2 suffices, with a no-larger objective than before. High forward now has a long uninterrupted projected-heavy ancestry condition, while backward pairs require at least one already-expired good prefix at the first true meeting. The top projected and physical mass cutoffs give additional constraints; they do not bound heavy group mass or imply the remaining top clock is o(NT). No proof of the final constant margin. Main and independent source branches unchanged. No Lean compilation.
