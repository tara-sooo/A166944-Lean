## 1. Exact low-height model and its translated copies

L=log R. At even position x and integer preheight h=x/2+n>=0, the next tick is n+1. For sign s=+1 (even next clock) or -1 (odd), an eligible odd prime p divides x+s and 2h+3s, with the usual clock residue 0 or p+2 modulo 2p. This follows directly from n+1=0 modp for plus and n+1=2 modp for minus. Thus in the reference interval 0<=r<M=D+3 any possible winner is <=2M+1, even when x is NOT a canonical birth. The height of the next state is nonnegative since p<=2r+3; idle adds one and a moving event changes height by 1-(p-1)/2<=0.

Let z=2D+11 and Q=2 product_{3<=p<=z prime}p, B=2Q. Consider the low-height trial started at (x,3), any even x of order R. Stop at first M. Its eligible primes before that stop are <=z; the recurrence can be evaluated by factors of 2r+3s without factoring the large neighbors. The spatial wall at any multiple of Q cannot be crossed downward by such labels: if a source is above the wall by even d with 0<=d<=p-2, eligibility would require d=+1 or -1 modp, impossible in that range. Hence total gain <Q, and all trajectories reach M in finite time. Indeed they stay in one finite cell, each move reduces x by >=2, and once moves cease idle reaches M. Equivalently, the finite DAG with x decreasing on moves and height increasing on idle has no other exit. At first M the elapsed time is D+G/2<=D+Q/2. Define the output map f_D(x), with x-Q<f_D(x)<=x. It is periodic up to translation by B, since x+B changes the signed clock by -B/2, divisible by 2p? Here B=4 product odd primes, B/2=2 product odd primes, so yes. Height stays unchanged under that translation. In particular at most Q/2+1 even inputs can share any output. The exact maximum fiber can also be obtained by a finite periodic calculation; the wall estimate suffices here.

Call a positive shift a CLEAN for D and Z>z if a=0 modB and
    no prime z<p<=Z divides any 2a+c, c=-3,-1,1,3,...,2D+7.    (1)
These k=D+6 distinct odd constants include exactly all 2r±3 for 0<=r<M. Their differences are <z. At height h=a+r, the eligibility conditions for every p<=z are IDENTICAL to those at r, since n changes by a multiple of 2p. Condition (1) excludes every intermediate p in (z,Z]. Therefore the full trial ignoring labels>Z, started at (x,a+3) and stopped at first a+M or below a, is exactly the translated low-height model. It never exits below a, reaches a+M, has gain <Q and elapsed time <=D+Q/2. This is true for every even starting x in the spatial range, not an average over x or h. ## 2. Existence of many growing-width clean shifts in every polynomial-length window

Fix epsilon>0, fixed 0<beta<theta<1/2 (e.g. theta=.4, beta=.2), and a real interval J of length R^beta contained in [c_theta R^theta,C_theta R^theta] for positive fixed constants. Let
    D=floor(c0 log L), z=2D+11, Z=exp(sqrt L).
Choose fixed c0>0 sufficiently small relative to epsilon. By the elementary Chebyshev bound on sum_{p<=z} log p, B<=L^epsilon for all sufficiently large R (decrease c0 with slack to absorb constants). Thus Q<=L^epsilon, Z>>z. Write a=B t for integer t in the corresponding interval I, length X~R^beta/B. We show there are
    >= (1/2+o(1)) X V,     V=prod_{z<p<=Z}(1-k/p)
    >= R^(beta-o(1))                                    (2)
clean shifts in J (the >= (1/2+o(1)) constant may be replaced by 1/3). This does not invoke a fixed-dimension fundamental lemma with unproved uniformity as D grows.

For each free prime z<p<=Z, the k affine forms 2Bt+c have exactly k distinct forbidden classes since p>z, p coprime 2B and all differences have absolute value <p. In particular k/p<1/2. For a squarefree product d of these primes, CRT gives k^omega(d) forbidden simultaneous classes, so the number in I is X k^omega(d)/d+O(k^omega(d)), with absolute remainder constant. Put S=sum k/p over free primes; the standard elementary upper on prime reciprocals gives S=O(k log log Z)=O((log L)^2). Also V>=exp(-2S). Use the odd Bonferroni lower sum truncated after j odd, where j is at least 10e(S+1) and O((log L)^2+1). The product V differs from that lower main sum by at most e_(j+1), the elementary symmetric sum of k/p of order j+1. This follows from the two adjacent Bonferroni bounds applied to independent events of probabilities k/p (a purely algebraic product identity, no assumption about primes in the interval). Since
    e_(j+1) <= S^(j+1)/(j+1)! <= (e S/(j+1))^(j+1),
it is <=V/2 with the stated choice, including small S. The total interval counting error in the truncated sum is bounded by
    O(sum_{i<=j} (k #primes)^i) <= exp(O(j log(k Z+2)))
       = exp(O(sqrt L (log L)^2)) = R^o(1).
In contrast X V >= R^beta exp(-O((log L)^2))/B = R^(beta-o(1)). Thus it dominates the error, proving (2) uniformly in J. This is an explicit growing width D~log log R, not a fixed-D diagonal argument. For fixed D one may also choose Z=R^alpha with alpha sufficiently small via the ordinary fixed-dimension interval sieve; the subexponential Z above is sufficient and avoids that input. No lower on canonical reachability follows from the count of shifts.

## 4. Basin-preserving repair: exact refinement, but small observed improvement

For a fixed finite acyclic first-height graph, use T(v) for the terminal sink of the deterministic trial path, or 0 for domain exit, and U(v) for the true sink. Define a Boolean certificate P(v) at terminal vertices as true, elsewhere if T(v)>0, the true successor w lies in the domain, T(w)=T(v), and P(w) is true. Process in reverse topological order. Then P(v) implies U(v)=T(v), by induction along the true edges. A defect that redirects to another vertex in the SAME trial basin need not be excluded. All defect-free common paths qualify, but the inclusion can be strict. A true path can also temporarily leave a trial basin and later return, so the exact same-sink predicate U(v)=T(v) can exceed P(v). For canonical witnesses or occupied entries with root budget B0, a certified true endpoint equal to the trial endpoint uses the same gain by monotonic x, so budget admissibility is inherited. One can count distinct certified output sinks rather than root incidences. This is a rigorous lower certificate without treating every changed edge as destructive; computing U globally is itself solving the true graph, not an analytic shortcut.
