## 1. Original ensemble and the absolute short-age sector

Keep dyadic X, B=ceil(sqrt(7X)), u=log B, N=Theta(X/u), T=floor(X/u^.25), H=T+B, F=T²Bu, L_H=B u^1.5. Original gain-stopped prime-source MIN paths include terminal snapshots. Completed selected gain p-1<=B, wait<=2r<=3(p-1). Natural high completed event weight h_M(v)=r, otherwise zero. For any segment its sum of 2h is <=3 times its gain. Ordered offdiagonal actual synchronous pair-hit weight w_(M,M')(x) is nonnegative and <=2h_M(x), M<M'; the complete original shared suffix starts at first physical birth v0 and lasts through the smaller source's stop. Retained holes and all previous feasible absolute payments may be arbitrary. Larger source follows the smaller's recorded suffix by original stop ordering.

Choose a fixed gain width W=ceil(Bu²). For sufficiently large X, W=o(H), and W/B->infinity. At every synchronous hit x write C=C_x and C0=C_v0. For any pair the total actual weight of hits with 0<=C-C0<W is <=3(W+B), since completed event prehorizons increase and the last selected gain can overshoot the window by at most B. This includes the birth event and all deficit levels, not just buffered last-ascent hits. Thus the absolute short-shared-age sector satisfies
    E_short <= 3 choose(N,2)(W+B)
            = O(N² B u²) = O(F/sqrt(u)) = o(F).          (1)
No arithmetic independence, phase count, or upper bound on original birth fibre is required. This is a partial hit-specific estimate, not deletion of a pair's full future shared suffix. The earlier age/whole-pair masks may already cover some or all of this sector; when used with them, pay only the remaining actual weight. The bound is valid on the full natural synchronous offdiagonal ledger and every retained subledger. It does not apply to asynchronous high hits or actual medium waits without physical meeting.

## 2. Fixed-window strict boundary allocation

Let z=max(3,floor(log u/32)) eventually, P=2 product of odd primes<=z. Elementary Chebyshev theta bound gives P=O(u^1/8); in particular W>=2P eventually. For any long-shared hit C-C0>=W choose every K multiple of P with
    C-W <= K < C.
There are n_P(C)=ceil(C/P)-ceil((C-W)/P) such boundaries, with
    W/(2P) <= n_P(C) <= W/P+1.
Divide the actual hit weight into n_P(C) equal parts. If there is already a nonconstant residence layer density, divide each part of that density by the SAME n_P(C); integration gives the same per-hit weights. Every part belongs to exactly one boundary, and is still eligible for all old arithmetic keys and its original layer-origin keys. Thus no new weight or duplicated payment.

For each K in this interval there is a unique actual common completed edge a->b before the hit, with C_a<=K<C_b<=C. This is because horizons are strictly increasing and the shared suffix has already started by C-W. Both tags traverse the edge, not merely visit its prevertex. Its selected p must be >z. Indeed for p<=z, p divides P and p divides C_a-1 or C_a+1. If C_a=K, neither divisibility is possible; otherwise the positive even distance K-C_a is congruent to +1 or -1 modulo p, hence at least p-1, so C_b=C_a+p-1 cannot strictly exceed K. Landing exactly on K is not the crossing edge used here. This argument does not require the boundary edge itself to be natural high or have positive deficit drift.

Define an exact crossing cohort at (K,a) as the full actual original tags completing that common physical edge. Its size n_{K,a} is the number of continuing tags b_a at a, independent of their later masks. It is the largest-source suffix of the visiting fibre. At a given K each original source crosses at most once.

The per-pair total weight assigned to any single K is at most
    U = 6P(1+B/W).                                    (2)
For all contributing hits, C lies in (K,K+W]. Their aggregate natural ordered weight is <=3(W+B) by the original gain bound and final overshoot. Each assigned fraction is at most 2P/W. Summing over actual hits gives (2). This holds across ALL deficit bands and layer origins simultaneously: the input hit weights partition over layers once, and the gain window W does not depend on the target's band. There is no logarithmic reuse factor. In the analytic regime U=O(P), not O(P sqrt(u)) as with the much narrower D_0/5 window. A pair's boundary key cannot use another crossing at the same K.

There is a narrower independent alternative for the buffered high deficit sectors of 8d268d3: take W=floor(D_0/5) with W>=2P for large X. Every buffered ascent or birth with d_birth<=D has shared age at least D/5>=W. In this case only a short high-deficit birth can fail the length condition, and U=O(P sqrt(u)). The all-sync fixed-age construction above is stronger in scope and is the main analytic reduction. The two constructions are alternative splits of the same current demand, not additive payments.

## 3. Joint boundary keys and the necessary thick crossing condition

Add the following eligible keys to the simultaneous incidence network of 8d268d3. For each original source M and each grid boundary K that it actually crosses, use key (K,M), capacity c=P L_H. Its physical crossing edge is uniquely defined by its original path; the key is NOT a free variable identifying arbitrary other physical states at K. Each routed pair part is eligible for the keys of BOTH actual endpoints at its own boundary, in addition to its previous keys. Async and short parts keep previous keys only. Every original source has at most G_M/P+1 grid crossings, so the total added capacity satisfies
    sum c <= L_H(sum_M G_M+NP)
          = O(NH L_H)=O(F/u^.25).                      (3)
No band or hit multiplicity factor. This construction can be used on the arbitrary retained ledger even when previous arithmetic payments were already optimized: use one simultaneous flow with all eligible keys. The old flow lifts by proportional splitting to the new demands. For a short-age absolute deletion, first remove those actual weights, or bound them separately as below. No removal of quadratic energy by a linear rare-root argument.

As in 8d268d3, residual is exact max_S(W(S)-c(S)). Choose an inclusion-minimal maximizing key set S (inclusion-minimal source side of a minimum cut, or a minimal-cardinality maximizer). For each k in S, internal incidence d_S(k)>c(k); removing k otherwise preserves or increases the objective. In particular for any boundary endpoint (K,M) in S, let q be the number of its distinct actual internal partners at K. These partners share that very physical completed edge. By (2),
    q U >= d_S(K,M) > P L_H,
    q > L_H/[6(1+B/W)].
Thus in the main analytic construction every internal long synchronous piece passes through an actual physical crossing with more than a constant multiple of B u^1.5 continuing source tags, with both actual endpoints in the same Hall set. This is stronger than just finding a thick possible reverse predecessor. The number of distinct such crossing keys (K,a), without counting endpoint tags, is at most O((NH/P+N)/L_H) by the total crossing membership bound, or by the complete thick-segment cover of a1c35db. The number bound does not control all their masses. In the narrower buffered variant the analogous necessary partner count is Omega(Bu).

For a fixed crossing with full continuing cohort size n and k active boundary endpoint tags in S, the actual internal weight assigned there is at most U choose(k,2). Both endpoints of every internal boundary demand are active at that crossing. Subtracting the boundary capacities of its k included tags gives an upper bound U choose(k,2)-c k. This quadratic is convex in k, and its maximum over 0<=k<=n is at an endpoint. Therefore the total boundary-assigned internal demand minus included boundary capacity is at most
    sum_(K,a) [ U choose(n_{K,a},2)-P L_H n_{K,a} ]_+. (4)
One may sum only occupied crossings of the cut, or over all actual crossings for an unconditional upper envelope. Only cohorts with n>1+2P L_H/U contribute positively to this crude bound. This is a net Hall bound, not just a bound on raw boundary weight.

## 4. Sharper nested natural local Hall envelope

The uniform bound U in (2) can be much larger than the actual local forward natural high residence, especially if the crossing edge is a large one or some original tags soon terminate. At crossing (K,a) list its n continuing tags M_1<...<M_n. Define
    rho_i(K,a)=sum_(x on M_i strictly after a,
                   K<C_x<=K+W) 2 h_(M_i)(x)/n_P(C_x).
Include only original completed natural high events; terminal snapshots have h=0. This is a frozen original natural quantity, independent of the other pair endpoint, prior quotas, deficit bands and earlier masks. By stop order every smaller tag's suffix is contained in every larger tag's suffix, so
    0<=rho_1<=...<=rho_n<=U.
The actual total weight of any pair M_i,M_j assigned to this crossing is <=min(rho_i,rho_j), since every assigned hit has the indicated horizon, and its exact weight is bounded by 2h on BOTH completed original suffixes. The count n_P(C_x) depends only on C_x and the chosen common grid and width. No assumption of uniform actual retained weights.

For any active subset of k tags at this crossing, the sum of these min envelopes is maximized among k-element subsets by taking the k largest sources. To see this, sort the chosen tags; its i-th smallest rho is no larger than the corresponding i-th smallest in the top k. Every pair min is then no larger. Define the explicit natural local excess
    H_(K,a)=max_(0<=k<=n) {
       sum_(i=n-k+1)^n (n-i) rho_i - c k
    },                                                (5)
where the empty term is zero. The weighted sum is exactly the min envelope over the top k rectangle of all unordered pairs. The maximum over k may be attained in the interior; do not replace it by k=n without further conditions. We have
    0<=H_(K,a)<= [U choose(n,2)-c n]_+.
For an included endpoint M_i in a minimal maximizing joint Hall set, the further necessary condition
    sum_(j!=i) min(rho_i,rho_j)>c
also holds. Thus the mere thickness of a physical crossing does not suffice: the local weighted high suffix must have sufficient natural occupation. The actual support need not attain any of these envelopes.

A useful sufficient reduction for the synchronous target follows by splitting the Hall objective into long, short, and async terms. For any S, subtract the included boundary capacities from the long term and bound by sum H_(K,a) as above. The short term is bounded by (1). The async term uses only the old pair keys; subtract the included old key capacities and bound it by the exact optimal residual R_async,old on the async-only demands with those capacities. Remaining origin-key capacities are nonnegative and can be ignored for this upper bound. Hence for the full-current-ledger all-sync joint network,
    R_joint <= E_short + R_async,old + sum_(K,a) H_(K,a). (6)
In particular, an upper bound sum H_(K,a)=o(F) together with R_async,old=o(F) suffices for this high residual. Alternatively use the crude envelope (4), which needs only continuing source counts and no natural suffix weights. For a bound on a smaller retained synchronous sector, apply the identical allocation to that sector and replace its short weight by the corresponding actual amount. Equation (6) does not claim the necessary upper estimate on the sum of H, nor that the isolated diagnostic ledger is the full cumulative analytic hybrid. The actual medium target is separate.

The significance of (5) is that it moves the remaining synchronous high estimate from arbitrary pairwise birth/ascent origin changes to explicit natural weighted suffix rectangles at strict grid crossings, with shared global capacity (3). It avoids summing independent per-level flows. Further arithmetic or dynamical estimates must control the large inherited crossing cohorts and their actual natural forward occupation; the crude total membership budget alone does not give o(F).
