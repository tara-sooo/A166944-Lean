## 1. Allow local groups: a first majority-loss mark on the retained forest

Use the canonical MIN and the actual barrier-cell/full-inverse stopping process of the preceding notes: N=X/2, B=ceil(sqrt(7X)), cutoff Y tending to infinity slowly, L=2 product_(3<=p<Y prime)p, cell admission T=ceil(X/log^2Y) and projected prime threshold ceil(B^2/p) for p>=Y. Retain ONLY the actual source tags that reach either kind of accounting stop, including their full executed prefixes and final proposed prime event if applicable. Their number S obeys S=N-o(X) on a no-twin window. This is not a claim that all original full paths are non-splitting despite different individual cutoffs.

Use the full physical graph of these retained tags, with arrival states V(t,C), proposed/actual next-event classes A(n,p,C), and one singleton injection per original source. Each V has a unique next A when continued, since next-event choice is deterministic and every retained continuing tag must reach an accounting stop. Each executed A has one successor V. A V in a stopped cell is terminal; an A at a stopped projected key is terminal. If two retained tags share such a vertex, the same accounting rule stops both there. A natural cutoff can truncate an original path but cannot split the retained accounting-stop suffixes. Thus this is a directed finite forest with no splitting, not the projected-key graph. V->A edges at one C are ordered before A->V edges with strict horizon growth. Original injections are separate singleton donors at V. No virtual terminal union is made.

Choose a second, much slower growing cutoff 5<=y<=Y, W=2 product_(3<=p<y prime)p. Then W divides L. Color each original root by its ORIGIN cell c=floor((C0-1)/W), whose physical horizons are in (cW,(c+1)W]. Each color has at most b_W=floor(W/4)+1 roots, by original horizon spacing 4. The same partition is used for current physical horizons C; horizon increases, so an origin color never exceeds the current physical cell index. Every p<y event remains inside its current W-cell. An executed edge between W-cells therefore uses p>=y, even when it skips cells.

At a physical vertex with m retained tags, let m_c be the count of origin color c. Permanently mark each tag at its FIRST vertex where its own color is NOT a strict majority, i.e. 2m_c<=m. Earlier local mergers of any number of same-color roots cause no marks. Even a mixed group does not mark its strict-majority color. A tag not yet marked is in a strict-majority color, so its entire current group has m<2b_W. This replaces the earlier fixed physical mass saturation threshold. Previously marked tags remain in the forest; marking is diagnostic, not a new stopping or deletion rule. The same height-priority convention as the previous note is used: first attained h>=H at V before or simultaneous with the first majority loss is classified as height first. A proposed unexecuted A has no attained post-height.

For first-height prefixes preceding any earlier majority loss, all earlier pre-states and executed A groups have mass <2b_W (including the A executing the first hitting step). Apply the preceding low-height rough-jump deletion and finite-time averaged potential with this bound in place of kappa. For H>=max(12,L,2(K+6)), H<=B, Y>=P>=11, 2P<=S_time, 0<=K<=S_time (S_time is observation time, not stopped mass), let
    eta=P[Pr_phase(D_(Y,2S_time)>K)+K/(S_time+1)]
          +1/log^2 P + sum_(q>=Y prime)(K-q+1)_+/q^2.
Then
    Z_maj(H)=O(W X eta),
    E_large=O(W X/log^2 H).
Here E_large counts tags having a p>2H+2 executed event before the first height/majority mark. The existing proof applies to the retained population because the per-physical-group unmarked mass bound is on the whole retained group, not only one donor. The same argument counts every premark executed arrival with p>=J>=7 and nonreset post-height h'<H by
    E_nr=O(W X H/(J log J)).
These are unconditional inequalities. The limit W eta->0 is still a separate analytic condition. If the previous infinite-CRT signed finite-time peak condition holds along a sequence, choose Y and P by the same diagonal argument, then y sufficiently slowly increasing that W eta->0 and W<=log^(1/4)Y. ## 2. Color variance and first majority-loss mass: a constant-factor equivalence

For a physical group A of size m define the exact color variance
    F_col(A)=(m^2-sum_c m_c^2)/(2m)
            =(1/m) sum_(i<j in A) 1{colors differ}.
This is the Hilbert variance of one-hot vectors e_c/sqrt(2); squared distance between different colors is 1. It is zero for a monochrome group of ANY size. Merge increments G(v)=F_col(A_v)-sum_(incoming donor groups)F_col(A_d) are nonnegative, and telescope exactly to terminal color variance F_col,term. Singleton injections have zero variance. At each merge,
    G(v)<=P_col(v)=(1/m_v) sum_(cross-donor pairs)1{colors differ}
by the same variance identity as in the previous clock-refined forest. Every pair counted in the proxy has a unique first common vertex, regardless of further merges.

Let M_maj count all permanently marked retained tags, without height restriction. There is a deterministic two-sided bound
    F_col,term <= M_maj <= 4 F_col,term.
Proof on one terminal tree: choose a largest terminal color of size a, total terminal size m, minority mass b=m-a. Then b/2<=F_col(A_terminal)<=b: use sum m_c^2<=am for the lower bound and >=a^2 for the upper. Every nonchosen-largest color has terminal fraction <=1/2, including ties, so all its tags are marked by termination. Thus F_col<=b<=marked mass. For the chosen largest color consider maximal vertices (closest to the terminal along the successor order) where its count is positive and at most half the group size. Their descendant/incoming trees are disjoint. Every marked tag of that color lies in one of these vertices' groups, where its color mass is at most the other-color mass. Their total other-color mass is at most b. Hence at most b largest-color tags are marked. All other marked tags number at most b, so M_maj(tree)<=2b<=4F_col(tree). Sum over disjoint terminal trees. This argument also handles ties and temporary loss followed by recovery of strict majority; it does NOT assume majority loss remains true after future mergers.

Unlike the previous first-saturation count M_res, this mark is quantitatively tied to actual cross-color merge variance and allows arbitrarily large monochrome groups within the origin capacity. It does not identify this variance with the older compressed high-event hat-kernel proxy: the present forest contains all actual V and A vertices and uses the color kernel.

Near cross-color pairs can be deleted even though this kernel is discontinuous. If 0<|M-M'|<=d and origin colors differ, both initial horizons lie within 2d of a W-cell boundary. Number of such roots is O(X(d+1)/W+W+d) for d<=W/4 (allow partial end cells). For each such root the sum of reciprocal first-common-group masses over near other roots is <=H_floor(d), by the earlier harmonic-rank argument in a non-splitting forest. Thus
    P_col,near(d)=O((X(d+1)/W+W+d) log(2d)).
Take d=floor(W/(log W)^2) for large W, with W slow enough that W log W=o(X). Then this is o(X), and
    P_col,far(d) >= M_maj/4-o(X).
The factor 4 is not asserted sharp; no upper bound on total far proxy is proved. This links the count of majority-loss marks, unlike all local saturation marks, to the actual far-pair obstruction on a growing distance scale.

## 3. Once-per-cell transport ledger

At any vertex in physical cell c, its resident tags are those of origin color c, all other tags are immigrants from strictly earlier cells. A source enters a physical cell at most once, and its entry (unless its origin cell) is an actual executed crossing edge. Let I_c be total immigrant admissions to c, counting each tag once in that cell (not each event and not each merge). Let E be the number of DISTINCT retained tags ever departing their own origin cell. Every immigrant admission is of a tag that has departed; a tag may contribute to I_c in several different cells.

Let L_c be the number of tags of origin c whose FIRST majority-loss mark occurs while still in c. Consider maximal vertices within c at which the resident color is nonempty and has lost strict majority. They have disjoint tag groups by the forest property and monotone C, and every tag counted in L_c belongs to one of these groups. At each such vertex resident mass <= immigrant mass. Thus
    L_c<=I_c.
This argument counts original tags, not group sizes multiplied repeatedly by the number of encounters. It works when immigrant witnesses are already marked and have huge mass. The proof uses the split-free retained forest; it is not claimed for a projected graph with arbitrary source expiration.

There is also a global once-per-departure bound: M_maj<=2E. Indeed marked tags that ever depart their origin cells are <=E. For marked tags that NEVER depart, their terminal class is in their own cell. In each terminal tree use the same maximal resident-loss argument to bound such marks by the number of immigrants in that terminal group. Those immigrants are distinct departed tags across terminal trees, so the never-departed marks total <=E. This does not imply E=o(X), and under a no-twin hypothesis large stopped classes can require extensive departure.

For depth d as in section2, discard resident roots whose origin horizon is within 2d of the LEFT boundary of their own cell. Their total is O(X(d+1)/W+W)=o(X). All other resident marks occur at C>=cW+2d. In the maximal-vertex proof restricted to these deep marks, each charged immigrant must itself attain some horizon >=cW+2d within cell c. Define I_c^deep(d) to be the number of immigrant tags attaining such a horizon in c before their retained accounting stop, including final arrival/mark. Then
    L_c^deep(d)<=I_c^deep(d).
Every corresponding resident-immigrant pair is separated in M by at least d, since C0=2M-2, foreign origin <=cW and deep resident origin >=cW+2d. At a deep admission, let e be the landing horizon and p its crossing label. Either p-1>=d, or e<=cW+(p-1)<cW+d because the pre-horizon was <=cW, and hence the immigrant must accumulate at least d additional horizon gain INSIDE c. This gives the exact transport dichotomy: a deep immigrant needs a sufficiently large entering jump or sufficiently large subsequent in-cell gain. Neither case is automatically rare; the ledger removes shallow immigrants and avoids repeated charges within one cell. A high event can skip cells; no admission is counted in any skipped cell.

## 4. Rare gateways: delete their local effects even at arbitrary incoming mass

Use H=floor(log^(1/4)B), Q=ceil(B exp(-sqrt(log B))), J=max(7,floor(H/sqrt(log H))), W<=log^(1/4)Y with Y<=c log log B chosen slowly, as in the preceding note. Declare a crossing entrance rare if ANY of:
  A. label p>=Q;
  B. label p>2H+2 AND r(pre-horizon)>=p/8;
  C. label p>=J and attained landing is nonreset of height <H.
Other entrances are ordinary. These predicates apply to actual executed crossings in the retained forest; no mass or prior-height restriction is imposed in defining rare entrances. A cell is exceptional if it admits at least one rare entrance, even one by a previously marked high-mass group. We bound the NUMBER of such cells, not total incoming rare mass.

For A, every landing horizon C' has p|C' or C'+2. There are O(X/p) possible C' per side for p<=B. Count destination cells by landing horizons, irrespective of clocks or history:
    #cells_A=O(X sum_(Q<=p<=B prime)1/p)
             =O(X/sqrt(log B)).
For B, the previous uniform sieve-cost bound S_p^cost=O(X/log^2 p) implies O(X/(p log^2 p)) possible pre-horizons C with r(C)>=p/8 and p eligible. Each determines one C'=C+p-1, hence one cell. Thus #cells_B=O(X/log^2 H). For C, parity-dependent nonreset congruences give 2p|(h-b(t)), h-b(t)>0 for p>=7 and h>=0; with h<H there are O(H/p) possible nonzero multiples, no additive 1. Count O(X/p) possible landings per side, so
    #cells_C=O(X H sum_(p>=J)1/p^2)=O(XH/(J log J)).
The last bound counts candidate physical states, which is also a valid bound on the number of destination cells. These estimates count all possible gateways, so are uniform over any selected cell set and arbitrary immigrant mass. No inference from a sparse number of gateways to small TOTAL transported mass is made.

Each exceptional cell contains <=b_W original resident roots. Hence the total number of resident first majority-loss marks IN ALL exceptional cells is
    O(W X [1/sqrt(log B)+1/log^2 H+H/(J log J)])=o(X).
Indeed W<=log^(1/4)Y and Y<=c log log B imply W=o(sqrt(log H)). This is the new unconditional source deletion for the local impact of rare arrivals at ANY previous depth, even after arbitrarily many internal merges or resets, not only when a rare prime is the immediate witness at the mark. A whole huge immigrant group entering through one rare gateway can affect at most b_W distinct resident roots in its destination cell. If that group later enters another cell via an ordinary gateway, that later local effect is NOT deleted by this bound.

For nonexceptional cells every immigrant admission is ordinary. By section3 and the deletion of shallow origins, the total resident majority marks are at most
    sum_(nonexceptional cells) I_c^deep(d) + o(X)
    <= I_ord^deep(d)+o(X),
where I_ord^deep counts ordinary immigrant admissions reaching the prescribed depth (one per source per cell; can include ordinary entrants in exceptional cells, harmlessly). The depth dichotomy splits this into large entering-jump and subsequent in-cell travel counts, not an estimate that either is small.

A migrant tag whose first majority loss precedes height H has already departed its origin cell before or at that mark. Its first departure was executed while still unmarked and below H; the arrival must also be below H if the mark occurs there or later before height (height priority). If its departure gateway was of type A or B, then p>2H+2 for large X, counted by E_large from section1. If type C, it is counted by E_nr. Those distinct tags number o(X), by the small premark physical mass bound, even if other marks occurred elsewhere. Therefore such migrant first marks total <= D_ord+o(X), where D_ord counts DISTINCT tags making a first departure from their own cell by an ordinary gateway BEFORE any prior height or majority mark (allow height/mark at its arrival in this upper bound). The count can be evaluated on actual prefixes without estimating counterfactual ghost histories.

## 5. Joint necessary inequality under no twins, with local saturation allowed

The stopped tags partition into height-first, majority-loss-first (strictly before any height hit), and neither. To bound the last type, each of its terminal physical groups has size <2b_W and the own attained pre-height (prime stop) or arrival height (cell stop) is <H. For a stopped projected prime key (p,C), the actual proposed n must lie in a range of O(H/p+1) clocks because 0<=2t-C<H and n-t<=2p. At most 2b_W tags per such A can be unmarked. With J_p<=N/B, total such prime-stop tags are O(W J_p(1+H/Y))=o(X). For cell stops, root injections O(J_c L)=o(X) since J_c=O(log^2Y). Every other entry crosses via one of at most two projected gateways per prime at the boundary, with O(1+H/p) actual arrival clocks at h<H. Each V containing an unmarked tag has size <2b_W, so even counting each gateway separately gives O(W J_c(pi(B)+H log log(3B)))=o(X). This uses H much smaller than B; no terminal clock moment assumption or virtual union is required for this unmarked low-height count.

Thus on no-twin windows
    Z_maj(H)+M_maj,low >= S-o(X)=N-o(X).
Combine with sections3-4 to get
    Z_maj(H)+D_ord+I_ord^deep(d) >= X/2-o(X).
Here Z_maj<=O(W X eta). A sufficient strict deficit is C W eta + (D_ord+I_ord^deep)/X <= 1/2-epsilon for a fixed positive epsilon and an actual uniform first-passage constant C on arbitrarily large windows. The stronger two o(X) statements also suffice. This is an alternative to the previous unweighted first-saturation count and old hat-kernel far proxy. The earlier proof with M_res counted local groups even when all roots came from one origin cell; the new ledger does not. Still, I_ord^deep is an incidence count across cells, can exceed X greatly, and may be harder than a direct far-variance estimate. Do not treat this inequality alone as a substantive upper bound on ordinary transport. It identifies a concrete source of the remaining deficit: first exits at ordinary gateways before majority loss, and deep ordinary immigrant occupation after any previous mergers. The global pre-stop cost O(X^2/log^2Y) is too large by itself to control these at the very slowly growing W. A new bound on ordinary gateway transport is necessary. The color variance relation offers a second route using actual merge energy rather than this possibly much larger incidence count. Neither eta->0 nor a sufficient ordinary-transport/far-variance upper bound is proved here.

## 7. Remaining work

A precise local-group accounting system is now available: arbitrary local mergers are free until an origin color loses strict majority, all majority-loss marks are controlled by terminal color variance, and resident marks caused in rare-entry cells have unconditionally negligible total source count even when the immigrant groups are large. Further analytic work must bound ordinary deep transport or actual cross-color merge energy, especially small-label repeated cross-cell transport and reset chains, and the earlier finite-time LOW potential coefficient. The deep-transport ledger is a reduction, not a vanishing estimate for ordinary flows. No new branch, no main edit.
