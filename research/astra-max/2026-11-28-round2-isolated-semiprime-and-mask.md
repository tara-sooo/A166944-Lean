# Real shifted semiprimes forced by the .334 first-hit bound

Issue #10, second round, 2026-11-28 JST. This deduction uses the [.334 rough-pair theorem](https://github.com/tara-sooo/A166944-Lean/blob/86c561d8cf0adfa58e57248fabb562e5c34648f4/research/astra-max/2026-11-28-round2-rough-pairs-past-one-third.md). It also gives an independent way to justify the squarefree restriction in the ordinary well-factorable distribution estimates.

## Stationary M85 or an isolated prime times a prime

Put \(c=.334,\ b=.4,\ H=\lfloor R^b\rfloor,\ \Pi_R=2C_2R/\log^2R\). Fix a sufficiently small positive constant \(\eta\), independent of \(R\). For each sufficiently large slab at least one of these conclusions holds:

* At least \(.002\Pi_R\) distinct roots reach \(H\) at the original position with zero displacement.
* A family of at least \(.002\Pi_R\) distinct canonical roots has actual first label \(p\) on one of its neighbors \(A+s=pv\) (\(s=\pm1\)), with **both \(p,v\) prime**, satisfying
\[
 R^{c+\eta}<p<R^{b-\eta},\qquad
 \min\{v,P^-(pv-2s)\}>pR^\eta .
\]
Both sides are still \(4R^c\)-rough. The displacement of this first event is exactly \(p-1\); its landing position is \(A-p+1\). It occurs before the would-be stationary first-\(H\) crossing.

Start with the at least \(.005\Pi_R\) two-sided \(4R^c\)-rough roots. If fewer than \(.002\Pi_R\) reach \(H\) at birth, more than \(.003\Pi_R\) must fire first. The smallest prime \(p\) on either side is between \(4R^c\) and \(2H+O(1)\) by the first preheight label bound. Its side is composite, and exactly \(pv\) with \(v\) prime, since \(3c>1\). Ties across sides are impossible at odd primes. Delete the lower and upper boundary strips \(p\le R^{c+\eta}\) and \(p\ge R^{b-\eta}\). In each strip, count semiprime rows by the exponent of the smaller factor and upper-sieve the other side to \(4R^c\) with Wu Lemma 2.9 at level \(4/7-.0001\). Their limiting upper divided by \(\Pi_R\) is \(O(\eta)\), uniformly for small fixed \(\eta\), since the differential mass is \(dt/[t(1-t)]\) and the upper coefficient is bounded.

If the other side also has a prime \(q\le pR^\eta\), take its least prime, so \(p\le q\le pR^\eta\) and \(q\ne p\). The other side is then \(qw\), \(w\) prime. For fixed \(p,q,s\) the congruences define one \(A\bmod pq\). Along it \(v=qj+v_0,\ w=pj+w_0\) and \(pv_0-qw_0=2s\). The parameter interval has length comparable to \(N=R/(pq)\gg R^{.18}\) when \(\eta<.01\). At every odd prime below \(R^{.01}\) the two affine forms have two distinct roots, because their leading coefficients are invertible and their determinant is \(\pm2\). A dimension-two upper sieve gives \(O(N/L^2)\) uniformly. For instance the elementary Selberg sieve with divisor weights supported on \(d\le R^{.01}\) has main \(\ll N/L^2\): the usual normalizer is \(\sum_{d\le R^{.01}}\mu^2(d)\prod_{\ell\mid d}2/(\ell-2)\) over odd \(d\), which is \(\gg L^2\) by restricting to primes below \(R^\kappa\) for sufficiently small fixed \(\kappa\), applying the Euler product and the first log moment to truncate. Its divisor-count remainder is \(O(R^{.02+o(1)})\) per fixed pair, absorbed by \(N/L^2\). The short-interval prime reciprocal sum \(\sum_{p\le q\le pR^\eta}1/q\) is \(O(\eta)+o(1)\) uniformly for \(p\ge R^c\), while the outer sum of \(1/p\) is bounded. The total deletion is \(O(\eta)\Pi_R+o(\Pi_R)\). Pick \(\eta\) so all deletions together cost less than \(.001\Pi_R\). The own cofactor satisfies \(v\gg R^{1-b}\), hence \(v>pR^\eta\).

The least prime has an opportunity by age \(3p/2+O(1)\). Every competing factor has earliest age at least \((pR^\eta-7)/2\). Thus the arithmetic \(p\) is the real winner, even allowing both signs and both cofactor phases. For either sign, the landing has \(p\mid x\) or \(p\mid x-2\). At most two such primes of exponent \(>.334\) divide either integer in this slab; each determines the ancestor \(A=x+p-1\). The moving branch has \(\gg R/L^2\) **distinct landing positions**.

If stationary M85 does not hold on an infinite sequence of slabs, the moving conclusion holds on all sufficiently large slabs once the count is below the stated first alternative (in particular eventually if M85 fails even on subsequences). Partition the compact interval \([.334,.4]\) into fixed bins of width at most .000050. Partition further by sign and the two clock phases. On an infinite subsequence a *single fixed* bin, sign, and phase carry \(\gg R/L^2\) roots. Thus this branch gives an actual fixed-window shifted-semiprime lower at roughness exceeding .334, up to orientation of the shift. The selected bin need not be [.374900,.374950]; the opposite side is prime or semiprime, whereas A1 prescribes a three-prime sector. In a HIGH phase the existing actual common-time theorem applies here with the stronger \(p>R^{.334+\eta}\); a RESET phase gives postheight 0 or 3. The [separate phase-selected lower](https://github.com/tara-sooo/A166944-Lean/blob/1abcb353b064d61f6a00b77dc1c2e8df4bd9ca30/research/astra-max/2026-11-28-round2-cofactor-phase-high-and-twin-criterion.md) obtains an alternative with only HIGH as the moving outcome at its stated exponents.

## Squarefree projection by multiplicative twists

Suppose a remainder \(\Delta(d)\) satisfies \(|\Delta(d)|\ll RL^j/d\) on \(d\le D<R\), for fixed \(j\). This holds on the original bounded-multiplicity shifted product boxes, including nonsquarefree moduli. Suppose its sum against every order-one well-factorable sequence of level \(D\) is \(O_A(RL^{-A})\), uniformly in that sequence. The same estimate holds for the sum restricted to squarefree \(d\) all of whose prime factors belong to any prescribed set \(\mathcal P\), uniformly in \(\mathcal P\).

Indeed, multiplication by a completely multiplicative \(1\)-bounded function \(h\) preserves well-factorability for **every** split: if \(\lambda=\lambda_1*\lambda_2\), then \(h\lambda=(h\lambda_1)*(h\lambda_2)\). First insert the completely multiplicative indicator of the permitted prime set. For a given prime \(p\), each projector \(\mathbf1_{v_p(d)=j}\) is the integral of \(e^{2\pi i\alpha v_p(d)}e^{-2\pi i\alpha j}\) over \(0\le\alpha\le1\), with twist of norm one. Hence \(\mathbf1_{v_p(d)\ge2}=1-\mathbf1_{v_p(d)=0}-\mathbf1_{v_p(d)=1}\) is a superposition of such twists of total variation at most 3. For squarefree \(e\), the projector \(\mathbf1_{e^2\mid d}\) has total variation at most \(3^{\omega(e)}\) by product measure. Insert \(\mu^2(d)=\sum_{e^2\mid d}\mu(e)\). For \(e\le E=L^K\), integrate the original uniform distribution bound and sum, losing at most \(E^{O(1)}\). For \(e>E\) use the trivial bound and \(|\lambda(d)|\le1\) (split \(D\times1\)): the tail is
\[
 \ll R L^j\sum_{e>E}\frac1{e^2}\sum_{k\le D/e^2}\frac1k
 \ll R L^{j+1}/E .
\]
Choose \(K\), then the original log-saving exponent. This proves the claim, without ever requiring the masked sequence itself to be well-factorable. It applies directly to the Wu part of both bilateral arguments. For a class of boxed modulus weights stable under completely multiplicative twists with the same uniform bound, the identical proof applies. This projection does not turn the additive condition that a shifted quotient is prime into a modulus weight.
