New: compress XX's almost-full tagged gain (conditional on small strengthened missing clock) into disjoint complete-event blocks with an exact constant core population per block; localized grouped Fourier cut estimates at the original lifetime scale; fixed-prime-scale periodic cut supply with macroscopic recovery alternative and no rate comparison between o(NT) and a shrinking cut density. ## 1. Exact common-event core blocks

Retain XX's true physical marked-edge chains, first large threshold L=floor(b0/2), m=floor(L/ell), good tags with first large vertex and no later edge flow <m. All parameters and XX's strengthened missing measure are unchanged. Write Mhat_full=int(1-chi_hat)dW; G_all=total actual tagged gain. XX gives total prefix age and deficit <=4 Mhat_full+O(NB), bad after-first-large returns <=N(m-1)/(L-m+1). For good tags after their first large vertex, marked chains cover all traversed edges and their chain index changes at most floor(log_2 N) times. In particular gain on complete events after first large of good tags is >=G_all-4 Mhat_full-O(NB)-NH(m-1)/(L-m+1).

Choose even D=2 floor(X^(.7)/2), so B<<D and D log N=o(H). On each actual marked chain take the ordered physical positive-gain jump edges (possibly with true wait edges in between). Form successive full blocks greedily: start at first unused jump, stop at first jump bringing block gained length to >=D; then repeat. Terminal incomplete block (<D) omitted. Every full block has D<=G^raw<D+B. Take the intersection of the ACTUAL tag sets of its first and last jump, restrict to good tags whose first jump predeparture vertex lies at/after their own first large vertex. Let b_Q be the size of this core (omit empty). Because marked edges are one deterministic directed path, each such tag really traverses the entire intervening path including wait edges, with no retirement there. The first jump could have different actual arrival times among core tags due to waiting-state merges. Remove its entire gain and wait, not just a fraction; all remaining complete events now have the SAME actual arrival tick, departure tick, pre/post horizon, label and wait for every core member. This is a true common event word, not just a common projected label sequence. Denote its gained length G_Q. Then D-B<=G_Q<D+B. No new continuation beyond an original terminal. Core sets may vary between blocks, including on the same chain; do not claim the full physical population is constant, only the selected common core.

Blocks are disjoint in physical edges; for each original tag, retained complete events are disjoint. A good tag's marked suffix intersects at most 1+floor(log_2 N) chain runs. In any run, loss from incomplete initial/final block at most 2(D+B), plus at most B per full block traversed (first jump omitted). A jump straddling first large or terminal boundaries costs at most O(B) per tag. Summed:
    sum_Q b_Q <= NH/D,
    G_core:=sum_Q b_Q G_Q
      >= G_all -4 Mhat_full -NH(m-1)/(L-m+1)
         -O(NB+N(D+B)(1+log N)+NB H/D).                (1)
Also G_core<=G_all and #nonempty blocks<=NH/(mD) from raw chain gained length; the latter counts blocks even with core smaller than m (or no core). Under finite twins and Mhat_full=o(NT), G_core=(1-o(1))NT. A core's size may be one; nevertheless total weighted loss is o(NT). No requirement for b_Q to approximate each instantaneous full slot mass. The gain proof deliberately counts original tags, so does not multiply unweighted arithmetic support by a typical mass. This construction enables any arithmetic estimate on an actual contiguous complete-event block, with uniform local error e_Q, to be summed using exact weights b_Q; if e_Q=o(D) uniformly, the total error is o(NT). No assumption that all blocks have shallow initial deficits.

## 2. Localize direct grouped cut smoothing to actual D-scale blocks

Use direct 2a47be1 grouped extra-prime construction R largest with P_R<=sqrt X, S=floor(R/loglog R), Z=floor(exp(3S)), smoothing length lambda=ceil(P_R^(4/11)), base alpha, extra density Q_cut, kappa=2/(alpha Q_cut). Its pointwise upper/lower polynomials have weighted absolute coefficient sum R^O(1), expectation gap relative to Q_cut O((log R)^(-2A-8)), and all term periods L_F=P_R q_F=X^(1/2+o(1)). Each normalized monomial f_F has mean 1 and moving-average variance <=exp(-.001r) over its FULL period, r=pi_o(R). The full-period variance is the direct certificate, not a new Fourier estimate.

For any translated integer interval I of length |I|=O(D) in j=J/2 coordinates, periodicity bounds its squared fluctuation sum by (|I|+L_F) exp(-.001r)=O(D exp(-.001r)), since L_F=o(D). Raw discrepancy in I is <=L_F (nonnegative normalized monomial). Apply the SAME pointwise polynomial comparison and weighted triangle inequality as direct, divide by Q_cut, and retain expectation errors as relative multiples of |I|, not X. Uniformly for |I|<=O(D) this yields
    sum_I |(f_sv)_lambda/Q_cut-1|² <<_A D/(log R)^(2A),
    |sum_I f_sv/Q_cut-|I|| <<_A D/(log R)^A.           (2)
For smaller intervals the error is in D units, not their own length. This removes the full-X error in the direct statement on these blocks. In particular the relative error on whole core blocks is o(1), despite H=X/u^.25 and log R~ell (a direct O(X/(log R)^A) error would NOT suffice). Padding by lambda for smoothing keeps O(D) since lambda<<D. On any chosen disjoint union of event intervals of total gain F and count n in a core block, normalized mass lies between F-2(lambda-1)n-O_A(D/(log R)^A) and F+2(lambda-1)n+O_A(D/(log R)^A), by the same inner/outer smoothing argument and Cauchy for the L1 deviation on the entire padded window. Mixed direct lattice and smoothed bounds use the error ONCE per block, not once per event. Eroded intervals in direct bypass estimate work identically. Consequently summing with b_Q costs O_A(NH/(log R)^A), regardless of changing full population. No uniform estimate for short intervals below X^(1/2+o(1)) follows; no new asymptotic Fourier constant numerically checked.

## 3. Fixed-prime-scale alternative (different order of limits)

Under finite twins choose an integer S>=11 strictly above all lower twin primes, FIXED as X tends to infinity. Choose fixed R>=S (can take R=S), V=4 floor(S²/4), K_cut=2P_S>2V (take S sufficiently large), L_cut=5S or any fixed integer >4S, a=1-4S/L_cut>0. Fix extra sieve cutoff Z>R, not depending on X, large enough that kappa/(Z-1)<=1/4. Here base cuts are multiples of K_cut and satisfy J mod f in {0,2,...,f-3} for S<f<=R, and J!=+/-1 mod f for R<f<=Z. Density in j coordinate alpha Q_cut, kappa=2/(alpha Q_cut). Such fixed Z exists since for fixed S,R the Mertens product gives kappa=O_(S,R)((log Z)^2). This uses a finite (possibly enormous) full CRT period P_Z in j. No computational enumeration of that huge period required. Normalized cut mass on every even half-open interval of gain G equals G+O(P_Z) by exact periodicity; nonnegative weight over one period sums to 2P_Z. Thus whole core mass G_core+O(P_Z NH/D)=G_core+o(NT) by (1), with constants FIXED FIRST. This does not use the grouped Fourier argument. In particular old comparison o(NT) vs V NT/kappa is now valid: V/kappa is a positive fixed constant, however small. With growing S it was not valid without rate information. No effective upper on the last twin is needed for this conditional proof.

At each core word classify every surviving cut into anchor (J=C), launch (0<J-C<=V), landing (J-C>V and 0<C+g-J<=V/2), bypass (both farther), using half-open jumps [C,C+g). Exactly as direct: all anchors have p>Z so their normalized mass <=kappa G_core/(Z-1)<=G_core/4. A launch corridor starts at crossing and has gain >=max(V,g_cross), a landing starts immediately afterward and has gain >=V/2, continuing to first plateau >=J+V. At most one incomplete (pending) endpoint corridor per core block, since K_cut>2V. All completed corridor prehorizons satisfy 0<|C-J|<=V. Finite twins plus S fixed beyond last lower member makes every such corridor fast w<=2S; high labels p>L_cut decrease ordinary deficit by >=a g. Corridors are disjoint in physical events within block (a large event can launch near start and land near end for distinct cuts, but landing starts after the event). Across blocks physical jumps already disjoint. Retain the actual core weights on all cuts and completed corridors. Let D_corr=sum b_Q[max(V,g_cross) for launches + V/2 for landings]. Let L_gain be tagged actual p<=L_cut gain INSIDE completed corridors, P_core the sum of positive ordinary deficit variation on core events OUTSIDE all completed corridors. Let G_byp be tagged gain of core events containing at least one bypass cut (count each such event once per core tag). These are different resources, possibly overlapping in events; no assertion of disjointness between G_byp and P_core.

For each original tag, total net deficit drop of its chosen corridors >=a times its demand -(a+5) times its low gain. Telescope on the ENTIRE original canonical path; positive variation outside chosen corridors in omitted events <=5 times omitted gain. Sum with exact actual tag counts:
    a D_corr <= (a+5)L_gain + P_core
                   +5(G_all-G_core)+6N.                (3)
No repeat of block initial deficits. Alternatively telescope over each good post-large suffix with initial deficit sum<=4 Mhat_full+O(NB) (XX), and retain exact positive variation in gaps between core blocks. This is not a separate additive credit. The canonical version (3) is convenient with small missing.

Every completed endpoint contributes >=V/2 demand, so normalized endpoint mass <=2 kappa D_corr/V + kappa NH/D (pending). Each bypass jump has g>3V/2, at most ceil((g-3V/2)/K_cut) bypass cuts. Thus its normalized mass summed <=kappa (1/K_cut+2/(3V)) G_byp; can retain exact erosion sum instead. Therefore
    (1-kappa/(Z-1)) G_core
       <= (2 kappa/(a V)) [(a+5)L_gain+P_core
                         +5(G_all-G_core)+6N]
          + kappa(1/K_cut+2/(3V)) G_byp + o(NT).        (4)
The o(NT) is uniform in all actual core blocks for the chosen fixed S,R,Z, from periodic and pending errors. This is a quantitative inequality on the strengthened missing branch even before assuming it is small: insert (1) into omitted-gain term and LHS. Under finite twins with Mhat_full=o(NT), it gives
    (3/4-o(1)) NT <= (2 kappa/(a V))[(a+5)L_gain+P_core]
                                  + kappa(1/K_cut+2/(3V)) G_byp. (5)
In particular at least one of localized low gain, positive variation OUTSIDE corridors, or gain on labels g>3V/2 in bypass events is Omega_(S,R,Z)(NT). If all three are o(NT) for one fixed admissible S,R,Z then this excludes the small-missing branch; equivalently (1),(4) provide a positive normalized missing lower (or contradict finite twins) in that restricted regime. For example if L_gain and P_core=o(NT), then G_byp >= (3/(4 kappa(1/K_cut+2/(3V)))-o(1)) NT. This is a constant-fraction necessity for actual gain, not a normalized count only. Constants can be tiny and depend on S; do not send S->infinity inside this conclusion or deduce a uniform tail tightness estimate. Positive variation or high bypass gain is NOT presently bounded by o(NT), so (5) is not a contradiction. Unlike growing cut threshold estimates, this formulation eliminates the mismatch of submacroscopic corridor demand and merely little-o missing clock, at the cost of only fixed-label thresholds. The growing-scale (2) retains stronger polynomial label distinctions and can use the old hybrid local joint witness machinery with the same core weights; its kappa grows, so that distinction remains.
