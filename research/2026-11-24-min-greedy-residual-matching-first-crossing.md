## 1. Separate the matching threshold from the original stopping threshold

Keep canonical MIN, odd M in [X,2X), N=X/2, B=ceil(sqrt(7X)), original individual cutoffs, LOW7 L=210. Run the original source-preserving projected-key absorption to completion, e.g. uniform cap T=B. Let S be the number of stopped original tags, J the number of stopped keys, and T_min the minimum stopping threshold, so J<=S/T_min. Retain all the true paths to their original stops. The prior arithmetic budget implies S=N-o(X) on no-twin windows under the original uniform cap. The following construction does not change that process or the paths.

Choose a separate integer s>=2 with s=o(T_min). Initially all S stopped tags are unmatched. In increasing horizon order visit every proposed high key (including original stops). At a key take ONLY its stopped tags still unmatched. If this pool contains m>=s tags, sort them by original odd M and pair the first floor(m/2) with the last floor(m/2), in the same order. Remove the paired tags from the future MATCHING pools, not from their original MIN trajectories. If m<s, do nothing. At most s-1 unmatched tags remain after any key, including an original stop. Let V be the total number of resulting pairs. Then
    2V >= S-(s-1)J >= S(1-(s-1)/T_min).
All pairs are disjoint globally and have original separation >=s, because the roots have spacing two and the paired positions differ by ceil(m/2). Pairs may belong to DIFFERENT final stop cohorts. That is harmless: each pair shares a high key at which it was formed. If no twins, V>=(1/2-o(1))N. The older sorted-half construction within final stops has greater minimum separation (of order T_min); this new construction intentionally sacrifices that separation to bound the earlier unmatched source masses.

For every pair charge at its FIRST common proposed high key (not necessarily its pairing key). Its two tags were still unmatched on arrival at this first contact. If the contact is earlier than the pairing key, the pool size at that contact had to be <s: otherwise at most one unmatched tag could survive. Also, if a previous executed high key feeds either arm, that tag survived its matching step and belonged to its residual pool, of size <s. Consequently, for fixed previous keys (q,D),(q',D'), the number of charged pairs between those previous keys, over ALL subsequent actual clocks and contact horizons, is at most s-1. If both tags have the same preceding high key then this cannot be their first high-key contact. These statements do not use the whole physical mass m(q,D), m(q',D') or m(p,C), nor require any projected group to be non-splitting.

At a key before matching, the number of still-unmatched stopped tags is at most O_L(s log(2X)/log log(3X)): initial-low arrivals are at most floor(L/4)+1, and distinct previous high keys have q|C-g or C-g+2, 0<=g<L even, and D=C-g-q+1. Each shifted integer O(X) has O(log X/log log X) distinct prime factors (elementary factorial/product bound), and each prior key supplies <s surviving tags. This pointwise pool bound is valid even at an original overshooting stop. The stronger fixed-parent-pair residual bound above is used in the arithmetic theorem.

## 2. A same-parent-label parity obstruction, without a lag bound

Use the individual arm formulas at a first projected contact (p,C):
    2kq=(2K+1)p+e1, 2k'q'=(2K'+1)p+e2,
    |e_i|<=E=7L+4r(C)+7, each e_i odd.
The previous high height indices k,k' are nonnegative, and K,K' are hypothetical post-height indices for the current clock, also nonnegative even at a proposed stop. There is no restriction on j=(K-K')/2. If p>2E, neither prior index can be zero, and an initial-only LOW arm is also impossible for sufficiently large p since its pre-height <=6+6L and p-1<=h_pre+4r(C). Moreover q,q'>=p/(4H) if k,k'<=H.

Suppose q=q'>L+2 at distinct preceding high projected keys. Their pre-horizons differ by <L and each is even. If both divisibility sides agree they coincide mod q, so are equal, contradicting distinct keys. Thus the sides are opposite, and if D_- is the pre-horizon with q|D_--1 and D_+ the one with q|D_++1, we have D_--D_+=2. The preceding clock phases are u_-=0 mod2q, u_+=q+2 mod2q, and the post-heights are 2k_-q and 6+2k_+q. Hence
    u_- - u_+ = (k_- - k_+)q-2 = q-2 mod2q.
So k_- - k_+ is odd. On the other hand, eliminate q modulo p from the two individual congruences:
    p | k'e1-k e2.
If p>2HE and k,k'<=H, the right side has absolute value <p, hence must vanish. But it is odd because k,k' have opposite parity and e1,e2 are odd. Contradiction. Thus for p sufficiently large relative to H,E and L, same prior label first contacts are IMPOSSIBLE regardless of clock lag or original mass. If p equals either prior label and p>L+3, the earlier consecutive-high horizon congruence excludes it. The initial/root and reset exclusions apply regardless of prior mass too.

## 3. All-product, unrestricted-lag, mass-free matching theorem for the new witnesses

Let s=ceil(log^kappa B), H=log^beta B, z=ceil(log^gamma B), P=log^lambda B, with fixed positive exponents and
    lambda>beta+gamma,  kappa+4 beta+2 gamma<2 lambda.
For sufficiently large B, P>max(2HE,4H(L+3),7+6L+4z) where E<=7L+4z+7. Consider first projected contacts of the forward-greedy witnesses with p>=P, r(C)<=z and prior indices <=H on both arms whenever present. The initial-arm and reset exclusions force both prior high events and positive indices; section2 forces all three labels distinct. There is NO actual mass bound on these previous or current keys, and NO lag, CRT product, offset, or lower previous-prime band condition.

Fix p,k,k',e1,e2,g,g' and the divisibility signs. The individual congruences specify two nonzero residue classes q mod p and q' mod p; q,q'>=p/(4H) and q,q'<=B. Write A_p and A'_p for the unweighted prime progression counts, S_p and S'_p for their harmonic sums over q>=p/(4H). By the preceding lag-free note,
    A_p,A'_p=O((1+B/p)/(1+log(B/p))),
    S_p,S'_p=O((H+1+log log(3B))/p).
All actual labels can be counted independently, dropping the lag relation entirely. The three congruences for C give O_L(1+X/(pqq')) possible horizons. Each determines both previous keys, and at most s-1 actual witnesses can be charged to this tuple by residual mass. Hence sum
    O_L(s H^2 (1+z)^2 sum_{p>=P prime}
                       [A_p A'_p+(X/p)S_p S'_p]).
For the +1 term, split p at sqrt B. Even summing over integers gives
    sum A_p A'_p=O(B^2/(P log^2 B)+B^(3/2)),
using 1+B/p<=2B/p and sum_{p>=P}1/p^2=O(1/P). For the volume term sum 1/p^3=O(1/P^2). Thus
    V_good = O_L(X s H^2 (1+z)^2
       [1/(P log^2 B)+B^(-1/2)+(H+1+log log(3B))^2/P^2]).
This tends to zero relative to X if the above conditions and additionally kappa+2beta+2gamma<lambda+2 hold (the latter is redundant for the example below, but retained explicitly for general exponents). No sum over j or actual group mass appears. Take kappa=beta=gamma=1/8, lambda=1/2, giving O_L(X/log^(1/8) B). It applies to all first contacts satisfying the local p,r,index conditions, including SAME-parent, reset and root cases (which are impossible there), and all lag sizes. It works at arbitrary actual overshoots, including original stops, under any original stop rule for which T_min/s tends to infinity. The result is for the NEW matching, not the earlier fixed sorted-half within final cohorts. This removes a substantive restriction of 2c5864bda3a26b9203ac0d087280581ff5b64e6a.

## 4. Much smaller prefix cost before greedy pairing

Truncate each stopped root at its pairing key before executing it, or at its actual original stop if it remains unmatched. This is an accounting prefix only. Let R_match be the total r-cost of executed events in those prefixes. Each executed high key in the truncated paths has fewer than s residual tags, by construction; initial LOW blocks and later LOW suffixes cost <=3L/2 each. The prior arithmetic sieve S_p=O(X/log^2(2p)) and #eligible horizons O(X/p) give
    R_match <= O_L(X + s X B/log^3 B + s X log log(3B)).
In particular this is o(X B) for s=log^kappa B, kappa<3, and far smaller than the original R_pre=O(X^2/log^3 B). At all attained states h<=6+4 R_i and gain<=6+4 R_i for each tag's own prefix cost R_i. This bounds high heights BEFORE pairing, including before the first common key of any formed pair. The hypothetical post-height of a proposed endpoint includes one additional r wait, so the previous paragraph's bound on attained states must not be applied to that post-height without adding 4r. For example s=log^(1/8) B and A=B/log^2 B imply at most O_L(X/log^(7/8) B) tags with an attained height >=A. Similarly the number of pairs whose original separation is >=A is at most O_L(R_match/A), up to the fixed gain constants: at the shared pairing horizon the earlier root's gain is at least twice the original separation. Thus this matching trades large original separations for small cost. It does not turn the old far matching of separation B into one of equal separation with small residual cost.

## 5. First-large-label theorem for every root before or at its pairing endpoint

Fix 0<theta<1, and let Y tend to infinity with Y<=B^theta. We may choose s growing very slowly. For each stopped tag truncate its accounting prefix further before its FIRST proposed p>=Y, if that event occurs at or before the pairing endpoint (including the pairing/stop proposal itself). Let Z(Y) be the number of distinct stopped tags having such a crossing. All executed prior high labels q are <Y and their masses <=s-1. By the same budget argument the total pre-crossing or endpoint r-cost is
    R_Y=O_L(X+s X Y/log^3 Y+s X log log(3Y)).
The number of crossing tags with preceding r-cost >=Y/16 is O(R_Y/Y). For every other crossing tag, pre-height h<=6+Y/4, p>=Y, nonnegative hypothetical post-height and wait<=2r(C) give p-1<=h+4r(C), hence r(C)>=p/8 for Y sufficiently large. This implication applies to a proposed endpoint, without charging its unexecuted cost.

Count these remaining rough first crossings by their immediately previous high keys, rather than bounding the whole arrival pool by its maximum. If there is no preceding high event, only O_L(1) original roots can feed any fixed (p,C). For a fixed p, each side p|C+sigma and r(C)>=p/8 gives O(X/(p log^2 p)) horizons by the same dimension-two sieve as the earlier S_p estimate. If a preceding high event has label q<Y, fixed LOW gain g and two divisor signs give CRT conditions on C with distinct p,q, and D=C-g-q+1. Writing C+sigma=p v, the q condition restricts v to a progression mod q. Its parameter interval length O(X/(pq)) is >=c B^(1-theta), and >=c p^(1-theta). The roughness r(C)>=p/8 implies both v and p v-2sigma avoid odd primes <=p/16 except p. Off the primes 2,p,q their two forms in the progression parameter have distinct roots and invertible slopes. Use the standard dimension-two upper-bound sieve at a sufficiently small fixed power of p depending on theta (e.g. take sieve level p^((1-theta)/4) and a suitably smaller cutoff power). Residue errors O(p^((1-theta)/4) log p) are negligible versus X/(pq log^2 p), and omitting 2,p,q costs a bounded factor. Thus uniformly the number of such horizons is O_theta(X/(p q log^2 p)). Each tuple of high keys supports fewer than s surviving tags, independently of the actual next clock and the total group at C. Summing the finitely many g/sign choices, q<Y using sum_{q<Y prime}1/q=O(log log(3Y)), and p>=Y using sum_{p>=Y prime}1/(p log^2 p)=O(1/log^2 Y) by partial summation and the elementary prime count bound, gives
    Z(Y) <= O_(L,theta)(R_Y/Y
                       + X[1+s log log(3Y)]/log^2 Y)
         = O_(L,theta)(X s log log(3Y)/log^2 Y)
for s>=2 and Y sufficiently large. This is an all-depth SOURCE bound, not a count of candidate keys. It includes all stopped tags whether matched or left unmatched, and the crossing may itself be the key at which they are paired.

Therefore if s log log Y=o(log^2 Y), only o(X) tags ever propose a label >=Y up to their greedy endpoint. E.g. s=ceil((log log B)^(1/8)), Y=sqrt(log B), satisfies this, and also s=o(T_min) for uniform or fixed positive-power hybrid caps. For every remaining pair both paths up to its pairing key contain ONLY labels <Y (even the proposed pairing label). By the existing LOW-only barrier with cutoff Y, gain since its root is <L_Y=2 product_{3<=p<=Y} p. With Chebyshev's sum log p=O(Y) this is exp(O(Y))=B^o(1) for Y=sqrt(log B). Since the paths share the pairing horizon, their original root distance is <L_Y/2. At most o(X) globally disjoint pairs contain a removed tag. In particular, on a hypothetical no-twin window one can pair almost all stopped tags before or at their actual stops using prefixes with only subpower (indeed polylog-size) selected labels, and pair distances B^o(1). This does NOT contradict the no-twin assumption: these distances can be much smaller than the original stop size B. It identifies a concrete structural difference between the new and old matching. A proof still needs to control the subsequent collection of many such near-source groups; matched tags can later split in the projected graph, so one cannot treat each pair as a single permanently transported mass. No such contraction is asserted.

## 7. What remains, and a change in research emphasis

The mass-normalization and bounded-lag assumptions have been removed from the specified local class by choosing a different witness matching. The new first-crossing estimate is a separate all-depth result, restricting the entire matching-prefix paths without any height-index or lag restriction. However the pairing scale can be too small; most early pairs may be near, and proving few near pairs is not the same as excluding concentration at a much later large stop. The residual global problem is to relate the formation and transport of these small-scale groups to the large stopped populations while retaining genuine distinct original identities, especially across projected splits. A multiscale construction with packet weights or a physical (non-splitting) forest may be useful, but no bound closing this step has been obtained. The fixed final-stop far-pair method is still a distinct option. Do not claim that either the small-cost matching or the near-pair conclusion proves a full matching deficit. No new branch, no main edits, no Lean compilation.
