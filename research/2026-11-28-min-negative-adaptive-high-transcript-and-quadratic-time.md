## 1. Input, exact pins and adaptive reconstruction

Let 0<a<=c<1/2 be fixed; use a positive-width prime anchor range R^a<=r<=R^c, and let u be odd, with ru comparable to R. Use one fixed sign family:
  HIGH plus:  x0=r(u-1),   h0=r,    source forms r,u,ru-2;
  HIGH minus: x0=r(u-1)+2, h0=r+3,  source forms r,u,ru+2.
Write c0=0 or 2 and b0=0 or 3 respectively, and shift=-2 or +2. Assume r prime, u and ru+shift are R^a-rough, and (x0,n0=h0-x0/2) is the actual first HIGH arrival of canonical A=x0+r-1 in [R,2R]. Assume distinct roots and a source family of size >=c_* R/L², with c_*>0 fixed. The HIGH plus family of c20b70c uses a=.2855,c=.2856; the HIGH minus alternative of e66355d uses a=.300,c=.3128. The theorem below only uses these source conditions, not the deep theorem supplying them.

At relative tick w, let g be cumulative gain BEFORE the tick. The sign s=+1 for even absolute n=n0+w, -1 for odd, and b_s=0 or3. Eligibility of odd prime q necessarily gives both pins
    q | 2(r+b0)+2w-g+1-2b_s,
    q | r(u-1)+c0-g+s.                                      (1)
Together with sign parity they are also sufficient for eligibility: substitute n0+w into the original clock congruence, using 2 invertible mod odd q. In particular for q!=r and known g,w,s these determine AT MOST ONE pair (r mod q,u mod q) with r nonzero mod q. This uses the coefficient 2 in the first pin and the nonzero r in the second. For q=r and 0<=g<r-3, even g cannot equal c0+s modulo r: the candidates are -1,1 for plus anchor, 1,3 for minus. Thus q=r cannot be eligible in this small-gain region.

Set z=floor(kappa L), Z=floor(L²), B=4 product_{3<=q<=z prime}q. Choose kappa>0 sufficiently small that B<=R^b, with b>0 fixed and small. Start the restricted deterministic graph with all labels q<=Z, retaining the original winner and clock rule; it is a trial, not an altered actual recurrence. The initial class pair (r,u) modulo B determines all residues for q<=z and the absolute clock parity. Record the first occurrence of each winning q in (z,Z], by its relative tick and label. At any time simulate only the known labels (initial q<=z and the already recorded fresh labels). When the actual RESTRICTED winner is a known label or idle, the known-alphabet trial agrees. At a fresh occurrence, the prescribed q must win over the known candidates, and we insert its movement and add it to the alphabet. By (1) there is at most one extension of the previous class pair modulo q. Since B contains 4, reduction modulo 4q suffices for all signed clock residues. Induction gives, for each initial class pair and ordered fresh transcript, all intermediate displacements, signs and winners and at most one pair of classes modulo B Q, Q=product of distinct fresh labels. Repeated labels impose NO additional modulus cost. The transcript describes a full T-tick path, or a prefix for a stopping argument.

For exactness in the restricted graph, at each tick with prescribed winner p (including p=0 idle), any other as-yet-unpinned q<p, or any q when p=0, must NOT be eligible. The forbidden pair for q is at most one among nonzero r residues, by (1). If q later becomes pinned, check its earlier denials against that pin; discard if inconsistent. For any q never pinned in the entire transcript, collect the union of these denied pairs. Add all source-zero pairs r=0, u=0 or ru+shift=0 modulo q. The source admissible residue pairs for an odd prime q number (q-1)(q-2); its forbidden density is 3/q-2/q². Thus for unpinned medium q in (z,Z] the forbidden set has size nu(q)<=3q+T. The allowed pair count is exactly q²-nu(q). Pinned q must also satisfy the source conditions. Check all known winners, and base source conditions, exactly; discard impossible transcripts. This yields a Cartesian product certificate. In particular given a base class, admissible full transcripts are disjoint and partition the source-admissible residue pairs in the finite CRT product (no assumption of statistical independence of the actual word).

For a leaf let beta=prod_{z<q<=Z, q unpinned}(1-nu(q)/q²). Its exact weight under the unconditioned independent uniform pair law on these primes is beta/Q². Sum over admissible leaves with Q<=R^delta is at most the source-admissible mass. Averaging additionally over ALL B² base class pairs (with r,u odd and source constraints there) gives
    (1/B²) sum_base sum_leaves beta/Q² <= V_3(Z),
    V_3(Z)=(1/4) prod_{3<=q<=Z}(1-3/q+2/q²) ~ const/(log Z)^3. (2)
The inequality becomes equality without product truncation. We will use the inequality, not any independent-residue approximation to the prime source. This is the extra information missing in the positive-only union bound.

## 2. Polynomial modulus budget through T~L²

Choose fixed positive b,delta,j0,beta0 sufficiently small that
    b+delta+2*j0+beta0+j0 < a,
    b+delta+2*c < 1.
For the concrete bands above b=delta=j0=.01,beta0=.05 work. (For a general a,c choose them smaller.) Set T=floor(tau L²), with tau a fixed sufficiently small positive constant chosen after these parameters and kappa. Cover the r range by O(L) dyadic boxes r~P, u in an interval of length V~R/P containing all actual u for that box. Bounds uniform in the boxes; integer pair counts below can overcover the exact ru slab, which only increases upper bounds.

First exclude any actual source whose RESTRICTED T-tick transcript first exceeds product Q>R^delta. The first overflowing prefix has R^delta<Q<=R^delta Z. Ignore all negative tests for an upper. For a fixed base and first-use prefix (times, labels), the unique class pair mod BQ has at most O(R/(BQ)^2) integer pairs in a box, because BQ<=R^(b+delta+o(1)) is polynomially smaller than both P,V. Sum over B² base classes, k times and ordered distinct primes; with s=1/log z the number of roots in all boxes is bounded by
    O(R L) R^(-s delta) [1+sum_{q>z, q prime} q^(-2+s)]^T.
The sum is O(z^(-1+s)/log z)=O(1/(z log z)), uniformly for 0<s<=1/2, by Chebyshev's prime upper and dyadic summation. Choose tau so small that the last exponent is <=delta L/(2 log z). The bound is R L exp(-Omega(L/log L))=o(R/L²). No union bound over repeated events. This step uses ordinary interval counts even without prime/rough restrictions and so is independent of the source lower proof.

For all nonoverflow full transcripts we will also use the positive-only weighted bound, for each base class,
    sum_leaves 1/Q² <= (1+sum_{q>z}1/q²)^T
                        <= exp(C T/(z log z)).                (3)
It follows by summing all ordered fresh label/time lists, dropping distinctness for the upper. This does NOT count them as disjoint; disjointness is only claimed for the exact certificate (2).

## 3. Transfer the exact transcript mass into interval pair counts

At the medium unpinned primes use the even-degree Bonferroni upper polynomial
    U=sum_{S subset, |S|<=n} (-1)^|S| I_S,
where I_S is the product of the forbidden-pair indicators at primes in S, and n=2 floor(j0 L/(2 log Z)). Pointwise U>=1{no forbidden pair}>=0. Under independent uniform pairs, its expectation differs from beta by at most
    E0=lambda^(n+1)/(n+1)!,
    lambda=sum nu(q)/q² <= C + C T/(z log z).
The constant C absorbs the prime harmonic sum in (z,Z], whose logarithmic endpoints have bounded ratio. Choose tau sufficiently small that lambda<=n/(100e) eventually and exp(C T/(z log z)) E0<=exp(-Omega(L/log L)), using the same constant in (3) after enlarging it if needed. This is possible since n is of order L/log L. Every divisor d_med in U is <=Z^n<=R^j0, and the absolute sum of independent expectations of its terms is <=exp(lambda). All these are exact elementary inclusion-exclusion bounds, not a claim that actual prime pairs are independent.

We need an upper sieve at free primes ell>Z disjoint from the pinned, base and medium moduli. Let D=floor(R^(j0/2)); use source forms r,u,ru+shift at all free ell<=D. Optionally add the two endpoint forms y-1,y+1 at free ell<Y, where y=r(u-1)+c0-g and Y>=Z is either polylogarithmic or <=D^eta for a sufficiently small fixed eta>0. An optional additional pinned prime q>Z is omitted from the free sieve. For the three forms the forbidden pair count is 3ell-2. For the five it is 5ell+O(1), uniformly in even g: r=0 already forbidden, and for r!=0 the four roots in u are 0, -shift/r, 1-(c0-g-1)/r, 1-(c0-g+1)/r. Each collision other than the distinct constant-difference pairs fixes at most one r residue; the two latter roots differ by 2/r, and the first two by nonzero shift/r. Thus total loss from duplicate roots O(1), even if g has many prime divisors. This two-variable count avoids the large singular-factor losses of treating r as fixed.

Write g_ell for this density, h(d)=prod_{ell|d}g_ell/(1-g_ell) and G(D)=sum_{d<=D}h(d) over squarefree free divisors. The standard elementary Selberg upper polynomial (sum_{d<=D}lambda_d I_d)^2 has lambda_1=1, independent expectation 1/G(D) and |lambda_d|<=prod_{ell|d}(1-g_ell)^(-1). For completeness the quadratic form diagonalizes as sum_m h(m)^(-1) (sum_{m|d}lambda_d g(d))², and choose the inner values mu(m)h(m)/G(D), then invert; the displayed coefficient bound follows by dropping the truncation. This is the same algebra as the earlier elementary upper sieve in 67d47169/first-use diversity note, now for uniform PAIR residues. At ell>Z, g_ell<=6/ell, hence |lambda_d|<=2 eventually for d<=D since sum_{ell|d}1/ell<=log D/(Z log Z). For G lower restrict the Euler product to ell<=D^eta, normalize it as independent inclusions of probability g_ell, and use E log d<=C eta log D, so at least half mass has d<=D for small eta. By Mertens and the uniform O(ell^-2) term in the densities,
    1/G(D) << (log Z/L)^3                          (source only),
    1/G(D) << (log Z/L)^3 (log Z/log Y)^2         (source + rough endpoint).  (4)
The constants are independent of Y in the indicated range. Omitting one free prime q>Z costs a bounded factor. The forms are positive for actual source pairs on the slab and roughness forces all the required nondivisibilities.

Multiply this nonnegative free upper polynomial by the nonnegative medium U. It upper-bounds the conjunction. To count its terms in one rectangle for a fixed pinned residue pair mod M=BQ, optionally also pin a unique residue pair at a prime q>Z, q<=R^beta0. CRT gives every remaining term's density exactly as a product of local pair densities, times 1/(M q)^2 (omit q factor if none). All resulting moduli are <=R^(b+delta+beta0+2*j0+o(1)), polynomially smaller than the shorter side P>=R^a up to fixed factors. For each individual residue pair, interval multiplication differs from its length-product main term by relative O(R^(-epsilon0)) for some fixed epsilon0>j0, by our strict parameter margin. The total absolute error normalized by the unconditioned rectangle count R/(M q)^2 is at most O(R^(-epsilon0) exp(lambda) D²): the medium sum costs exp(lambda), the two free divisor sums cost O(D²) since coefficients bounded, local densities <=1. Thus it is R^(-epsilon1+o(1)), epsilon1>0. This is uniform even for small nu(q) or missing local classes; the error is absolute relative to the baseline, not to the possibly tiny survivor mass. No prime progression asymptotic is needed here.

Consequently the count for that transcript is at most a fixed box-volume constant times
    R/(B² Q² q²) [(beta+E0)/G(D) + R^(-epsilon1+o(1))].        (5)
Without extra pin omit q². On summing over histories and base pairs, (2) handles the beta term and (3) handles BOTH errors. The errors are smaller than any fixed inverse power of L, even after O(L) boxes and polynomial-log tick sums. This exact negative-transcript mass is what removes the previous exp(O(T/(z log z))) factor from the main term.

## 4. Actual common time: exclude every outside prime and control gain

For nonoverflow restricted histories g<=T Z=O(L^4), much smaller than r. At a first difference between actual and restricted trajectories, an eligible label q>Z must occur at the restricted state (indeed restricted must be idle). Count all such outside eligibilities along the complete restricted transcript, which overcounts first differences.

For Z<q<=R^beta0, (1) gives the unique extra pair pin; if r=q it is impossible by section 1, and if the calculated r residue is zero there is no actual prime r!=q. Apply (5) with source-only (4). Summing the beta term using (2) cancels all log Z factors. Summing q and w gives
    # exceptional moderate outside roots
       << R/L² * T sum_{q>Z}1/q² + o(R/L²)
       << R/L² * T/(Z log Z) + o(R/L²)
       = o(R/L²).                                           (6)
The O(L) dyadic boxes are included. Contrast with the older single-class fixed-alphabet argument whose loss was proportional to T/(z log z).

For q>R^beta0, fix r in the known residue class mod BQ and a tick of the transcript. The first integer in (1) is positive O(r), hence has O(1) distinct prime divisors above R^beta0, and q!=r. For each, u must occupy one residue modulo q in addition to its class mod BQ. The u interval remains polynomially longer than BQ*q because q=O(r) and b+delta+2c<1. Ignore primality/roughness and all denied tests; count r by O(P/(BQ)) and u by O(V/(BQ q)). Summing q costs O(R^-beta0). By (3) and O(L) boxes, total <= O(R L T R^-beta0 exp(C T/(z log z)))=o(R/L²). No upper sieve applied at an excessively large modulus.

Discard the overflow and outside exceptional sets. The remaining >=(c_*-o(1)) R/L² actual HIGH anchors follow their restricted trajectories through the entire common T=floor(tau L²). Every winner <=Z, and number of distinct labels <=pi(z)+log Q/log z=O(L/log L). For each fixed label p, its two possible clock residues modulo 2p allow at most T/p+2 firings. Its total gain is <=T+2p. Thus the full suffix gain obeys
    G_suffix = O((T+Z)L/log L)=O(L³/log L),                  (7)
sharper than the initial T Z bound. Every height is >=r+b0-G_suffix/2=(1-o(1))r; upper height <=r+b0+T. This is genuine elapsed time order L², not just a stopped time-or-depth alternative. We did not infer a new high record: the earlier first prefire height ~1.5r is still larger than r+O(L²).

Two such connectors of the same sign family meeting a physical state have anchor positions differing by <=D0=O(L³/log L) and clocks by <=T. Since h0=r+b0, their labels differ by <=T+D0/2. For equal labels the two anchor positions are equal modulo r, so D0<r forces identical anchor and root. For distinct r,r', fixed position offset d gives one CRT class x0=c0 mod r, x0+d=c0 mod r', with O(R/r²) positions because r'~r and c<1/2. Summing even over all integer labels r>=R^a, offsets and nearby r' gives O(R^(1-a)(D0+1)(T+D0+1))=o(R/L²). Delete both endpoints of every near pair. The retained connectors have pairwise disjoint PHYSICAL states at all relative times 0..T, not just at corresponding times. In particular the cohort supplies Omega(R) distinct high physical states in these connectors. This is not common absolute-clock occupation, nor the normalized square-root-horizon survival lower.

## 5. Apply exact mass again: rough absorption, and exclusion through half time

For each tick t=0..T of a nonoverflow restricted transcript, g is determined. Let Y=L^K with fixed K>2. Count those roots at a center with qmin(y)>=Y using (5) WITHOUT additional pin and the second free upper in (4). For each t the count is
    << R/L² * (log Z/log Y)^2 + o(R/L²).
This includes all required source roughness, via the exact source-admissible mass (2) and the free three/five-form mixed sieve. Constants uniform in fixed K (once R large enough that Y<=D^eta). Summing over t gives an upper on the total number of such root-time incidences. On the actual matched trajectories, reaching a center with qmin>=Y>Z forces every remaining restricted tick to idle, since neither neighbor has any label <=Z. Thus if this roughness is visited at any time t<=floor(T/2), that trajectory contributes at least T/2 rough incidences. Divide the incidence upper by T/2. The total number of actual matched roots to discard is
    << R/L² * (2/K)^2 + o(R/L²).
Choose a sufficiently large FIXED K depending on the source lower c_* and sieve constant. Retain a positive density-scale population with qmin(y)<L^K at EVERY visited site through T/2. In particular every visited site is non-twin, since both primes at a twin site would be of order R. This absorption argument is why no factor T is lost in removing roots which touch a rough center. Merely union-bounding the individual ticks would not work. It supplies non-twin actual high connectors without assuming the finiteness of twins. No assertion about qmin below the much smaller cutoff z or Z.

## 6. Growing actual event depth WITHIN the common time

The preceding proof counts physical states, but could still retain many nearly stationary high connectors. Use a separate word sieve to show they complete a growing number of actual moving events within T'=floor(T/2).

Let l=log L, and k=floor((1/2) log l/log log l), tending to infinity (for large enough R). Suppose a matched trajectory has j<k moving events through T'. Its j+1 quiet segments have total length T'-j, so some segment has length >=(T'-j)/(j+1). At that segment's center the next possible first event is within 2 qmin by the exact calendar. Thus one visited prefix endpoint has qmin>=W=floor(T'/(4(k+1))). In particular W~L²/k and log W~2 log L. This also handles the final incomplete idle segment; no assertion about the full elapsed wait of the preceding event.

For each signed spatial word of at most k labels <=Z, let g be its total gain, and g_i its prefix gains. Fixing each label q at its source imposes
    q | r(u-1)+c0-g_i+s_i.
For each distinct q, for each nonzero r residue it determines one u residue; repeats must be consistent, or discard. The actual prime r>Z ensures it is nonzero at every such q. Thus with Q=product of DISTINCT word labels there are at most Q allowed pair residues modulo Q, not Q², and Q<=Z^k=R^{o(1)}. Ignore timing/priority in this upper; every actual word obeys these pins. At endpoint y=r(u-1)+c0-g count source forms r,u,ru+shift rough to R^a and y±1 rough to W. Use the same pair-residue Selberg upper, now omit primes <=11 and all prime divisors of Q. On all remaining ell<=D require the source nondivisibilities; additionally endpoint nondivisibilities for ell<W. Free densities are again 3/ell-2/ell² or 5/ell+O(ell^-2), uniformly in g. The restricted Euler-product proof of G(D) yields
    1/G(D) << [L³ (log W)^2]^-1 prod_{q|Q,q>11}(1-g_q)^-1,
since W<D^eta for large R. Here g_q denotes the corresponding forbidden density before omitting q. Its factor is <=1+C/q for q>11 after enlarging absolute C. For the error, |lambda_d|<=prod_{ell|d,ell>11}(1-g_ell)^-1<=polylog R by Mertens (or use the crude |lambda_d|<=d and choose a smaller D). CRT remainder relative to the baseline R/Q² is polynomially small after summing D² terms with these weights, since Q=R^{o(1)} and D is a small fixed power. Sum over <=Q residue pairs, dyadic boxes, and the words. The upper for a fixed word is
    << R/[L² (log W)^2] * prod_{distinct q in word} w(q),
with w(q)=1/q for q<=11 and w(q)=(1+C/q)/q otherwise, plus a polynomially small error per residue pair. The total raw word and residue count for length<=k is R^{o(1)}, so all errors are o(R/L²). No loss from determinants depending on fixed r, because r was sieved as a variable too.

Let J_Z=sum_{odd primes q<=Z} w(q)=O(log log Z)=O(log l). In sequential word enumeration a new label adds its weight (sum<=J_Z); repeating an old label adds no factor and has at most k choices. Each sign adds factor2. Therefore the sum of distinct-label weights over words of length <=k is <=(k+1)[2(J_Z+k+1)]^k=l^(1/2+o(1)). Since (log W)^2~4 l², the number of roots with any such rough prefix is o(R/L²). This proves that after deletion of another negligible set, each retained true connector executes >=k additional actual moving events through T'. This result includes the time bound, unlike the prior growing-depth theorem whose final ages need not share a polylogarithmic bound. Its k grows more slowly than the older stopped-depth result; the two statements are complementary. Total gain is at least 2k and at most (7); no lower bound on the number of distinct labels or positive net height increment is inferred from k alone.

## 7. Combined theorem and next obstruction

For either HIGH sign source in section 1 and the given positive density-scale lower, there exists tau>0 and fixed K such that for all sufficiently large R at least c' R/L² actual canonical trajectories, c'>0, have simultaneously:
  - actual HIGH first anchor h0=r+b0, r>=R^a;
  - common subsequent time floor(tau L²/2), all heights (1-o(1))r and gain O(L³/log L);
  - every visited physical state pairwise disjoint between different retained trajectories (including nonmatching relative ticks);
  - all visited postarrival centers qmin<L^K (hence non-twin);
  - at least floor((1/2) log log L/log log log L) actual additional events within that same time.
In particular distinct actual high physical states number Omega(R), not merely Omega(R log L/L) as the older common elapsed L log L result. Applying to the earlier HIGH plus source gives the above at exponent .2855 (inherits its prior Wu lower). Applying to e66355d and its limiting parameter version yields either the previous stationary roughness breakthrough above 5/16, or the stronger dynamic HIGH family for every fixed a<5/16 sufficiently close to it, subject to the SAME old Pascadi specialization review item. The static branch is not shown to occur infinitely often.

This is a time/occupation improvement, not M85 or polynomial transport. The method controls product of fresh pinned labels and denied-test sieve support on the same log R budget. In the present proof expected positive-pin log product and medium total forbidden density scale with T/(z log z); z<=const L is required to materialize the base primorial; T~L² is where the exponential modulus budget is consumed. Extending this particular global-transcript argument unchanged to T=R^epsilon does not meet its own estimates. It is not an impossibility theorem for other proof methods. Next investigate whether the transcript can be cut into spatial/height sections while passing only necessary conditional residue information rather than preserving all prior pins, or use the exact local occupation transport and repeated-label structure to derive a different polynomial-scale bound. M85 still needs a mechanism for sustained new height records and density-scale transport, not just longer polylogarithmic high persistence.
