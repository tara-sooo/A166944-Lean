# Upward deficit cutsets and monotone thin-crossing peeling before short resets

2026-11-25 JST. Continues f3ebd2e8066c6674b360c1bd8a9f492825b098d7 (reset ancestry), 01a2afcdf80c128207aab767c50619ece5025d6f (deficit divisors), and bc057caad390631a6a94b6bcab5ca3bb690c0fb6 (primorial clock transfer). New: count upward deficit crossings uniformly at each integer level, assign a full band of actual last-upcrossing witnesses to each short high reset, and remove o(X) original roots by monotone peeling of thin crossing groups. Unlike a frozen mass classification this gives actual surviving thick groups after deletion. Also gives actual upstream full-prefix second-moment transfer. These statements apply to short-old as well as short-recent resets. Not a proof of small reset energy, general heavy mass bound, or twin infinitude.

## 1. Setup and positive drift

All-odd MIN short-clock prefix: X dyadic,N=X/2,B=ceil(sqrt(7X)),H=4B,a=2B,G=2H+B+6, initial C0=2M-2,t0=M+2, K=floor((7M-1)/4). Assume H+2B<=min(K-t0). Include original first crossing post-state at/beyond relative H or twin terminal. All horizons in [2X,7X), gain<=G, elapsed<=3G, deficit delta=2t-C in [0,12B+6]. At selected q on pre-horizon D, actual wait w<=2r(D)<=2q, and delta changes by 2w-(q-1). Define positive drift if d1>d0, where d0,d1 are pre/post deficits. Then
    w>(q-1)/2, r(D)>(q-1)/4.
For q>=3 the FULL, UNCLIPPED wait weight w/(2r(D))>(q-1)/(4q)>=1/6.
The original prefix has full clock cost sum w/2=O(B) per root. This differs from the earlier relative slab [2B,4B), which is used only for the original energy certificate and deletion cost. Full-prefix energy below uses actual entire waits, including the final crossing wait, not extrapolated or restarted dynamics.

Write u=log B, ell=1+log u, P=ceil(B/u^1/8), Y=max(7,ceil(B^1/8)), V=max(1,floor(B^1/16)), h=max(1,floor(u^1.5)). A high short reset is an event selecting p>=P, wait<=V, post-state with deficit=6s, s=t mod2. Its predecessor deficit is p-1+6s-2w>=p-1-2V. Root is a reset (delta=6). Other reset vertices are any actual state with delta=6(t mod2), regardless of incoming label. All asymptotic assertions are for sufficiently large B.

## 2. Uniform upward-crossing arithmetic envelope

At an integer level L, an event crosses upward if d0<L<=d1. For q>=Y label its post anchor (J,t,q), s=t mod2, J=D+q-1. Arithmetic identities:
    q|J+2s, d1=6s+2qk, J=2k mod4.
Crossing implies L<=d1<L+4q, so for fixed q,s,L only O(1) k possible, even though unrestricted clock deficit can vary by O(B). Also r(D)>(q-1)/4. Fix q,s; write J+2s=qm, m even, and D+-1=q(m-1), q(m-1)+eta, eta=2-4s. Two linear forms in m/2, namely m-1 and q(m-1)+eta, must avoid all odd primes <=Z=floor(B^1/64), eventually Z<(q-1)/4 and q>Z. Two distinct excluded residues modulo each odd prime <=Z, since q invertible and eta=+-2. The uniform elementary dimension-two Selberg estimate from e15eb81dd0e0e1fe2b98086421b9c5023fd2f313 gives O(X/(q u²)+Z^6)=O(X/(q u²)) possible J. The parameter range length upper bound O(X/q)>=cB absorbs error. For each k,s,J the post-time t is fixed. Thus per level the number of candidate labeled post keys is
    A_Y(L)=O((X/u²) sum_{Y<=q<=B}1/q)=O(X/u²),
    A_P(L)=O((X/u²) sum_{P<=q<=B}1/q)=O(X ell/u³).
The first reciprocal bound follows from dyadic Chebyshev estimates, Y>=B^1/8; the second from the near-B interval length in logarithmic scale. These count post-state keys, NOT edges with distinct predecessor times. Many predecessor times can feed the same key; the weight group defined below includes all such tags. Summing over L in [1,12B+6], or the smaller necessary band, gives total key-level supports
    S_Y=O(XB/u²), S_P=O(XB ell/u³).                 (1)
This is a deterministic arithmetic envelope, uniform in L, not independence between crossings. Can be applied to any subset of original roots.

## 3. Actual last-upcrossing witnesses

For each high short reset selecting p and original root tag, take every integer L in [ceil(p/4),floor(p/2)]. Eventually every such L>6 and <=predecessor deficit; number of levels >=p/5. Between the previous reset and the predecessor of the current reset, assign the LAST actual upward crossing of L on this tag's original path. It exists by starting deficit<=6 and ending deficit>=p/2. The excursions between previous reset and predecessor for different high short resets on the same root are disjoint. For any fixed root and (J,t,q,L) at most one assignment, since no horizon is revisited. Several levels can use the same event. Assignment uses actual history, not retained-path dynamics, and remains unchanged when other roots are removed.

Classify assigned crossings with q<Y as small, q>=Y as large. For each large key-level let n_{alpha,L} be number of assigned roots, counting only the particular last-upcrossing assignments. The same root can be in multiple groups but once per group. Group membership is a subset of roots visiting the actual post-state (and selecting q there). It may depend on later reset, but is precisely defined from full original paths. Let C_small,C_large be counts of assigned tag-levels. Let R_<Y^full=sum actual wait/2 over q<Y in the entire original prefix of the family. Positive drift length <=2w and disjoint excursions imply
    C_small<=4 R_<Y^full,
    sum_{short entries} p <=5(C_small+C_large).     (2)
This holds for any reference family, not just all roots. No factor for the number of resets or levels beyond actual drift length. A short-old reset from f3ebd2e is included without any additional assumption about a recent high ancestor.

## 4. NEW monotone pruning with retained, not frozen, thick crossing masses

For a current retained family, call a large key-level thin if its CURRENT assigned count <=h. Starting with any reference family, while a root has a short reset entry with at least one third of that entry's levels assigned to currently thin large groups, delete the entire root (with all its assignments). Use one triggering entry per deletion, and charge only its thin witnesses. For each group, the first time it is charged its active mass<=h, and thereafter its mass cannot increase. Each charged root belongs to that group at most once and is deleted immediately. Thus the group's total lifetime number of charges<=h, even if it became thin during pruning; this is not the bound from merely frozen initial counts. Each deletion charges at least p/15>=P/15 distinct group-levels. Therefore
    #deleted <=15 h S_Y/P = O(X (B/P) h/u²)
              = O(X/u^3/8)=o(X).                    (3)
Root deletion costs at most B of the original clipped slab clock per root, so lost slab budget=o(XB). No upper bound on actual previous energy is assumed. The procedure terminates (finitely many original roots) and can be used after other root deletions. In final retained family, every surviving short entry has <1/3 of its levels in thin large groups. Hence either >=1/3 are small-label crossings, or >1/3 belong to actual retained groups with >h assigned original roots. All these companions actually reached the same earlier post-state, not merely a common projected horizon, and have the same labeled key and level. They need not share the same later reset label or entry history. Because deterministic, they share the subsequent physical suffix while their prefixes continue. This guarantees many levels with thick group mass, not many DISTINCT physical states (one event may account for many levels).

Can instead use band-specific h_Y=u^1.5 for Y<=q<P and h_P=u^11/4/ell for q>=P; lifetime charges <=h_Y S_Y+h_P S_P, giving O(X/u^3/8+X/u^1/8) root deletion. Thus the surviving near-B crossings can require almost cubic-log mass. This variant is paper-only; audit uses single h.

Small-dominant root deletion is conditional: if R_<Y^full=o(XP), deleting all roots having any entry with >=1/3 small assigned levels costs <=60 R_<Y^full/P=o(X) roots by (2), then repeat the above thin peeling. In final family every short entry has >1/3 levels in actual retained thick large groups. The condition cannot be inferred from an energy bound restricted to the previous late slab. One sufficient strengthening is the FULL natural medium energy E_m^full=O(XB^13/8 u³) for z<p<=ceil(B^1/4), z largest prime with L_z=primorial<=B^1/16. Indeed apply full-prefix LOW-block transfer with intermediate cutoff Y (eventually z<Y). Every LOW block has cost <=1.5L_z; discard trailing blocks and those followed by p>=Y at cost O(X L_z+XB L_z/Y). Every remaining LOW block maps injectively per root to a full actual next event z<p<Y with cost>=1/2, no right-edge trimming needed. By S(Y)=O(XY/log³Y), the inherited support bound,
    R_<Y^full <= (1+3L_z) sqrt(S(Y) E_m^full)
                    +O(X L_z+XB L_z/Y)
                = O(XB^15/16)=o(XP).
This is an explicit sufficient FULL-prefix strengthening of the previous MED condition, not an established estimate on actual full-prefix MED energy. Existing natural late-slab MED and retained HIGH targets are not replaced unconditionally.

## 5. NEW upstream actual second-moment transfer

Let E_+^full be the projected energy on all positive-drift q>=Y events using full natural wait weights, and E_+,P^full for q>=P only. These include no negative-drift or reset wait unless positive drift, and no fractional late-slab truncation. At fixed pre-horizon D, n_D=#actual positive-drift tags in the chosen label band. Full weights>=1/6 imply n_D<=6U_+(D). Distinct post keys alpha of those events partition the tags at D, even if predecessor times differ. At a single alpha each assigned tag contributes at at most 4r(D) levels (its actual positive drift <=2w<=4r(D)); hence sum_L n_{alpha,L}²<=4r(D)n_alpha² by n_{alpha,L}<=n_alpha and summing first moments. Summing over alpha, D and using sum n_alpha²<=n_D² gives
    sum_{alpha,L} n_{alpha,L}² <=144 E_+^full.       (4)
Same bound with E_+,P^full on near-B groups. Valid on any retained reference family, without assuming all crossings assigned or their masses identical. By Cauchy and (1),
    C_large <=12 sqrt(S_Y E_+^full),
    C_near  <=12 sqrt(S_P E_+,P^full).
Combining with (2):
    sum_short p <=20 R_<Y^full + O(sqrt(XB E_+^full/u²)).
Or, using the near-B split with actual full smaller-label wait cost,
    sum_short p <=20 R_<P^full + O(sqrt(XB ell E_+,P^full/u³)).
These are actual linear bounds on short reset gain from EARLIER clock/energy, not pointwise caps on future pair product. For example a positive fraction of roots having such reset implies sum p>=c XP; if small cost=o(XP), E_+^full>>XP²u²/B (order XB u^7/4 at P=B/u^1/8). In the near-B version if all smaller-label cost=o(XP), E_+,P^full>>XP²u³/(B ell) (order XB u^11/4/ell). These are lower bounds forced by abundance, not upper bounds already available. They fall short of contradicting the cubic-log energy goal. Also if after peeling all surviving short entries have >=p/15 thick assigned levels, then sum_short p<=15 sum_thick n<=15/h sum n²<=2160 E_+^full/h. Does not bound final reset pair energy or prove short-reset mass negligible under the existing late-slab hypothesis.

## 7. Remaining problem

This is a different kind of reduction from the previous rare-anchor product caps: unconditional o(X) root pruning even with recomputation of assigned masses, plus actual large ancestor groups in the nonreset excursion. It applies to long-distance short-old cases. Need control of the thick crossings themselves or a transfer of their upstream full-prefix energy to the late-slab residual. Smaller-label-dominated excursions remain without the additional full-prefix MED bound. Large future reset cross products, other physical mergers, asynchronous overlaps, full sector estimates and Lean compilation remain open. Main unchanged.
