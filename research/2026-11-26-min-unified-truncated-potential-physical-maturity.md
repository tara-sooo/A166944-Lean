## 2. Exact truncated potential over original true graph

Every actual true vertex V=(t,C) has full original population n_V, incoming group sizes a_{V,i}, and e_V members terminating there. Incoming groups are actual distinct predecessor vertices plus singleton root injection. All continuing members have the same true successor; on its outgoing edge flow is n_V-e_V. Each such edge contributes exactly once as an incoming group at another vertex. The graph is finite, directed in strictly increasing tick time. At most d=O(u/ell) incoming groups: jump labels at V divide C or C+2 by old true exit congruences, distinct odd prime factors number O(u/ell) (factorial product bound), plus wait and injection. Take a sufficiently large integer d as a uniform bound. This includes root/terminal meetings. No monotone population assumption.

For real/integer s>=1 define convex f_s(n)=n² for n<=s and 2sn-s² for n>=s. It has slope <=2s and is superadditive on nonnegative inputs (integrate the nondecreasing derivative). Define D_V(s)=f_s(n_V)-sum_i f_s(a_{V,i})>=0. Exact finite cancellation of all continuing edges and root injections yields
    sum_V D_V(s)
      = sum_V [f_s(n_V)-f_s(n_V-e_V)] - N
      <= (2s-1) N <=2sN.                              (1)
All root injections contribute f_s(1)=1; all original tags terminate exactly once. This is the independent branch potential on the complete actual graph rather than its high tail layers. Pure wait/retirement vertices with no new incoming group have D=0; one may compute at actual recorded arrivals alone when every terminal is a recorded arrival.

Let h_V count incoming groups with a_i>=m and s_V=sum_(a_i<m) a_i. For h_V>=1, exact expansion gives
    D_V(m)=(h_V-1)m²+sum_(a_i<m)(2ma_i-a_i²)
           >= (h_V-1)m² + m s_V.
Therefore
    m sum_(h_V>=1)(h_V-1)+sum_(h_V>=1)s_V <=2N.      (2)
In particular the number of vertices with two or more heavy incoming branches is <=2N/m, and all SMALL incoming memberships at vertices with at least one heavy incoming branch sum to <=2N. The latter is a bound on membership incidences, allowing the same tag to enter such a branch at multiple different vertices; no per-tag uniqueness claimed. Vertices with all groups small but n_V>=2m satisfy D_V(m)>=m n_V/2, so their total original n_V<=4N and their number<=2N/m. These are bounds on actual physical birth support without factors for candidate labels or times.

## 3. Global all-small first-pair payment and heavy-vertex cutoff

Partition exact already surviving backward first-meeting charges by FULL original incoming branch masses at their actual first true meeting V: both small (<m), both heavy (>=m), or one of each (attachment). Old induced-cohort masks and old moving/nonmoving birth types remain applied; this is a separate refinement, not a recomputation of first meetings.

For the both-small sector at V, the total ORDERED pair count is s_V²-sum_small a_i², with s_V defined even if h_V=0. Since s_V<=d m, it equals f_(dm)(s_V)-sum_small f_(dm)(a_i). By superadditivity, D_V(dm) is at least this expression even if arbitrary heavy groups are also present. Thus the total ordered first-meeting pair count of this type across the entire graph is <=2dmN by (1). Each fixed ordered original pair has total backward clock over its entire future <=c_a(H+P_max)/b_min, using original strict atoms and full denominators. Pay ALL such surviving charges:
    B_ss <=2 c_a (H+P_max) d m N / b_min = o(NT).     (3)
Unlike old per-source both-small cuts, this uses full actual incoming branch sizes and a global retirement budget, without adding a depth factor to the allowed size. Some charges may already be removed by old masks; only remaining fractions are paid.

For both-heavy pairs aggregate exact remaining clock by true V, summing all such incoming-branch pairs at that vertex (no extra d² factor). There are <=2N/m candidate vertices by (2). Pay each exact aggregate if <=eta H m, total <=2 eta NH=o(NT). This is a much larger absolute threshold than the old single-label or two-label thin cutoffs in some sectors; the old individual pair-key payments are still kept first. It is an aggregate cutoff, not an assertion that every individual branch pair at a surviving V has this much clock. If desired apply per-heavy-pair cutoff eta H m/d since sum binom(h_V,2)<=d sum(h_V-1)/2<=d N/m, total O(eta NT). Either valid mask may be added sequentially. A sufficient local capacity for an aggregate thin vertex is the sum of its old harmonic envelopes over heavy branches; that sum need not be globally small. This result does NOT bound the total exact heavy-heavy charge by o(NT), only the number of potential vertices and their thin part.

## 4. Attachment incidence budget and aggregate induced-heavy-share mask

For a vertex V with at least one heavy incoming branch, define full small-member set S_V as the union of incoming groups of size <m, so |S_V|=s_V. By (2) sum_V |S_V|<=2N. Keep all original source cohorts and weights. At a later backward source slot G (size b>=b_min) and atom K, let H_G(V) be the number of G members that entered V on ANY of its original heavy incoming branches. This is an induced cohort count, not the full original heavy population. Every attachment first-meeting pair at V and source G has one tag in S_V and one in those H_G(V) members. If H_G(V)<=eta b, the combined two-orientation clock of all such pairs involving a fixed small tag i at this atom is <=2 c_a P eta. For a fixed membership (V,i) and each grid the continuing trajectory can cross at most H/P+1 atoms after V. Summing over its eligible atoms, and then over all small memberships (not all potential heavy partner labels), yields
    B_attach,small_share <=2 c_a eta(H+P_max) sum_V |S_V|
                          <=4 c_a eta N(H+P_max)=o(NT). (4)
Crucially the test is on the SUM of induced heavy branches at V. Testing each heavy branch separately against eta b would introduce an unjustified multiplicity factor; use the aggregate or per-branch threshold eta b/d. The exact surviving attachment pairs now have H_G(V)>eta b at their later source. Old larger-branch, first-meeting-parent, root-separation and weighted-moving predicates remain. The test says the full heavy-side induced union is large, not necessarily each individual branch. May combine with the old chosen single-branch lower bound.

There is a direct global remaining-attachment upper envelope that respects actual lifetimes: for each (V,i) with i in S_V, at grid K after V, let b_G be original source slot population if the tag actually crosses K and the source is positive; set ratio H_G(V)/b_G or 0 if unavailable. Keep only eligible later K, old terminal strip and old source masks; relax any pair-specific conditions for an upper bound. Total attachment clock <=2 c_a mean_P P sum_(V,i) sum_K H_G(V)/b_G. By the global incidence budget the relaxed envelope is <=4 c_a N(H+P_max), even if the same small tag has multiple actual attachment memberships. A sharper threshold can be made from lifetime lengths: pay every attachment membership with total available remaining gain <eta H (using actual terminal or final-tail eligibility). Cost <=2 c_a(eta H+P_max) sum s_V=o(NT). No global contraction of the remaining long attachments proved.

## 5. First physical maturity combined with final weighted maturity

Let A_i^phys be the projected horizon of the FIRST actual true vertex of tag i having full physical population >=m, if such a vertex exists. It may be a waiting state, not necessarily a recorded jump arrival for that tag. It is an original-history predicate. At any surviving backward source with b>=b_min, the common physical predeparture vertex has at least b visitors, hence both source participants have such a first state and A_i^phys<=C_source<=K. Pay both-role backward atoms within h gain of this first physical maturity, namely K<A_i^phys+h for either participant. Per tag and grid at most h/P+1 such atoms, and row+column capacity <=2 c_a P. Total O(N(h+P_max))=o(NT). Earlier final-tail strips of 685c795 and Bad-tag payment remain.

For each original non-Bad terminal-nonmoving tag with a first physical maturity, put J_i its global final weighted expiry posthorizon, F_i actual terminal horizon, and
    M_i=max(J_i,A_i^phys),
    R_joint=sum_i (F_i-M_i-2h)_+.
Every remaining backward source atom satisfies M_i+h<=K<=F_i-h for BOTH participants. Counting one row per tag per eligible atom gives
    B_new <= c_a [R_joint+N P_max] <= c_a [R_final+N P_max]. (5)
Thus the scalar residence sufficient condition is weakened to liminf [R_joint+D_l,new+D_h,new]/(NT)<1/6, with same c_a->3. This comparison is with the previous scalar residence sufficient test, not the exact residual or exact weighted tree tests. No claim of a constant positive difference between R_final and R_joint.

There is a root-prime lower bound on the gain needed to form a genuinely new physical heavy cohort. Call a heavy vertex V a seed if EVERY original visitor has its first physical heavy state there. Such full cohorts are disjoint over seed vertices (one first state per original tag), so #seeds<=N/m. At any seed with n=n_V>=m, origins C0 are distinct even horizons corresponding to distinct odd prime roots, with C0<=C_V. The earlier uniform one-form prime-interval upper sieve gives #prime roots in any interval of length D>=B^.8 and <=O(X) at most O(D/log D), uniformly (use cutoff D^1/40, error D^.2). Hence, for a sufficiently small fixed c>0, among these n roots at most n/2 have age C_V-C0 < c n u; log(c n u)=Theta(u) since m=B^(1-o(1)), n<=N. At least half have spent >=c n u gain. In particular
    sum_(i first heavy at seed V) (A_i^phys-C0_i) >= c' u n_V².
Summing over disjoint seeds yields a lower bound c' u sum_seed n_V² on total first-physical-maturity gains. This can reduce (5) where such seed masses are large. It is not a lower bound of order NT: m u=o(H), and a tag can first become heavy by joining an already existing cohort. No assumption that every first-heavy state is a seed or that full physical mass is monotone. Under finitely many twins F_i=C0_i+T+O(B) for gain-stopped tags, so R_joint is equivalently a sum of truncated T minus the later of the two actual maturity gains, with total O(NB)=o(NT) overshoot error. Bounding the population-weighted maturity gains on nonseed attachments remains necessary to obtain a constant-margin result from this scalar route.
