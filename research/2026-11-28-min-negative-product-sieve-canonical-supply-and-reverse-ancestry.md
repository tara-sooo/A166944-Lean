## 1. Why use a bilinear product theorem

In e51533d and 7e90c0f the canonical composite-source families fixed p and applied BV to v. With X=R/p and sieve cutoff comparable to p, the lower-sieve condition X^.5>p^2 limits p to exponent <1/5. Rather than separately estimate prime v in each growing congruence class depending on p, use a distribution theorem for the product p*v at the fixed residue 2. Unlike the previous shifted-prime .285 bound, this forces the fired neighbor to be a product of two distinct primes and does not leave a twin alternative.

## 2. Canonical reset and nonreset supplies at density R/L^2

Put L=log R. For each dyadic band with lower endpoint P>=R^theta1 and 2P<=R^theta2 (there are >>L bands for sufficiently large R), restrict
    P<p<=1.2P,  1.1 R/P<v<=1.4 R/P.
Products lie strictly inside [R+1,2R+1] for sufficiently large R, and smaller/larger factors are uniquely determined. In Wu Lemma2.5 take N=P, M=R/P, X=R and the sequences described above. The fixed reduced residue for v modulo420 is one of:
    RESET: v=1 mod420.
    HIGH: v=7 mod12 and v=1 mod5,7.
These are separate families. The latter is a unique reduced class modulo420. All fixed AP prime counts use ordinary fixed-modulus PNT. In either box the unsifted number of prime pairs is B_P asymptotic to a positive constant times R/L^2, uniformly since log P and log M are fixed positive multiples of L. This is true for the full real intervals after integer endpoints are rounded. Choose sieve cutoff z=4R^theta2. The sequence is the multiset (in fact distinct products within and between boxes) {pv-2}. For odd squarefree d0 with all prime factors below z, use g(d0)=1/phi(d0). Then
    # {pv-2=0 mod d0} - B_P/phi(d0)
equals the error in Wu Lemma2.5 with residue +2 plus the correction
    -# {(pv,d0)>1}/phi(d0).
The second term is needed because the small prime p can itself be below z; do not assume every product is coprime to all sieve moduli. Its total absolute contribution against any 1-bounded weights supported on d0<=D=R^d is negligible: for a fixed product pv, v>D eventually, and
    sum_{d0<=D, p|d0, d0 squarefree} 1/phi(d0)
      <= (p-1)^(-1) sum_{e<=D/p} 1/phi(e)
      << log(2D)/p.
The elementary bound sum_{e<=X}1/phi(e)<<log(2X) follows from n/phi(n)=sum_{d|n}mu^2(d)/phi(d) and the convergent sum mu^2(d)/(d phi(d)). Thus the correction is O(B_P L/R^theta1), smaller than any log-power error.

The ordinary Iwaniec linear lower sieve with well-factorable remainders (Wu Lemma2.2, equations(2.3),(2.5),(2.6) p220, or Lichtman Theorem2.10 pp8, (5.15)–(5.17) p23) gives a lower with main B_P V(z)(f(log D/log z)+O(eta)), where the small fixed approximation parameter eta can be chosen arbitrarily small and V(z)~C/log z. The finitely many well-factorable order-one remainder weights at level D are the ordinary sieve weights, with the support restriction to squarefree products of sieve primes built into their construction; equivalently use the zero extension in Lichtman (5.20) p24. Do not truncate an arbitrary well-factorable sequence by an arbitrary mask. Level D is below X^(theta(nu)-epsilon_dist) uniformly by the margin above. A well-factorable weight of level D is also well-factorable at any larger level Q, by refining any factorization of Q to one of D with smaller factor bounds. Each weight is 1-bounded (or absorb a fixed bound into the constant), so the coprimality correction above and Wu's weighted error give arbitrarily small log-power errors. The sum is over odd moduli, (d0,2)=1. For large R, s=log D/log z tends to d/theta2>2 with fixed margin. Thus f(s)>0, choose eta sufficiently small first, then R sufficiently large. In each box there are
    >> R/L^3
pairs p,v with P^-(pv-2)>=z. Summing >>L disjoint boxes gives, independently for RESET and HIGH,
    >> R/L^2 distinct A=pv-1 in [R,2R],
    R^.2855<=p<=R^.2856, A+1=pv composite,
    P^-(A-1)>=4R^.2856.                                (1)
The lower uses a fixed positive main coefficient (small due to parameter margin), not a finite sample extrapolation. In particular each A is canonical: v=1 mod3, p cannot be 2 mod3 since 3 would divide pv-2; hence p=1 mod3 and A divisible6. The neighbors are odd, and qmin(A)=p because v>>4p. This is an unconditional non-twin supply in every sufficiently large slab, distinct from a twin-or-many-rough-centers dichotomy. The exponent .2855 can be replaced by any fixed exponent less than 2/7 sufficiently close to it, taking narrower fixed bands and compatible d. ## 4. Exact event-compressed reverse recurrence

Let allowed canonical roots be the multiples of6 in a fixed integer interval [Alo,Ahi] with Alo>=6. Fix a target (x,n) with even x>2, and if desired target age bound B. Since a root A has birth time b(A)=3-A/2, its age at n is n-b(A). For the age bound simply replace Ahi by min(Ahi,2(B+3-n)) at the outset, rounded to the largest allowed multiple of6. Keep that bound FIXED at every recursive step; imposing a fresh target-age bound at every subproblem is not the algorithm. If the interval is empty the answer is zero.

Let C(x,n) be the exact number of distinct allowed roots whose actual orbit occupies (x,n) at a nonnegative age. If x>Ahi or n<b(Ahi), or height x/2+n<0, set C=0. In the positive even slab define ell(x,n) to be the largest eligible tick <=n at position x, whether or not any root was there at that tick. It is obtained by taking the maximum over all odd prime divisors of x+1 and x-1 of the last residue occurrence modulo 2p, including n if eligible. Both numbers exceed1; the factor calendars exist. The birth indicator I(x,n) is 1 iff x itself is an allowed root and b(x) lies in [ell(x,n),n]. This inclusive interval is important: birth at an eligible clock is a state at that clock, not the result of a tick from the previous clock.

For every incoming label p, sign s=+1/-1, define c_s=1-s and b_s=0/3. Incoming event at tick tau into x is possible only if
    p|x-c_s,
    tau=0 mod2p (plus) or tau=p+2 mod2p (minus),
    p | x/2+tau-b_s,
    parent y=x+p-1.
The height divisibility follows from the first two and the event identities; it can also be used to enumerate candidates by gcd. Require ell<=tau<=n and test the ACTUAL minimum eligible prime at (y,tau-1) is p. The exact identity is
    C(x,n) = I(x,n)
        + sum_{(p,tau) passing these tests} C(x+p-1,tau-1).    (2)
In the sum one may also impose y<=Ahi and tau-1>=b(Ahi). The last arrival into x is unique for each root since every nonidle step strictly decreases x. A root present at n either was born there during [ell,n] and never moved, or entered by a moving event during that interval. If it were at x strictly before ell and still there at ell-1, it would have left at ell and never returned. Conversely after entering at tau or being born there at a clock in [ell,n], no eligible tick at x occurs in (tau,n], so the actual trajectory stays there. Distinct last-arrival cases and the birth case partition the root set. Thus the sum counts each root once, even if the reverse search visits the same subproblem from multiple branches. Recursion is finite: each moving reverse step increases x by at least2 and decreases n by at least1. Memoization is valid with the fixed allowed-root bounds. This exact recurrence compresses arbitrarily long idle intervals without treating arithmetic eligibility as actual occupation; the minimum-prime test at the parent is essential. It works also for target states which have no allowed ancestor. It does not need a random predecessor assumption.

For an arbitrary high local key from e9f611d, (2) can start at its poststate or at the source just before the top event. The latter plus the actual top-event test counts the roots realizing that key, whereas poststate counts can also include other incoming events or idle occupants. The exact small-port equivalence of the previous checkpoint is now the first layer of (2); iterating it explores all actual earlier states up to a canonical birth, not just candidate ports. The age cut is particularly simple: h=x/2+n, G=A-x, so age=h-3+G/2. Any birth ancestor must therefore have at least h-3 ticks of earlier history, regardless of which moving reverse edges are chosen. A top local state of height comparable to T requires genuinely long canonical ancestry, not merely the short label-three gateway; (2) alone gives no positive asymptotic lower for its count.
