## 1. Setting and deletion insertion lemma

Use the common CRT ghost, infinite alphabet or a finite initial segment of odd primes. Conditional on the common initial time parity, the c_p,b_p pairs are independent uniform modulo p. At each event p, pre-gain g increases by p-1, with completed preceding wait w<=2p. The exact tick-history law for an unused p gives conditional hazard <=2/p^2 throughout ticks <=U whenever p>=sqrt(2U). This law remains true if another fixed prime q is omitted from the alphabet and if the coordinates of a fixed disjoint small-prime set are initially revealed. No fresh hazard is attributed to an already selected p.

Take a LOW cutoff A>=5, L=L_A=2 product_(3<=ell<A)ell. Consider two consecutive HIGH events q,p (labels >=A) with q globally fresh, p repeated and p>q. The intervening low gain d is even and 0<=d<L. Restrict to events through U in the prefix whose POST-gain is <=2U; every positive attained peak lies in this prefix. Every p in it is <=2U+1 and the total sum of event weights p is <=3U. Write G for the pre-gain of q, G1=G+q-1+d for the pre-gain of p, and u for the pre-gain of the first selection of p. Since p>q and p>=A, its first selection must precede the fresh q. Hence u+p-1<=G. As both u and G1 were eligible for p, for p>=7 there is a unique representation
    G1=u+mp+a, m>=1, a in {-2,0,2}, m<=(2U+2)/p.
The uniqueness is due to offsets differing by at most 4, less than p. We use this FIRST selection of p even if it repeated before the present q.

Fix q, and condition on all coordinates OTHER THAN q and on common parity. Run the q-deleted model through U. Before the first selection of q in any insertion of its coordinates, the full and deleted histories agree. At a given even gain G, there is at most one frozen interval of the deleted trajectory (strict gain growth), with last event at time t, or initial time zero; allow a final quiet interval through U. If q is first selected at G, its tick lies in [t+1,t+2q], since the actual q is eligible throughout its own preceding wait, and it cannot be after the next deleted event at a strictly later gain. At each such tick, conditional on common parity, there is exactly one pair (c_q,b_q) modulo q that matches at G and that tick. Consequently the number of coordinate pairs allowing first selection at this G is at most 2q. If also w_q<=q/H for real H>=1, it is at most floor(q/H). Ties and repeated test pairs can only reduce these counts. This is uniform in the fixed deleted trace, its first-discovery rank, whether q has a deeply depleted allowed set, and the amount of time elapsed since the start. In particular a candidate ticket specifying G, computed using only the deleted trace and other coordinates, can be weighted with the bounds 2/q or 1/(Hq) on insertion probability. The actual following high event is not presumed independent of q; it is simply overcounted by necessary tickets.

## 2. Short and long predecessor branches and necessary tickets

Fix 0<alpha<1, H a positive power of log U (or H>=1 bounded by such a power), and Q0=ceil(exp((log U)^alpha)). Take z>=ceil(sqrt(2U)), z<=2U+1, L<=log^(1/8) U, and assume U sufficiently large that Q0>A and Q0>L+10. Consider pairs above with q>=Q0 and p>=z and the extra condition w_p>p/4. Write W(U;z,Q0) for the sum of p over all such pairs in the gain-capped prefix, with no restriction on the time or exposure of the fresh q. Partition into short predecessor (w_q<=q/H) and long predecessor (w_q>q/H).

An actual w_p>p/4 implies no eligible odd ell<=p/8 at G1, by the strict next-clock wait <=2ell at fixed horizon. For the long predecessor, w_q>q/H implies in addition no eligible ell<=q/(2H) at G. We will count the candidate return and insertion positions from the first p selection in the q-deleted path. For a known first-p pre-gain u, assign tickets indexed by m,a,d with
    G1=u+mp+a, G=u+mp+a-(q-1+d).
Require 0<=G,G1<=2U, p<=2U+1, and all the appropriate roughness conditions at a fixed sieve-prime set specified below. We may overcount these restrictions (such as parity, future chronological realizability, and the actual LOW suffix). The assigned tickets depend only on the other coordinates, q, and the deleted trajectory. If the actual pair occurs, its first p selection occurs in that deleted trace, and its actual ticket is included. No relation after the insertion of q is required to hold in the deleted trace. In particular the repeated p itself need not occur at all there. All candidate G that are not attained frozen positions of that trace have zero insertion probability.

For each actual q there is at most one following HIGH event; the sum of its pair weight indicators is bounded by the weighted sum over tickets whose insertion occurs, using the previous paragraph. Conditional on the deleted model, short-branch tickets carry insertion probability <=1/(Hq), and long-branch tickets <=2/q. This conditioning includes the prospective repeated p's coordinates, which may depend on the deleted history. It does not reveal the actual q pair. We never apply a conditional hazard 1/q^2 after conditioning on the original history.

## 3. Uniform equal-slope sieve for the two different positions

Set L0=ceil(log^2 U), R=floor(exp((log U)^alpha/4)), D=floor(U^(1/20)). Initially reveal all coordinates for odd primes ell<=R, which excludes every q>=Q0 and every p>=z for large U. We have R<=min(q/(2H),p/8). For fixed q,m,a,d,u and a candidate integer x in [P,2P), P>=z, write v=u+a, B=q-1+d. The short-branch rough condition at a sieve prime L0<ell<=R is
    c_ell+v+mx != +/-1 mod ell.
The long branch adds
    c_ell+v+mx-B != +/-1 mod ell.
Also x !=0 mod ell, because candidate x is a prime above R. In the long branch omit primes dividing m B(B-2)(B+2), and in the short branch omit primes dividing m. All factors are nonzero: m>=1, B>=q-1>2. Each has absolute size O(U+L) in the allowed q,m ranges. The omitted reciprocal mass of primes exceeding L0 is O(log U/(L0 log L0))=o(1).

In the long branch, the four neighbor roots in x are distinct: m is invertible, and the two pairs of signs differ by B,B-2,B+2, all nonzero mod ell. There is no need to assume independence between the two neighbor conditions; they have the SAME slope. The only remaining possible collision among the five roots (including zero) is when c_ell+v equals one of 1,-1,B+1,B-1. Each such collision restricts the independent uniform c_ell to at most four values. In the short branch there are two distinct neighbor roots and at most two ways to collide with zero.

For each fixed tuple, the mean number of collision primes is O(log log U), and the probability of at least ceil(log U) is exp(-Omega(log U log log U)), by the elementary independent Bernoulli exponential moment estimate. There are polynomially many tuples (q<=2U+1, m<=2U+2, u in [0,2U], a in {-2,0,2}, d<L). A union bound therefore gives one initially measurable Good event of complementary probability exp(-Omega(log U log log U)), on which ALL tuples have at most ceil(log U) collision primes. They have reciprocal mass <=ceil(log U)/L0=o(1). This event uses only the initially revealed primes, so it does not depend on the deleted q pair. We may discard collision primes in the sieve.

For n=3 or 5 the remaining local survival product is O((log L0/log R)^n) = O(lambda_n), where
    lambda_n=(log log U)^n/(log U)^(n alpha).
Use the explicit Selberg quadratic majorant of research/2026-11-25-min-ghost-gain-capped-top-repeat-rough-sieve.md section 3. For completeness: if nu(ell)=n, g(d)=nu(d)/d, h(d)=product_(ell|d)(1/g(ell)-1), the squarefree divisor sum G(D)=sum_(d<=D)1/h(d) over remaining sieve primes yields main term <= (P+O(1))/G(D) and error O(D^6). The unrestricted divisor sum is product_(ell)(1-g(ell))^-1. Its normalized weights have independent divisor inclusions with mean log d <= n sum_(ell<=R)log ell/ell=O(log R)=o(log D). Hence at least half of the unrestricted weight lies at d<=D. This proves, uniformly over the tuples on Good, the candidate counts
    # short candidates in [P,2P) <= O(P lambda_3),
    # long candidates in [P,2P) <= O(P lambda_5).
The discarded primes only affect the products by a bounded factor; D^6=O(U^.3) is negligible relative to P lambda_n for P>=sqrt(2U). Thus the subpolynomial sieve range is enough, without any prime-distribution statement in a short progression.

## 4. Expected ticket and insertion weight

For fixed q>=Q0, consider a tick s of the q-deleted model and condition on the initially revealed low coordinates and all previous deleted tick history. On Good with u<=2U at this tick, count tickets if an unused candidate p>=z is first selected here. For p in band [P,2P) there are O((U/P)(L+1)) choices of m,a,d, overcounting positions outside [0,2U] if needed. For each tuple the candidate count is O(P lambda_n), and ticket weight p times fresh hazard <=2/p<=2/P. Conditional expected ticket weight per tick in this band is therefore O((U/P)(L+1)lambda_n), for the corresponding n. Sum geometrically over P=z,2z,... through 2U+1 and over U ticks to obtain
    E[1_Good T_n(q)] <= O(U^2 (L+1)lambda_n/z),
where T_n(q) is the total candidate ticket p-weight assigned on the deleted trace. This holds even if the deleted trace later runs past the gain cap (we stop assigning tickets when u>2U), and also for finite initial segments containing q and some p>=z. The other case is vacuous. The initially revealed coordinates are all available because R<q,p. The hazard applies only to unused p, not the actual later return.

Now integrate over the independent q coordinate pair using the insertion lemma. For each fixed deleted trace, the actual short-pair weight on Good is bounded in expectation by T_3(q)/(Hq); the long-pair weight by 2 T_5(q)/q. This is a two-stage expectation, not an independence assertion about the actual following p. Sum over q in [Q0,2U+1], bounding the sum of prime reciprocals by O(log log(3U)); all actual q in the capped prefix lie in this range. On the shared exceptional Good complement use the pathwise W<=3U. Thus
    E W(U;z,Q0)
      <= O(U^2 (L+1) log log(3U)/z * [lambda_3/H + lambda_5])
         +3U exp(-Omega(log U log log U)).
This is the new unconditional bound. It has no constraint on the first selection tick s of q or the number of its remaining coordinate pairs, and no requirement that w_q be large. The two branches cover all waits. The gain cap, q>=Q0, p>=z, p>q, and long w_p are required. No comparable-ratio bound is imposed.

For example choose alpha=19/20, H=log^3 U, z=ceil(U/log^(9/2) U), L<=log^(1/8) U. Then Q0 eventually <sqrt(2U), z>sqrt(2U), and
    E W/U = O((log log U)^4/log^(49/40) U
                 +(log log U)^6/log^(1/8) U)=o(1).
The first exponent is 3+3alpha-9/2-1/8=49/40; the second is 5alpha-9/2-1/8=1/8. More generally for L<=log^ell U, z=U/log^b U and H=log^c U, the same proof works when b+ell<min(3alpha+c,5alpha). This reaches a smaller p boundary than the earlier general weighted single-rough theorem b<3, for the specified fresh-to-known pairs with sufficiently large q. It does not provide a general five-dimensional sieve for arbitrary repeated p without such a fresh predecessor.
