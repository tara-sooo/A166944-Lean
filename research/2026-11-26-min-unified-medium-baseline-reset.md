# Integration XXIV: medium-scale macroscopic baseline, reset separation and precursor supply

2026-11-26 JST. Parent 6dda1b5a3ff98944b47f42eafee1ce90e63e05f3. Direct preflight [3214564](https://github.com/tara-sooo/A166944-Lean/blob/3214564a96ec7bab6234d37f253dcb0636b7c229/research/2026-11-26-min-direct-multiband-weighted-deficit-sieve.md). Main new theorem: at the label scale sqrt(H), bounded ending-label-weighted relay and selected-post variation, together with macroscopic nonreset gain, force macroscopic inherited deficits on a positive fraction of original tags. No polynomial multiplier cap, no full physical variation hypothesis, no single dyadic band dominance, no initial deficit assumption. Reset-bearing selected lists cannot supply macroscopic nonreset gain in this bounded-budget regime. The key is a small FIXED fraction of the quadratic deficit scale, rather than a fixed exponent below two. This extends XIX's precursor result, but does not establish that weighted budgets are bounded or that this medium band dominates.

## 1. Retain resets inside the selected list

Use original canonical prime-root tags and actual complete events, lifetime gain <=H. For ANY chosen actual list of labels Y<=p<=P, including adjusted resets if desired, write x=(d'-6I)/2=hp>=0. For consecutive selected events define E=C2-C-(p-1), v=d2'-d1', c=x2-x1=v/2-3(J-I), ending label r. Sum R_w=sum rE, V_w=sum r|v| over original tags, as XXIII but now include resets rather than delete them. Let V_sel,i=sum |v| on tag i. Then Y sum V_sel,i<=V_w, and for each tag any two selected postdeficits differ by at most V_sel,i. This is SELECTED POST variation, not full physical ordinary variation. Relay intervals and post-to-post intervals do not overlap within each list. No weighting of the same tag twice.

If exactly one endpoint is reset (x=0) and the other nonreset (x>=its label>=Y), then |v|>=2Y-6. Thus total ending gain of mixed reset/nonreset edges <=V_w/(2Y-6). In particular if Y->infinity and V_w=O(NH), the mixed ending gain is o(NT). If a tag has any selected reset, all its selected ordinary postdeficits are <=6+V_sel,i. If not, it may have a high inherited baseline: the consecutive variation budget does not include its first selected deficit. If we include the optional root entry cost p_1 |d'_1-6| in V_w, denoting V_w^*, then every nonempty list has max d'<=6+V_w,i^*/Y. These are exact deterministic statements, also valid for core-restricted lists with gaps.

## 2. Direct multiband local transfer

Direct 3214564 uses labels [s,P], P<=K_R s^b, cap x<=K s^beta, beta<2, m=1+floor(log2(P/s)), nu(beta-1)<theta<1, W0=ceil(s^theta). It assigns edges by ENDING band [L,2L) and FIRST horizon bin of length sL. On the original lifetime use at most O(1+H/(sL)) bins per band, not O(X/(sL)). Keep its one/two-dimensional prime-line bounds. For a fixed ending band the selected-post index intervals between first and last endings in each bin are disjoint, even if other edges are omitted. Therefore sum local ranges <=V_sel,i/2+3 times the number of bins. Across bands use sum L<=2P, not disjointness. The resulting aggregate close ending bound (both nonreset endpoints capped) is
    F_close << (A+1)(Bcut+1) m^(nu-1)/log^nu s
        * [N W0(H m/s+P) + P V_w/s].                 (1)
Here W0 absorbs constants, and V_w/s bounds aggregate selected variation. The added P term is essential if bin sizes exceed H. The direct proof allows both endpoint labels different bands; exact same-label spacing E>=p-1, c!=0 under cap. Bound applies on full original lifetime even when selection is from multiple cores. No independent summing of full-X error per core. For fixed ratio P/s or polylog ratio with P/s*m^(nu-1)=o(log^nu s), and appropriate W0 such that W0(H m/s+P)m^(nu-1)/[H log^nu s]=o(1), fixed-cutoff close gain is o(NT) under bounded V_w. The following result improves the cap and yields a macro baseline in the particular medium-scale range.

## 3. Uniform small fixed quadratic cap

Fix positive eta,K0 with eta sqrt(H)<=Y<=K0 sqrt(H), Y<=P<=K0 sqrt(H), so P/Y and H/Y² bounded. Fix integer cutoffs A,Bcut>=0, eventually Bcut<Y-1 and Q=floor(A/2)+3<=Y/2. Fix 0<kappa<=1/4 independently of X. Count only close consecutive selected edges with BOTH endpoints nonreset and x1,x2<=kappa Y². Both coordinates uniquely determine their prime >=Y (since x<Y²); p!=r by relay spacing, c!=0. For fixed ending x2, c,E,I,J, horizon CRT determines one residue mod pr>=Y²; in the original lifetime interval of length H there are O_eta(1) actual candidates. Charge ending gain <=P=O_K0(Y). One possible first horizon specifies at most one edge on this fixed selected list. No independence or equidistribution of horizon residues.

On tag i let W=max(Y, range of these capped ending coordinates+1), with empty list range zero. Then W<=Y+V_sel,i/2+4. Count ending x2 in an integer interval of length W using just x2=k r, Y<=r<=P. The starting label remains unique if its condition holds, so dropping its primality test does not increase the per-coordinate CRT count. If the minimum x2=a>=W, k belongs to [a/P,2a/Y], a bounded-ratio interval, and k<=kappa Y. For each k pad the prime interval to ceil(W/k)+1. Its length is >=1/kappa and <=O(Y²), so the ordinary translated one-dimensional upper sieve already used in direct gives O((W/k)/log(2+1/kappa)) primes, uniformly for fixed kappa and sufficiently large Y. In fact the constant is independent of kappa: log(2+N)>=log(2+1/kappa), N=O(W/k), cutoff N^(1/12) below Y. The harmonic sum over k is bounded only in terms of P/Y. For a<W, cover [Y, min(2W,kappa Y²)] by dyadic shells [D,2D) with total lengths O(W). Each shell has the same multiplier bounded-ratio estimate and D/k>=1/kappa. Thus count <=C W/log(2+1/kappa) uniformly. This argument uses a FIXED fraction cap, not the previous power-saving beta<2; the denominator is log(2+1/kappa), not log Y. It can be made arbitrarily large by choosing kappa sufficiently small BEFORE taking X large.

Sum c,E,sides and weight Y, then sum over original tags:
    F_close,cap <= C_(eta,K0) (A+1)(Bcut+1)
                    (N Y²+V_w) / log(2+1/kappa).     (2)
All constants uniform in kappa,A,Bcut within the stated close assumptions (asymptotic threshold may depend on fixed parameters). No single-band hypothesis, no multiplier restriction beyond the small absolute cap on counted edges, no full physical V_d. A cap of this fixed fraction is adequate precisely because the weighted variation controls the range and H/Y² is bounded.

## 4. Uncapped medium-band precursor theorem

Now allow ALL multipliers and resets in the selected list. Let F_nr be total nonreset gain, F_small nonreset gain with x<=kappa Y²-Q. First selected vertices cost <=NP. Nonclose ending gain <=R_w/(Bcut+1)+V_w/(A+1). A close edge ending in such a small nonreset cannot start at a reset, because |v|>=2Y-6 on mixed edges; it cannot start above cap because |c|<=Q. Therefore by (2):
    F_small <= NP + R_w/(Bcut+1)+V_w/(A+1)
        + C (A+1)(Bcut+1)(N Y²+V_w)/log(2+1/kappa).  (3)
This holds for arbitrary actual selection, not just high-long events. No omitted high-deficit vertices inserted into a separately filtered list; all edges counted in ONE list including resets.

Suppose along an unbounded sequence F_nr>=rho NH, R_w+V_w<=K NH, fixed rho,K>0. Choose fixed A,Bcut sufficiently large in terms of K/rho to make the tails <=rho NH/4, then fixed kappa sufficiently small in terms of eta,K0,K,rho,A,Bcut to make the last term <=rho NH/4 (one can use rho/8 for each to leave slack). NP=o(NH). Thus nonreset gain at x>kappa Y²-Q is >=rho NH/2 eventually, after choosing constants with slack.

Discard tags with V_sel,i>kappa Y²/2. Their number <=2 V_w/(kappa Y³)=O(N/Y), so they carry o(NH) total actual gain. Every remaining tag with a high ending has all selected ordinary postdeficits >=kappa Y² for large X, by the range bound and d'_high>=2 kappa Y²-2Q. In particular no selected reset on that tag. Since each tag gains <=H, at least (rho/3)N tags have such a baseline (for sufficiently large X), and their first selected postdeficit is >=kappa Y². This is a macroscopic lower cH, not a polynomial subscale. Their actual pre-first-selected gain Gpre and wait Wpre satisfy
    Gpre >= (kappa Y²-6)/5 - P,
    Wpre >= (kappa Y²-6)/2 - 2P.
First inequality uses d'<=6+5 cumulative actual gain from canonical root, second exact canonical clock and first wait<=2P. Hence total Gpre,Wpre on this population >=c_(rho,K,eta,K0) NH. This prefix gain is ALL complementary to the selected list, so F_all cannot approach G_all while F_nr remains macro and both weighted budgets bounded. In the anchored version V_w^*<=K NH no such high baseline tags are possible except O(N/Y), hence F_nr=o(NT). Also if only select reset-bearing tags and their selected nonreset gain were macro, the same theorem would contradict their mandatory reset, so their aggregate nonreset gain is o(NT) under bounded unanchored budgets. These implications do not require every tag to have a reset; no claim that high actual deficit is intrinsically impossible.

Connection to missing clock: for a tag in the high baseline population, if its first selected prehorizon is after the first true large vertex of XX, let G_gap be gained length between these two, else set G_gap=0. Then Wpre<=Wpre_large+3G_gap+O(B) (one straddling event). For never-large use terminal, same inequality. Sum over the high baseline tags; XXII Wpre_large<=(1+o)Mhat+o(NT) with theta=1/ell. Thus
    (1+o) Mhat +3 sum_high_tags G_gap >= c NH-o(NT).  (4)
If their first selection lies within o(H) of first large in aggregate, this forces macro missing. Otherwise there is macro actual complementary precursor gain AFTER first large. This replaces XIX's full-physical-variation and complement O(sqrt H) assumptions by ending-label-weighted consecutive budgets and makes the missing-vs-complement alternative explicit. Still conditional: no proof R_w,V_w=O(NH), F_nr macro, or medium label dominance. A prefix outside selection need not itself be missing.

## 5. Reset resource in the uniform core clock inequality

For any adjusted reset event d'=6I<=6, d>=0, hence 4w-g=g+2(d'-d)<=g+12 and (d'-d)_+<=6. In XXII high sector p>=Y_pin (growing), write F_R for all high reset gain, F_NR for high nonreset gain with 4w>=g. Since #high resets<=F_R/(Y_pin-1), the former E_H obeys
    E_H <= (1+12/(Y_pin-1)) F_R
                +(7+8/(Y_pin-1)) F_NR.
So the signed outer inequality improves to
    11 M_low/(1+eps) + F_R + (7+o(1))F_NR +4 Mhat
          >= (1-11beta)G_all+2Z_all-o(NT).            (5)
Here pin cut family and beta as XXII. This is a sharper coefficient specifically for resets, not a subtraction of their base gain. If G_all-G_core=o(NT), U_low=o(NT), and ALL high nonreset gain (including fast) is o(NT), then core positive variation <=6 F_R/(Y_pin-1)+5(U_low+F_high_nonreset)=o(NT); omitted positive variation <=2 W_omit=o(NT) on small missing branch. Total variation and Z_all are then o(NT) by the canonical telescope. Such a reset-dominated branch can still supply the base signed clock G_all. For two successive selected resets, even with a relay, exact elapsed time 2 W_between=E+g_end+6(J-I), and a contiguous reset-to-reset transition has ending label 1 mod4 (both posthorizons multiples of4). The side changes telescope; they are not independent sources of variation. Direct constructive reset words (2026-11-25-min-direct-reset-words.md) show that local reset-only continuation is possible for arbitrarily prescribed finite lengths at very large prime roots; do not infer an asymptotic supply bound from the signed ledger alone. Equation (5) does not exclude the reset-dominated branch.
