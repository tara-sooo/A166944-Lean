## 2. Same-slot resampling without changing the compatible clock

At each source actual physical slot (C,q,v), let G be the b original distinct tags executing its completed jump. All share the true predeparture state (v-1,C) and continue to the poststate (v,J). For EVERY positive c_s of a focal tag M, distribute c_s uniformly over the b choices of partner M' in G, including M itself. Split equally over n_k source atoms, so each ordered tag pair and atom receives c_s/(b n_k). Sum over all partners/atoms recovers exactly c_s.

This is a NEW accounting of the already-defined compatible CLOCK, not an assertion that its original same-word partners were all synchronous. In particular, for low words the original a_word/n_c may count other physical slots; that factor is already part of c_s and is not replaced by b/n_c. The resampled pair does NOT need to be stable individually, rank-unpaid, or of the same size as another slot; both partners execute this very actual jump. No change to the old normalized defect measures.

At one original source atom/slot, each focal row has total <=c_a P, and each partner column also <=c_a P, since every focal tag's c_l+c_h<=c_a P n_k and there are at most b focal tags in G. This capacity is valid with arbitrary earlier holes in c_s and all previous compatible masks. It is the reason we can route backward to either actual pair member, not multiply a bound for one pair by the unweighted pair count.

Pay diagonal M=M' at cost <=C_rem/b_min=o(NT). For distinct members of G, their actual paths until the common predeparture state have a unique first true meeting U. They share the rest of that segment by deterministic tick evolution. Include root injections as separate incoming singleton branches and terminal snapshots when needed; every member of G actually continues to the source. No path extension or new MIN dynamics.

## 3. Merge-tree relative payments on the frozen SOURCE cohort

Fix G, and trace only its b original members through their actual histories before the source. This finite reverse merge tree has no loss of its own members: at each true state V count g_G(V)=#members of G there and partition by incoming true predecessor, with a separate injected root singleton if present. All members present continue together along the unique suffix up to the common source predeparture. For a pair's first meeting its two members enter distinct branches. Counts in this induced tree are source-cohort-specific, not full historical population counts. At a true state (t,J), at most one incoming waiting predecessor (t-1,J), one injection, and one predecessor per eligible incoming odd prime p; the latter satisfies p|J+2s, s=t mod2 (actual post-event congruence). The number of distinct odd prime factors of J+2s=O(X) is O(u/ell), by the product of the first k distinct integers and the factorial lower bound. Thus a common integer degree bound d_*=ceil(20u/ell) holds for all sufficiently large X. Enlarging the constant if needed affects no estimate.

Take eta=tau. Two elementary relative pair payments:

(A) For a fixed member M, the number of distinct partners whose FIRST meeting has parent population g_G(U)<=eta b is <=eta b. The first-meeting partner sets along M's prefix are disjoint; g_G grows monotonically and all partners of meetings before the threshold stay with M until the source. Including a root injection is valid. Equivalently the cumulative first meetings up to the last parent <=eta b fit in that parent. If there is no such parent, the set is empty. Thus this deletion costs <=eta times each focal row's clock.

(B) Put k=eta b/d_*. Pay pairs if BOTH incoming branch populations (in this same induced tree at their first meeting) are <=k. On a fixed member's path, while the parent population is <=k, its cumulative first-meeting partners number <=k. At the first state where parent population exceeds k, it enters a branch of size <=k (or is injected), and at most d_* other incoming branches each of size <=k can contribute such partners, hence <=d_* k. Subsequently its own incoming branch is >k, so no further such partners. If the threshold is never crossed only the first bound is used. Consequently each focal row has at most (d_*+1)k<=2eta b partners of this kind. This holds even in highly asymmetric comb-like trees and with other large branches at the same meeting. It does not assert that every first meeting is balanced or that every incoming branch is large.

By uniform resampling, union (A),(B) costs O(eta NT) in the grid-averaged compatible clock, without a factor log b or number of scales. No separate entropy bound for the induced trees is needed. These are relative payments at each source, not a small count of all global first meetings. For every remaining pair the parent g_G(U)>eta b and at least one of its TWO incoming branches has mass >eta b/d_*.

Two further useful payments:

(C) Old ancestry: choose ONE stable representative of each nonzero source slot (exists because each good contributing focal source tag is stable with D_s=floor(T/(u ell^6)) and delta=1/ell). Take its last original recorded vertex A0 with horizon <=C-D_s/2. Endpoint margin ensures existence and overshoot <=B; all transitions from A0 through the source postdeparture lie in its D_s window. The full-cohort intersection lemma of 3fe50cf6 provides a subset G0 of G of size >=(1-O(delta))b sharing this entire actual segment, since the intersection includes the two states of the source jump. Pay resampled pairs with either member outside G0. Focal-row payments on excluded partners cost O(delta) times total assigned clock; focal tags outside G0 may carry atypically large compatible fractions, so use the per-atom row capacity c_a P and sum over at most O(delta)b such focal tags. Across all atoms/slots and grids raw measure <=N(H+P_max), so both together cost O(NT/ell). Every surviving pair first met at or before A0; hence C-C_U>=D_s/2. This is a common actual older anchor, not inferred from sharing a projected horizon.

(D) Root separation: pay pairs with |M-M'|<=eta b u. For every focal root, by the uniform elementary upper sieve for primes in an interval, the number of such original prime-root partners is O(eta b u/log(2+eta b u))=O(eta b), since b>=b_min=B^(1-o(1)) and eta>=1/log ell. Even if the interval crosses the dyadic window edge this upper estimate applies. Row payment O(eta NT). Remaining pairs have root separation >eta b u. At first meeting the smaller original root has already gained at least 2|M-M'|, because both horizons equal and the larger root started at horizon 2M'-2. This is a depth constraint for the actual first meeting, not for every individual member of the chosen large branch.

All these are payments of exact compatible fractions only, not of the corresponding entire original clock or old forward defect terms. The union, including diagonal, is o(NT). No new grid-good predicate or denominator change.

## 4. Backward transport to an actual heavy incoming branch

For each surviving ordered pair choose the member in the larger of its two incoming branches at U (tie choose focal member). Its branch mass b_in>eta b/d_*. This exceeds 1 uniformly, since b>=b_min, eta=tau and d_* is polylogarithmic. Consequently that chosen branch cannot be a root injection, even if the other member is injected. There are two cases.

* Incoming JUMP: route to the chosen original tag's actual completed event whose poststate is U. All b_in members of this branch execute that same true jump from its immediate true predecessor to U. The full original actual exit slot of the destination therefore has population at least b_in.
* Incoming WAIT: route to the chosen original tag's next actual completed jump at the horizon of U, i.e. the jump terminating this waiting interval. All b_in members continue through that jump, because they share the deterministic suffix to the common source predeparture and the source completed jump. Its full original actual exit slot likewise has population >=b_in. The wait can have started before U, and other members may have joined before its departure; we do not claim equality of recorded arrival times.

The actual destination event E=(C_D,p_D,v_D) belongs to the chosen original member and C_D<=C_U<=C-D_s/2. Source K>=C_D and K-C_D<H, since both lie in the same original tag's gain interval (the destination can be the source only before the old-ancestry payment). The original tag has gain <H, so at most H/P+1 source grid atoms in its entire path can contribute to a fixed destination. At each source atom, sum over all partners is <=c_a P for this tag in either role by section 2. Hence exact backward clock charge B_P(tag,E) satisfies
    B_P(tag,E) <= 2 c_a (H+P).
The two roles are counted explicitly, no multiplication by the number of distinct first meetings, original pairs, or word classes. The chosen incoming branch is source-cohort-specific, and may vary with the pair, grid and source. This bound does not require one destination to have one designated source cohort.

Average over the SAME M grids. Define Bbar(C,p,v) as routed clock grouped by actual destination exit slot, Bbar(C,p)=sum_v Bbar(C,p,v). The original full slot population b_v(C,p) and projected n(C,p) give
    Bbar(C,p,v)<=2 c_a (H+P_max)b_v(C,p),
    Bbar(C,p)<=2 c_a (H+P_max)n(C,p).
All nonzero destinations have an original true exit-slot population
    b_v(C,p)> eta b_min/d_*.
This is stronger than only requiring a large projected group. For each individual contribution the stronger source-dependent bound b_v>eta b/d_* holds. Also that incoming branch consists of distinct prime roots, and has root span >>u b_in by the same prime interval sieve, since b_in>=B^(1-o(1)). This gives a large actual ancestor span before the chosen branch's common state. The chosen wait and jump sectors may be kept separately.

At each original source exact conservation: old compatible c_s equals all newly paid fractions plus the sum of backward charges. Thus sum Bbar<=C_rem and C_rem=sum Bbar+o(NT). There is NO extra factor on the backward measure, which is already in clock units. This is not a bound on the unweighted number of global birth events. Nor does it prove their total charge small.

## 5. Additional arithmetic types at a true birth

If both pair members enter U=(t,J) by jumps, their selected labels p,q are distinct (equal labels would have the same true predecessor). Put s=t mod2. Exact exit congruences give p,q dividing t-2s and J+2s, so both divide k_U=(2t-J-6s)/2. The original postdeficit form for an incoming jump ensures k_U>=0, integer. If k_U>0 then
    pq<=k_U<=O(H).
In particular if BOTH incoming labels are >= B/u^(1/16), their product exceeds O(H) eventually, since H=Theta(B²/u^.25); such a double-jump birth must be RESET (k_U=0). This only constrains the incoming labels, not the later selected label of an incoming wait branch.

At a root-injection birth of prime M, the state is (M+2,2M-2), with M odd >B. There cannot be another incoming jump at that tick, since odd exit requires p|t-2=M. Thus the other branch is a wait. Its original recorded arrival at that horizon precedes the root tick, and cannot itself be another root. The incoming postdeficit form 6s+2pk and 0<=d<6 force even RESET arrival at t'=M-1, since p>=3. The waiting branch must still be present at M+2 and continue to the source. If M=5 mod6 then p=3 divides J+1 and the next candidate at t'=M-1 (4 mod6) occurs at M (5 mod6), before injection, contradiction. Thus root-injection births of this kind necessarily have M=1 mod6 (large M). This does not pay all such births. The chosen large branch in section 4 is then a wait branch. Other wait/jump births and nonreset two-jump births with smaller incoming labels remain. These arithmetic types are deductions using the exact MIN exit formulas; the finite audit below checks the more general transport rather than these refined arithmetic subcases.

## 6. Thin destination payment and no-larger sufficient condition

The old graded support h(r)=log²(2+r)/(1+log log(2+r))² satisfies sum_C h(r(C))=O(X/p) for fixed eligible selected p, as established by the two-form upper sieve and layer integration in 5b56248/aa04b0. It applies also to the backward destination labels, which need not exceed y0. Pay Bbar(C,p) whenever
    Bbar(C,p)<=H eta h(r(C))/(u ell).
Total O(XH eta/(u ell) sum_(3<=p<=B)1/p)=O(eta NT). The same optional mid/rough refinements as before apply with their proved support and same budget. This is an absolute charge cutoff; it need not correspond to a large effective destination mass in the long H capacity. No claim that a large actual destination slot necessarily carries a large charge. Let B_rem be exact remaining backward charge. All previous forward defect measures and payments unchanged. Then on the full route under finitely many twins
    NT/2-o(NT) <= B_rem+(3+o(1))D_all,rem.
Hence a new sufficient condition is
    liminf_(dyadic X) [ B_rem+3D_all,rem ]/(NT)<1/2.       (1)
It is implied by (6) of 673f238 on the identical frozen ensemble, since B_rem<=C_all,rem and the other term is unchanged. For the hybrid replace C_rem in (5) of 673f238 by B_rem, preserving 3D_M,rem+(3/tau)D_mod,rem. These are now two directions of actual event charges (backward first meeting and forward first translation defect), both with linear row/column capacities; their destinations may overlap but their source fractions are complementary. One must not subtract the same previously paid original clock twice. No strict separation on actual asymptotic ensembles, nor the upper contraction required by (1), has been shown. The improvement is the unconditional reduction of the compatible residual to old first meetings at heavy incoming branches, together with arithmetic constraints on birth types.
