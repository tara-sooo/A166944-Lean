## 1. Improve the unconditional quiet-time exponent from .234 to any theta<2/7

Rather than sift both neighbors of an arbitrary integer (dimension two), make one neighbor a prime and sift only the other (dimension one). Let 0<theta<2/7 be fixed, H=floor(R^theta), z=2H+8. Consider A={p+2: R-1<=p<=2R-1, p prime}, P=odd primes, and X=|A|~R/L, L=log R. For odd squarefree d the remainder is
    r(d)=pi(2R-1;d,-2)-pi(R-2;d,-2)-X/phi(d),
with g(d)=1/phi(d). The residue is FIXED -2, coprime to all these moduli. Mertens gives the dimension-one product condition (2.3) in Lichtman, arXiv:2109.02851v2, and V(z) asymptotic to a positive constant /log z: compare (1-1/(p-1)) with (1-1/p), their ratio differs from 1 by O(1/p²).

Take D=R^(4/7-epsilon) with 2theta<4/7-epsilon<4/7, e.g. theta=.285 and epsilon=.001. Apply Iwaniec's linear lower sieve with well-factorable remainder (Lichtman Theorem 2.10, equations (2.4)-(2.5)). Here s=log D/log z tends to (4/7-epsilon)/theta>2, so f(s)>0; the fixed auxiliary approximation parameter in that theorem can be chosen small enough that its O(error) is less than half this positive constant. The remainder is a bounded (in R for fixed approximation parameter) sum of well-factorable sequences of level D. Use the Bombieri–Friedlander–Iwaniec theorem (Lichtman Theorem 2.2, original BFI Theorem 10) with fixed residue -2 on BOTH endpoints of the prime interval, decreasing epsilon slightly for constant-factor endpoint changes. It gives an arbitrarily large fixed log saving. The sieve weights here are supported on squarefree products of the allowed sieve primes, as in the construction (5.14)-(5.17); the standard well-factorable lower-sieve remainder is the one being used, not an arbitrary squarefree truncation of a well-factorable sequence. Therefore
    # {p in [R-1,2R-1] prime: P^-(p+2)>=z} >>_theta R/L².    (1)
Only the ordinary well-factorable level 4/7 is needed; the stronger 10/17 result in that article is a MODIFIED UPPER sieve, not substituted for a lower sieve. The larger levels in subsequent papers are not automatically levels for the lower weights.

For each such p, x=p+1 is even and divisible by 6 for sufficiently large R (neither p nor p+2 is divisible by 3). Both neighbors have least factor >=z, since p itself is prime of order R. By the checked height label bound p_event<=2 age+7, the canonical orbit birth(x) has no firing through age H. Thus for all sufficiently large R,
    # {canonical x in [R,2R]: G_floor(R^theta)(x)=0} >>_theta R/L².
In particular theta=.285 improves the explicit prior .234; can take any fixed theta<.285714... . The asymptotic lower on birth-reward objective at a longer common horizon is correspondingly >>D_reward R^(1+theta)/L² by choosing distinct stationary prefixes. This is a lower for actual trajectories and not an inference from finite samples. It does not reach sqrt R; p+2 may have up to three prime factors at theta>.25. No assertion that p+2 is prime or that these roots are all non-twins.

## 2. Nonzero-gain trajectories and growing reset fans at every fixed theta<1/4

The BFI theorem above fixes a single residue across varying moduli. It does not immediately allow the additional CRT reset constraints x=0 mod selected p_i, because the prime variable p=x-1 must then satisfy a different residue at those fixed primes. Instead use the ordinary Bombieri–Vinogradov theorem (maximum over reduced residue classes), with the same linear lower sieve at D=R^(1/2-epsilon). This works for each FIXED admissible CRT class, since the fixed extra modulus times D is below R^(1/2-epsilon/2). Use P of free odd primes not dividing the fixed modulus. The dimension-one product condition holds with a constant depending on this fixed modulus. For a fixed B, b with gcd(b,B)=gcd(b+2,B)=1, the sequence A={p+2:p prime in the slab, p=b mod B} has X~R/(phi(B)L) by PNT in a fixed progression. CRT gives one reduced class modulo Bd for d|P(z), g(d)=1/phi(d). The Iwaniec remainder, bounded pointwise by a constant for fixed approximation parameter, is dominated by the BV absolute-error sum. Choose 2theta<1/2-epsilon, z=2 floor(R^theta)+8. Hence
    # {p prime in [R-1,2R-1], p=b mod B: P^-(p+2)>=z}
       >>_(B,b,theta) R/L².                              (2)
This is a fixed-modulus result, not a uniform growing-modulus BFI theorem.

Concrete single-reset construction: x=32760 mod 60060, equivalently x=0 mod 12,13,5,7 and x=2 mod 11. Both x+-1 avoid all primes dividing B=60060, and source neighbors x+13 and x+11 have no prime factor <13 (x+13 divisible by 13). Apply (2) with b=32759. for any theta<1/4, e.g. .249. If needed, define H using a fixed larger constant cutoff z to cover synchronized retirement too. Both roots' physical equality is actual, not a relaxation.

Reuse nonnested wide fan construction from 67d4716, for each FIXED r: choose fixed labels as in that note using its prime/rough-prime theorem, fixed B and locally allowed class x modulo B. x+-1 are reduced at every pinned prime; A_i=x+p_i-1 satisfy actual reset lemma. ## 3. Exact first firing and four-form exclusion at its destination

Let x be a canonical z-rough center, z>=R^theta with fixed theta>0, not a twin. Write q=qmin(x)<=sqrt(2R+1). Its first wait w satisfies (q-7)/2<=w<=2q by the height label bound and static calendar. Winner p>=q and p<=2w+7<=4q+7. Thus p in [R^theta,O(sqrt R)]. The neighbor x+s=p v with s in {-1,+1} is composite (p=o(R)); v>1. The other neighbor is pv-2s. The first destination y=x-p+1=p(v-1)+1-s.

In fact the exact canonical birth calendar for any divisor p>=11 of x+s is as follows, with v odd:
    s=+1: wait = ((v mod 4)*p-7)/2;
    s=-1, v=1 mod4: wait=(3p-1)/2;
    s=-1, v=3 mod4: wait=(p-1)/2.
These follow by positive residues from n0=3-x/2 to 0 or p+2 modulo 2p. Take the minimum (wait,p) lexicographically to get actual first event. In particular the first label need not be the least neighbor factor; the upper bound p<=4q+7 is uniform. For the shifted-prime family in (1), if the other neighbor has three prime factors then q<= (2R+1)^(1/3), so the first event occurs by O(R^(1/3)), before square-root scale. This is conditional on its having three factors, not a density statement.

Fix 0<alpha<theta (can be arbitrarily small) and any fixed 0<epsilon<2. Let J=floor(L^(2-epsilon)). Count initial z-rough non-twin centers whose first destination has some even center u=y-d, 0<=d<=J even, with qmin(u)>=R^alpha. Then ALL FOUR linear forms in v,
    v, pv-2s, pv-p+(1-s-d)-1, pv-p+(1-s-d)+1,              (3)
are free of odd prime factors below R^alpha. The first two are rough by the initial condition, the latter by the condition at u. For each p in [R^theta,O(sqrt R)], d<=J, and sign s, these forms have nonzero pairwise determinants, since p>J+4 eventually. Coefficients and constants are O(R^C) for absolute C. Let B_bad be product of their six absolute nonzero determinants and 2*3*5*p. It is a nonzero integer of size R^O(1). Every prime l not dividing B_bad has exactly four distinct forbidden residues for v. We may ignore all bad primes in an upper sieve.

Here is a uniform elementary four-form upper (same diagonal Selberg proof as in 0e9b47b but with dimension four and omitted bad primes). Put X=(2R+1)/p, cutoff D0=floor(min(X^(1/12),R^alpha/2)); log D0 >>_alpha L. Sieve by good primes l<=D0, density g(l)=4/l<1. Weights lambda_d with support d<=D0 have lambda_1=1 and main quadratic form 1/G(D0), G=sum squarefree d<=D0 prod_{l|d}4/(l-4). Mobius inversion of u_d=mu(d)H(d)/G as in the earlier two-form proof gives |lambda_d|<=prod(1-4/l)^-1<=d because l>=7. CRT remainder <=4^omega(lcm(d,e))<=de, total O(D0^6)<=O(X^.5).

For G lower, use only good primes l<=D0^eta with a sufficiently small fixed eta. Weighted independent inclusion probability 4/l gives expected log product <=C eta log D0 by elementary Chebyshev; at least half the Euler-product weight is on d<=D0. The unrestricted product for l>=7 is >>log^4 D0 by (1-4/l)^-1>=(1-1/l)^-4 and harmonic Euler product. Omitting the bad primes loses at most O((log L)^4): for bad primes <=L² use Mertens reciprocal bound and log(1-4/l)^-1=4/l+O(1/l²); for larger ones there are O(L) divisors of B_bad and reciprocal sum O(1/L). Thus G(D0)>>_alpha L^4/(log L)^4, and (3) has at most
    O_alpha((R/p) (log L)^4/L^4)
solutions in the relevant interval, uniformly in p,d,s. Summing p, elementary dyadic counting gives sum_{R^theta<=p<=C sqrt R}1/p=O_theta(1), and summing d costs O(J+1). Therefore the exceptional birth count is
    O_(alpha,theta,epsilon)(R L^(-2-epsilon)(log L)^4)
      = o(R/L²).                                         (4)
This counts actual first exits as a subset of allowed four-form tuples, not arbitrary relaxed edges asserted reachable. The d=0 version is O(R (log L)^4/L^4), so immediate re-entry to a polynomially rough center is exceptionally rare compared to (1)'s population size. No independence of adjacent factorizations is assumed.

## 4. Use the exclusion to control actual motion after the long quiet phase

Take the population from (1) at theta=.285 (or any fixed theta<2/7), and any fixed additional time exponent 0<beta<theta. Use alpha<beta in (4), for example beta/2. Apart from o(R/L²) exceptional non-twin births, every even u=y-d, 0<=d<=J even, has qmin(u)<R^alpha. As long as the actual post-first-event orbit has moved at most J from y, each successive phase has wait <=2 qmin(u)<2R^alpha from ANY current clock. Each event decreases the even center by at least two. Hence within at most
    2 R^alpha (floor(J/2)+1) = o(R^beta)
additional ticks it must move more than J. This proves an actual first-passage lower on subsequent gain, not just a statement about existence of a static rough successor:
    G_(w+floor(R^beta))(x) - G_w(x) > floor(L^(2-epsilon)).
For large R this time is o(R) and positions remain in valid range by canonical gain. This bound holds for all but o(R/L²) of the entire initially rough NON-TWIN population. In particular, unconditionally either the slab already contains >=c_theta R/L² twin centers (with c_theta chosen less than half the lower constant of (1)), or there are >>R/L² genuinely exiting prime-rough trajectories satisfying the first-passage bound. Under finite twins, the latter alternative necessarily holds for every large slab. Thus the longer initial quiet time cannot generally be prolonged by keeping the next cumulative displacement polylog-small; repeated exploration of actual trajectories beyond that first-exit neighborhood is necessary. This is an obstruction to that specific amplification mechanism, not a contradiction to low gain as a FRACTION of a square-root horizon: L^(2-epsilon) is far smaller than sqrt R. No gain upper at square-root time or strict witness (c0e45b0) is obtained.
