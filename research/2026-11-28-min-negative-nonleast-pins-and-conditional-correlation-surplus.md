## 3. The missing information in the preceding models: exact nonleast pin marginals

Let mu be the bare factor anatomy measure from base note cbb95b68. A source anatomy row of least exponent t is weighted by the joint kernel w(x,y)=1-F(x)F(y). In both prior models F=-1 for least in [.23,.4), F=1 for least>=.4, and F<=0 for all least<.23. The first model actually has F=-1 already above5/24 (below .4). We will show that the new nonleast pin lower (1) excludes both, and more generally EVERY rank-one kernel with these same sign/saturation conditions, regardless of the detailed F on the low interval and its tail normalization.

Consider the FIRST source box in §2, source least [.37495,.37505], so F(source)=-1. Target q exponent u∈[.23,.2302], target rough cutoff z=.185. The target cannot have least>=.4 because it has a factor of exponent u<.4. In the proposed kernel the target weight is w=1+F(target), and vanishes if target least>=.23. On the remaining low band r∈[z,.23), the weight is <=1 by the sign assumption. Since r<u, the least r and the designated pin u are distinct. Integrating the bare anatomy measure for the remaining factors (whose sum exponent is 1-u-r, and all are >=r), the exact unweighted target incidence density, conditional on r,u, is
    (du/u) (dr/r) omega((1-u-r)/r)/r.

Ordered factor integrals and the prime atom of the cofactor are included by Buchstab's identity. The density counts each designated q separately, just as (1) does; no assumption of distinct count equality is made. The diagonal r=u has no mass in this region; repeated same-exponent primes are a measure-zero boundary.

## 4. A concrete arithmetic alternative and a new sufficient subtarget

We can turn the same calculation into a useful actual inequality rather than just rejecting a model. Let the source row S and pin band Q be as in the first row, let K=J log(.2302/.23) (~3.7085327723e-7), and let I be the actual incidence count of (n,q), n in S, q in Q dividing m, P^-(m)>=R^.185. Split it into I_low and I_high according as P^-(m)<R^.23 or >=R^.23. In the high case the target least is <=R^.2302 by the pin. All high incidences are on true pairs with both least in [.23,.4) in exponent units. Let N_high be the count of distinct high pairs in this fixed source and pin family.

For all sufficiently large R, (1) gives I>=.632 K Pi, and I_high<=4 N_high. Therefore one of the following holds:
    (A) N_high >= .002 K Pi > 7e-10 Pi; or
    (B) I_low >= .624 K Pi.
This alternative is UNCONDITIONAL. In (B), the independent factor-anatomy product reference for I_low would be at most .614 K Pi by (2), so (B) is a definite positive excess of at least .010 K Pi relative to that reference. The reference is a mathematical integral, not an assumption about actual correlation. Thus failure of the desired high-square density forces an explicit correlation surplus for a fixed low-least / nonleast-high pin configuration on an exact prime-product row.

A sufficient OPEN subtarget (strictly weaker than the full desired high correction) is therefore:
    I_low <= .62 K Pi
on infinitely many growing slabs. This upper plus (1) forces a density-scale lower on N_high. In this subtarget the actual low target has two specified distinct prime divisors r=P^-(m) and q, with r exponent [.185,.23), q exponent [.23,.2302], and remaining quotient rough to r. This is a fixed 2-pin conditional UPPER problem, not the previous lower of a single least factor. Ordinary residual-level upper sieve on such double pins is far too large: even the ideal .625 total cap leaves at most .625-r-u <=.21; the plateau upper 2/(.625-r-u) integrated against dr/r is much larger than the .62 target. Thus it needs additional correlation input (e.g. actual quotient-factor structure or a stronger constrained dispersion/switching estimate), not a substitution of independent anatomy mass.

There is a related actual factor-count alternative at z=.197. By the third row of §2 the distinct count >=1e-9 Pi. If at least half have target least>=R^.23, the high square has a positive lower. Otherwise >=5e-10 Pi actual pairs have target least r∈[R^.197,R^.23) and contain q in Q. Then the target must be P3 or P4: a product of r,q only is far too small (r exponent+q exponent<=.4602), and q+4*.197>1 excludes P5 or more. Source is exact P2 at exponent~.375. In the P3 case the other factor is a prime of exponent 1-r_exp-q_exp up to o(1); in P4 the remaining two primes are both >=r and have combined exponent 1-r_exp-q_exp. Thus under scarcity of high square, a density-scale low branch has a very restricted factor anatomy. This can be used as input for a targeted fixed-shift P2/P3/P4 correlation or for first-event dynamics (the initial first candidate minimum is r; no HIGH-phase lower asserted). We have not obtained an upper below the required threshold for this branch.
