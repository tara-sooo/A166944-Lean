## 1. Three placements of a specified target factor

A source row consists of ordered prime factors of n, restricted to fixed exponent boxes and product slab, with least exponent at least a>0. The bare mass of a row in units R/L is J. Choose one unrestricted source factor at exponent t, or an unrestricted subproduct of source factors at exponent t, as the short coefficient N. The remaining subproduct is the long coefficient M. The current proofs use bounded multiplicity: for a fixed ordered factor box, group chosen indices into N, others into M; unique source factorization after excluding equal or same-bin factors. See §4 for SW of the composite short coefficient.

Specify a prime q dividing m, at exponent u in a narrow fixed interval. Require m/q to be rough to the UPPER endpoint of the q interval. This is a lower count for P^-(m)=q, with repeated q excluded if necessary, and gives distinct n across disjoint q intervals. In particular one cannot merely count q|m without the quotient roughness or count the same n once for each possible q. We use ordinary order-one well-factorable lower components for the quotient sieve, not upper-only support as a lower weight.

Pascadi, arXiv:2505.00653v2, Def4.3 and Prop4.4 (4.15), original HTML §4 https://arxiv.org/html/2505.00653v2#S4, gives arbitrary independently 1-bounded modulus coefficients at exponent capacities (x1,x2,x3) satisfying strict margins in
    x1<t,  2t+x2+2x3<1,  2t+5x2+2x3<2.
The original paper was reopened on 2026-11-28 including (4.15), (4.16), §5.1 Prop5.2 and §5.2 Prop5.5/Lemma5.6. The global product ceiling is 5/8; no claim of a higher level. Short coefficient needs full SW and rough support. No phase-restricted short prime is used here. Use the known unit-block convention Q_i=.5 when factor is the integer 1.

For a fixed t,u pair, there are THREE useful schemes. In the following limiting expressions leave strict positive slack before applying the theorem. Residual level D_res=R^d for the quotient; total modulus includes q.

(1) Ordinary two-factor scheme: x1=t, x2=min(1-2t,(2-2t)/5), x3=0. Put q into whichever of x1,x2 can hold it, and decompose the residual well-factorable component into the remaining capacities. Thus d=t+x2-u. This is the earlier two-factor placement, only feasible if capacities are nonnegative and at least one can hold q.

(2) Put the entire independent q indicator in Q2. Use Q1 and Q3 for the TWO ordinary lower-weight components. Capacities x1=t, x3=min((1-2t-u)/2,(2-2t-5u)/2); residual d=t+x3. The total modulus level exponent is min((1+u)/2,1-3u/2).

(3) Put the entire independent q indicator in Q3. Use Q1 and Q2 for the two ordinary lower-weight components. Capacities x1=t, x2=min(1-2t-2u,(2-2t-2u)/5); residual d=t+x2. Total modulus level
    u+d = min(1-t-u, (2+3(t+u))/5).
Thus choosing t+u near 3/8 is useful EVEN WHEN the source short exponent t itself is far from 3/8. This is the new placement. For example t≈.17, u≈.205 allows a total exponent near .625, where the ordinary two-factor choice for this same source has total exponent only ≈.502. The residual lower condition is d>2u. The globally possible target least exponent in this ordinary pinned lower scheme is strictly below 5/24 (not 5/16 or .4).

For source interval [t_l,t_r], pin interval [u_l,u_r], set slack s=.0001, x1=t_l-s. In (1) take x2=min(1-2t_r,(2-2t_r)/5)-s, and subtract u_r from the chosen capacity. In (2) take x3=min((1-2t_r-u_r)/2,(2-2t_r-5u_r)/2)-s, independent q in Q2 of exponent u_r. In (3) take x2=min(1-2t_r-2u_r,(2-2t_r-2u_r)/5)-s, independent q in Q3. These choices obey all three power conditions with strict slack. Choose the largest feasible residual d cell by cell. The actual code floors fractional capacities down to integer grid units and checks the original inequalities. A choice with d<=2u_r contributes zero (do not use a negative lower).

For each source/pin cell, sum over q in its interval BEFORE applying the ordinary lower weights at level R^d to the quotient multiset. These weights are independent of individual q; the two remaining factors can be split at their fixed capacities by ordinary well-factorability. The coefficient bounds after appending q in scheme1 have only a bounded polynomial-prime divisor multiplicity. For schemes2/3 the q factor itself is independent. Dyadic decomposition and short-relative-box localization as in prior bilateral notes preserve all strict margins; fixed finite exponent grid, then fixed log-relative box approximation and arbitrarily large log saving. Squarefree odd sifting masks on the residual two components use the previously proved common-divisor Mobius projection (small gcd via restricted coefficient theorem, tail by trivial progression counts). With q sharing a sieve factor the total modulus is divisible by q². Since q>=R^a and q<=R^.2083, actual weighted count of such terms is O(R^(1-a+o(1))+R^(.2083+o(1))) by square-divisor sum and divisor bounds. Reference error obeys the same power saving. Coprime reference correction for shared original polynomial source factors is negligible by summing reciprocal moduli containing one of the boundedly many factors. Therefore the quotient multiset has reference local density 1/(ell-1) for odd sieve primes, with mass J(R/L) sum_q 1/(q-1).

For 2<d/u_r<4 the lower coefficient in units Pi is at least
    J * log(u_r/u_l) * B(d,u_r),
    B(d,u)=2 log(d/u-1)/d.
This is the usual lower linear sieve coefficient with V(R^u)~2C2 exp(-gamma)/(u L). The lower weight is used only where the parameter is in the displayed range. Monotonicity of B in d there permits selecting the largest feasible residual cap. Requiring quotient rough to the upper endpoint ensures q is its genuine unique least divisor except q² (also removed); no unproved prime quotient is presumed.

## 4. Unrestricted composite short variables and bilateral bin matrix

For the actual .16 correction we can do substantially better than §3.

Allow ordered source P_k, 2<=k<=6, with every exponent >=.16, least <.4. No 7-factor source can occur at these scales for large R. The inner boxes in the certificate capture no P6 rows on the coarse grid; no P6 contribution is claimed. In addition to any one source factor as short N, allow any pair of unrestricted source primes as N, if the exponent capacities allow. The new short coefficient is the indicator of a product of two primes in its separate fixed factor intervals and a dyadic N interval. It is 1-bounded in a box with distinct ordered exponent bins (otherwise at worst bounded multiplicity and normalize). It is rough on the required subpower scale. It is SW in the sense of Pascadi Def4.3: for log-small modulus, fix one prime and apply SW to the other in its allowed interval (including the N range), reduced residue determined by invertible fixed prime; remove coprimality with auxiliary d at cost bounded by omega(d) times counts of the other factor, power-saving relative to N. Terms where fixed prime shares a modulus factor are excluded from both sides by the reduced-class/coprime condition. For q above a sufficiently large log power, trivial count of integers in a reduced residue in the N interval and N/phi(q) reference give arbitrary log saving; use phi(q)>>q/loglog(3q), and when q>N the O(1) term is absorbed. This works for all auxiliary d with the allowed tau(d)^{O(1)} loss. Thus no claim that an arbitrary rough coefficient is SW. The long coefficient is bounded and needs no SW. Choose only unrestricted groups: no fixed mod4 restriction inserted into N. Source factor cell partitions are disjoint before regrouping; choosing a different grouping for another q cell does not duplicate n because q is its least target prime.

Use a common least-exponent grid [a,b) of width .002, a=.16, b=.4. The target q bins stop at Q=.208. For actual pairs classified only by the two least bins, not the source factor count, we have lower M_ij in the direct orientation and M_ji by reversing the shifted equation. The reversed theorem uses shift -2 instead of +2 and the other slab; both are allowed by the same distribution theorem and have identical limiting coefficients. No actual equality or statistical symmetry of finite populations assumed. Therefore take MAX rather than sum on a given ordered bin pair, since both lower counts can overlap. For i>=Q, j<Q, the reverse orientation lies in a DISJOINT ordered bin pair: one side is above Q, the other below. For i,j<Q use the larger of the two directional lower coefficients; for i=j use only the one. Hence a rigorous bilateral lower coefficient for the union of disjoint ordered bin pairs (at least one least factor below R^.208) is
    2 sum_{i>=Q,j<Q} M_ij
    + sum_{i<Q} M_ii

The latter terms are not assuming independence and do not add duplicate incidences. All endpoint exponent strips can be assigned half-open intervals. ## 6. Asymmetric high-rough / prime-P2 supply

A second use of factor anatomy leads to new actual starting populations. Define S_c(n)=1{Omega(n)<=2, P^-(n)>=4R^c}. For b>=.4 (b=.5 means primes in our slab), C_b is exactly prime or P2 with the high cutoff, and
    C_b(n) S_c(m)
       = C_b(n) U_a(m) - C_b(n)[U_a(m)-S_c(m)]
for a<c<b. This is an exact asymmetric exchange. In the positive count enumerate C_b: prime row and semiprime rows t>=b (none at b=.5). Use lower coefficient
    P_b(a)=B(d0,a)+integral_b^.5 B(max(d0,d2(t)),a) dt/[t(1-t)]
where d0=4/7-.0001 (small additional grid slack), d2(t)=min((2+3t)/5,1-t) minus strict slack. For the subtracted count enumerate rejected opposite source, whose bare mass is M(a)-M2(c), M2(c)=1+log((1-c)/c). It includes all P3+ rows above a, including those with least>=c, since S_c specifically excludes them. Upper-sieve C_b side at a smaller cutoff on 2/dU plateau (dU=.5999); rejected least t<=1/3 for P3+ and <c<1/3 for semiprimes, so Prop5.2 applies. For P3 rows improve upper via any unrestricted factor with better d2, integrating positive saving exactly as in preceding note over t>=a to1/3, not just t<c. P4+ use baseline. Thus
    coefficient >= P_b(a) - (2/dU)[M(a)-M2(c)] + J3(a,1/3).
