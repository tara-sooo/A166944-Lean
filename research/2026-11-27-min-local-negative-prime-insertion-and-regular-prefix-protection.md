# Negative MIN: prime insertion, regular collision moments, and protected growing medium prefixes

2026-11-27 JST. Continuation of [2fdbf53](https://github.com/tara-sooo/A166944-Lean/commit/2fdbf5300bd13ae7fff6e615b91cd94b6006e1de). The requested next step was to attack the restricted periodic terminal moment and then use it in actual unprotected merger intervals. This note proves an exact prime-insertion identity for short fibers, a full signed defect recurrence, a polylogarithmic collision bound and gain-tail bound for a recursively selected subensemble, and a growing-event actual prefix protection theorem. It also proves a uniform FULL collision moment for sufficiently separated prime alphabets and transfers that theorem to the previous protected anchor loss sieve. The full contiguous-alphabet moment and sufficiently long live time are still open.

## 1. Periodic convention and exact first-interruption law

For a finite alphabet S of odd primes including 3, let Q=2 product_(p in S) p, F=2Q. The restricted negative MIN rule uses the actual earliest eligible firing, least prime at ties, but only labels in S. A state is (x,n), x even, height h=n+x/2. For e=-1, p|x-e=x+1, firing n=0 mod 2p; for e=+1, p|x-e=x-1, firing n=p+2 mod 2p. A completed p decreases x by p-1, with full wait <=2p<=3(p-1). Starting at nonnegative height preserves it. This follows from the postheight congruence 0 or3 mod p and the fact that the one-step negative change cannot reach a negative integer of that residue. The common-multiple cell wall confines every restricted path to its initial width-Q cell. The total gain G<Q, total completed time <=3G. Termination follows by strictly decreasing even positions. The terminal map is translation-equivariant under (x,n)->(x+F,n-F/2); in particular h is unchanged and every clock residue is preserved.

Use singular starts v divisible by4 in [0,F), h in {0,3}, n=h-v/2. There are F/2 starts. Let y_S(v,h), G_S(v,h), J_S(v,h), H_S(v,h) denote terminal position, gain, event count and terminal height (H_S here is per-root terminal height, not the uniform gain bound of section 7). For any periodic subset of these starts put m(y)=its terminal mass, K=(1/F)sum m(y)^2. For the full subset this is K_z of 2fdbf53 when S consists of primes<=z.

Add a new prime p>max S. The new period is pF. Lift each old start to v+jF, h unchanged, j=0,...,p-1. Its old restricted path translates exactly. Before the first p-event in the new rule, the new path agrees with the old one. At any nonterminal old state (x_i,n_i), the possible p eligibility signs pin at most two lift indices j modulo p. Each such lift fixes n_i-jF/2 mod 2p; compute the positive wait of p. The first deviation is at the earliest old state at which this wait is STRICTLY smaller than the old winning wait (p loses ties), or at the old terminal if p divides one of its neighbors and no earlier deviation occurred. This is an exact finite lifting algorithm, not an independence assumption.

For each old root, at most 2 J_S lifts first deviate before the old terminal, and at most two lifts first use p at the old terminal. A deviation before the old terminal implies
    p-1 <= 2(h+elapsed) <= 6+6G_S,
using nonnegative postheight and old completed time<=3G_S (the new p clock is before completion of its old competing event). Thus the exact probability of early interruption is bounded by
    Pr(early p) <= (2/p) E[J_S 1_(6G_S+7>=p)].
If p>6G_S+7 there are no early deviations and exactly two terminal-p lifts, all other p-2 lifts unchanged. This separates genuine interruption of the old path from adding an event after its terminal.

## 2. Exact Euler identity for truncated collision moments

Fix D>=0 and p>6D+7. A new path of gain<=D cannot use p, because p-1>D. Hence it must be an unchanged old path of gain<=D. Conversely every old short start has exactly p-2 unchanged lifts, by section1. For an old short terminal fiber y, the two removed lift indices are determined by y+jF=+1 or -1 mod p, common to EVERY short root in the fiber. Consequently its short mass is preserved intact in each of the other p-2 lifts.

Write s_S(D)=Pr(G_S<=D) and K_S(D)=(1/F)sum_y m_(G<=D)(y)^2 for the full old ensemble. Exact identities:
    s_(S+p)(D)=(1-2/p) s_S(D),
    K_(S+p)(D)=(1-2/p) K_S(D).                       (1)
They also hold for an arbitrary specified subset of old short roots lifted in the same way (before imposing additional selection). For the full contiguous alphabet, w>=6D+7, z>=w,
    s_z(D)=s_w(D) product_(w<p<=z)(1-2/p),
    K_z(D)=K_w(D) product_(w<p<=z)(1-2/p).
This controls the short part EXACTLY and shows why it is insufficient for the full moment. By Mertens, for fixed 0<theta<1, D=floor z^theta and w=ceil(6D+7), s_z(D)<=theta^2+o(1). Thus a positive fraction of full paths have gain>z^theta. More strongly choose D=floor z/16 so w<z/2 eventually; the prime number theorem in [z/2,z] gives 1-product_(w<p<=z)(1-2/p)>>1/log z. Hence E G_z>>z/log z. A full polylogarithmic bound on mean gain is impossible, even though the desired polynomial bound on K_z is not precluded. Neither implication uses a lower sieve in the actual R slab.

## 3. Regular insertion transport and the full signed defect

Let B be an integer 0<=B<p-1, B<=F/2. Call an affected lift REGULAR if its first p-event is at its old terminal y, its old terminal height H_S <= (p-3)/2, and the restricted old continuation from the post-p state has gain<=B. For p>=5, a second p-event requires intervening even gain>=p-1: after p the postposition is c=0 or2 mod p, and the next source must be ±1 mod p. The first such nonnegative even displacement is at least p-1. Thus the old continuation of gain<=B cannot be interrupted by another p. Its terminal is exactly the new terminal of the regular root.

For a fixed actual old terminal y and sign e of p, the first post-p height is fixed if H_S <=(p-3)/2: it is in {0,p} on the even sign, or {3,p+3} on the odd sign. Indeed its residue mod 2p is determined by y and the firing congruence, and h_new=H_S+w-(p-1)/2 is nonnegative and <2p. Therefore all regular roots from one old base terminal fiber and sign use the same lifted y and same new target t_(y,e). The moved mass s_(y,e)<=m(y). There are at most two such lifted sources per base y.

Replicate old mass m across p copies. Let A(t) be arrivals of regular moved roots. Because each regular move has displacement p-1+E, 0<=E<=B<p-1, any new terminal t receives regular mass from at most two source tuples (one possible y for each sign mod p). Modulo F, each target residue can receive at most 2(floor(B/2)+1) source/sign tuples, because y=t+p-1+E mod F and E is even. Put C_B=floor(B/2)+1. Then
    sum_(y,e) s_(y,e)^2 <=2 sum_y m(y)^2,
    sum_t A(t)^2 <=4 sum_y m(y)^2,
    sum_t m(t mod F) A(t)
         <= 2 sqrt(C_B) sum_y m(y)^2                     (2)
by Cauchy-Schwarz and the target-residue multiplicity. Ignoring outgoing mass is valid for an upper bound, so either keeping only unaffected and regular roots, OR transferring regular roots and leaving every other affected root at its old terminal, has collision moment at most
    K_old [1+(4 sqrt(C_B)+4)/p].                        (3)

For B=0 there is a sharper bound. Target is y-p+1, so distinct actual sources have distinct targets, and every outgoing source is p-eligible whereas every target is p-ineligible. Subtract the moved mass s at each source, add it at the unique target. The squared mass increment is exactly 2s(m(target)-m(source)+s) per source, <=2s m(target) since s<=m(source). By translation and Cauchy-Schwarz the sum of s m(target) over at most two lifts per base y is <=2 sum m(y)^2. Thus the bound becomes
    K_regular <= (1+4/p) K_old.                        (4)
Deleting exceptional roots can only decrease the nonnegative masses; (4) applies also to the recursively selected subset below.

For the FULL old ensemble define m_reg on the new period by moving only regular roots and leaving all others at their old terminal. Let d_exc(t) be the signed arrival minus departure counts of all EXCEPTIONAL affected roots, moved from their old terminal to their actual new one. Then m_new=m_reg+d_exc, sum d_exc=0, and the exact signed recurrence is
    K_new=K_reg+E_p,
    E_p=(1/(pF)) sum_t [2 m_reg(t) d_exc(t)+d_exc(t)^2].   (5)
This identifies a sharper actual target for the full moment than the mass of all exceptional roots. With B=floor(log p)^2 capped below p-1 and F/2, (3),(5) would give K_z=O(z^C) if
    E_p <= C_1 K_old log p / p
uniformly for consecutive prime additions (or suitable summable integrated versions), since sum_(p<=z) log p/p=O(log z). This is conditional, not established. With B=0 a bound E_p<=C_1 K_old/p would even give polylog growth. A crude absolute-mass bound on exceptional transfers is insufficient: one can bound exceptional fraction by early deviations, (2/p)Pr(H_S>(p-3)/2), and (1/(pN_old))sum_(y,e) m(y)1_(old post-p tail gain>B), N_old=F/2. The last term uses the unique low-entry-height poststate, discarding candidates with high entry height. But bounding their collision moment by exceptional mass times the common-multiple cell size returns the exponential scale. The observed signed cancellation in section10 does not supply an asymptotic inequality. Full contiguous-alphabet K_z remains open.

## 4. Unconditional polylog moment for a regular selected subensemble

Start with all singular states at S={3}. At each consecutive prime insertion p>=5 keep only lifts of previously kept states which either are unaffected or are regular with B=0. All choices refer to the exact restricted paths, so the terminal map of a kept start is the TRUE full restricted map, not a modified algorithm. Every initially rough start at the final cutoff survives all stages. Denote the retained subset by C_z, its unnormalized moment by K_z^C. Iterating (4) and Mertens yields
    K_z^C << (log z)^4,                                 (6)
while Pr(C_z)>=product_(3<=p<=z)(1-2/p)>>1/log^2 z. The conditional size-biased mean fiber is 2 K_z^C/Pr(C_z), not 2 K_z^C. This is a bound for a specified subset, not for all singular starts.

There is also a useful tail estimate, independent of (6). A kept lift either has the old gain or adds p-1 and one event, and at most two of p lifts can append. For every nondecreasing nonnegative function f, the average of f(G_new) over kept lifts (unnormalized by retention) is at most (1-2/p) f(G_old)+(2/p) f(G_old+p-1), for each retained old start. Iteration stochastically dominates the unnormalized selected gain by the sum of independent Bernoulli increments with probabilities 2/p, plus the base bound 4. This is a domination inequality, not independence of actual insertion choices. In particular
    E[1_C exp(lambda G)] <= exp(4lambda) product_(5<=p<=z)(1+(2/p)(exp(lambda(p-1))-1)).
Taking lambda=(loglog z)/(2z), use exp(lambda(p-1))-1<=lambda p exp(lambda z) and pi(z)<<z/log z. The logarithm of the product is O(loglog z/sqrt(log z))=o(1). Thus
    Pr(C_z and G>10z) << (log z)^(-5).
For event count J, the base bound is 2, and
    E[1_C exp(J)] <= exp(2) product_(5<=p<=z)(1+(2/p)(exp(1)-1))
       << (log z)^(2(exp(1)-1)).
It follows that Pr(C_z and J>10 loglog z)=o(log^-2 z). In addition every kept path has deterministic gain <=4+sum_(5<=p<=z)(p-1) and labels appended in increasing order, so no new repeated label after the base. These estimates hold for all z and will be used in actual finite-slab arithmetic below.

## 5. Transfer selected seeds to actual canonical MIN, remove early interruptions

Put L=log R, N=#A in [R,2R], 6|A, N~R/6. Use the previous synchronized T'=T or floor(T exp(-L^delta0)), T=ceil sqrt(2R+1), windows of width6 floor(T'/6), latest birth n0, retirement n0+T'. Actual negative MIN roots have birth n_A=3-A/2. Choose z=floor(c_z L) with fixed c_z>0 sufficiently small that F_z<=R^(1/10) for all large R (Chebyshev for log primorial suffices). Restrict to A divisible by12, so their birth height3 and position modulo F_z are among the singular starts. Use the exact subset C_z of section4, and retain only starts with
    G_z<=D=10z, J_z<=10 loglog z,
    full selected terminal mass m_C(y)<=M0=ceil((log z)^7).
Rounding the real thresholds down is harmless. There is an unconditional supply of >>R/log^2 z such starts: the initially rough A divisible12 have density (relative to canonical roots) (1/2)product_(5<=p<=z)(1-2/p)>>log^-2 z; all belong to C_z. The tail exclusions from section4 lose o(log^-2 z) of the full singular starts, and the fiber exclusion loses at most 2K_z^C/M0=O(log^-3 z). Transfer periodic subsets to the slab with remainder O(F_z/R) in root fractions. No random arithmetic assumptions are used; this is a full CRT period of length much smaller than R. Since Q_z is divisible6 and the map is confined in each Q_z cell, at any actual terminal y there are at most M0 selected canonical starts from its full singular fiber, even across period edges.

The actual unrestricted rule may use a label >z before the restricted terminal. But if this occurs for one of the selected starts, the first such label p satisfies p<=6D+7 by the height bound in section1. For each selected base start v mod F_z and its at most J_z old states before the terminal, the condition p divides a neighbor pins at most two lifts modulo p. Since p>z, p is coprime to F_z. Over lifts within [R,2R] count at most O(J_z sum_(z<p<=6D+7)(R/(F_z p)+1)). Relative to the O(R/F_z) lifts of that base start, this is
    O(loglog z / log z)+O((F_z/R) z loglog z)=o(1),
using the elementary upper prime count in a fixed-ratio band. The estimate is uniform in the selected residue; summing over them loses only o(1) of the selected supply. Discard these early-interruption roots (this uses the actual past/future prefix as a selection, no causal change to the MIN rule). Every remaining seed follows its entire restricted path in the full actual dynamics, arrives at the restricted terminal by own age<=3D, and has terminal height O(D). This is a genuine collection of actual trajectories, not hypothetical restricted moves.

Before reaching that terminal, all selected seeds can be protected with a dedicated cap M0 at each physical state, even in the presence of other seeds at different clocks. If two actual selected prefixes meet at the same position and clock, their restricted continuations are identical, hence they have the same restricted terminal and belong to a static light fiber. Therefore at most M0 can be present at that state. All stage-one costs in the weighted live criterion of 5a22180 are negligible: its maximum per-event increment epsilon_R is superpolynomially small in L, and the selected event count is O(loglog z). This initial protection reaches a cutoff z~log R (rather than loglog R), but does NOT guarantee a long residence at its terminal in the full unrestricted rule.

## 6. Growing medium-prefix protection by a reverse-word second moment

We now use the above seeds beyond their restricted terminal, not merely at the first outside jump. Choose 0<gamma<1, b>0, U=exp(L^gamma). Let w=floor(c_w loglog L) with sufficiently small fixed c_w>0 so that Q_w=2 product_(3<=p<=w)p <= (log L)^b eventually. This is a smaller relay alphabet than z. Put
    k=floor(kappa0 log L / loglog L),  kappa0>0 fixed small,
    rho=2 kappa0(b+2).
Use the full actual continuation from the seed's restricted terminal y until k completed events of labels >w, OR until the first state whose NEXT label is >U, whichever comes first. All labels <=w in between are allowed, not discarded or assumed rare. Each maximal <=w run has gain <Q_w by the primorial wall, even if interrupted before its restricted terminal. At y no label<=z is eligible, so the first event, if it completes in this prefix, is >z. Every visited center through the stopping state lies in I0=[R-D-k(U+Q_w)-Q_w-O(1),2R], of length O(R). The total gain and age through this prefix are O(D+k(U+Q_w)); the number of events is at most O(loglog z+k+(k+1)Q_w). These bounds also hold when the next label is >U (it has NOT fired).

For any visited x in stage two, reverse its j<=k completed >w events. For each such event specify prime p_i in (w,U], its postposition sign c_i in {0,2} (even/odd firing), and the cumulative <=w gain E_i following it until the next high source or the observation x, 0<=E_i<Q_w, E_i even. There is no low prefix before the first >w event at y. If x is the observation position, the ith postposition is
    v_i=x+E_i+sum_(t>i)(p_t-1+E_t).
Necessarily v_i=c_i mod p_i. For a fixed word this pins one residue of x modulo every distinct prime; repeats must be consistent, not multiplied as independent primes. Moreover y=x+sum_i(p_i-1+E_i) is determined. At most M0 selected actual seeds belong to that initial terminal y. Thus if W_k(x) counts all compatible reverse words of lengths 0..k on these labels and offsets, the TOTAL number m(x) of stage-two seed histories visiting x (over all clocks, each root once) satisfies
    m(x)<=M0 W_k(x).                                    (7)
No departure-phase ambiguity or clock independence is required.

Count the second moment by pairs of words. For each compatible pair, union the pins; if their distinct-prime product is d, there are O(R/d+1) possible even x in I0. Decorate each of <=2k slots by a fresh prime or a repeat of an earlier slot, at most (2k+1)^(2k) patterns. Each fresh slot is summed with reciprocal prime weight, and sum_(w<p<=U)1/p=O(log L). Signs and offsets cost at most 2(Q_w+1) per slot. Therefore the main sum is at most
    O(R)(k+1)^2 [C(Q_w+1)(2k+1)(1+log L)]^(2k).
The +1 remainder is bounded by the unweighted total pair count
    (k+1)^2 [C(Q_w+1) U]^(2k)=R^o(1),
because gamma<1 and k=O(log L/loglog L). This holds even when repeated constraints are incompatible; dropping them only enlarges the bound. Since log Q_w<=b loglog L, log k=(1+o(1))loglog L, and M0=L^o(1), (7) proves
    (1/N) sum_x m(x)^2 <= L^(rho+o(1)).                  (8)
This is an actual unconditional second moment of cumulative spatial visits of the chosen roots, not an entropy estimate for the full ensemble and not a count of arbitrary unbounded words.

Now show that the >U stopping alternative is rare. At any stage-two state the full gain from birth is G<=Gmax=D+(k+1)(U+Q_w), and height<=3+(5/2)G. If its next winning p>U but p<=20(Gmax+1), x has a neighbor divisible by a prime between U and O(kU+D), so the count of such possible x is
    O(R (1+log k)/log U + kU).
This follows by summing divisibility counts O(R/p+1) and the dyadic harmonic prime upper bound. If p>20(Gmax+1), nonnegative postheight requires wait>(p/4), while wait<=2q(x) in the full actual rule; hence q(x)>=p/8>U/8. The ordinary two-neighbor upper sieve gives O(R/(log U)^2) such x in I0. One can use the earlier explicit Selberg weights on x±1, level U^c, small fixed c; error O(U^(6c))=R^o(1), and reciprocal main product >>log^2 U. The same count includes any true twin centers. Thus the union of all possible >U stopping sites has size O(R L^(-gamma+o(1))). Cauchy-Schwarz and (8) show
    #seeds stopped by next label>U /N <= L^((rho-gamma)/2+o(1)).   (9)
Choose rho<gamma. Because selected supply is >>1/(log L)^2, the loss in (9) is negligible relative to that supply. Every remaining seed completes k outside events: a low-only infinite run is impossible by the Q_w wall, and every positive actual center has some next prime-factor firing. Through these prefixes x remains positive of order R.

For actual cap protection, choose M=L^beta as in previous work, M_o=floor(M/2) and reserve M0<<M/2 slots for stage one. Declare x heavy if m(x)>M_o. Discard seeds whose stage-two prefix visits a heavy x, including the stopping state. The fraction lost is at most
    (1/N)sum_(m(x)>M_o) m(x) <= (1/(N M_o))sum m(x)^2
       <= L^(rho-beta+o(1)).                            (10)
Choose rho<beta as well. All remaining histories can be retained in stage two with cap M_o simultaneously, even ignoring clocks, since cumulative mass m(x)<=M_o at every visited site. Stage-one cap M0 proved in section5 handles their initial prefixes. Total cap <=M. This is an allowed deterministic lookahead/selection policy for the arbitrary-policy live inequality (it discards whole histories using their prefix; it does not change actual trajectories). Since the number of events through the prefix is only polylog in L, total cost is o(L^-C0) for every fixed C0 under the additive cost of 5a22180. In particular no budget deaths need occur through these prefixes.

We have proved: for any fixed beta>0, gamma in (0,1), b>0, kappa0 with 2 kappa0(b+2)<min(beta,gamma), there are >>R/(loglog R)^2 actual canonical roots which can ALL be protected simultaneously with cap L^beta and negligible cost through k~kappa0 loglog R/logloglog R completed events of labels >w after their full initial <=z terminal, all intermediate labels <=U, with arbitrarily many intervening <=w events subject only to the automatic walls. This proves genuine cap-loss control on a growing portion of the formerly unprotected history. It is not a mere numerical test.

## 7. Why this does not yet close the live criterion; further attempted extension

For the earlier proof parameters beta=2.1, sigma=.25, one may choose gamma=.5,b=1,kappa0=.06, so rho=.36<.5 and beta. The weighted live budget is safe. However these protected prefixes have own age AT MOST O(kU+D)=R^o(1), and the deterministic lower age is only at least (z-1+(k-1)(w-1))/2-3 from nonnegative height. The earlier required live fraction is of order L^-sigma across T'=R^(1/2-o(1)) synchronized ticks. These initial prefixes can even finish before the common observation start n0. They cannot supply that live lower bound. No implication from the count of k events to macroscopic lifetime is made.

An attempted iteration by the same reverse counting would replace k by the total number of outside events. The right side of (8) grows as exp(O(k log(k(Q_w+1)log L))). To maintain a fixed power of L one needs k=O(log L/loglog L). Even the +1 CRT remainder ceases to be small at sufficiently large k. Splitting into blocks without a new control on the distribution of mass and clocks entering each block does not remove this accumulated branching. Thus the simple prefix argument cannot be repeated to reach the required age; an arithmetic estimate for LONG actual histories with many repeated primes or contraction of the inherited mass distribution is still necessary. This is the explicit remaining barrier after applying (6) to actual trajectories.

## 8. Full moment theorem for separated prime alphabets

The signed defect of section3 can be removed UNCONDITIONALLY for a different full alphabet. Let S0 be any finite base prime alphabet including3; let H0>=2 bound the gain of every relaxed spatial negative path using S0, regardless of starting clock. One can always take H0=Q0=2 product_(p in S0)p by the common-multiple wall. Add primes p1,p2,... all above S0 such that
    p1>=8H0+12,  p_(i+1)>=16 p_i.
Define recursively H_i=2H_(i-1)+p_i-1. Between consecutive uses of p_i the intervening smaller-label relaxed path would have gain>=p_i-1 by the repeat rule in section3, but its maximum is H_(i-1)<p_i-1. Thus p_i occurs at most once in any relaxed path using S_i, and the two old-alphabet blocks have total gain<=2H_(i-1). This proves the bound H_i for ALL clocks and even for relaxed choice. Inductively H_i<=1.25 p_i and p_(i+1)>=8H_i+12 for all sufficiently large p_i (the displayed initial lower bound is already ample).

For singular starts, old completed time<=3H_(i-1), so p_i cannot preempt before the old terminal (p_i>6H_(i-1)+7). Old terminal height<=3+2.5H_(i-1)<=(p_i-3)/2. After the p_i event, the old continuation has gain<=H_(i-1)<p_i-1, so it cannot repeat p_i. Thus every affected lift is REGULAR for B=min(H_(i-1),Q_(i-1)), and there are exactly two affected lifts per old root. There is NO exceptional signed defect E_p. Applying (3),
    K_(S_i) <= K_(S_(i-1)) [1+C sqrt(H_(i-1)+1)/p_i].
Since H_(i-1)=O(p_(i-1)), and p_i grows geometrically, the product of these factors is bounded by a constant independent of i and of the chosen successive prime magnitudes. Therefore
    sup_i K_(S_i) << K_(S0).                              (11)
This is a bound on the FULL singular-start ensemble, with NO selection or small-tail hypothesis, for this class of sparse alphabets. The period may grow very quickly, but the gain only O(p_max). No inference that the same bound holds for the dense contiguous alphabet; the measured exceptional defect for consecutive additions is nonzero.

## 9. Apply (11) to actual protected rough-anchor mergers

Use the two-tier protected loss proof of 2fdbf53, with P=T'/L^a near-square rough anchors, high roughness q(source)>=p^d/2, cap M=L^beta, separate old and young capacity. Instead of requiring all following completed labels <=z0, protect an anchor while ALL following labels belong to a specified sparse alphabet S. Include a contiguous base of primes <=w_b with w_b~b0 log L, Q_b=L^(b0+o(1)), and separated additions as section8, largest added prime at most Z=exp(L^nu), with
    0<nu<min(1/2,alpha,gamma0).
Here alpha and gamma0 refer to the weighted cost and lower high successor cutoff Y0=exp(L^gamma0) of 5a22180. Bertrand's postulate permits many such additions, though the proof holds for any fixed chosen list obeying separation. The full period Q_S has log Q_S=O(log Q_b+(log Z)^2)=o(L) since the added primes are geometrically separated. Every S-only relay has gain<=H_S=O(Q_b+Z)=R^o(1), while (11) and the earlier Fourier gap moment give K_S<<K_base<=L^(b0+o(1)). Thus very large selected relay labels need NOT enlarge the singular moment exponentially.

Reapply the prior proof with period F_S=2Q_S and B=H_S. For close nonsingular pairs the height-gap and spatial count remains R^((1+d)/2+o(1)) because B=R^o(1). For singular pairs, actual S-only coalescence implies equality of the two restricted S-terminal positions. Allowed singular residue pairs number F_S K_S; as before a fixed allowed pair fixes the actual relative displacement since the two anchors belong to the same common-multiple cell and hence the same F_S period. In the hyperbola sieve omit primes dividing F_S (not all primes <=Z), the finitely small primes and discriminant divisors. The base omissions cost at most (log L)^C, and sum reciprocals of separated added primes is bounded. Uniform residue count errors F_S R^(3/8+8c+o(1)) remain power-saving since F_S=R^o(1). Thus the earlier singular bound is O(R(1+K_S)L^-6+o(1)). The equal shifted-pin case is counted separately without a residue loss as in 2fdbf53. For long residents, q(y)>=P^d/2>Z, so the incoming S-only relay equals the exact restricted S-terminal. Fix source prime, sign, height and residue mod F_S; the terminal displacement is fixed, the progression sieve length R/(p F_S) is still of power size, and summing residues cancels F_S. This gives the same long-key count O(R L^(a-5+o(1))). Hence the two-tier policy has
    protected cap deaths/N << L^(beta+b0-6+o(1))
                               +L^(beta+a-5+o(1))
