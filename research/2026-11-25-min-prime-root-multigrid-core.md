## 1. Setup and all-scale precontact assignment

Keep the original dyadic X, B=ceil(sqrt(7X)), u=log B, ell=1+log u, N=Theta(X/u) prime roots M in [X,2X), T=floor(X/u^.25), H=T+B, F=T²Bu, m_g=u^4.75/ell^4. Original paths are gain-stopped and include actual terminal vertices; every completed gain is p-1<=B, total <H, actual wait <=2r<=3(p-1). Use the unchanged original medium directed allocation ledger and the frozen natural high reference, with all previous linear and absolute masks. For each unordered pair M<M' write w_high for its actual remaining ordered high energy and w_med for the sum of its two actual remaining directed medium charges. These are nonnegative. For the whole pair there are uniform upper bounds
    U_high=3H, U_med=C_med H/m_g,
where the latter follows from the inherited endpoint bound and disjoint actual focal attached blocks, in both directions. Take C_med a sufficiently large absolute constant. Directional rank masks pay just the admitting direction. Subsequent graph peeling may pay the sum of both directions using U_med. For finite diagnostic capacity choices one may use H/m_g as a unit with independent numerical thresholds; no finite claim that C_med=1.

Let d_j>=1 be a finite list of distinct integer grid spacings <=H, ordered from coarse to fine. Allow a growing number of spacings, choose positive theta_j with sum O(1), e.g. theta_j=(j+1)^(-2) for zero-based j. Include 1 as the final spacing, for example d_0=floor(H/u²), floor(d_0/4), floor(d_0/16), 1 with duplicates removed. At first common projected horizon C* assign each pair to its FIRST eligible grid j:
    x_j=d_j floor((C*-1)/d_j),  x_j>=2M'-2.
The grid 1 has x=C*-1 and is eligible exactly when C*>2M'-2. If none eligible, C*=2M'-2 and the pair has a ROOT CONTACT at the larger source. Do not invent a preceding crossing for that root. This is an exhaustive partition of first contacts. The assignments use original full histories, not remaining weights or the outcome of a quota flow. In the implementation the assignments are evaluated only for queried pairs; every reference crossing cohort uses the full original paths.

At each eligible x_j both original paths have unique completed crossing edges A<=x_j<D=A+p-1<=C*. Their pre-horizons differ; if the two post-horizons agree they equal C*. For each p there are at most two projected crossing slots with D even and D=0,-2 mod 2p in (x_j,x_j+p-1]. If both slots occur, their separation is 2. Same p for the two roots therefore means opposite slots with posttime lag >=p-2. These are the previous arithmetic restrictions, now at the selected finer grid when the coarse grid is ineligible.

Let V_(j,x) be the full original crossing list. For each grid j,
    sum_x |V_(j,x)| <= N(1+ceil(H/d_j)).
For medium split by its existing fixed source cells, with no increase in the sum of memberships. All reference cohorts and rank orders are on these original lists, including roots omitted by all later masks.

## 3. Actual weighted cores, independent of reference population and flow outcome

After any prior absolute masks and a feasible quota allocation, let w_e be the remaining UNPAID weight of each unordered source pair e, separately for high and medium. It can be fractional, and each pair occurs only once for the chosen metric. For medium aggregate the two unpaid orientations while keeping their amounts separately for subsequent updates. The following theorem works for any nonnegative input weights bounded by U_metric, including arbitrary earlier quota fractions. It does not require that the weights come from an optimal flow.

For a family of constraints v, each incident with a specified set of pair edges e, fix a capacity b_v>=0. The degree of v on the current support is the sum of its incident edge weights, not a count of reference roots. Whenever a constraint has positive current degree <=b_v, remove every remaining incident pair edge, charging the removed weight to that constraint. A zero-degree constraint may also be discarded. Once processed it never acquires a positive degree again. Every removed pair is charged exactly once, and the total charge is <=sum_v b_v. At termination, every surviving pair has degree >b_v at every incident constraint.

The terminal support is the unique greatest admissible subset of the input edges: any subset with all its incident degrees > their capacities survives every peeling step, since its degrees are bounded above by the current degrees. Thus different removal orders or simultaneous-round deletion have the same support. This is a deterministic weighted core theorem for overlapping constraint families, not just a graph with one degree condition per vertex. It applies to fractional weights even when b_v<U_metric. It is not valid to replace actual unpaid degrees by the entire original cohort masses in the stopping condition.

Use the following four constraint families (a root-contact pair uses the first and second; a crossing pair uses the first, third, fourth).
- Global source: a vertex for every original root M, incident to all remaining pair edges containing M, capacity U_metric L_metric. Sum <=N U_metric L_metric. Since each pair weight<=U_metric, every surviving active original root has strictly more than L_metric distinct actual positive-weight partners globally. This applies even to those with first contact at a root horizon.
- Root contact: one vertex for each larger source M' at its own initial horizon, incident only to the root-contact pairs with that larger source; capacity U_metric L_metric. Sum <=N U_metric L_metric. If a root-contact edge survives, that larger root has strictly more than L_metric distinct ACTUAL earlier sources with first contact at its root. This restriction is stronger than the previous arbitrary source count at the initial horizon, and the initial prime property is respected.
- Cut-source: for each assigned grid j,x and each root M in the full crossing list (or medium source cell), incident only to pairs assigned to this cut containing M. Capacity b_(j)=U_metric L_metric theta_j d_j/H. Sum over memberships <=O(N U_metric L_metric).
- Opposite cohort: at the same cut and source-cell restriction, for each focal root M and each distinct occupied partner projected crossing edge alpha, constrain the pairs incident to M whose other source crosses alpha. Let a_(j,x) count occupied projected crossing edge cohorts in the full relevant list. Capacity b_(j)/a_(j,x) (only if nonempty). There are at most a_(j,x) constraints for any one membership. Thus the sum of these capacities is <=sum_(j,x) b_(j)|V_(j,x)|=O(N U_metric L_metric). No extra factor for the total number of possible pairs of cohorts, for their masses, or for the number of grids. Each crossing pair belongs to exactly two opposite-cohort constraints, one for each end, and to two cut-source and two global constraints.

The simultaneous peeling cost for all four families together is
    O(N U_metric L_metric)
       = O(T²Bu^.75)=o(F) high,
         O(NT/u^.25)=o(NT) medium.
The theorem uses no lower bound on retained incidence density. In particular it is compatible with every prior linear deletion and the earlier relative absorption bounds, without reinterpreting the removed quadratic energy of a linear deletion as an absolute error.

A surviving crossing pair is in two actual source sets with >L_metric theta_j d_j/(H a_(j,x)) distinct actual partners in each corresponding opposite cohort for EVERY live endpoint. On the coarse high grid d_0~H/u² with theta_0=1, a<=2pi(B)=O(B/u), so each live vertex has >c sqrt(u) charged partners in its opposite edge cohort. The earlier thin-reference-cohort result required only a large number of possible partners. The core now forces a large number carrying positive residual weight simultaneously. For medium the same conclusion with its own scaled threshold is meaningful when the number of occupied cohorts is small. One could add the small-p-only version of the opposite constraints as another family using its own a_small and the same membership budget; this is not included in the audit. Global high min degree >Bu^1.5 and medium >m_g/u^.25 hold in the corresponding asymptotic core, not for finite diagnostic caps. The global and root-contact constraints need no grid eligibility.

## 4. Actual crossing rectangles and prefix packing

Fix a cut, medium cell if applicable, and two distinct occupied projected crossing edges alpha,beta. Consider only the surviving actual core edges assigned to this cut with these two edge cohorts. They form a weighted simple bipartite graph between the sets A,B of their actual live endpoint sources; a source cannot belong to both crossing edges at one cut. Let k=b_(j)/(a_(j,x) U_metric). Each endpoint has >k distinct neighbors on the other side, so |A|,|B|>k, the number of actual edges is >k max(|A|,|B|), and total actual weight exceeds (b_(j)/a_(j,x)) max(|A|,|B|). In particular, if k>=1 then the rectangle has >k² actual edges. These are lower bounds/structural restrictions on any nonempty core rectangle, not upper estimates for the total number of actual edges or proof that no rectangle exists.

For an endpoint source a, take its entire original projected prefix P_a through its crossing pre-horizon inclusive. Because the crossing pre-horizon is <=x<C* for every actual assigned pair, the two prefixes must be disjoint on every actual edge. This condition is stronger than excluding merely identical pre-horizons; it uses complete original earlier histories, regardless of physical meeting or accounting holes.

Write
    B_forbid(a) = union_(h in P_a) {b in B: h in P_b}.
Then the actual degree is at most U_metric (|B|-|B_forbid(a)|). Thus for every a in a nonempty core rectangle,
    |B_forbid(a)| < |B|-k,
and likewise on the other side. For each earlier horizon h met on both sides, the forbidden rectangle A_h x B_h gives |A_h|<|A|-k, |B_h|<|B|-k. More generally the union over the entire focal prefix yields the preceding stronger condition. This is a restriction on simultaneous earlier membership products imposed by actual charged degrees. It does not imply a contradiction for disjoint prefixes; for example, independent earlier histories could leave all cross pairs allowed.

An explicit upper envelope for the ACTUAL remaining core weight in this rectangle is the sum over the live endpoint product A x B with disjoint P_a,P_b of 2 min(retained high tails at their crossing prevertices) for high, or C_med times the sum of their remaining original gains divided by m_g for medium (increase C_med if necessary). It ignores the exact later contact time and earlier rank, optional physical-key, age and quota conditions, so overcounts safely. This is not the same envelope as the previous full original anchor groups: the source sets have been restricted to positive actual residual endpoints. For counts define Z_(a,b)=|P_a intersect P_b|; the witness moment inequality of d31bbf0 gives
    #allowed <= |A||B| - (sum Z)^2/(sum Z²)
when the second moment is positive. One can use the same weighted version for the tail envelope. This reduces part of the remaining analysis to earlier one- and two-horizon membership intersections on the ACTUAL core source sets. To derive a limiting upper bound on core weight, further arithmetic/dynamical control of those sets is necessary. The finite audit enumerates these rectangles and exact prefix intersections and verifies every surviving actual edge is allowed. It does not assert that the moment inequality proves asymptotic exclusion of all rectangles.

## 5. Combining quota flow and peeling without double payment

In the finite audit, first use all earlier absolute masks, then the multigrid rank mask, then the existing extended maximum flow. It gives a feasible allocation and a remaining unpaid fraction f_e in [0,1] for every original pair (proportional on an aggregated intersection). Apply the joint peeling theorem to these fixed unpaid weights f_e w_e, not the entire pre-quota weights. For every peeled edge, subtract JUST f_e w_e from the original pair weight and the proportional never/async components, preserving the other directed medium fractions. The previous quota payments remain feasible and disjoint from this new absolute deletion. The original fixed feasible allocation with the surviving unpaid core therefore leaves a remainder equal to the core weight.

Optionally reoptimize the same quota network on these reduced original pair weights. This cannot increase the remainder, because the old allocation is still feasible. Do not count its full payment a second time; use the new optimized remainder. A newly reoptimized flow may change which individual pairs carry unpaid weight, and its support need not itself obey the earlier core stopping conditions. For a proof requiring those conditions, use the fixed allocation's core (or perform another separately budgeted peeling pass). The finite audit reports both the core and the reoptimized total; the detailed rectangle analysis is for the fixed-flow core before reoptimization.

The entire set of new absolute rank and core payments costs O(F/u^.25), O(NT/u^.25), respectively. The earlier relative sync/exit absorption remains valid in the original retained-energy inequality. The final simultaneous sufficient targets remain actual medium o(NT) and high o(F) on the same unbounded dyadic sequence; it suffices to prove these for the surviving cores. Nothing here establishes those bounds or twin-prime infinitude.
