## 5. Separate local lattice transfer theorem at large heights

This result is about a full lattice of possible states, NOT assumed canonically occupied. Let H be polynomial, e.g. H=floor(R^.4), x even in [R,2R], starting height h in [H,2H]. Take z=c L with B<=R^.01. For each pair of residues x,h mod B, the trial path with labels<=z has a single profile of displacement, time and height change. Consider a fixed successful trial connector that first reaches h+D (where D=O(z)) within total gain<=4D; its elapsed length t=D+G/2<=3D. Stop when target is first reached or gain cap exceeded. Up to the successful endpoint, all heights are h+O(D), positive for large R. For a fixed class pair the lattice has N_x~R/B, N_h~H/B points in each coordinate. At a given trial tick, an outside eligible q>z requires one spatial residue modulo q and one starting-height residue mod q, by the same two pins with p replaced by the variable initial h in the height expression. Each q is <=O(H), since the eligible height integer is positive O(H). Hence the number in that pair of B-classes is at most (N_x/q+1)(N_h/q+1). Divide by N_x N_h and sum q and ticks:
    fraction with any outside eligibility
       << D [ 1/(z log z)
              +(B/R+B/H) loglog(3H)+ B²/R ]             (4)
where the final term follows already from #q=O(H). The latter two terms are polynomially small for H=.4 and B<=R^.01. Thus relative loss is O(1/log z) uniformly per successful trial class pair for D=O(z). There is no claim of an absolute upper this small at every individual height h; the height-coordinate averaging over [H,2H] is essential.

Moreover a collection of successful trial class pairs with distinct OUTPUT residue pairs (x-G,h+D) mod B gives distinct actual endpoint states after deleting its bad starts: distinct classes cannot overlap outputs, and within each class the trial displacement is constant and translations inject. One can choose a representative input class for each distinct trial output class. This transfers almost all of the resulting full-lattice trial distinct-output count to the true graph; it is a rigorous local version of the previous one-sided residual strategy. Boundaries cause only O(B/H+B/R) relative rounding when comparing equal full-class counts. It does not establish that any of these inputs have a canonical ancestor.

As a concrete local application, take cutoff 2z (reduce c if necessary to keep B<=R^.01), D=floor(z/4). Among even x mod B force exactly one prime r in (z,2z] to divide x+1, and forbid all other primes<=2z from either neighbor; r does not divide x-1. The disjoint CRT class densities sum to order

For each such x, at most D/(2r) fraction of starting h residues have an r calendar firing within D ticks (at most D residues out of 2r, repeated exactly within B); all other h classes give a vertical successful trial. Thus after (4) there are >>R H/(log z)^3 distinct true local vertical connectors, with qmin(x)=r<=2z=o(H) and height increment D, over h in [H,2H]. Their endpoint states are distinct. The number per h is O(R/(log z)^3) by exact periodic x counting (B<<R), so a positive fraction of h in that interval have >>R/(log z)^3 such local connectors.
