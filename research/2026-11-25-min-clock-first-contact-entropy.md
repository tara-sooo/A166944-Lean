# Actual first-contact forest, entropy capacities, and small-jump rough recipients

2026-11-25 JST. Continues 253a2402110644db7e02a7d2aad5944ea69c3520. New paper results: aggregate actual OLD terminal charge by distinct original pair, build a chronological merger forest on the charged pair support alone, and allocate vertex/event quotas by logarithmic component growth. Both endpoints can be constrained at every merger with an absolute budget O(XB log^(11/4) B), without multiplying by the number of small jumps. A separate uniform two-form sieve with two conditioned prime congruences gives a three-logarithm saving on small-jump, very rough stationary recipient first-contact keys. This also pays actual aggregate pair charge over all subsequent old origins. Dense residual merges and short/small-recipient contacts remain open; no Lean compilation or twin infinitude proof.

## 2. NEW first-contact forest built from actual charged edges

In one cell form the finite weighted graph with vertices incident to W_ij>0. Every pair has a unique true first physical meeting (t*,J*) from its full original histories (253a240); aggregate ALL its weights into that one event. Process the events in increasing absolute time, batching all keys at the same time before any union. Initially each vertex is singleton. At a key (t,J), merge all current components containing endpoints of charged edges assigned to that key. Multiple separate charged subgraphs at that same key can be merged together: all these endpoints actually share that physical state. Keep all vertices of a component, even if their original short cutoffs have passed; the proof uses the common deterministic reference, not fictitious charged pairs. No need to enumerate contacts that have zero charge.

The following assertions hold:
(i) Each charged edge at (t,J) joins DIFFERENT components just before time t. If connected by earlier charged edges, each earlier edge joins paths sharing a true state, hence its endpoints have identical deterministic reference suffix. Along a chain of earlier edges, all endpoints have a common state at the maximum of those earlier times and thereafter, as long as a path is active. Both endpoints of the current edge are actually present at t, so their earlier reference meeting occurs within both original prefixes, contradicting true firstness. A twin terminal cannot cause a split or permit later active contact. All involved tags have already started at the corresponding connecting times.
(ii) A component can participate at at most ONE physical key at a fixed time. Any active representatives in that component share the same deterministic state. Therefore batches can be processed in arbitrary key order, using the BEFORE-time sizes, not artificial pairwise intermediate sizes at simultaneous multiway merges.
(iii) Component sizes never decrease, and every vertex begins with size1. The final size is bounded by the cell root count O(B). It may include pairs with zero actual charge; these pairs are not added to the energy ledger. This forest need not coincide with full reference ancestry, and can be much smaller.

Write n_v^- and n_v^+ for a vertex's component size before and after a merger involving it. The sum over its growth events telescopes exactly:
    sum_e log(n_v^+/n_v^-)=log n_v(final) <=log(5B+1).
For any edge joining distinct precomponents of sizes a,b, at least one endpoint is in a component of size <=n^+/2. Call that endpoint light (both may be light). Each vertex is light at no more than log_2(5B+1) mergers. These statements refer to the fixed charged graph, not an assumption that the physical cohort has a bounded number of small-p events. Components are frozen from the starting positive graph for subsequent quota peeling; do not rebuild sizes after removing edges and retain the old caps without proof.

## 3. NEW logarithmic growth quotas on actual aggregate pair energy

Set m0=u^(7/4), base=U m0. At each event and each endpoint vertex v constrain the sum of current remaining edge weights incident to v at this event by a quota capacity
    b_(v,e)=base log(n_v^+/n_v^-).
This permits both endpoints to have capacities, including the stationary recipient of a small jump. Repeatedly pay all remaining incident weights of any constraint whose current degree <=its capacity. Each edge is paid at most once, and each constraint charged at most once. This is the fixed-weight core peeling used earlier, now on the global pair weights, not the individual origin-level graphs. The sum of all capacities across cells is at most
    base * sum_(cell,v) log(n_v(final))
       =O(X B u^(7/4) u)
       =O(XB u^(11/4))=o(XB u^3).                  (1)
The bound includes all contact types and any number of small-label jump-stationary mergers, and is independent of future reference W. It is an absolute quadratic payment. One can also make a partial payment min(degree,b) via sequential allocation, but the strict core statements here refer to the all-incident peeling and frozen capacities. Earlier birth, multiscale and arithmetic payments can be applied first on the actual remaining pair-level weights before aggregating; after global pair payment allocate the paid part back to the original pair-levels by any consistent proportional or priority rule. No addition of overlapping independent raw payments.

For every surviving edge, each of its endpoints has current event-degree >b_(v,e). Since each aggregate pair has weight <=U, it needs >m0 log(n_v^+/n_v^-) distinct actual charged partners AT THAT FIRST MEETING, rather than all reference ancestors. In particular the light endpoint has >m0 log2 partners; if its component grows by a large factor the threshold increases proportionally to the logarithm. Every event with total new component size <=1+m0 log2 is entirely paid: each edge has a light endpoint whose maximum possible degree <=U(n^+-1)<=base log2. More generally, for a light component of size a the full possible event degree is <=U(n^+-a); if this is <=base log(n^+/a), all edges incident to its vertices are paid. This applies even when many earlier shared rough states contribute to a pair. For a two-child jump-stationary event, the two components correspond to disjoint precontact histories, but the actual charged graph can also have more than two components at a physical key (some contacts have zero charge). Do not identify a charged component with a full donor or recipient ancestry without an independent check.

Optional paper refinement: split each endpoint's capacity among actual opposite precomponents in proportion to their positive sizes, normalized by sum of opposite sizes. Total allocated at the endpoint stays <=b_(v,e); peeling on these split constraints yields a cohort-specific partner lower bound, with the split factor. It may be small for a tiny opposite component, so this is not a uniform thick-cohort theorem. Not implemented in the audit.

## 4. NEW small-jump/rough-recipient support

Focus on a true first contact of jump-stationary type (neither endpoint a root at t*). The incoming jump p at t from D=J-p+1 lands in a stationary target J, occupied by the other tag since its own actual wait arrival a<t. Its recipient age A=t-a satisfies
    1<=A<2r(J).
The incoming donor's completed wait is <=2r(D)<=2p; the recipient can have a much longer wait. If a first meeting is followed by an original rough charge, J is nonterminal. We distinguish incoming label p (may be small) from recipient r(J) (may be large). In particular A>=2R implies r(J)>R. This is not a condition on the outgoing event of the incoming donor at its next state. A recipient arrival may be root or earlier jump; no condition on that earlier label is imposed here.

Choose R=c B/u^2 with fixed c>0, P=B^(1/8), and consider small incoming odd p<=P with recipient r(J)>=R. For sufficiently large B, p<R. For each fixed p and parity s=t mod2, the incoming arithmetic requires
    J+2s=0 mod p,  t=rho(p,s) mod 2p,
    J/2<=t<=(J+12B+6)/2.
At fixed J the number of candidate phase times is O(B/p). Since r(J)>0 and is <=B, choose r'=r(J) in [R,B], side sigma=+/-1 with r'|J+sigma. Here r'!=p. The two congruences on J give one residue modulo p r' (and even J gives a single residue modulo 2p r'). Parameterize J=J0+2 p r' h, with h in an interval of length O(X/(p r')). Enlarge to a comparable-length interval if necessary, at least c' B^(7/8), since p<=B^(1/8),r'<=B and X~B^2/7.

For odd sieve primes ell<=z0=B^(1/64) other than p, both (J+sigma)/r' and J-sigma are affine in h with invertible coefficients (r'>z0 eventually). Their forbidden residues are distinct because the second minus r' times the first is -2 sigma. Exclude p from the sieve if p<=z0; this changes the dimension-two Mertens product by a bounded factor <=3. For p=3,s=1 the congruence forces 3|J-1, so no rough recipient anyway, but the relaxed sieve excluding p remains an upper bound. No independence of prime divisibility is assumed. Apply the uniform two-form upper sieve from research/2026-11-24-min-growing-cutoff-sieve-weighted-reset.md section2 (the established Ford Theorem2.4 application): CRT remainder O(2^omega(d)), summed d<=z0 gives O(z0 log(2z0)), negligible against B^(7/8)/u^2. Thus uniformly for fixed p,r',s,sigma there are
    O(X/(p r' u^2))
possible recipient horizons. This explicitly checks the smaller interval length after conditioning on BOTH primes, rather than reusing a bound requiring length >=B. Sum over r' in [R,B] via sum 1/r'=O(log u/u), then multiply by O(B/p) clock phases. For each small incoming p the total distinct relaxed first-contact keys (t,J,p) satisfies
    S_p <= O(XB log u/(p^2 u^3)).                   (2)
This is a three-logarithm saving (up to log u), independent of future time, and works also for fixed tiny p. It uses r(J)>=R AND r(J)<=B, not merely absence of small prime factors. A weaker general rough-receiver condition r(J)>=B^delta would yield the two-log saving O(XB/(p^2 u^2)) from the direct single congruence sieve excluding p; (2) uses the extra near-top r' band. Actual MIN selection at donor and stationarity at recipient only reduce this relaxed support.

At each key aggregate its actual old pair weights W_ij across all cells, old origins and levels for small-p/rough-recipient jump-stationary first contacts. Pay min(actual key weight, p u^(23/4)). By (2) and sum_{p<=P}1/p=O(log u),
    P_small,rough <= O(XB u^(11/4)(log u)^2)
                  =o(XB u^3).                       (3)
This is an absolute quota even for p=3,5,7; it differs from the earlier large incoming prime key quota of 253a240. It can pay all weights at a thin small-p key and part of a heavy one. For a tiny p, the cap is polylog rather than B times polylog, so (3) does not alone pay an arbitrary entire pair of weight O(B). A very large recipient r does not imply a small incoming p is rare among actual charged pairs. The new estimate bounds keys, not the number or mass of ancestors at a key. For long recipient age A>=2R and p<=P this quota applies automatically. Shorter age may also have r>=R; no exclusion claimed.

## 6. Next target and remaining obstruction

The old terminal graph now has an absolute entropy payment over actual first contacts (1) and small incoming/rough recipient key quotas (3), compatible with previous large-label contact, origin/destination and multiscale budgets on actual unpaid charges. Dense residual edges require actual many partners at the same first contact and, in the small-p very rough sector, high total key weight. Next aim: use the stationary recipient's earlier arrival a and the donor's short pre-wait <=2p to exploit temporal separation, e.g. an actual first-contact interval split by donor last nonLOW anchor and recipient age, or a weighted growth inequality for the remaining unbalanced mergers. Do not equate the charged forest component size to the true reference population or claim that paying all light events is possible; only the capacity budget is small. Need independent control of thick same-event partners, including small labels at nonrough recipients, and all earlier immediate/later, distinct-slot, low-r, MED/full-positive residuals. No Lean compilation. Main/other branches unchanged.
