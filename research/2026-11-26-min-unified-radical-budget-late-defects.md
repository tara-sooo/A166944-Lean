## 2. Backward radical cap stronger than renewal

For an original destination event e at prehorizon E with selected p, scan its actual preceding completed events backwards, maintaining R initially L0, and stop BEFORE the first preceding event whose label is p or whose inclusion makes R=lcm(R,2q)>4H. The accepted suffix is contiguous and includes no p. Let C_rad^-(e) be the earliest prehorizon in the accepted suffix, E if empty, and G_rad(e)=E-C_rad^-(e). If scan reaches root, use the actual origin. It is permissible to additionally stop at the earlier of the old pursuit distance bounds. The true suffix radical may be capped at 4H+1 solely as an overflow flag.

Every actual source crossing routed to e occurs in this suffix. Indeed its source label and all labels in the unchanged common prefix before e have periods dividing Delta, whereas p does not. Therefore the radical of the entire interval from source crossing (INCLUDING its selected label) to e's prehorizon divides |Delta|<=4H and does not contain p. Any earlier suffix failure is incompatible. Also p cannot divide L0. It follows that every source boundary K for the chosen tag lies in
    [C_rad^-(e),E] intersect [E-L_s-b_s,E],
where b_l=P/a for good low sources, b_h=B, or use B in both sectors. Since C_rad^- is at least the posthorizon of previous p (or root), G_rad<=G_p of dc348287.

With the old row/column argument, for each tag/event/grid/sector,
    D_(P,s)(tag,e) <= 2[min(G_rad(e),L_s+b_s)+P].        (1)
This is in ENERGY units, not clock; multiply by c_a for clock. It is pointwise no larger than the old renewal cap. Moreover, define the discrete raw capacity U_(P,s)(tag,e) to be 2P times the number of original crossed atoms of this tag in preceding source events satisfying:
  * E-J<L_s and J<=E;
  * R_prefix=lcm(L0, 2q of source, 2r of all actual selected labels between J and E) <=4H;
  * 2p does not divide R_prefix.
One may restrict to geometrically usable sources; for the upper bound allow all original source crossings. These are necessary for actual chosen-side defects. In particular 2p not dividing R_prefix excludes p in L0 as well as p at a preceding event. U<=the right side of (1); it can vanish when the continuous interval cap does not. The projected averaged sum of U over actual original tagged events at (E,p) is an admissible capacity, with zero on unusable grids. Use the same m denominator. Comparing it with the old absolute thin-charge cutoffs produces a subset of the old admissible targets. No actual residual is lost. This does not require assuming that every accepted suffix can translate.

## 3. A global capacity budget over distinct labels

Fix ONE tag and ONE original source crossing atom at (C,q), with R0=lcm(L0,2q). Scan forward after J through the selected-event prefix, stopping at the appropriate pursuit horizon. A possible chosen-side destination in U occurs only if the prefix radical R<=4H and the new label p does not divide R (all labels odd). Such a label multiplies R by p. Thus possible labels form a list of DISTINCT new prime factors, up to and including the first multiplication taking R over 4H. Stop there: all subsequent prefixes have radical>4H. If R0>4H there are no candidates, though R0<=L0 B<<H eventually. The log weight of all candidates from this atom is bounded by
    sum_candidates log p <= log(4H/R0)+log B.            (2)
The final term is necessary for the potential last record that crosses the cap. No bound on the total number of repeated low labels is needed. This is a combinatorial bound on actual candidate DESTINATION events, not on the number of eligible prime divisors at a state.

Exchange the finite sums over source atoms and destination events. For every grid/sector,
    sum_(tag,e) U_(P,s)(tag,e) log p_e
        <= 2 P sum_(original crossed tag atoms K)
                        [log(4H/R0(K))+u]
        <= 2 N(H+P)(log(4H)+u).                         (3)
This is a summed capacity bound over ALL labels, unlike the earlier renewal estimate separately for each label. Averaging over grids, for the two sectors combined the bound is <=4 N(H+P_max)(log(4H)+u)=O(NT u). We used the full atom count in each sector; taking sector-specific source eligibility may decrease the bound. Consequently total discrete raw capacity for destinations p>=B^alpha is O_alpha(NT) for any fixed alpha>0, uniformly in the original histories. For p>y0 one also obtains O(NT u/log(2+y0)). This still does not give a constant less than 1/2, and cannot be multiplied by an extra reciprocal prime density without a justified independence or arithmetic argument. It is a genuine global capacity bound, not merely the trivial bound on already-normalized actual charges.

## 4. Arithmetic prefix equivalence and modular candidate capacities

At one projected source class, fix sector length L_s. Each original stable slot v has its own actual deterministic continuation. At a recorded prehorizon E before first completed gain crossing L_s, define R_v(E) as the lcm of L0, 2q, and the periods of all actually selected labels from source posthorizon J up to, but excluding, the next event at E. For two such slots present at E, their entire prefixes up to E are unchanged translates IF AND ONLY IF
    R_v(E)=R_v'(E)=R and v=v' mod R.                    (4)
If R>4H, only the same physical slot is possible for translated prefixes. Proof: equality of translated prefixes gives equal radicals and divisibility of the actual exit lag. Conversely equal radical and residue ensure that every chosen label on BOTH prefixes is periodic under the initial lag. Starting from their common J, the two-way completed-candidate MIN comparison implies identical selected label and wait at each step until E, inductively preserving lag. Both reach E and all gains are positive, so no strict-prefix ambiguity. For R>4H group by actual v instead of the artificial cap; determinism at a given slot gives its unique prefix. The arithmetic key (E,R,v mod R) therefore identifies the exact trie prefix classes, not just a necessary relaxed divisibility test. Different prefixes cannot rejoin in this key even when their projected horizons rejoin. This is not a claim that radical divisibility alone of one path suffices.

There is a cheaper relaxed one-sided upper bound which does not require both prefixes or computing the prefix tree. For a slot v and its actual candidate event e at E,p, set R=R_v(E)<=4H and 2p not dividing R. In the ORIGINAL stable source slot population define
    n_candidate(v,e) = sum_(v':R|(v'-v), 2p not dividing(v'-v)) a_v',
    W_candidate(v,e) = sum_(same v') sum_(stable j at v') w_j^s.
Both are differences of two residue-class counts: modulo R minus modulo lcm(R,2p), and can be computed from original source slot tables. For each original stable tag i in v, candidate capacity in energy units at this atom is
    [w_i^s n_candidate(v,e)+W_candidate(v,e)]/(c_a n).  (5)
This includes the tag's two roles: when focal it has its own weight w_i, when partner it can receive the weights of other focal tags. It ignores the requirement that the other member not have an earlier nonperiodic selected label. Every actually routed charge is covered, including the old tie-to-focal rule. Sum (5) on the actual tag/event over all original atoms and grids for a modular projected capacity; take the minimum of this, (1) and U when constructing admissible residual targets. Since each term in (5)<=2P, and is zero outside the discrete raw candidate set, the new capacity is also <=U pointwise.

For a fixed source ordered pair with nonzero lag, each member has at most ONE candidate event along its own word: its first selected label whose period does not divide the lag. It qualifies for (5) because all preceding selected periods divide lag, and no subsequent prefix radical can divide that lag. The first actual defect is the earlier in prehorizon of these two first nonperiodic events, with ties assigned to focal; absent event means infinity. To see this, compare the actual words from J until that first prehorizon: before it both selected periods divide lag, so both paths translate by induction; at that prehorizon at least one is nonperiodic, which precludes unchanged translation. If neither has such an event within its own completed pursuit then both words translate. The chosen-side condition at a tie is exactly the old rule. Thus the sum of one-sided candidate charges before later pair-specific masks is between the exact full differing-word energy and TWICE that energy. Indeed every differing pair produces one actual first defect and at most two candidates, while compatible pairs produce none. This upper envelope is useful for target capacities, not claimed to be smaller than the full exact trie sum. In the finite audit it equals twice the exact forward clock on all runs; no aggregate saving over the exact forward measure is asserted. Formula (4) provides exact arithmetic regrouping if both sides are needed, and the residue-exact ordered child formula of dc348287 still applies.

## 5. Late high-radical defects: both source slots must be large

This is an additional all-mass payment in the existing FULL route. Set
    S=H/ell^(1/4), kappa=ell^(-1/2).
Call a forward pair late if at the prehorizon of its first defect the common prefix radical R (including source q and L0) is >=S. It necessarily remains <=4H, since all prefix periods divide the nonzero lag. In particular |Delta|>=S. At each fixed source class, atom and sector, stop each stable slot's prefix at the FIRST recorded prehorizon with radical>=S, before the next completed selected event in its pursuit. Ignore slots that never reach this condition or cross above 4H, since no late nonzero-lag pair can use those stops. Include source posthorizon if R0>=S. Group these stopped slots by the arithmetic key (4). This is a partition of a subset of the original stable slots: each slot has at most one stop. Every late pair belongs to the SAME stopped group, since it translates up to its actual first defect and first threshold hit is a property of the common prefix. This is why summing over many later prefixes is unnecessary.

At a stopped group radical R>=S, the source departure times are one residue modulo R and their span<=4H. There are at most K_S=1+floor(4H/S) slots. Declare source slot small if its FULL original b_v<=kappa n. Within any group g, the number a_g,small of stable tags of small slots is <=K_S kappa n, independently of other slots' masses. Let a_g be total stable tag count of g. Sum w_i^s<=c_a P per tag; hence the total late forward CLOCK charge having a small focal slot is at most
    sum_g (c_a P a_g,small a_g/n)
       <= c_a P K_S kappa n.
The same bound holds for a small PARTNER slot: sum focal weights in a group <=c_a P a_g and multiply by a_g,small/n. No rank, maximum partner size or exchangeability assumption. The union costs <=2 c_a P K_S kappa n at this atom and sector. Sum over all original classes/atoms/grids and the two sectors with same denominator m, using raw boundary measure <=N(H+P_max). Total <=4 c_a K_S kappa N(H+P_max)=O(NT/ell^(1/4))=o(NT) in clock units. This pays exact forward fractions only, not the original entire focal waits and not backward compatible charges. All previous masks remain.

Therefore every remaining late forward pair has BOTH original physical source slot populations b_v,b_v'>kappa n. There are fewer than 1/kappa=sqrt(ell) such slots in the entire source phase class. In each stopped arithmetic group there are also <=K_S=O(ell^(1/4)) distinct slots. Their nonzero lag is at least S and divisible by the group's radical. The remaining late defect contribution may be evaluated on just these large slots; it is not proved small. At a good focal witness of an original retained moderate event (n_e<=H/[256 max(P_max,L0 p_e)u ell³]), the old b_v<=2n_e, n>H/(A q u ell²) and A q<=max(P_max,L0 p_e) give b_v/n<=1/(128ell). This is <kappa eventually. Consequently ALL late defect fractions assigned to moderate focal events are paid (even if partner huge). For every remaining positive late focal incidence the corresponding original focal physical mass is
    n_e >= b_v/2 > kappa H/(2 A q u ell²).
For a moderate threshold this is at least 128 sqrt(ell) times that cutoff, since max(P_max,L0 p_e)>=A q. The inequality holds for every focal event contributing at that source; splitting by original assigned good waits preserves the masks. This increases the physical mass requirement only in the LATE sector, not for earlier defects or for backward compatible clock.

## 6. Updated residual and what is left

Let D_early be old residual forward charge at R<S, D_late,large the remaining old late charge with both original slots large. Then
    D_rem = D_early+D_late,large+o(NT),
where the o(NT) denotes nonnegative paid charge, and the new sum is <=old D_rem. Under finitely many twins,
    NT/2-o(NT) <= B_rem+(3+o(1))(D_early+D_late,large).
A sufficient test is liminf [B_rem+3(D_early+D_late,large)]/(NT)<1/2. It is implied by the old sufficient test, but the new payment is little-o, not a proven constant-width improvement. The strengthened arithmetic targets from (1),(5), and the tree upper bound from dc348287 can be applied separately to the two surviving sectors. The global budget (3) is an actual new total upper bound on raw candidate capacities for fixed polynomially large labels; its coefficient is not enough for the required contraction. No analytic bound on early defects or on remaining large-slot late defects sufficient to close the route, nor on backward births, has been established. Main and independent source branches not modified. No Lean compilation.
