## 2. Exact earlier first meeting

If a recorded birth has same positive incoming selected p from different recorded children, both predecessor horizons are D=J-p+1. Their predecessor times a<b<t must differ. Each waits at D up to but not including t, since the deterministic next event is the same. Hence the true first common tick state is (b,D), NOT (t,J). It cannot have occurred earlier: after any earlier common tick, both deterministic paths would have the same next event, and that event would be a common recorded state; if it were t, both would already be at D then, requiring time>=b. Similarly if two birth children have different incoming selected labels, or one is a root singleton at v, the first common tick is v. This argument concerns the full recorded prefixes, with the crossing wait included; it does not assert that the earlier root is still within its clipped time slab. Actual full half-open waits plus the final singleton recorded state give an independent interval oracle.

Let d*=2b-D, delta=d_v-d*=2(t-b)-(p-1). Define I*=d* - min(d*,d_min) and K_first=min(delta_+,I). The compressed common suffix that begins at (b,D), takes the first shortened wait t-b, and then uses recorded common edges has descent D*=D_shared,pair+(-delta)_+, ascent A*=A_shared,pair+delta_+, matched L*=L_pair+K_first. Indeed
    I*+K_first-I=(-delta)_+.
Since J_pair<=(-delta)_+ (including reset case J_pair=0), exactly
    C_pair + [(-delta)_+-J_pair] = I*+K_first.        (1)
The bracket is nonnegative. This may INCREASE the raw debit if one replaces C_pair by I*. Thus do not do that. Keep the old actual debit and earlier priority payments. Let c be remaining debit of a pair after those payments. Transfer k=min(c,K_first) into a separate matched-first-rise ledger. Remaining c-k<=I*<=d*. It is nonnegative and zero if the old pair debit was already fully paid. This transfer is NOT an unconditional small-error payment.

## 3. Matched-first-rise credit and full positive energy

When K_first>0, the later arrival's shortened full wait w=t-b satisfies w>(p-1)/2>=p/3. The earlier wait is longer, so BOTH original full events have positive drift. At D their full weights are z_a=(t-a)/(2r(D)), z_b=(t-b)/(2r(D)). Since r<=p, r z_a z_b>=w²/(4p)>=w/12. Thus K_first<=2w<=24 r z_a z_b. In particular sum of transferred credit k<=24 times the actual full projected pair occupation restricted to these birth pairs. This is a conditional upstream bound, not a proof that the transferred amount is negligible. There is also a useful no-double-count refinement: L_shared consists of matched ascents on already common recorded edges, each bounded by 24 times full positive-drift pair occupation as in d6f0d82. K_first occurs at the unique earlier waiting-state birth at a DIFFERENT predecessor clock pair, with neither pair counted as sharing that recorded edge. For any ordered root pair and predecessor horizon, these two cases are disjoint. Therefore
    L_shared + sum k <=24 E_+,off^full,              (2)
where E_+,off^full is the ALL-LABEL full unclipped positive-drift projected offdiagonal energy, including actual crossing events. It needs no new factor 2 from adding a second independent upper bound. The refined (2) follows from pair-incidence disjointness; audit checks the transferred-credit inequality and original exact L, not a separate combined full-energy oracle. It does not provide the full positive-energy estimate at the twin sufficient scale. One can instead keep L_shared+sum k as a more precise matched level ledger. Do not add all K_first if only k was transferred.

## 4. Later arrival arithmetic

For a same-label recorded birth, write q for the later predecessor state's actual incoming selected label (0 if original root singleton); q0 for the earlier predecessor state's incoming label. Lag h=b-a is positive and h<t-a<=2r(D). If q=0 then d*=6. If q0=0 then earlier d_a=6 and d*=6+2h. If q=q0>0, both arrivals at D have the same side since an odd q cannot divide both D and D+2. Their times belong to the SAME progression modulo 2q. Thus h is a positive multiple of 2q and r(D)>q. In particular same incoming large q forces roughness of the next predecessor neighbors, without any condition on the later selected p. For arbitrary q>0, parity s=b mod2 gives q|D+2s, d*=6s+2qk0. For d*>6, k0>=1. q may be 3. These conditions use incoming q, not outgoing p; those labels can differ.

Additional low true-birth payment: if d*<=m, pay all remaining c-k<=m. Every pair born at most once and has |M-M'|<=G/2, so total <=O(XBm). This includes later canonical roots, and even if d_v is large can pay a part of their old debit. Do not reuse old small-birth threshold on vertex mass at a new state without a separate potential proof. The audit's preliminary alternative new-debit comparison used old recorded mass; the final improved ledger uses the original small/source/mixed payments and only the new d* threshold.

## 5. Adaptive lag quotas on actual residual

Set u=log B, ell=1+log u, Y=max(17,ceil B^1/8), lag threshold L0=ceil B^1/8. For later arrival q>0,d*>6 define rough case if (q>=Y and h>=q) OR h>=L0. Then 2r(D)>L0 (since q>=Y>=L0); even small q can be rough via the second condition. Otherwise plain. All same incoming q>=Y pairs belong to rough. Group actual remaining OLD debits after credit transfer and low true-birth payment by (D,b,q,case). A pair belongs to at most one group; previous priority masks retained. For fixed q and deficit band D_j/2<d*<=D_j, D_j=(12B+6)/2^j, possible nonreset phases O(D_j/q), because q<=D_j/2. For each phase/parity q|D+2s gives O(X/q) horizons. Thus plain key support O(X D_j/q²).

For rough keys, the same uniform dimension-two sieve used previously applies on D±1 in D=-2s+q k, with parity and arrival phase imposing at most extra congruences. For every odd prime l<=B^1/64, l!=q, two distinct forbidden residues in k since q invertible and forms differ by 2. If l=q divides one form identically and is below the rough cutoff, there are no admissible horizons; otherwise omit l=q, changing the sieve bound by at most an absolute constant. This includes small q, not just q above sieve limit. With interval length O(X/q) and error O(B^3/32), the latter absorbed since X/q>=cB, obtain O(X/(q u²)) horizons per phase. Consequently rough support O(X D_j/(q² u²)). This is a count of relaxed true-birth keys, not of ordered pairs; it permits arbitrary earlier label and arbitrary outgoing p.

For a key in band j set h_q=1 for q>=Y, ell for q<Y. Cap actual ordered residual-pair count with
    c_key = B m q / [D_j² (j+1)² h_q]
in plain case, and u² times that in rough case. Pay uniform fraction min(1,c_key/n_key) of the remaining debit at key, n_key actual ordered pair count under this classifier (can restrict to positive residual). Since each remaining debit<=d*<=D_j, summing support times D_j c_key gives O(XBm) after summing q reciprocal (O(1) above Y, O(ell) overall) and the summable j factor. This does not bound all residual products by the caps. It is an unconditional partial debit payment, not a linear occupation deletion. With m=u^2.5 it costs o(XB u³). The pair-specific source caps were already applied before this grouping, and mixed recorded birth caps applied before transfer; different-label recorded births remain under the old ledger.

Writing C_rem for final unpaid OLD debit and K_paid for transferred matched first rise (NOT a small term),
    C_init = C_rem + K_paid + O(XB u^2.5)
in the sense of nonnegative difference bounded by the big-O. Thus the previous cheap-descent relation can use L_shared+K_paid+C_rem+o(XB u³). The new arithmetic bound has not proved C_rem=o(XB u³) and (2) has not proved L_shared+K_paid small. The method now localizes large same-label debit at true birth and permits the extra u² cap factor in rough class. No general statement about projected pairs without physical birth, no change to LOW destination transfer.
