New: transfer the uniform pin-only gap sieve to exact weighted common cores, without a repeated starting-deficit penalty. Replace XXI's three resources by two actual weighted resources, low crossing mass and high clock excess. Control omitted wait directly by the strengthened missing measure, retaining the positive terminal deficit. The result is a necessary arithmetic supply inequality, not a strict contraction.

## 1. Sharper omitted wait and signed clock budget

Retain XX's strengthened missing measure mu_hat, total Mhat, and XXI exact common cores. One can use any fixed theta in (0,1/2] in XX, or take theta=1/ell with L=floor(theta b0), m=floor(L/ell). Then L,m tend to infinity, m/L=o(1), and all retirement/restart and logarithmic switch arguments remain valid. In particular total number of exceptional returns is o(N); using theta=1/ell increases chain count to O(N ell/b0), which does not affect per-tag core fringe bounds. No need to retain the former O(N/b0) count for this stronger budget.

Let W_pre be the sum of actual elapsed ticks from each root to its first true vertex with population >=L, with its actual terminal substituted if there is none. The full-exit denominator cap in XX gives
    W_pre <= Mhat/(1-theta)+2NB.
The prefix may end inside a wait, but at most one complete event straddles the vertex, costing <=2B. For good tags, the omitted complete events after first large cost at most O((D+B)(1+log N)+BH/D+B) gain each by XXI. Bad return tags have entire wait <=3H, and there are o(N). Never-large tags are already included in W_pre. Thus for the complement of the complete-event cores in the original tagged event ensemble,
    W_omit <= W_pre+o(NT) <= (1+o(1)) Mhat+o(NT),       (1)
with theta=1/ell (Mhat<=W_all<=3NH). This is a direct wait bound, stronger than multiplying all omitted gained length by 3. Also G_omit<=2W_pre+6N+o(NT) by canonical prefix identity and the same fringes. All statements are on actual tag-event incidences, not the unweighted skeleton length. For fixed theta retain 1/(1-theta).

Write Z_all=sum terminal ordinary deficits. Exact signed clock:
    4W_all-G_all=G_all+2Z_all-12N.
Set R_core=4W_core-G_core, where W_core=sum b_Q W_Q. Then
    R_core=G_all+2Z_all-12N-4W_omit+G_omit
          >=G_all+2Z_all-4Mhat-o(NT).                 (2)
In fact keep +G_omit if desired. Under finite twins G_all=NT+O(NB). Thus even without assuming Mhat=o(NT), the loss cost in this signed ledger is 4Mhat, rather than 44Mhat from multiplying the old gain-loss estimate by the maximum signed density. This is a one-way lower bound; no independent addition of earlier graph credits. The nonnegative terminal deficit improves it and need not be upper bounded.

## 2. Uniform pin-only sieve on complete cores

Use direct 47a1442 section 3 pin-only construction: S->infinity slowly with S=o(log X), Z=floor(exp(3S)), odd primorial P_S, K=2P_S, normalized mass M_cut of surviving pinned cuts, kappa=2P_S/Q. Choose fixed eps=1/1000 and sufficiently large fixed k0; uniform moving-average length lambda=ceil(P_S Z^(4k0+6) S^10)=exp(O(S))=X^o(1). Every event label p<=S crosses no cut, and every exact anchor has p>Z. For any disjoint intervals total gain g and count n, direct gives
    (1-eps)(g-2(lambda-1)n)<=M_cut<=(1+eps)(g+2(lambda-1)n).
This is uniform, no Fourier exceptional set. Alternatively choose S sufficiently large FIXED beyond all twins under finite twins; all constants then fixed, and the same bound applies, since the analytic estimate holds for sufficiently large S. Fixed S not sent to infinity as X varies. Set Y=512lambda+1 (integer; distinct from original lifetime T), high events p>=Y, low events p<Y. Eventually Y<=B if S grows slowly; for fixed S this also holds. Each high event has a deep bypass cut by direct (5). This is a lower mass statement, not a supply upper.

For each core let U_Q be low gain, M_Q low cut mass, n_Q number of low events actually crossing at least one surviving cut, h_Q number of high events. Apply the lower bound to the whole core and upper to disjoint high jumps:
    U_Q <= M_Q/(1+eps) + [2eps/(1+eps)] G_Q
           +2(lambda-1)h_Q+2(lambda-1).                (3)
In the last term we enlarge the factor (1-eps)/(1+eps) to 1. Since h_Q<=G_Q/(Y-1), summing exact core weights gives
    U <= M_low/(1+eps) + beta G_core+o(NT),
    beta=2eps/(1+eps)+2(lambda-1)/(Y-1) < .006.
The last block fringe <=2lambda sum b_Q<=2lambda NH/D=o(NT). Likewise
    M_low <= (1+eps)(Y-1+2(lambda-1)) n_low
           <= (1+eps)(514lambda) n_low.                 (4)
Each crossing event counted once per core tag, even if several cuts. No weight by full physical cohort unless it equals actual selected core. All nonnegative mass is from actual cuts; no independence assumption across cores. No o(X) error summed per short block.

## 3. Replace bypass by actual high clock excess

Define on the exact core events the high excess
    E_H=sum_(p>=Y) b_Q (4w-g)_+,
and F_H=sum_(p>=Y,4w>=g) b_Q g. Low events have 4w-g<=11g from w<=3g. High events with 4w<g have negative signed clock and can be dropped for an upper. Thus
    R_core <= 11U+E_H,
    E_H <= (7+8/(Y-1)) F_H.
Combining (2),(3) and G_core<=G_all gives the main bound
    11 M_low/(1+eps)+E_H+4Mhat
       >= (1-11beta) G_all+2Z_all-o(NT).               (5)
Here 1-11beta>.934. With finite twins, either Mhat has a positive normalized lower along a subsequence, or on the small-missing branch
    11 M_low/(1+eps)+E_H >= .934 NT+2Z_all-o(NT).
In particular either F_H>=.05 NT or M_low>=.04 NT for sufficiently large X (use Y>=513 and the strict numerical slack). By (4), the second gives n_low>=c NT/lambda, all low crossing labels in (S,Y). These are TAGGED counts, with exact common-event weights; physical unweighted counts not asserted equal. On the first branch each positive-excess high event has w>g/4, hence min eligible neighbor prime r>=w/2>g/8. Its prehorizon has both neighbors free of odd prime divisors <=g/8 (up to equality and eligibility, since p<=B and prehorizon>=2X-2; can use g/9 to avoid boundary). The high long resource is real expensive clock, not simply large bypass gain. It does not follow that these labels are above the cube threshold or have prime cofactors.

For an explicit quantitative rich-block statement define R_Q=11 M_Q/(1+eps)+E_Q. Uniform whole-block upper M_Q<=M_cut(core)<= (1+eps)(G_Q+2lambda), and E_Q<= (7+8/(Y-1))G_Q, so R_Q<=19 G_Q eventually as lambda=o(D) and G_Q>=D-B. Under small missing, total weighted R_Q>=.934NT-o, while G_core=NT+O(o(NT)). Blocks with R_Q<G_Q/4 contribute <=G_core/4. Therefore blocks with R_Q>=G_Q/4 carry >=(.03-o(1))NT tagged gain (indeed (.934-.25)/19 >.035). This identifies a macroscopic population of actual common blocks with arithmetic supply; no demand to prove every block starts shallow. Each such block has either M_Q>= (1+eps)G_Q/88 or E_Q>=G_Q/8. The two possibilities can overlap.

## 4. Positive variation and fast high jumps share the same resource

Let P^+_core=sum b_Q (d'-d)_+ (including both inside/outside old corridors). For high events, 2(d'-d)_+=(4w-2g)_+ <=(4w-g)_+; low positive variation<=5g. Hence
    P^+_core<=5U+E_H/2.
Let G_fast be high gain with 4w<g. Each such event drops d by >g/2. Exact canonical telescope gives total negative variation on all tags <= total positive variation+6N (since d_terminal>=0). Outside cores positive variation <=2W_omit, so
    G_fast/2 <=5U+E_H/2+2W_omit+6N
             <=5 M_low/(1+eps)+5beta G_core+E_H/2
                    +2Mhat+o(NT).                     (6)
This replaces treating large fast bypass jumps and positive variation as independent supplies. It does not show high fast gain is o(NT), because the fixed smoothing beta and both other resources may be macroscopic. One may reduce beta by choosing fixed eps smaller and Y/lambda larger, with constants in count branch changed, not assume a uniform vanishing of M_low. High bypass gain is <=F_H+G_fast; low bypass gain<=U. Thus both positive variation and all bypass gain in the pin-only construction are controlled by low crossings, high excess, and missing budget. Equation (5) is the stronger signed budget and does not require paying these upper estimates again. No additive use of the separate hybrid sieve or XXI different cut family.

## 5. Localized variable-order hybrid transfer

Direct section 2 extends grouped hybrid to arbitrary log Z=o(R) with k0->infinity slowly, support log q_F <=(4k0+6)log Z=o(R), full term period <=sqrt X exp(o(R))=X^(.5+o(1)), same previous full-period Fourier variance. XXI localization applies unchanged on D=X^.7 cores: period o(D), weighted coefficient sum R^O(1), term fluctuations exponentially small in r and relative envelope errors o(1). Thus normalized raw discrepancy o(D) and squared moving-average error o(D), uniformly on all O(D) translated core windows, aggregate o(NT). Unlike 2a47be1 fixed relative-power error, for arbitrary log Z=o(R) the rate depends on k0; no specified O(D/(log R)^A) rate. The smoothing length remains P_R^(4/11); the offset twin radius remains S². Use the hybrid independently if cube-scale expensive label witnesses are needed. No claim that the pin-only and hybrid crossing sets coincide.
