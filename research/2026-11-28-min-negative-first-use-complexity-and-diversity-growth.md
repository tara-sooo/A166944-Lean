## 1. Compress arbitrary repeats using first-use pins, not raw event depth

Write L=log R and let T~sqrt(R) exp(-sqrt(L)). All canonical roots A in [R,2R], 6|A, have spatial positions in I=[R-3T-6,2R] through retirement, by the checked height/gain theorem. Only completed actual firings matter for spatial ancestry. Each firing x->x-p+1 satisfies p|x+-1 at source and p|y or p|(y-2) at arrival y. These are the checked source and incoming spatial pins. All centers are even. A history is a decreasing sequence of event sites; idle ticks do not contribute to the spatial code.

Use a positive even Q, with log Q=O(loglog L), and two variants of the same argument:
- cell variant: base events are those that remain within a Q-cell (cell=floor(x/Q)); all others are marked;
- alphabet variant: base events have p|Q, all others marked. The verified wall lemma implies every base run lies in a Q-cell.
In either case each consecutive base run has gain <Q and at most Q/2 distinct even positions. There is no bound on the total number of base runs. Marked events with p>=Y are HIGH, all other marked events MID. Take Y=T/(log L)^a for fixed 0<a<1. Since Y^3>2R+1 eventually, both source and arrival have at most four candidate HIGH labels (two odd forms, at most two distinct factors >=Y each). Total HIGH count h=O((log L)^a) by canonical gain, including pre-observation.

Split a completed event word at each HIGH event, and optionally at arbitrary free cuts between events. A resulting block has no HIGH event; let m be its number of MID occurrences and k its number of DISTINCT MID labels. Let b=ceil(log_2(m+1)), q=ceil(log_2 Q), and w=ceil(log_2 K), K>=2 a bound on the number of MID candidates at every site in the path. Define the code length in bits
    C_K = 2h + sum_blocks [4+k+b+(k+1)(b+q)+b k(k+1)/2 + k w].       (1)

Construction from either known endpoint, in the corresponding traversal direction:
* Write a 2-bit continuation flag: stop, HIGH separator, or free cut. Write k and b in unary (k+1 and b+1 bits).
* Traverse the block, keeping MID labels in the order of their FIRST use in this traversal direction. The chunks between first uses contain only previously seen MID labels and base events. For chunk j (j=0,...,k) write each of the j known MID occurrence counts in b bits, and the sum of base gains in b+q bits. Each count <=m. The base sum is <(m+1)Q because at most m+1 base runs occur in the block. This is true also for partial chunks. For m=0, b=0 is allowed.
* The chunk's net displacement is reconstructed exactly from these counts and the base sum. From the resulting source (forward) or arrival (backward), the next NEW MID label is specified by its index among ordered candidate odd prime divisors of the appropriate two forms; w bits suffice. The ordered list may include labels not used by the actual word. After a HIGH separator, use two bits for its candidate index; free cut makes no displacement. Then reset the known-label list.

This decodes the opposite endpoint exactly even though it does NOT recover the internal order, clock, or actual least-prime choice of the compressed chunks. Only an upper bound on possible endpoint pairs is claimed. Actual words always admit this code; arbitrary decodings need not be actual paths. Length (1) is independent of traversal direction, and a contiguous subword with inherited cuts has no larger cost (partial block has smaller m,k; delete unneeded separator). In particular if the original word meets a site v, both parts to v have cost <=C_K. The choice of free cuts need not be uniquely determined; all possible descriptions are counted, which is an upper bound on the distinct starting roots. This avoids treating multiple encodings as independent actual trajectories.

## 2. Maximum-degree-resistant bound on low-complexity endpoint pairs

Let D(v)=1+omega(v-2)+omega(v-1)+omega(v)+omega(v+1) on I. It bounds the MID choices in both directions. Let K=ceil(A0 log L) with A0 a sufficiently large fixed constant. The elementary factorial-moment tail from the preceding reverse-depth note gives
    # {v in I:D(v)>=j} <= C R exp(-j), j>=K.                         (2)
For clarity, sum_{p<=2R+1}1/p=O(log L) by Euler product at s=1+1/log(2R+1), and count integers with omega>=r by selecting r distinct divisors, <=(2R+1)(C log L)^r/r!. One of the four counts is >=(j-1)/4; choose A0 large. Constants independent of R.

Fix a budget B=floor(beta log_2 L), beta>0 fixed, and endpoint set S. If a word of cost <=B stays at sites with D<=K, starting from its endpoint it has at most sum_{i<=B}2^i<2^{B+1} possible binary descriptions. Each description decodes one initial site. Consequently the number of distinct pairs (initial site, endpoint in S) in this class is <=2^{B+1}|S|. Per single endpoint this also bounds the possible canonical roots; no product over paths or time bins.

For an exceptional path, choose a visited site v of MAXIMUM degree d=D(v)>K. Count both parts using v as the known endpoint. Each part admits cost <=B at width w; changing to width ceil(log_2 d) adds at most J(ceil(log_2 d)-w) bits, J=floor(B/w), since its total number of new MID indices is <=J. Every relevant visited site in either direction has degree <=d (choose max over ALL sites, not just the original traversal's marked sites). Each part has at most 2^{B+1}(2d/K)^J possible descriptions. Thus over all bad vertices, the number of distinct pairs is at most
    2^{2B+2} sum_{v:D(v)>K}(2D(v)/K)^{2J}.
This is O(R L^{-C0}) for any fixed C0>0 after increasing A0. Indeed J=O(log L/loglog L), and the ratio of k^{2J}e^{-k} at successive k>=K is <=exp(2J/(k+1)-1)<e^{-1/2} eventually. Sum using (2), and K=A0 log L absorbs the fixed powers of L. This argument includes intermediate sites in a base run. Counting arbitrary spatial starts or multiple cuts only overcounts. Therefore
    P_B(S) <= 2^{B+1}|S| + O(R L^{-C0}).                            (3)
P_B counts distinct spatial endpoint pairs admitting a partition of cost <=B along an actual history, not paths with repetitions. Unlike a raw event-depth bound, (3) allows arbitrary repeats whenever they can be encoded with sufficiently small count fields. K is chosen first for beta,C0; no limit A0->infinity with R.

## 3. Independent dimension-two upper sieve, including uniform rough frontier

This is an independent paper derivation of the particular upper-sieve inputs used below. It does not verify all six-/multi-form weighted good-phase scans. No lower sieve or parity-breaking theorem is used.

For an integer interval of length X with two distinct forbidden residue classes modulo each odd prime p<=D, for squarefree d composed of these primes the forbidden-product divisibility count is X g(d)+O(2^{omega(d)}), g(d)=2^{omega(d)}/d, by CRT. Put H(d)=prod_{p|d}g(p)/(1-g(p)), G(D)=sum_{d<=D}H(d) over such squarefree d. Choose Selberg weights lambda_d supported on d<=D, lambda_1=1. Squared divisor sum majorizes the sifted indicator. The main quadratic form diagonalizes as
    sum_{d,e}lambda_d lambda_e g(lcm(d,e))
      = sum_r H(r)^(-1) u_r^2,
    u_r=sum_{d:r|d}lambda_d g(d).
This follows by expanding 1/g(gcd(d,e))=sum_{r|gcd}1/H(r). By Mobius inversion, lambda_1=sum_r mu(r)u_r. Set u_r=mu(r)H(r)/G(D), invert, and obtain main form 1/G(D), lambda_1=1 and
    |lambda_d| <= H(d)/g(d)=prod_{p|d}(1-2/p)^(-1)<=d.
Consequently absolute total CRT error is O(D^6) (use 2^{omega(lcm)}<=de, then sum d^2 e^2). To see G(D)>>log^2 D, restrict to primes p<=D^eta, eta small fixed. Normalize their product weights prod(1+H(p))=prod(1-2/p)^(-1), so independent inclusion has probability 2/p. Expected log of product is sum_{p<=D^eta}2log p/p=O(eta log D) by elementary Chebyshev theta bound and partial summation. Choose eta sufficiently small and Markov gives at least half of the product weight on d<=D. The full product is >>log^2 D since (1-2/p)^(-1)>=(1-1/p)^(-2) and Euler product over primes <=z majorizes harmonic sum through z (omit factor 2 at fixed cost). Take D=floor(X^{1/12}) and obtain O(X/log^2 X) uniformly whenever the roughness cutoff contains D and the two residues are distinct for every odd p<=D. More generally take D=floor(min(X^{1/12},Z)) for any growing roughness cutoff Z; gives O(X/log^2 D + X^{1/2}). This proof does not assume distribution of primes in progressions. Floor effects absorbed in constants.

Apply first to centers x with both x+-1 prime in I, omitting bounded small values: O(R/L^2). Next to H/2-rough centers for H=T/L^C or H=T L^{-O(1)} (fixed powers), log H = L/2 - sqrt L - O(log L). Each non-twin center has one composite neighbor qr, primes H/2<q<=r, q<=sqrt(2R+1), since (H/2)^3>2R+1. The other neighbor qr+-2 is H/2-rough. For each q use variable r<=X=(2R+1)/q, sift r and qr+-2 by odd primes <=D=X^{1/12}; D<H/2 and D<q. These forms have two distinct forbidden classes for every odd prime <=D, regardless of q. Count O(R/(q L^2)). Dyadic subdivision and elementary Chebyshev prime counting give sum_{H/2<q<=sqrt(2R+1)}1/q=O((1+log(sqrt(2R+1)/(H/2)))/L)=O(L^{-1/2}). Thus
    |S_H| <= twinCount(I)+O(R L^{-2.5}).                            (4)
This includes both sides composite, squares, and boundary centers. For arbitrary earlier t=exp(L^gamma), 0<gamma<1 fixed, H=t L^{-O(1)} or H=t exp(-o(log L)), use the direct two-form sieve x+-1 with D=min(R^{1/12},H/2), obtaining |S_H|=O(R L^{-2gamma}). Neither estimate is a lower bound. These proofs supply an independent check of the rough-center upper estimate, not of the entire old GoodFieldEstimate.

## 4. Unconditional lower on actual spatial description cost at near-square time

If a canonical root's completed prefix by age floor(T/2) has a partition of cost <=B, then there are <=B+1 blocks, <=B high events, and every m<=2^B. Its total marked event count <=O((B+1)2^B). Hence the number of spatial phases <=Q/2 times one plus this count: every base run lies in one cell. Since Q is polylogarithmic in L, there is a quiet interval of length H>=T L^{-C} for some fixed C depending on beta (firing ticks themselves are negligible). By actual calendar wait <=2p for each odd neighbor factor, its center lies in S_H. Take the path from birth up to that phase; inherited code cost <=B. Count distinct roots by (3),(4). For fixed beta>0 and C0:
    # {A: some partition with C_K<=floor(beta log_2 L) by T/2}
     <= O(L^beta twinCount(I) + R L^{beta-2.5} + R L^{-C0}).        (5)
Unconditionally beta<2 gives o(R), using twin upper O(R/L^2). Under finitely many twins beta<2.5 gives o(R). Thus almost all canonical roots require > (2-o(1)) log_2 L bits in this specific bidirectional code at near-square time (or (2.5-o(1)) under finite twins, interpreted via every fixed beta below the threshold). This is a lower on the defined admissible code budget, not a Kolmogorov complexity claim. Neither code nor bound says that a high-cost root has large actual gain.

For fixed partition rule using HIGH separators only, code cost increases along prefixes (last m,k increase, new block adds positive cost). Restrict to prefixes with C_K<=B and no visited exceptional site D>K. For any physical observation bin there are <=2^{B+1} possible canonical roots by (3)'s nonexceptional part. If beta<2.1 this is o(M), so the restriction is cap-safe including pre-observation. The raw occupation restricted to low cost is small under finite twins for beta<2.25: choose H'=T L^{-a'} with a'>beta+.25 (plus fixed slack). Short phases contribute at most O(Q(B+1) L^{beta-a'}), normalized by NT. Each long phase has rough site by calendar and contributes <=T per (A,x); (3),(4) bound all such pairs including exceptional ones. Therefore
    eta_code <= O(L^beta twinCount/N + L^{beta-2.5}
                  + Q(B+1)L^{beta-a'} + L^{-C0}).
Under finite twins this is o(L^-.25) for beta<2.25 and sufficiently large a'. Unconditionally beta<1.75 cannot provide L^-.25 even with infinite twins. This is a genuine extension of prior depth/cap result to paths with many repeated events and variable distinct support; it is still an upper bound, not a construction of the needed good-phase lower.

## 5. Consequence for diversity inside high-free blocks (not just across entire history)

Reuse the already checked finite-support theorem: a relaxed decreasing word with at most s odd labels has event count <=F(s), with log(F(s)+2)<=C(s!)^2 (prior quantitative support note 943848e...). Use the alphabet variant and EXACT old slow-growing cutoff
    z=Ddiv=max(3,floor(loglog L/(16 logloglog L))), Q=2 product_{3<=p<=z}p.
Here s0=# odd primes<=z=o(loglog L/logloglog L) by Chebyshev; log Q=O(loglog L). This is compatible with the existing low-history scan, not a new fixed small-base assumption.

Suppose every block between HIGH events has at most
    k0=floor(c loglog L/logloglog L)
distinct nonbase labels, where fixed 0<c<(1-a)/2 and HIGH p>=Y=T/(log L)^a. Then total labels in each block <=s0+k0. Its MID occurrence count <=F(s0+k0); by Stirling, log(F+2)<=(log L)^{2c+o(1)}. In (1), b<=O(log F), k<=k0, w=O(loglog L), q=O(loglog L), h=O((log L)^a); the total bit cost is
    C_K <= (log L)^{a+2c+o(1)}=o(log L).
Apply (5) at any fixed beta>0 (or track 2^{B+1}=L^{o(1)} directly in (3)): at most O(R L^{-2+o(1)}) roots can have all blocks this sparse. More precisely, for every eta>0 the exception count is O_eta(R L^{-2+eta}), and under finite twins O_eta(R L^{-2.5+eta}). Thus almost all actual roots have a HIGH-free block, already by T/2, containing >c loglog L/logloglog L DISTINCT nonbase primes <Y. In-cell repeats and all allowed large events may occur. This is a diversity lower inside a single block, stronger than the preceding lower on total intermediate-crossing count in that particular respect. It does not force all these primes to exceed the old medium threshold exp(L^.1).

## 6. Apply the free-cut code to the original low-chunk counter at earlier scales

Now t=floor(exp(L^gamma)/4), any fixed 0<gamma<=.10; Y0=ceil(exp(L^.10)). By checked height label bound p<=2t+7<Y0 for all actual firings up to age t (large R), so there are NO >=Y0 resets yet. The old greedy low chunk counter cuts at each (Ddiv+1)-st DISTINCT narrow label z<p<Y0, including the trigger in the completed chunk. This is exactly the free-cut version of the code. Every chunk, including unfinished last one, uses <=Ddiv+1 distinct nonbase labels plus base. Each has at most F(s0+Ddiv+1) events. Hence log F <=(log L)^{1/8+o(1)}, and bit cost per chunk <=(log L)^{1/8+o(1)}. If the number of completed saturations is <=(log L)^u for fixed u<7/8, there are <=1+(log L)^u chunks and
    C_K <=(log L)^{u+1/8+o(1)}=o(log L).
Use (3) with budget beta log_2 L and the earlier-horizon version of the quiet-phase argument: t=exp(L^gamma)/4 is far bigger than any power of L; a prefix of cost <=B has a quiet interval >=t/L^{O(1)}. The direct sieve in section 3 bounds its rough endpoint set by O(R L^{-2gamma}). Therefore, for each fixed u<7/8 and eta>0,
    # {A: completed low saturations up to age t <= (log L)^u}
       = O_{u,eta,gamma}(R L^{-2gamma+eta})                    (6)
(take beta<eta, C0 large). In particular almost all canonical roots have more than (log L)^{7/8-o(1)} completed diversity saturations in this sense, at any such fixed subexponential age. The original nonempty chunk counter Jlow is at least the completed-saturation count. This is an unconditional quantitative lower on an ACTUAL history statistic used by the weighted criterion; not a product-residue simulation. Its direction is opposite to the cheap-history upper sought in that criterion. Its cost after multiplying by (B_support+1)Y1/T remains negligible at these scales; (6) does not contradict cheap survival. It shows why simply restricting to very sparse narrow supports across an entire prefix will not give a substantial population.

## 7. Further target check

Sections 4-6 strengthen compressed ancestry and show required diversity; they do not imply a positive lower on G_M/(NT) at T~sqrt(R)exp(-sqrt L), or a square-root low-gain witness. In particular, large encoding cost does not imply large physical multiplicity, small gain, or a good phase. The good-field upper scan has its own multi-form/terminal transfer obligations, not discharged by the independent two-form argument in section 3. There is no proof of level 4.85 or twin infinitude.
