## 1. Actual translation lemma and first defect

Keep the original prime-root gain-stopped MIN paths, including terminal snapshots; X dyadic, B=ceil(sqrt(7X)), u=log B, ell=1+log u, T=floor(X/u^.25), H=T+B, N=Theta(X/u), Q=ceil(B/u), F=T²Bu. A completed actual edge (C,t)->(C+p-1,s) has selected odd prime p, actual wait w=s-t>0, r(C)<=p and w<=2r(C)<=3(p-1). Candidate ticks for a fixed p|C-1 are 0 mod 2p and for p|C+1 are p+2 mod 2p. The MIN choice is lexicographic minimum (next tick, p). A candidate at an earlier positive waiting tick than an actual completed tick lies before its original cutoff, so comparisons between actual completed edges do not require continuation beyond any stop.

Suppose two original paths visit a common horizon C at actual times t,t'=t+Delta and both complete outgoing edges, of labels p,q respectively. If 2p|Delta and 2q|Delta, their respective candidate progressions translate by Delta. The candidate (s,p) on the first path gives (s+Delta,p) on the second, and (s',q) on the second gives (s'-Delta,q) on the first. Both have positive waits. Lexicographic minimality in the two directions forces s'=s+Delta and p=q. In particular their waits are identical and their next horizons are equal. Conversely if p=q and their actual exit lag equals their input lag, that lag is a multiple of 2p because the sign of eligibility at C is unique. Thus the EXACT condition for a common projected edge with unchanged lag is that both completed outgoing labels have periods dividing the input lag.

Starting at any common horizon with fixed nonzero Delta, take the maximal consecutive translated prefix of actual common edges, stopping if either original path stops. If there is a first defect (both still complete but fail the condition), it is either a projected divergence, with at least one of its two periods not dividing Delta, or a common projected edge whose lag changes. A terminal on either path is NOT a first defect. All labels along the translated prefix divide Delta/2 when Delta is even; in particular if |Delta|<=4H and Q³>2H, at most two distinct high labels can occur there. These are deterministic assertions, not an assumption that different physical paths cannot share many projected edges after the lag has changed.

For any FIXED y>=3, let P_y=2 product_{3<=p<=y prime} p. If Delta=0 mod P_y at the start, every all-small step (both selected primes <=y) is a translated step. Hence a first defect necessarily uses a selected label >y on at least one side. In particular a primitive projected diamond containing only labels <=y on BOTH sublists cannot start at equal phases modulo P_y. Such diamonds can occur later after an earlier defect; the condition is at the actual start of the diamond, not the initial high hit.

## 2. Fixed primorial phase energy reduction and a bounded-gain first defect

Apply the previous exit-rank linear mask, with arbitrary additional incidence deletions. At fixed C and selected high p, retained physical prevertices correspond injectively to actual exit ticks s. Write n(C,p,s) for their retained mass. Partition by s mod P_y. At fixed C,p the exit parity is fixed, so at most P_y/2 phases occur. At C there are eventually at most four possible high selected primes. By two Cauchy applications,
    E_high <= 2 P_y E_phase(y),
    E_phase(y)=sum_{C,p,a mod P_y} r(C)(sum_{s=a mod P_y} n(C,p,s))²
              = E_phys + E_async,phase(y).                  (1)
The diagonal/synchronous term is the full physical energy of the retained set, and the remaining products are exactly same-p distinct physical buckets with equal exit phase. This is an energy upper bound, NOT an absolute estimate of different-phase products. For fixed y and sufficiently large X, p>=Q>y, so gcd(2p,P_y)=2. Every retained distinct-bucket phase pair has nonzero exit lag divisible by lcm(2p,P_y)=pP_y; hence |s'-s|/(2p)>=P_y/2. Choose a fixed y with P_y/2>K for any prescribed fixed K; the entire async phase term is then in the earlier far sector. One may choose any fixed y (however large) before X tends to infinity. The factor 2P_y must not be treated as a constant if y grows with X; no rate is supplied for that use.

In the combined analytic route after the macroblock density and future-room linear masks of lag-witness-crt, each retained high incidence has at least TWO different natural high future labels distinct from the current p completed within gain s0+B, s0=ceil(B^1.5), from the original prehorizon. For a nonzero same-label exit lag Delta, the current p divides Delta/2. Suppose neither a projected divergence nor lag change occurred in the translated prefix before the two future labels of either path; both labels would divide Delta/2 too, impossible since Q³>2H. More precisely, if one path stops before a first defect, its own two witnesses were completed, and if the prefix spans s0+B beyond the original high prehorizon, both paths have completed those witnesses within the span. Thus the first defect is an actual completed comparison at a common horizon within gain s0+B from the original high prehorizon. For phase-equal pairs this first defect contains at least one label >y. This removes pure small-label mismatches from the first-defect classification of the sufficient phase residual; it does not force the first defect to be a natural high event. The finite audit below does NOT combine the macro density and future-room masks, so terminal-before-defect is allowed there.

## 3. A broad close-prime support bound: prime pairs plus horizon CRT

Take Y=ceil(u²), J=max(1,floor(ell^1/8)), kappa=max(2,floor(ell^1/4)); asymptotically 5<=Y<Q, J=o(Y). More generally use Y tending to infinity, J=o(Y), log(2+J)=o(log Y) in the sieve below. Unlike projected-diamond-halos, a centre is ANY actual original selected (C,p) with Y<=p<=B, with no condition on r(C) or p>=Q. Call it bad if another actual centre (E,q) has p!=q, |p-q|<=J, |C-E|<=J. The previous high-only estimate used the short q interval bound O(J) and the reciprocal prime sum on [Q,B]; simply replacing Q by Y there would lose the saving. Instead exploit that BOTH selected labels are prime.

For each fixed integer d with 1<=d<=J, estimate the reciprocal sum over primes p,p+d in [Y,B]. Odd d gives no such pair since Y>=5. In a dyadic label interval V<=p<2V with V>=Y/2, use the two-form Selberg upper sieve for n,n+d. Sieve primes l in (max(2,J),z] with z=floor(V^1/16) exclude exactly two residues modulo l, since l does not divide d. For squarefree a<=z supported on those primes put g(a)=prod_{l|a} 2/l, h(a)=prod_{l|a}(l-2)/2. The quadratic main term diagonalizes as sum_l h(l) Y_l², Y_l=sum_{l|a} lambda_a g(a). The inverse minimizing choice Y_l=mu(l)/(h(l) G(z)), G(z)=sum_{a<=z}1/h(a), has lambda_1=1 and |lambda_a|<=prod_{l|a}(1-2/l)^(-1)<=a. Thus the remainder in the quadratic count is O(z^6): for mod lcm(a,b) the number of forbidden classes is 2^omega(lcm(a,b))<=ab, and sum_{a,b<=z} a²b²=O(z^6). For the lower bound on G(z), use the full product over allowed primes l<=z^c for a fixed sufficiently small c>0. By elementary Mertens bounds this product is >> (log z/log(2+J))². In its normalized squarefree distribution inclusion probabilities are 2/l, so the expected log of the product integer is O(c log z)<=.5 log z; Markov retains at least half the mass on integers <=z. Thus G(z)>> (log V/log(2+J))². Here J is smaller than z^c eventually uniformly in V, by log(2+J)=o(log Y); for our choices it is polylogarithmic in Y. The remainder z^6<=V^3/8 is absorbed by V/log² V. This proves
    #{V<=p<2V: p,p+d prime} << V (log(2+J))²/(log V)²
uniformly in d. Dividing by V and summing dyadic intervals from Y upward,
    sum_{Y<=p<=B, p,p+d prime} 1/p
         << (log(2+J))² / log Y.                          (2)
This proof uses an upper sieve, not an asymptotic count of prime pairs or the twin-prime conjecture.

For fixed distinct p,q, shift h=E-C and signs, the congruences C+sigma=0 mod p, C+h+tau=0 mod q give O(X/(pq)) horizons, since pq<=B²=O(X). Weight by p+q. Since J=o(Y), q comparable to p. Sum over p<q=p+d, d<=J, |h|<=J, and signs. From (2),
    sum_{distinct bad centres} p
      <= sum_{conflicting centre pairs} (p+q)
      << X (J+1)² (log(2+J))² / log Y.                   (3)
The arithmetic support is relaxed to ALL integer horizons; no primality or independence of the source roots is used in (3). This is a bound on distinct key weight, not on their multiplicity.

Each completed centre has O(T/u) original root tags by the prime interval upper bound. For each original source mark every completed gain interval fully contained in one of its actual bad-centre intervals [C-kappa p,C+kappa p]. The total marked gain is at most 2kappa sum of its bad-centre selected labels, by disjoint gain intervals; waits and proxy are at most 3 and 1.5 times gain. Sum (3) over source multiplicity:
    W_halo, R_halo
       << NT kappa (J+1)² (log(2+J))²/log Y
       = O(NT (1+log ell)²/sqrt ell)=o(NT).              (4)
This replaces the restriction to natural high centres in the previous halo construction. The general sufficient condition for this new saving is kappa (J+1)² log²(2+J)=o(log Y), under the sieve hypotheses. The all-event halo is paid linearly on original completed events; no whole pair tail, arbitrary attachment, or entire quota block is inferred to be paid without its own accounting.

Consider a primitive projected diamond [A,D] with exactly one selected label >=Y on each side, p,q, and other gain b_a,b_b<=J. If p!=q, L=D-A=p-1+b_a=q-1+b_b implies |p-q|<=J and the two large prehorizons lie in [A,A+J]. Both keys are bad and the entire respective diamond intervals are contained in their own kappa>=2 halos. Thus ALL actual completed events on both sides, including diamonds containing no high event at all, are marked. This is a uniform extension to medium-scale labels, not just a change in a finite diagnostic threshold. Unlike the preceding rough-high proof, p=q is possible without r>3, so must be treated separately.

## 4. Equal-label small-background diamonds: return phase separation

In the same setup with p=q>=Y and background <=J<p-1, the distinct large prehorizons C,E must obey E-C=0,+/-2 mod 2p. Since |E-C|<=J<p-1, they differ by exactly 2 and have opposite divisibility signs. Their actual large exits s_a,s_b have opposite parity and s_b-s_a=+/-(p+2) mod 2p. From each large postvertex to the common projected return D the actual remaining wait is e_a or e_b with 0<=e_i<=3J. The return lag Delta_D=(s_b+e_b)-(s_a+e_a) therefore satisfies
    dist(Delta_D, 2p Z) >= p-2-3J.                      (5)
A sharper individual bound is p-2-|e_b-e_a|. For p>=Y and J=o(Y), the right side of (5) is positive eventually. These diamonds do not end in physical synchronization; they return to the same projected horizon but opposite, separated phase neighborhoods. They are NOT paid by (4). If additionally the initial lag is divisible by 2p and by P_y with y>=J+1 (so all background labels <=y), the full two lists would be translated edge by edge by section 1, contradicting a primitive diamond. Therefore such a diamond starting at equal small phase must have initial lag not divisible by 2p, or at least one background label not included in the fixed phase modulus. This last statement is conditional on the small-phase equality and on the indicated y; no growing y is required in (1).
