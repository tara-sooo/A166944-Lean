## 1. Why a density-scale top supply must use later events

Write L=log R, T=ceil(8 sqrt(2R+1)), Y=T/L². As before, canonical birth(A) for even A divisible by6 has height3, signed clock 3-A/2, height h=x/2+n. For a fixed C>0, all actual histories from A in [R,2R] through age CT stay in [R-2CT-6,2R], h in [0,CT+3], winner p<=2CT+7.

At a non-twin canonical birth the first winning p satisfies p<=4 qmin(A)+7 by w<=2qmin and the canonical height bound, where qmin=min(P^-(A-1),P^-(A+1)). Thus first top event p>=Y requires qmin(A)>= (Y-7)/4, comparable to sqrtR/L². The non-twin rough frontier estimate in 2026-11-27-min-local-negative-square-root-horizon-and-rough-frontier.md section2 applies at this cutoff (constant factors do not alter the log-width). Indeed at least one neighbor is a semiprime with least factor q between cY and sqrt(2R+1), and the other neighbor and the cofactor are rough. The uniform two-form upper per q is O(R/(qL²)), while reciprocal prime sum over that thin logarithmic band is O(log L/L). Hence the number of canonical roots with an actual first top firing during age CT is
    O(R log L/L³) = o(R/L²).                              (1)
A twin birth cannot fire during CT since both labels are of order R, beyond the canonical age bound. This is a genuine obstruction to getting R/L² top keys just from first exits, even if one somehow proves a new lower sieve at that frontier. Later top firings may have much shorter full waits and smaller qmin because the height has accumulated.

## 2. Two-sided local top keys: statement and exact pins

Put P=Y. Select p prime in [P,2P], p=1 mod420, v prime in [(R+1)/p,(2R+1)/p], v=1 mod420. Let
    a=pv-1,   x=p(v-1),   n=mp-x/2,
where m is even and T/(4P)<=m<=T/(3P). There are asymptotically a positive constant times L² choices of m, uniformly. Since p,v=1 mod420, a divisible6, x divisible420 and n=0 mod2p. At the intended firing clock n, p is eligible at a, its destination x has height mp in [T/4,2T/3]. The height of the source at this clock would be mp+(p-1)/2. Both are positive and of order sqrtR. The source is non-twin since a+1=pv with v>>p. These are high-height physical states; the local starting state (a,n-U) below is NOT birth(a).

Fix sufficiently small constant kappa>0 (chosen after a fixed sieve approximation parameter, below .0001), and let H0 satisfy L<=H0<=R^kappa/(2L^10). Put K0=ceil(H0 L^10)<=R^kappa. There are two versions:
  A: K=R^.12, require P^-(pv-2)>=K, P^-(x±1)>=K0, and force a prime r in [R^kappa,R^(2kappa)] dividing x+1.
  B: K=R^.08, same requirements plus force a prime lambda in [R^.081,R^.082] dividing pv-2. Then K<=qmin(a)<=lambda, and the last preceding eligible source clock is at most 2lambda ticks before n.
Both versions use auxiliary r not dividing (p-1)(p-3), via v=1-p^(-1) mod r. The v residue is reduced and pv-2=p-3 mod r is nonzero. In B use the additional reduced residue v=2/p mod lambda. The auxiliary ranges are disjoint from each other, p, fixed primes and the small sieve. They force x+1 and (in B) pv-2 composite. The intended destination x is non-twin. The fixed progression avoids 2,3,5,7 in all three forbidden forms pv-2,x-1,x+1. No assumption of twin infinitude is used.

Let U=floor(K/L^10). We prove for every sufficiently large R that, in either version, at least
    J >> R/[L (log K0)^2]                               (2)
distinct local firing keys of this form have the exact recurrence from the noncanonical state (a,n-U): it stays at a through n-1, fires p at n, and stays at x through n+H0. In particular this is a real recurrence segment with two quiet parts and a near-square firing, with no relaxation of the minimum-label rule. It is not a canonical trajectory lower.

## 3. Lower supply by a split sieve at p~sqrtR/L²

Fix p and let X=R/p, interval I for v of length X, with X~sqrtR L². Fix r (and lambda in version B). Use ordinary BV for the prime variable v, with max over arbitrary reduced residue classes. The modulus B is 420r in A, 420r lambda in B.

For free primes 11<=ell<K0 forbid zeros of pv-2, p(v-1)-1, p(v-1)+1 among reduced v classes. There are nu(ell)<=3 such classes, with g1=nu/(ell-1)<1 and uniform one-sided dimension-three product. Its local product V1>>1/(log K0)^3 by Mertens and the summable O(1/ell²) corrections. For free primes K0<=ell<K forbid only pv-2, local g2=1/(ell-1), V2>>log K0/L. Exclude p and r, lambda where appropriate (p and lambda>K, r>=K0 and source form nonzero modulo r). Fixed factors already avoided. Since r>=K0, the forced target composite does not invalidate the smaller target cutoff.

Choose in version A small level D1=R^.004, large D2=R^.241. In version B use D1=R^.002, D2=R^.161. Thus log D2/log K>2 with a fixed margin in either version. For a fixed sufficiently small relative error eta, choose kappa small enough that log D1/log K0 is sufficiently large for the ordinary fixed dimension-three combinatorial fundamental lemma.     X_plus >=(1-eta) Xstar,  0<= X_diff <=2eta Xstar,

Since I_small>=U1-(U1-L1), the difference of the resulting sifted sums bounds the true joint count from below. The lower main coefficient f(s2)>0 and bounded upper F(s2) give positive c V2 Xstar uniformly by choosing eta sufficiently small before kappa, allowing ordinary o(1) errors. This uses the one-sided product hypotheses for the truncated groups, not a dimension-three lower sieve all the way to K.

For remainders expand the small weights and then sum absolute large-sieve errors over the auxiliary primes.     A: m0 <= 420 R^(.245+2kappa);

Both are below X^(.5-eps0) for fixed eps0>0 and sufficiently large R, since kappa<.0001 and X~sqrtR L². Weighted BV gives O_A(X/L^A) for any fixed A, uniformly for this p and all the class selections. ## 4. Make the keys actual local corridors, not merely eligible p's

For any odd prime q eligible at an even center y and absolute tick tau, the exact congruences imply
    q | 2(tau+y/2)+1-2b_s,  b_+=0,b_-=3,                (3)
where s is clock sign. This holds for ANY eligible q, not just the winner: for plus tau=0 modq,y=-1 modq; for minus tau=2 modq,y=1 modq.

At source a and tick n-u, for 0<=u<=U, (3) becomes
    q | (2m+1)p-2u-2b_s.                               (4)
Apart from p at u=0, any eligible q is distinct from p (its period is 2p>U, and p does not divide a-1). It is >=K by source roughness. The integer in (4) is positive, O(T), with O(L/log K) prime divisors >=K. For fixed p,m,u,b_s and such q, spatial eligibility a+s=0 modq prescribes one class for v modulo q. Since q<=O(T) and X~sqrtR L²>>T, even the count of all integers v in I is O(X/q). This ignores all other sieve constraints, hence gives an upper. Summing u,m,p and signs bounds the number of bad local keys with any premature or competing source eligibility by
    O(R L (U+1) L/(K log K)) = O(R/L^9).
Here the factor R L comes from sum_p X times O(L²) m choices, and log K~L. We excluded competitors at u=0 as well, a stronger condition than necessary. This proves p is the actual winner at n and there is no source firing in the preceding U ticks of the local interval.

At target x and tick n+u with 1<=u<=H0 the corresponding divisor condition is
    q | 2mp+2u+1-2b_s.                                 (5)
Any eligible q>=K0 and q!=p since p|x. Again the integer is positive O(T) and the spatial pin gives one class v modq. The count of keys with any such postarrival interruption is bounded by
    O(R L H0 L/(K0 log K0)) = O(R/L^8).
Both exception sets are o(R/L³) and hence o(2) since log K0<=kappa L. Remove them. Every remaining key satisfies the exact literal recurrence asserted in section2: no eligible label in the source interval, p is sole eligible at the chosen tick, no eligible label in the target interval. This uses no statistical independence or unverified reachability.

## 5. Order-free two-sided separation and extension of connectors

Represent either anchor point at clock n by (position,n,k,p,c): source uses a, k=2m+1, 2h=kp-1, position=-1 modp; destination uses x,k=2m, 2h=kp, position=0 modp. For two anchors of distinct keys, if positions differ by |d|<=D and clocks by |e|<=H, then
    |kp-k'q|<=2H+D+1.
For p=q and 2H+D+1<P, k=k'; parity identifies the type, spatial congruence forces d=0 if D<P, then e=0 and keys equal, a contradiction. Otherwise p!=q. For fixed p,k,k' (both O(L²)), possible integers q lie in an interval with O(H+D+1) integers; the two position residues give one CRT class mod pq per offset d and choices of types. Count O(1+R/(pq))=O(L^4) positions, with clocks determined by heights. Summing at most O(T) p's (ignore primality), O(L^4) pairs k,k', offsets and q candidates yields
    E_two(D,H) << T (D+1)(H+D+1) L^8.
Take D=H=W=floor(R^.25/L^7). Then E_two<<R/L^6=o(2), including all four source/destination type combinations. Delete both keys of each such pair. Since U,H0=o(W), the pre and post local quiet intervals of the remaining keys are pairwise disjoint physical-state sets (not necessarily distinct positions). They have at least J(U+H0+1) distinct states. Moreover following the actual deterministic suffix from each destination up to W/2 additional ticks or before its first displacement exceeding W/2 (exclude crossing landing) still yields disjoint physical states even from other keys' preintervals. Indeed any common state forces the two relevant source/destination anchor points within W in both coordinates. The large jump itself, of size p-1>>W, does not cause a contradiction, since we use its two endpoints separately rather than incorrectly regarding all skipped sites as visited. Thus this improves 7e90c0f from post-only to two-sided isolation for these local keys.

Examples: H0=ceil(L^a), fixed a>=1 gives J>>R/[L(log L)^2], and H0=floor(R^delta), fixed 0<delta<kappa, gives J>>R/L³. The former exceeds the first-top upper (1) by an unbounded factor. This counts distinct local keys, not distinct canonical roots. Even a single canonical root might complete O(L²) top firings in a CT horizon.

## 6. New arbitrary-history rapid top-return capacity

We can also bound pairs of actual top firing keys linked by an intervening short block. This is distinct from two post-anchor contacts: the first destination is near the SECOND SOURCE, so the large second jump is not included in the connector displacement. Let previous top arrival at y, clock n-u, label q, sign t, height j q+b_t, and current top arrival at x, clock n, label p, sign s, height m p+b_s. Current source a=x+p-1. Suppose
    0<=d=y-a<=D,  1<=u<=H.
This covers two successive top events with any number of intervening lower-label events whose total displacement is d, and also no intervening events. All labels >=Y, both times within arbitrary canonical histories of age CT. The exact relation is
    (2m+1)p-2j q = 2u+1-d+2b_t-2b_s.                  (6)
If 2H+D+7<Y, then j>0 even when m=0, as the right-hand side is smaller than p. If D<Y-3, p=q is impossible: a=-s modp and y=c_t=1-t modp would require even d congruent to one of -1,1,3, none possible in that range. Thus p!=q, and for fixed p,m,j the possible q lie in an interval of length O(H+D+1). The CRT a=-s modp, a+d=c_t modq determines one class per d, with O(L^4) positions; heights determine clocks. The same bound as section5 therefore gives
    E_return(D,H) <<_C T (D+1)(H+D+1) L^8.             (7)
It even bounds potential pairs satisfying these necessary pins in the age strip, without reachability. No slow-wait assumption needed, no constraint on intervening event count or labels. In particular with D=0,H=floor(T/L^12) or D=floor(L²),H=floor(T/L^14), this is O(R/L^4)=o(R/L²). For the original cap M~L^2.1 and full cohort N~R, retiring actual paths at the later key of such rapid returns costs at most O(M T E_return) future observed ticks (O(1) standard window copies/key), normalized O(L^-1.9)=o(L^-.25). Consequently any retained top-to-top recurrence of this form must exceed either the elapsed-time cutoff or the intervening displacement cutoff. This does not bound ALL top recurrences or guarantee many top events are reached. The pair theorem is unconditional for arbitrary actual canonical histories and is a new reusable upper independent of the local supply.

## 7. Reduce canonical occupation to a recent SMALL-label incoming port

For a key retained in version B, the forced lambda|a-1, lambda<=R^.082, guarantees that the latest eligible calendar tick nu_* STRICTLY before n at source a satisfies n-2lambda<=nu_*<n. It is computed using all divisors of a±1, with no supposition that the source is occupied at that earlier tick. Since no eligibility after nu_* before n, the actual incumbent at a at n-1 must have arrived at some tau in [nu_*,n-1]: if it were present at nu_*-1, it would have moved away at nu_* and never returned. The canonical birth of a itself occurred at n-[mp+(p-1)/2-3], strictly before nu_* since mp~T>>lambda. Hence ANY canonical root realizing this top firing must have entered a by a moving event at tau in that interval. To realize the full preinterval starting n-U, require tau<=n-U. This is also true for version A with 2p replacing 2lambda, but the much shorter window in B is useful.

If an incoming prime q at tau=n-u has sign t, then q divides a-c_t and the postheight pin implies
    q | (2m+1)p-1-2u-2b_t.                             (8)
Here 1<=u<=2R^.082. Necessarily q!=p since a=-1 modp,c_t=0 or2. For any q>=Z=R^.09, for fixed p,m,u,b_t the positive integer in (8) is O(T) and has O(1) prime factors >=Z. Its spatial pin fixes v mod q, with O(X/q) possible integers. Summing p,m,u gives a bound on ALL local keys admitting such a large incoming pin of
    O(R L R^.082 / R^.09)=O(R^.992 L)=o(R/L³).         (9)
This counts potential incoming pins, so no earlier actual history is assumed. Remove these keys as well as sections4-5 exceptions. Lower (2) remains unchanged in version B. For every remaining key, if any canonical history reaches it, the immediate incoming event into its source must have prime q<R^.09 and occur at most 2R^.082 ticks earlier. Its previous position is a+q-1, thus within R^.09 of a. The prior height is still of order T. This is a substantial reduction of the actual reachability problem: near-square top events can be sought through a short, sub-.09-scale small-label entry problem at high height rather than a second top anchor. It is not a lower bound on the occupation of those prior states.

Exact entry-port formulation: enumerate tau in [nu_*,n-1], odd primes q|a-c_t of the appropriate clock sign, q<Z, with the incoming clock congruence. Test the actual winner at (a+q-1,tau-1) equals q. Such a state is an actual entry port: its next state is (a,tau), and the recurrence then stays at a through n-1 and fires p at n because there is no intervening eligible tick. A canonical realization of the local key within the specified age CT exists iff at least one such port is reached from some canonical birth in the slab with enough remaining age to reach n. For realizing the full prequiet interval, restrict tau<=n-U. Necessity is the last-calendar argument; sufficiency is determinism and the absence of intermediate eligibility. This exact equivalence does not assume that an eligible port is itself reachable.

There is always an actual label-three port with a long prequiet segment. Since a divisible6, at any even tau=0 mod6 the predecessor a+2 has its plus neighbor divisible by3, which is the minimum possible odd prime label. Choose such tau in [n-U,n-U+5] (possible for large R, U>=6). At this port the next actual winner is 3, entering a, then the local p event at n follows after at least U-5 quiet ticks. It yields a literal TWO-EVENT local segment 3 then p, followed by H0 quiet ticks. Alternatively enlarge the pre-exclusion in section4 by 6 ticks, with unchanged asymptotic errors, and choose tau in [n-U-5,n-U] to guarantee at least U ticks after entry. This proves a genuine short local entrance path for every key. It does not produce a canonical ancestor. If two such chosen port states coincided, determinism would force the corresponding prequiet corridors to meet (their entry into a is at the same tick and state), contradicting the two-sided separation; hence distinct ports. The next analytic task is a lower on actual occupation of this high-height small-label port set, or some other route to a density-scale canonical top-key supply. Merely counting more high-height local keys would not answer it.
