## 1. Setup and the exact split ledger

Use the original all-odd MIN prefix, dyadic X, N=X/2, B=ceil(sqrt(7X)), H=4B, a=2B, G=2H+B+6, root (M+2,2M-2), K=floor((7M-1)/4), H+2B<=min(K-M-2) eventually. All horizons in [2X,7X), strict increase at events, gain<=G, 0<=2t-C<=12B+6. A selected odd prime p at physical pre-state (t,C) arrives at next time v>t, wait<=2r(C)<=2p. Stop at a twin terminal or at original age H, with the crossing post-state recorded. Each natural weight is length([t,v) intersect [M+2+a,M+2+H))/(2r(C)), <=1. R=sum r U<=NB, E=sum r U². Any original-root deletion or fractional incidence mask can be used: for a smaller weight than natural, split it proportionally between its two natural pieces in this note. No assertion about masks that independently discard one piece and preserve the other.

Canonical indices m odd, state (m+2,2m-2), including a virtual point inside an actual wait. A virtual canonical boundary at C occurs if C=0 mod4 and t<c=C/2+3<v. There may be a source cutoff before c; it then contributes no occupation after c. Split the original wait into pre-boundary portion w^- and the remainder w^+ (non-virtual wait has w^-=0). Use the original fractional occupation restricted to the two intervals. For a virtual canonical point reached by a tag, w^- belongs to the preceding canonical arc, w^+ to the new canonical arc. If c is past cutoff, w^- is its whole clipped weight and w^+=0; no fictitious canonical point is required. At a canonical point already equal to t, use the new arc throughout. Thus U=sum of all arc weights exactly as before, and E_cross=E-sum_m sum_C r U_m² includes both different-tag pairs and within-tag pre/post split products. Distinguish this exact algebraic cross-arc ledger from distinct-root pair energy.

## 2. NEW absorption of large-label virtual-boundary pieces

For t<c as above, c-t<=3 since 2t-C>=0. The deterministic continuation from time c has the SAME next event (v,p). It starts at a canonical point, so p divides 2(v-c)+7 or 2(v-c)+1 according to even or odd arrival. Consequently p>=17 implies v-c>=p/4, and r(C)>=p/8 since v-c<=2r(C). This holds even if the particular tag's cutoff precedes c, because its recorded event is the original deterministic crossing event; no later occupation is invented.

Choose Y>=17 and consider only w^- with p>=Y. Fix C and partition tags with positive such w^- by their original late-window end M+2+H, denoted b_M. If b_M>=c+p/4, then the lower endpoint of its relative slab is <c (positive pre-boundary occupation), and the full interval [c,c+p/4) lies in its selected wait and slab. Therefore
    w^- <= (12/p) w^+ <= (12/Y) w^+.
The same holds after multiplying both parts by any common incidence factor in [0,1]. Otherwise, b_M<c+p/4<=c+2r(C), while positivity gives b_M>t>=c-3. The number of distinct odd original roots whose cutoffs lie in (c-3,c+2r(C)) is <=r(C)+3. This is ONE interval independent of the possible selected labels p at this horizon. Each contributes w^-<=3/(2r(C)), and r(C)>=3. Thus the sum of these exceptional near-cutoff weights is <=3. We obtain for the selected large-label pre-boundary weight V(C), in any fixed final sector or the whole ledger,
    V(C) <= (12/Y) U(C)+3.
Pay the full actual energy incident to these pieces, including same-root cross terms and all other weights at C:
    E_boundary=sum_C r(C)[2U(C)V(C)-V(C)^2]
              <= (24/Y) E + 6R.                         (1)
This is an absorption estimate, not a linear deletion. It also bounds the subset of (1) actually contributed to the cross-arc ledger. It requires no restriction on any other endpoint or ancestry, includes projected asynchronous contributions, and is unchanged by arbitrary original root pruning. For the natural MED sector one can choose Y growing slowly and retain the p<Y pre-boundary pieces as unpaid. For HIGH selected p>T=ceil(B^1/4), take Y=T eventually; all virtual pre pieces in HIGH are absorbed. In normalized E_*=E_H+B^(-5/8)E_M, (1) contributes o(1) E_*+O(XB), for both sectors using a common growing Y or their respective thresholds. It makes no new demand on the LOW transfer. Do not remove these pieces from the linear clock ledger on the basis of the quadratic absorption.

## 3. Synchronous cross-arc pairs have only one different-arc episode

For this statement temporarily omit ALL pre-virtual pieces, including small-label pieces (the latter may still be unpaid in final energy). Each remaining tag at a fixed C belongs to a single canonical arc. Consider two distinct original tags which have the same physical pre-state (t,C) but different remaining arc indices. After their first physical meeting, their deterministic actual paths coincide until the earlier cutoff or terminal. Once the common continuation reaches a canonical point, both tags that reach it have the same latest canonical index thereafter; a subsequent virtual split cannot create a new distinct-arc pair of the remaining pieces. If a virtual point at a currently shared wait is reached by both, both remaining pieces already belong to that canonical arc. If one stops before that point, its remaining piece is zero. Thus the positive synchronous different-arc pairs after trimming pre-virtual pieces occur only before their FIRST common next canonical point after physical synchronization. This is one episode per original pair. There may be no such next point in their common realized suffix.

For each pair with a nonempty episode, define e as the earliest of:
 (i) its next common canonical time in the observed continuation, if reached by at least one before stopping;
 (ii) the two original cutoffs M+2+H, M'+2+H;
 (iii) a common terminal time if a realized prefix terminates before its cutoff.
Equivalently, take the minimum of the known next canonical time(s) and each actual clipped stopping time. When both next canonical times are observed from a synchronous different-arc state, they agree. The endpoint e is fixed for the entire episode, not separately chosen at each horizon. This follows from deterministic continuation, strictly ordered canonical times, and fixed original stops. If one tag's longer prefix reaches a canonical point after the shorter tag's stop, the stop takes precedence. The construction does not require extending a truncated prefix. Any positive residual occupancy from the earlier-ending tag is before e; the other tag's current wait can extend beyond e and is NOT discarded by this definition.

For a length D>=0, let each tag's tail weight at such a shared horizon be its remaining occupation restricted to the absolute time interval [e-D,e), including fractional incidence factors if used. For that unordered pair, pay the part of its ordered product having at least one endpoint in this interval, i.e.
    2r(C) [w_i^+ w_j^+ -(w_i^+-w_i^tail)(w_j^+-w_j^tail)].
Since all original weights <=1, this is bounded by 2r(C)(w_i^tail+w_j^tail), the sum of the two tags' charged time lengths. Their actual waiting intervals are pairwise disjoint along each prefix; each tag spends at most D time in the fixed interval. Therefore the TOTAL paid energy of this pair, summed over ALL horizons in its episode and any fixed sector, is <=2D. This proof works for pairs that have no observed common return, even if the larger root remains active after the other stops. It does not bound the whole earlier part of a long episode. In fact the same terminal-time charging argument at a fixed cutoff could also be used for more general pair classes; the one-episode property ensures here that a return endpoint can be used without a multiplicity factor for repeated canonical returns.

## 4. Source-distance-dependent terminal tail budget

Every pair contributing at a common horizon has source distance d=|M-M'|<=G/2<8B. Divide distances into disjoint dyadic bands d_j/2<d<=d_j with d_j=8B/2^j, j>=0. Only bands containing positive even integer distances matter. Number of possible unordered original pairs in band j is O(X d_j). For a positive parameter m choose
    D_j=B m/[d_j (j+1)^2].
Apply section 3 separately to each pair with its band length D_j, charging actual restricted occupation (not an entire long wait just because it touches the tail). Summing over all bands,
    E_tail <= O(X sum_j d_j D_j)=O(XB m).               (2)
In particular choose m=u^(5/2) for HIGH and m=B^(5/8)u^(5/2) for MED, u=log B. Then (2) is o(XB u³) in HIGH and o(XB^(13/8)u³) in MED. Unlike a fixed tail threshold this allows larger terminal regions for close original sources, with a summable budget over all bands. Even if the two latest canonical origins are far apart or their basins are arbitrarily massive, the bound uses original root separation. No uniform bound on renewal arc length or return existence is assumed. Any synchronous distinct-arc pair whose full remaining occupation after the first physical meeting lies in the corresponding terminal interval is paid entirely, after pre-virtual removal. Otherwise only tail incidences are paid. For the unpaid synchronous different-arc contribution, at least one endpoint is outside the tail and both outside pieces contribute to the remaining product. Small-label boundary pieces not paid by (1) must be accounted for separately; do not treat their removal in this decomposition as a proved bound. Asynchronous different-arc pairs and within-arc energy not paid by (1) are not estimated by (2). No previously excluded terms need to be paid twice; take unions or priority partitions.

## 5. NEW cumulative-gain reverse word bound

Complement the previous count bound (<=2 intermediate nonLOW labels). Set Q=ceil(sqrt B), u=log B, ell=log u, K0=floor(sqrt ell). For each integer 0<=k<=K0 define G_k=floor(u^(5/[2(k+1)])).
Call a prefix eligible at pre-horizon C if exactly k incoming labels >=Q have been selected before reaching C, and the TOTAL gain sum(p-1) of every selected label <Q before reaching C is <=G_k. There is no separate restriction on the number of these smaller labels or their ordering, including all LOW and intermediate primes. The currently selected label at C is unrestricted. Use full original prefix, not a suffix that omits older ancestors.

In reverse, between each of the k large events and the endpoints of the history lie k+1 blocks of smaller labels. If their total gains are 2g_0,...,2g_k, then all g_i are nonnegative integers, sum g_i<=floor(G_k/2). Number of possible tuples is exactly binomial(floor(G_k/2)+k+1,k+1) by stars and bars (including slack). Given these block gains, the preceding horizon of each smaller block is determined by subtraction regardless of its internal length or labels. Each reversed large event ending at J has p|J or J+2, p>=Q, predecessor J-p+1. The number of distinct allowed large prime labels at J is at most A0=2 floor(log(7X+2)/log Q)=O(1), by product bound. Consequently number of possible initial horizons, hence original odd roots, at any C with eligible prefixes is at most
    M_gain = sum_{k=0}^{K0} A0^k binomial(floor(G_k/2)+k+1,k+1).
Uniformly k<=sqrt ell, G_k grows faster than k, and (G_k+k+1)^(k+1)<=u^(5/2+o(1)); A0^k and number of k are u^o(1). Thus M_gain=O(u^(5/2+o(1)))=O(u^(11/4)). This is a deterministic superset count; no distributional assumption about repeated intermediate primes and no exceptional-degree pruning. For k=0 this is simply the source interval bound for total gain<=G_0; k>=1 extends it through large jumps with multiple intervening arbitrary smaller-gain blocks.

Let U_good(C) be actual fractional occupation of these prefixes. In any sector and arbitrary original reference family the full energy incident to eligible weights is
    E-E_other=sum_C r(C)(2U U_good-U_good²)
              <=2 M_gain R=O(XB u^(11/4))=o(XB u³).   (3)
Includes all same/different arcs and synchronous/asynchronous pairs, arbitrary other endpoint. This is complementary to the previous mixed reverse word count: that count permits up to two intermediate selections of arbitrarily large gain, while (3) permits arbitrarily many smaller selections whose total gain is bounded. Their union is paid at the same o(XB u³) scale. It does not bound intermediate-rich histories of arbitrary cumulative gain, and the chosen G_k shrinks as k grows. The larger MED energy allowance permits analogous polynomial gain thresholds with fixed k, but such a generalization was not used or audited here. No linear deletion follows from (3).

## 7. Remaining

The late cross-arc problem is now separated into pre-virtual boundaries (HIGH absorbed; small MED still needs accounting), actual projected asynchronous overlaps, and synchronous different-arc pairs with long remaining episodes before return or cutoff. Finite data show the latter episodes are overwhelmingly still unreturned at late age; a bound relying only on completed canonical arcs would miss them. The cumulative-gain reverse bound gives an independent small class of actual pair energy but does not address the large cumulative intermediate gain of these late prefixes. Next seek a deterministic bound on long nonrenewing shared suffix mass, or an arithmetic constraint on original source pairs that have synchronized while retaining different canonical origins for a long time. Natural MED and retained HIGH sufficient residual bounds, twin infinitude and Lean formalization remain open. Main unchanged.
