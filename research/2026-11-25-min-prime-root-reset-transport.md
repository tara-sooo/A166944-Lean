## 1. Original ensemble and deterministic stop order

A genuine reset is a recorded physical vertex (t,C) with d=2t-C=6s, s=t mod2. This includes the original root, and small incoming labels whenever the equality holds. It implies C=0 mod4 and t=C/2+3s. Do not include virtual reset states not visited by the original paths.

If M<M' share a recorded physical vertex, they follow the same deterministic MIN suffix as long as both are active. On this suffix the smaller original source has the earlier gain threshold C>=2M-2+T and earlier absolute cutoff K(M). Hence the larger source includes every subsequent original recorded vertex of the smaller source, up to the smaller source's terminal snapshot. Actual twin terminal is common. The larger source cannot stop earlier on this shared suffix. In particular if the smaller source reaches a next reset, the larger source also reaches it. This is the long-prefix stop ordering, not the short-clock reference extension of the parallel branch. No extension beyond original stop is used.

The original prime root state is a singleton physical fibre. If another tag arrived at its recorded (odd) time through a selected label q<=B, the incoming congruence would require q|2M, impossible since M is prime >B. Two distinct original roots cannot be the same state. This is also a reset-line injection statement.

## 2. Exact reset mass transport and first-shared-reset births

Compress each actual path to its ordered sequence of genuine resets, starting at its own root. Let A_v be the set of original source tags visiting a physical reset v, n_v=|A_v|. If any tag continues to its next recorded reset, determinism gives a unique successor w(v) for all continuing tags. Let B_v be that continuing source set, m_v=|B_v|; m_v=0 if no successor. The ordering in section 1 shows B_v is exactly the suffix of the sorted list A_v of length m_v, not an arbitrary subset. The remaining n_v-m_v sources exit the renewal graph at v (their actual trajectory can continue through nonreset vertices until original stop). Every original source exits once. Each reset edge has strictly positive gain and time, so the union of these edges is acyclic and outdegree <=1.

Let j_v be the number of original prime injections at v (0 or 1). Nonzero j_v implies n_v=1 and no incoming renewal edge, by the singleton root argument. The exact disjoint set recurrence is
    A_v = {injected root, if any} disjoint-union union_(w:w(w)=v) B_w,
    n_v = j_v + sum_(w:w(w)=v) m_w.
Here w(w) denotes the successor map evaluated at a predecessor w. A tag has exactly one previous reset or is an injection. This is a recurrence on original source sets with their actual stop losses; no relaxed reverse ancestry or independence assumption. It implies
    sum_v (n_v-m_v)=N.

For any two sources that share a reset, their sequence of shared resets is contiguous from the first such reset up to the smaller source's last reset, by deterministic stop ordering. At v the number of pairs sharing their FIRST reset there is
    b_v = choose(n_v,2) - sum_(w:w(w)=v) choose(m_w,2).
Each pair already sharing an earlier reset necessarily comes from the same predecessor reset at v. The sum over all v counts every distinct pair sharing any recorded reset exactly once and telescopes:
    sum_v b_v = sum_v [choose(n_v,2)-choose(m_v,2)].       (1)
No assertion that the first common reset is the first physical meeting; earlier nonreset contacts are allowed. This provides a compressed original reset-fibre graph whose loss term depends on source order, not just the total number of recorded resets. There are O(X) possible physical reset states on the two reset lines in the original horizon strip. In particular the graph is sparse in its number of distinct successor arcs even if original source-reset incidences are huge.

## 3. Exact weighted renewal energy identity (natural synchronous part)

Use the full natural high list I of this branch (p>=Q, r>p/sqrt(log p)); define h(e)=r(C) for a completed original event in I, otherwise zero, including terminal vertices. At a reset v with m_v>0 let R_v be the sum of h over the common deterministic recorded interval from v inclusive to w(v) exclusive, on any continuing tag. This is independent of the chosen continuing tag. For each exiting tag M at v let R_M(v) be its full original natural high tail from v inclusive to its own stop.

Write A_v in increasing source order M_0,...,M_(n_v-1). Its exiting tags are precisely indices i<n_v-m_v. The full ordered offdiagonal NATURAL synchronous high energy accumulated at or after the first common reset of each pair is exactly
    E_reset =
        2 sum_v choose(m_v,2) R_v
      + 2 sum_v sum_(0<=i<n_v-m_v) (n_v-i-1) R_(M_i)(v).   (2)
The first term counts complete common renewal intervals. The last common reset of a pair is the last reset of its smaller source, which is an exiting tag; every larger source in that same fibre follows its recorded terminal suffix. This gives the second term, with no double payment for pairs that also have earlier common renewal intervals. Intervals are half-open at next reset so boundary events are not duplicated. Formula (2) requires neither the older path to start at a composite virtual root nor all original tags to survive to the next reset. It handles early true twin termination.

Equivalently, at each physical recorded natural high hit, partition its actual visiting tags by their latest genuine reset PHYSICAL key. Two tags in the same latest-reset bucket have already shared a reset, and any pair that has already shared a reset is in the same bucket. Thus the independent pointwise ordered offdiagonal sum over squared bucket masses minus their diagonals is exactly (2). The rest of the natural synchronous offdiagonal energy consists of pairs whose latest reset keys differ (no common reset yet at that hit). Pairs before their first common reset and pairs with no common reset are not in (2). Asynchronous overlaps and diagonal terms are also separate.

Replacing R_v by the gain of its renewal arc, and R_M(v) by its remaining gain, gives the exact sum of unordered pair gains from first common reset until smaller-source stop. Since h-sum <=1.5 gain on each completed interval, 3 times that pair-gain sum bounds (2). If any retained natural high incidence masks or pair holes are imposed, the corresponding same-reset residual is <=(2), not generally equal. One may use exact retained tag counts at the deterministic arc events instead; arbitrary holes preclude factoring their quadratic counts into one constant m_v. The identity is a new way to separate the natural synchronous estimate into renewal-arc and exit-tail problems. It is not itself an estimate o(F).

A useful additional transfer from the parallel reset-ancestry note: for a noninitial reset entry with incoming q>=5, wait w<=V, previous reset f and entry prehorizon D, the predeficit is q-1+6s-2w. The elapsed time on the preceding excursion is <=3(D-C_f), hence 5(D-C_f)>=q-2V-7. Short entry waits therefore require a long preceding nonreset excursion when q>>V. This remains true even with long total original gain H. The audit checks it for V=max(1,floor(q/8)); it does not pay the whole short-entry class by itself.

## 4. A sliding window on actual reset ancestry

For each original source M and original physical vertex v let n_M(v)=1+# larger original sources visiting v. Section 1 proves that n_M is nondecreasing along the original path, including between resets, and n_M(root)=1. Counts include all original tags, even those deleted from the actual high/medium accounting; they are used only as reference ranks and ancestry. Let the reset sequence of M be v_0,...,v_l. For a chosen integer k>=1 put
    b_i=v_max(0,i-k),
    g_i=log(n_M(v_i)/n_M(b_i)),
    s_i=C(v_i)-C(b_i).
Both are nonnegative. Each consecutive increment of log n_M or reset-to-reset gain appears in at most k of these windows. Thus
    sum_(M,i) g_i <= k N log N,
    sum_(M,i) s_i <= k sum_M G_M <= k NH.                  (3)
These budgets count RESET VISITS with their actual original source multiplicity. They do not count every pair in a large reset fibre and do not assume short resets are sparse. A reset visit is unstable if g_i>eps, long-window if s_i>=P. The numbers of such actual visits are at most k N log N/eps and k NH/P, respectively.

A stable reset satisfies
    n_M(b_i)>=exp(-eps)n_M(v_i).
Moreover every larger companion at b_i persists through v_i, and visits every one of the intervening actual reset states. The inherited group is an actual source set, not just equality of counts at different projected horizons. If i<k then b_i is the original root and n_M(b_i)=1, so for eps<log2 stability forces n_M(v_i)=1. Consequently a stable visit with n_M(v_i)>1 has a full k-reset backward window (i>=k).

## 5. Whole-pair exclusions at a reset near a younger prime root

For M<M' with a first common projected horizon, use the one-sided older crossing landing D of the cut x=2M'-3 from c6bf00ef. Assign the pair to the older source's LATEST actual genuine reset v at or before D, unique even if v is its original root. This assignment is defined without physical synchronization and independent of all prior pair payments. Write C0'=2M'-2. Call it near when |C(v)-C0'|<=2A. The older tag is the reference focal for its reset rank n_M(v); do not confuse it with the focal direction of a medium charge.

Fix A=ceil(8 u^(2/3)), k=max(1,floor(sqrt(u))), eps=u^(-1/8), P=ceil(B/u^.5) for sufficiently large X. The coefficient 8 is a convenient choice: the backward-cone result of 9bb7547 guarantees, for sufficiently large X, that an older reset reached by tracing at most floor(log u) nonreset events from the crossing landing of a shallow physical meeting (younger age <=floor(u^.25)) lies within this near range. Indeed its absolute half-offset is <=S0+(6+12S0)((3/2)^floor(log u)-1)/2, which is O(u^(.25+log(3/2))) with exponent strictly <2/3. The first reset reached by that backward trace is exactly the latest reset at/before D. P here is a window gain threshold, not the incoming prime at v.

Use whole-pair upper bounds U_high=3H and U_med=C_med H/m_g (sum of both actual directed charges). For either metric let L_high=B u^1.5,L_med=m_g/u^.25 as before. The following are disjoint priority deletions if applied in this order to actual remaining weights; overcounting for the budget is harmless.
1. Unstable: for every near pair whose assigned older reset has g_i>eps, pay its whole remaining weight. For any one marked reset visit (M,v), at most 2A+1 younger integer roots M' can be near. By (3) cost <=O(U_metric A k N u/eps). Here A k u/eps=O(u^(55/24)), which is o(L_med) and o(L_high). In particular both costs are O(N U_metric L_metric)=o(NT) medium, o(F) high; the instability estimate alone has a smaller order. This is an ABSOLUTE deletion of pair energy, rather than the earlier relative recent-companion bound at an evaluation horizon. It applies to the whole later shared tail even if the older reset was well before the actual first meeting.
2. Small older reset rank: pay any near pair with n_M(v)<=m_metric, m_metric=floor(L_metric/(2A+3)). For a fixed younger M' there are O(A+1) physical reset keys v in the strip (two reset lines). At each v the value n_M(v) is exactly its descending source rank on the full original fibre, so at most m_metric older sources qualify. Total cost O(N U_metric L_metric). It can pay both directed medium charges because U_med bounds their sum; this is not a linear deletion of a partner root.
3. Long previous window (HIGH only): pay any near high pair with s_i>=P. By (3) its total cost is <=O(U_high A k NH/P)=O(F/u^(1/3)), using N=O(B²/u), H~T and A k B/(P u²)=O(u^(-1/3)). This counts a whole k-reset interval, even if its current incoming label is small and even if its long gain was spread over many transitions. No comparable medium smallness is supplied by this gain budget. This is distinct from the previous absolute large-crossing-edge estimate and from rare-root linear deletion. There is no need to recompute the high density gate, because only actual nonnegative pair weights are paid within an absolute budget.

For a near pair surviving the respective exclusions, n_M(v)>m_metric and stability holds. Eventually m_metric>=2 and eps<log2, so the complete preceding k-reset word exists. At least exp(-eps)n_M(v)-1 larger companions of M are present at its first state b_i, hence all k earlier resets and v. The number of those companions with source >=M' is at most A+1: any root visiting v has initial horizon <=C(v)<=2M'-2+2A. Thus the number of strictly older-than-M' companions inherited from b_i (excluding M) is at least
    exp(-eps)n_M(v)-A-2.
This is a diverging lower bound at the asymptotic m_metric, and in particular an actual thick old source set shared across several consecutive reset states. For high survivors the entire k-reset word also has gain <P; hence its time span is <=3P, and all its reset horizons are in a strip of width O(P+A) about the younger start. No bound on the number of older origins flowing into b_i is implied. The reduction specifically excludes rapid recent growth, small current mass, and long recent renewal span as explanations of this near-reset obstruction. It does not exclude a large, stable old group entering from still earlier history. Pairs far from a reset in the defined sense, and the preceding paper's shallow nonreset-depth alternative, require other estimates.

The whole added absolute error is O(F/u^.25) high and O(NT/u^.25) medium. The two sufficient residual estimates on the same unbounded dyadic sequence remain open. The new masks are applied to the actual unpaid fractions of an already feasible flow. Subtracting just these amounts preserves all earlier paid capacities; reoptimizing is optional and cannot increase the residual. No use of a finite diagnostic zero as an asymptotic theorem.
