## 2. Many clock-rich local subblocks on every persistent source

Take all regular tiles wholly inside [x,x+L]. In each take the contiguous full completed events wholly inside the tile, gain G in [M0-2B,M0] (all shared by the two tags). Omitted gain from irregular tiles, two end tiles and partial events at tile boundaries is
    o(L)+2M0+O(B L/M0)=o(L).
The omitted clock is <=3 times omitted gain plus O(B); initial complete suffix clock >=L/2-O(B). Hence the sum of waits on regular full tile blocks >=L/2-o(L). Call a block rich if W>=G/4. Other blocks contribute <=G/4, all rich blocks <=3G. Thus rich gained length >= (1/11-o(1)) L; at least L/(12M0) distinct rich tiles eventually. No assumption that local drift is positive in EVERY tile, and no need for sparse B or canonical populations.

Put A=max(V+1,ceil(lambda ell²)). On EACH rich tile classify large if F_large (gain on p>A)>=23G/24. Then short waits <=G/8, large waits >=G/8, and expensive large waits w>=g/16 give >=G/16>=M0/20 actual ticks, with r(C)>=g/32. Otherwise the normalized mass of the entire contiguous block is G-o(M0), while large events' mass is at most F_large+2lambda G/(A-1)+M0/ell. Short events thus contain at least G/24-o(M0) normalized mass. For all sufficiently large X they cross >=alpha Q M0/128 distinct raw cuts; choose the majority subtype R<p<=V or V<p<=A, leaving >=alpha Q M0/256 cuts (constants deliberately weakened). In a short-rich tile, ALL labels with w>=g/8 carry >=G/8>=M0/10 actual expensive ticks. Thus short-rich requires the cut AND an expensive event within the SAME tile, distance <=M0, not merely somewhere in [x,x+L]. Both may be in the same event if eligible. Under finite twins moderate cut event has w<=2S and g>=R-1, so w/g=o(1): it cannot itself be the expensive event eventually. Moderate event also crosses at most one cut since V<2P_S. The expensive event may have any label, not necessarily >A. This local refinement uses a different rich-tile classification from VIII; no inclusion of whole-suffix branch populations is claimed.

## 3. Exact joint transport and marginal capacities

For each surviving original ordered pair/level source x, first choose uniformly among all its rich regular tiles. If chosen tile is large, choose uniformly among its expensive-large ticks. If short, choose independently and uniformly among its chosen subtype raw cuts AND its actual expensive ticks in the same tile. Preserve source identity, true first birth and both original tag identities. This is a partition of the positive measure. Can additionally retain old VIII witness masks by product choices, with marginal costs bounded by unmasked marginals.

For a fixed original tag in either ordered role and witness tile, sources x lie within a gain interval of length <=L+M0, with density<=5. Tile selection probability <=12M0/L. Conditional tick probability <=20/M0 (large) or <=10/M0 (short), and cut probability <=256/(alpha Q M0). Consequently marginal caps, with absolute constants:
    large or short tick O(1), short cut O(1/alpha).
For a JOINT short key (J,C,p,C',q), where (J,C,p) is the projected actual cut crossing and (C',q) its expensive projected event, aggregate all occupied ticks of the selected expensive event. A tag has a unique crossing at J and at most one event at C'. Its tick choice probability is <=2q/(M0/10). Thus per-tag per-role joint cap
    O(q/(alpha M0)).                                      (1)
The factor 1/M0 is real: this is a coupled local pair of witnesses, not a product of two separate effective populations. Multiple sources, levels, slabs and partners already summed by the density bound. No independence assumption about the actual event labels or horizons. Count at most one actual event per C' per tag.

## 4. Physical phase matching at each projected joint key

Delete fractions with live log variation source exit to either witness exit >epsilon=ell^-1/8. As in VII-VIII, total <=O(N(L+B)log N/epsilon)=o(NT). Both witnesses lie on the same deterministic continuation; therefore the accumulated variation between their two actual physical exits is <=epsilon. For earlier exit E and later F the two ORIGINAL full cohorts have
    |S_E intersect S_F| >= (1-epsilon exp(epsilon)) max(b_E,b_F)
and sizes within exp(epsilon). For fixed projected joint key order its two horizons (if equal, actual event must coincide). Each physical earlier slot can have at most one later slot at the given horizon by determinism. Conversely any given later slot can have at most one earlier slot satisfying the displayed strict-majority overlap, since full cohorts at the fixed earlier prehorizon are disjoint (each tag executes at most one event there). Thus the surviving physical phase pairs for every projected joint key form a PARTIAL BIJECTION, not an arbitrary bipartite relation. This matches physical phases without asserting all projected mass is synchronous. The same statement holds after all further masks. It uses actual original cohorts and actual continuing trajectories, not historical retired members. In particular for a joint key with effective mass m and maximum participating earlier cohort size b_max, at least m/b_max distinct physical pairs must participate; they have distinct departure times in BOTH projections. Each departure time at fixed C,p is in one residue mod2p (C,p fix the selected side; p cannot divide both C±1). Consequently if k such matched phases participate, their deficit span is >=4p(k-1) at the cut and >=4q(k-1) at the expensive event, respectively. Hence either some phase is large or both phase spectra must spread, with no many-to-one merging explanation after turnover trim. This is a structural restriction, not yet a bound on the possible spread. Full cohort masses may differ slightly and projected effective mass is still a sum over phases.

After stability the joint ordered-pair cap at a physical phase pair is O(q/(alpha M0 max(b0,b_E))) and also with b_F, by the same denominator comparison as VI. Apply old induced birth ranks at BOTH witnesses using the marginal caps: total tick population <=3NH, cut population O(alpha NH) by the uniform raw mass over each full life. Therefore rank failure costs O(rho NT), rho=ell^-1/4. These are actual physical ranks, not ranks of the union of matched phases. No change of denominator at projected keys.

## 5. New joint projected mass gate

For fixed raw cut J and p there are <=2 prehorizons C. Globally #J=O(alpha X). For fixed J,C,p,q the expensive prehorizon C' lies in the SAME deterministic tile as J (with the event wholly inside); w>=g/8 implies least eligible r>=(q-1)/16. For each q, possible C' number
    O(M0/[q log²(2+q)])
uniformly in tile and q<=B. Indeed two progressions C'=+/-1 mod q, length O(M0/q)>=c X^1/4, use the earlier two-form upper sieve with odd primes <=q^(1/40) excluding q, below the least-eligible threshold, with remainder O(q^(8/40)) absorbed uniformly. For bounded q use the trivial bound. This is an upper count without assuming the expensive event is independent of the cut. All candidate C' inside the tile, even if no actual common path, are allowed in the upper count.

Define joint effective mass as its retained load divided by C0 q/(alpha M0), C0 fixed sufficiently large by (1), hence <= distinct original focal tags executing BOTH actual events. For subtype p<=V use K=pi(V); for other p<=A use K=pi(A). Delete keys with effective mass <=
    m_joint(q;K)= H log²(2+q)/(u ell² K q).                  (2)
Total paid <= sum_q O(alpha X K * M0/(q log²(2+q))) *
                          O(q/(alpha M0)) * m_joint(q;K)
                = O(XH/(u ell))=o(NT),
since sum_q 1/q=O(ell). For polynomial expensive q>=B^delta with fixed delta>0, log q~u and reciprocal sum O_delta(1), strengthen to H u/(K q ell), at same o(NT) cost. Can additionally impose single projected cut gate H/(u ell K) of VIII via marginal cap, and projected expensive event gate on large branch as VIII (r>=g/32 still sufficient). One may also apply single projected expensive event marginal gate to short branch, using all-label sieve and per-tag O(q) marginal, threshold H log²(2+q)/(u ell² q), if desired. All gates refer to actual joint load or its appropriate marginal; do not substitute a lower bound for actual cohort mass at either individual phase.

For moderate subtype, K=pi(V)=X^o(1). Thus if q=X^o(1), (2) requires a JOINT projected mass X^(1-o(1)): almost macroscopic mass of original tags must execute the cheap cut and a nearby expensive event, with the actual phase relation a partial bijection. The prime-root interval upper sieve also forces origin span >=c m_joint log(2+m_joint). For polynomial q the gate is weaker; large expensive label supply remains possible. The matched phase spectra can be wide because available deficit range is O(H); no upper theorem for this joint mass has been obtained. In particular (2) cannot be combined with the older nonzero-lag forward-prefix radical bound on same-slot backward continuations.

    NT/2-o(NT) <= U/2+(P_local-L_*)/2+c_a(D_l+D_h).
The sufficient strict normalized liminf [U+P_local-L_*+6(D_l+D_h)]/(NT)<1 remains open.
