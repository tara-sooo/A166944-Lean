## 1. First profitable passage and exact threshold packing (paper)

Integer reward D per observed tick, monotone nonnegative completed-prefix cost c(A,k) with c(A,0)=0. For positive observation length l, g_A(l)=D*l-c(A,observeAge(A)+l); define g_A(0)=0. Fix an integer q>0. Let l_A be the first positive l<=T with g_A(l)>=q, or 0 if absent. For any eligible prefix reaching q, replacement by l_A preserves q and all caps and only shortens the prefix. Full root multiplicity at l_A-1 is minimal among q-attaining endpoints, by laminarity. With nondecreasing cost and g_A(l)-g_A(l-1)<=D (also at l=1), q<=g_A(l_A)<q+D. The positive threshold is essential: a truncated Nat subtraction equal to zero does not imply nonnegative signed profit. An initial Lean candidate omitted q>0 in its net accounting theorem; the elaborator exposed the false q=0 case, now fixed.

Write K(q) for the largest number of successful roots whose first-passage prefixes can be simultaneously selected with observed cap M. Observed-only cap is equivalent for the net problem by the preceding verified clip lemma, which preserves observation lifetime. In fact if all chosen roots have positive observed lifetime, any earlier pre-observation collision persists at observation start, so the selected policy itself is capped before observation. No need to keep roots that never attain q.

Exact cardinality algorithm: process successful roots in nondecreasing l_A (ties by ID), accept if adding its whole prefix does not exceed M in any bin; otherwise reject. For M=0 the result is zero. For M>0 this greedy algorithm is optimal for the *unweighted threshold count*, not for arbitrary unequal profits. Proof by exchange: maintain an optimal feasible subset that contains all earlier greedy accepts and none of the earlier rejects. If the next greedy decision rejects A, a saturated bin consisting of forced accepts prevents A from being in any such optimum. If it accepts and A is absent from the optimum, find the earliest saturated bin along A in that optimum. If there is no saturated bin, adding A contradicts maximal cardinality. Otherwise some occupying root B is not forced, since the forced set alone is compatible with A. B is a not-yet-processed root, so l_B>=l_A. By physical coalescence, B follows A from that earliest saturated bin through A's entire remaining prefix. Replace B by A. Before the first saturated bin adding A cannot exceed M; after it B's removal offsets A at all A's bins; all other bins lose load or are unchanged. The result is an optimum agreeing with this additional greedy decision. Finish by induction. This uses all prior decisions, not an erroneous assertion that merely sharing a later endpoint implies sharing all earlier states.

Let V* be the optimal net profit under the original cap. Integer layer-cake gives
    max_(1<=q<=DT) q K(q) <= V* <= sum_(q=1)^(DT) K(q).
The lower bound chooses a single threshold-optimal subset. For the upper bound, discard negative-profit roots of any policy, then count those whose profits are >=q for every q; their shortened first-pass prefixes remain feasible. K(q) is nonincreasing. The upper bound is not generally attained by one common policy.
If V*/(NDT)>=s>0, let q0=floor(s DT/2)+1. The q<q0 terms contribute <=s NDT/2; the remaining terms are bounded by (max qK(q)) times the harmonic sum on [q0,DT]. Consequently max qK(q)/(NDT) >= c s/(1+log(1/s)) for an absolute c (including small DT via q0>=1). For s>=L^-sigma on large slabs, the cost of passing to a single threshold is at most O(log L). With strict exponent slack this is harmless; at an exact L^-sigma target with no slack it must not be dropped. This gives a nearly equivalent asymptotic target, not merely a stronger sufficient criterion.

## 2. Successful-prefix collision graph and a reduced moment target

Take only successful roots S_q, n_q=|S_q|. Two distinct candidates conflict iff their observed bins coincide at u=min(l_A,l_B)-1, including window ID. If they ever shared an earlier live bin, they share this last common-live clock by coalescence; converse immediate. Thus the closed neighbor count d_A+1 involves only successful prefixes before their own stops, not every root in the full endpoint group and not post-success coalescence.

Choose a uniform total ordering of successful roots (or of all roots). Keep each candidate iff it lies among the first M of its own closed neighborhood. All live roots at any bin form a clique in this graph. The last in priority among any retained clique has all other clique members in its closed neighborhood; its rank <=M implies the cap. Uniform priority gives the bound
    K(q) >= sum_(A in S_q) min(1,M/(d_A+1))
          >= sum_(A in S_q) M/(M+d_A)
          >= M n_q^2/(M n_q+2 e_q),
where e_q is the number of unordered conflict pairs, M>=1,n_q>0. The last step is Cauchy-Schwarz; empty candidate set contributes zero. This method and previous full-endpoint weighting are different lower bounds; neither per-root neighborhood count universally dominates full endpoint multiplicity because earlier successful paths may have exited before the root's own endpoint. Exact greedy K(q) dominates both. For a positive integer threshold with D=1 every first-passage profit is exactly q.

At L=log R,M~L^2.1, a sufficient actual-trajectory criterion is: some q/(DT)>=c L^-a, n_q/N>=c L^-b and 2 e_q/N<=C L^h, with a+b<.25 and a+2b+h-2.1<.25 (nonnegative a,b; h may be negative). Indeed qK(q)/(NDT) >= (q/DT)/2 * min(n_q/N, M(n_q/N)^2/(2e_q/N)). If no conflicts, K(q)=n_q for M>=1. The strict inequalities allow negligible errors. The unresolved task is to bound actual e_q and n_q at growing scales, not to assume independent residue classes. Existing native/foreign cell lemma applies to successful live cohorts, and a rejected root has a saturated bin where at least M earlier successful roots already meet it. For Q/6<=M such a bin contains foreign ancestry among the M+1 roots, hence a genuine nondivisor boundary entry. This does not bound how many pairs an entry can carry.

## 3. Main new structural result: history cost has bounded concatenation defect

All statements in this section apply to arbitrary finite label words, including idle label 0, not a probabilistic MIN model. Let S(w) be the greedy saturation count with a reset at each label >=reset and saturation on the (Ddiv+1)st distinct narrow label z<p<Y. Let N(w) be the nonempty-chunk-start count of the same automaton. Let J(w) count reset blocks (including the unfinished last block) containing both >K broad narrow occurrences z<p<Yb and >Ddiv distinct narrow labels z<p<Y. For the joint counter do not clear on saturation; charge at most once per large block.

The exact concatenation inequalities are
    S(x)+S(y) <= S(xy) <= S(x)+S(y)+1;
    N(x)+N(y)-1 <= N(xy) <= N(x)+N(y);
    |J(xy)-J(x)-J(y)| <= 1.
Here the lower bound with -1 is an integer inequality, not a truncated subtraction requirement. For S: two automata with equal saturation counts and larger vs smaller seen sets evolve with interlaced saturation counts. Invariant: a.saturated=b.saturated and seen(b) subset seen(a), OR a.saturated=b.saturated+1 and seen(a) subset seen(b). At a reset the sets clear; at a saturation the inclusions reverse when only one saturates. If b has Ddiv members and p is new to b but belongs to the larger set a, that larger set cannot have size <=Ddiv, so this apparent exceptional case is impossible. Initialization with incoming actual seen set versus empty set and the same initial sat count establishes the first inequality after any suffix. Alternatively S is the maximum number of pairwise disjoint reset-free intervals containing Ddiv+1 distinct narrow labels (earliest-finish greedy); at most one such interval crosses the concatenation cut. This also proves reversal invariance of S.

For N: until the first reset, each newly begun chunk either remains incomplete (seen set nonempty) or saturates. Exactly
    N_end + S_start + 1_seen(start) = N_start + S_end + 1_seen(end).
Compare the two automata with interlaced S counts; if the incoming seen set is nonempty, starting the suffix fresh can add at most one extra nonempty start, and never fewer. If both seen sets empty the starts agree. The first reset synchronizes the seen sets, so this difference does not grow over later resets. That proves the second inequality. No reversal invariance for N is asserted.

For J: only the block touching the concatenation cut can change; two partial blocks become one. The score of a joined block is in {0,1}, is >= each original score by monotonicity, and differs from their sum by at most one. All other blocks are unchanged. This works even when either word is empty, or the split is at a reset. Each of S,N,J is nondecreasing on appending and on prepending a label. For N the latter follows from the append bound if the prepended label is relevant (its one-letter count is 1), while a leading reset or irrelevant label leaves the empty initial state unchanged. For J the block predicate is monotone under adding letters at either end. J is invariant under reversal of the word with reset labels retained.

Consequently both the earlier formal diversity cost wS*S+wL*N and stronger joint cost wS*J+wL*N have absolute concatenation defect E=wS+wL. Different reset cutoffs may be used for the two terms. For the actual paper cost use reset=Ystar,Y=Y0,Yb=exp(L^.45), and N reset=Y0. There is no factor counting how many resets occur. All the inequalities also hold for the common future after a physical contact: two histories x and y, same next word z, have incremental charges C(xz)-C(x) and C(yz)-C(y) each within E of C(z), and within 2E of each other (sat-only or nonempty-only one-sided difference is <=1). This is an exact bounded-memory property, not mixing.

## 4. Reduce history-dependent net optimization back to the checked additive record optimizer

Let C be a nonnegative word cost with |C(xy)-C(x)-C(y)|<=E and C(w)<=C(p::w) for every prepended label p. For a globally common absolute clock n_end at or beyond all window retirements define the state-only potential
    F(s)=C(labelTrace(s,max(0,n_end-s.n))),
    inc_F(s)=F(s)-F(tick(s)).
Because prepending a label cannot decrease C, F(s)>=F(tick(s)); inc_F>=0. For states at or beyond n_end, both potential values equal C(empty). Telescoping yields for every canonical prefix k ending before n_end
    C_F(A,k)=sum_(i<k) inc_F(orbit(A,i))=F(birth(A))-F(orbit(A,k)),
    |C_F(A,k)-C(labelTrace(birth(A),k))|<=E.
The latter is exactly the concatenation-defect inequality applied to the complete word up to n_end and split at k. Crucial: the cost error is per prefix, hence per root, NOT per tick. A single n_end for all windows makes the potential a function of State alone, with no hidden window index; an implementation may exploit a per-window end for independent windows, but the kernel bridge uses a global end. Evaluation of this potential may require following trajectories past their observation horizon, potentially a long time. The proof does not assume any arithmetic law for that future.

For any policy d, total history cost and additive proxy differ by <=NE. Thus net values differ by <=NE. The existing exact additive optimizer optimalNetDeath inc_F D R T M and its record mass V_F^* are applicable. Its true history net is within 2NE of any other full-capped history net. In fact for the optimum values themselves |V_history^* - V_F^*|<=NE by the uniform error. These claims include zero-live prefixes, cost through the killing tick and full physical cap.

This resolves the previous technical barrier that the additive record optimizer and its contact pair-energy identity could not directly be used with history-dependent joint diversity costs. The use of a potential with a fixed finite terminal clock is essential; using the unshifted prefix count as a state function would be invalid.

Under the still OPEN uniform HistoryLiveEstimate for this chosen C, the checked bridge is
    V_F^* > err + N E  =>  a TwinCenter above B, provided B+3T+6<R.
The record mass is exact (not an upper/lower proxy). At the paper scale, after common integer rounding of the two weights, E/(DT) is of order
    exp(-L^.6)+(B_support+1)(Y0+Y1)/T+O(1/(DT)),
which is super-polynomially small in L=log R for the chosen T,Y0,Y1 and B_support=L^o(1). The scaling must explicitly include the constant from the paper upper estimate and rounding errors. Therefore the replacement does not consume the L^(-.25) margin. As a result one may re-use the verified record-energy machinery on the actual joint history criterion: normalized S_rec and Q_rec are the threshold-layer record mass moments for inc_F; V_F/(DNT)>=.5 min(s,M s^2/e) where s=S_rec/(DNT),e=Q_rec/(DNT), if e>0. The exact common-suffix contact pair formula now applies to the potential increments. Sufficient exponents remain s>=c L^-tau,e<=C L^(2.35-2tau), tau<=.25, with strict slack if needed. There is no proof of those growing-scale estimates yet.

A new arithmetic interpretation: for each state, inc_F is the decrement in the number of future chargeable blocks when its current label is removed. In the saturation term these are reverse-greedy saturation events, since S is invariant under reversal and greedy reverse saturation increments exactly by the suffix difference. In the joint term, the current label is charged iff it is essential to satisfying both thresholds in its remaining reset block. The nonempty term also has nonnegative suffix differences bounded by 1, but is not generally reversal invariant. Thus the expensive history charges can be located at actual backward thresholds, shared by all roots after contact. This opens a way to analyze record-energy using the existing first-use CRT pins and cell-entry constraints, without controlling entire automaton histories. It does not imply rarity of those reverse threshold events.
