## 1. Two rough horizons from three actual selected resets

Take one arbitrary selected reset list on each original tag, Y<=p<=P<=B, with fixed bounded ratio P/Y<=L0, Y->infinity. May be exact common-core selections; keep actual relays across gaps. Write R_w=sum ending r E as XXV. Fix integer K>=0. Remove first two list vertices (cost <=2NP). A remaining ending is good when BOTH preceding selected relays <=K. Every bad ending can be charged either its own nonclose incoming edge or the immediately preceding nonclose edge, ending gains comparable within factor 2L0. Therefore
    F_R <= 2NP+(1+2L0)R_w/(K+1)+F_good.             (1)
No double-count issue for this upper bound. For a good triple let middle p, ending r, middle side e=1-2I, middle C=e+pq, and shift D=p-1+E to next prehorizon C+D. Both preceding postdeficits <=6; actual predeficit of middle and end <=6+5K. If 6+5K<=(Y-1)/2, both events long 4w>=g and both neighbors at both horizons avoid every odd f with 9f<Y-1. The four integral forms in q are
    q, pq+2e, pq+p+e+E-2, pq+p+e+E.
For f>=5, f<Y, coefficient p is a unit. Their zero residues coincide only if f divides D(D-2)(D+2) (or 2, already excluded). D>=6; determinant nonzero. Thus all FOUR residues distinct at generic small primes. This needs no prime cofactor or knowledge of r beyond its lower scale. It uses actual two successive rough horizons, not independent rough probabilities. If the intermediate relay is large or preceding reset absent, this four-form deduction need not hold.

## 2. Single middle-label count and four-form pair CRT

Fix middle p, e, E<=K. Original prehorizon interval length <=H, so q lies in interval padded to L=ceil(H/p)+2. Use the fixed-dimensional upper Selberg proof in direct 9e3203a, with cutoff floor(p^(1/24)) (eventually below (Y-1)/9 by bounded ratio). Uniformly H/p>=p^(.9) for p<=B and sufficiently large X, since H~X/u^.25 and B=O(sqrt X). The z^6 error is absorbed in L/log^4 p. The generic singular correction is
    J4(D(D-2)(D+2))=prod_(f>4,f|D(D-2)(D+2))(1-4/f)^(-1)
        << (loglog(3p))^4
for fixed K, from the usual small/large prime split (D=O(p)). The count times middle gain is
    << H J4(D(D-2)(D+2))/log^4 p.
An actual q gives at most one middle event, and that event at most one successor in the chosen list. Sum sides and E, and charge ending gain at most 2L0 middle gain. With S_i actual label support on each original tag,
    F_good <<_(L0,K) H sum_i sum_(p in S_i)
                    (loglog(3p)/log p)^4.             (2)
More precisely keep the sum of actual J4 factors over E and sides rather than the worst bound. This improves the support lower based solely on marginal two-form roughness, including at P²~H or larger, where the next CRT parameter interval may be bounded.

Alternatively fix BOTH p,r, E and sides. Close p!=r by repeated-label spacing, C in one residue mod pr. Along C=C_*+pr z, the four horizon-neighbor forms C-1,C+1,C+D-1,C+D+1 have four distinct roots for f<Y, f>4 not dividing D(D-2)(D+2). Equivalently divide the two selected neighbors by p,r, which are units modulo f. Parameter interval padded length L=ceil(H/(pr))+2. Use sieve cutoff floor(min(L^(1/12),Y^(1/24))) and absorb L^.5 error, with bounded cases trivial. The ending weighted count is
    << (r+H/p) J4(D(D-2)(D+2))/log^4(2+min(Y,H/P²)).
Indeed log cutoff comparable to log(2+min(Y,H/P²)), and padding error bounded by constant times L^.5. Sum tuples as XXV. Put Lambda=log(2+min(Y,H/P²)) and s_i=|S_i|. Then
    F_good <<_(L0,K) (P+H/Y) (loglog(3P))^4 / Lambda^4
                        * sum_i s_i².               (3)
Use min of (2),(3), or exact singular factors if smaller. If Lambda bounded, use the unsieved XXV pair estimate instead. For P²<=H^(1-epsilon) with fixed epsilon>0 and bounded ratio, Lambda comparable to log Y. Then (3) improves the XXV two-form factor by essentially another log² Y, up to the discriminant loss. No equidistribution of CRT residues in short horizon windows is assumed.

If F_R>=rho NH and R_w<=K0 NH for fixed rho,K0>0, choose K fixed large enough to leave F_good>=rho NH/2 eventually. To obtain a positive fraction of TAGS with large support, restrict (1)-(3) to tags below the relevant threshold and choose K for the entire available relay budget (with additional slack, e.g. tail<=rho NH/8); first term o(NH). The low-support population then contributes at most rho NH/2, so >=rho N/2 tags remain since each contributes <=H. Equations (2),(3) respectively imply necessary diversity
    s_i >> (log Y/loglog(3Y))^4,
    s_i >> sqrt(H/(P+H/Y))*Lambda²/(loglog(3P))²
on positive fractions (may also impose both by paying union of low-support populations using more slack). For bounded-ratio polynomial labels below sqrt H by a fixed power the second improves XXV's sqrt(Y) log Y to sqrt(Y)(log Y/loglog Y)^2. At medium/top labels the old unsieved pair lower can be much stronger than (2); do not claim this logarithmic bound dominates it. Actual upper on s_i not established. R_w cap not forced by small unweighted complement.

## 3. Local direct extended-clock sieve, with no full-X loss

Direct 26ada2c uses k=floor sqrt Y, extended if k w>=g and short otherwise. For ANY selected label p>=Y (resets allowed for this marginal count), extended occurrences exclude all odd factors <=p^(1/24) eventually, since w>=g/k>=(p-1)/sqrt p. At fixed p and side, cofactor interval padded to ceil(H/p)+2, no full-X padding. Same two-form q,pq+2e sieve with cutoff p^(1/24), error absorbed as above. Thus extended gain of any selected events on an original tag with actual support S_i is
    F_ext,i << H sum_(p in S_i) 1/log² p.            (4)
Uniform down to arbitrary Y->infinity, independent of common-core length D. For a shorter block replace H by G+p in the main term provided the error p^.25 times p is paid, or reduce cutoff; no automatic O(G) estimate for arbitrarily short blocks. Apply once per ORIGINAL tag, even for disjoint core subsets. This removes the factor X/H from direct's global marginal count. No additional claim on prime density among actual physical slots.

## 4. Canonical height guard and exclusion of reset recovery

For each original tag choose any actual subset of high nonreset events Y<=p<=P with ordinary ending heights a=d'>6. All selections may depend on that tag's whole path, including exact common cores, and can use different guards for different tags. Form one union E_i of [a,a+g_a] over USED selected heights, with g_a maximum selected gain at height a; omit unused heights. Set Phi_i(d)=length(E_i intersect [0,d]). Because every a>6 and canonical initial d=6, Phi_i(6)=0 exactly. Unlike direct's arbitrary internal block version, no M_A initial guard penalty, regardless of the width or diameter of E_i. Each original event counted once per tag, even in gaps between cores. Let F_short,F_ext be tagged selected gains with k w<g and >=g. Selected short drop in Phi>=alpha g, alpha=1-2/k. Selected positive flux <=(3+4/(Y-1)) F_ext (using w<=2p). Let P_out be actual outside positive Phi flux, Z_guard=sum_i Phi_i(d_terminal). Exact full canonical telescope:
    alpha F_short + Z_guard
          <= (3+4/(Y-1)) F_ext + P_out.             (5)
In fact replace the first RHS term by exact selected positive flux and keep all unselected negative flux on LHS. All adjusted resets have d'<=6, so Phi_i(d')=0: they CANNOT produce positive flux in any guard, even if their physical gain or wait is very large. Thus P_out only comes from outside nonresets. No assumption of small ordinary variation, common baseline, small guard width or local shallow block start. A large initial deficit of one core is not a free budget.

In the XXII core setting, choose selections contained in actual complete cores, and split outside sources into omitted events and core events. Omitted positive Phi flux <=2 W_omit<=(2+o(1))Mhat+o(NT); this uses the actual wait bound, not five times omitted gain. Define P_core,out as actual positive guard flux in unselected core events; resets contribute zero. Then
    alpha F_short+Z_guard <= (3+4/(Y-1))F_ext
                       +P_core,out+(2+o(1))Mhat+o(NT). (6)
By (4), if sum_i sum_(p in S_i)1/log²p=o(N), extended gain o(NT). In particular for polynomial Y and o(log²X) distinct selected ending heights per tag in mean, the support bound from positive divisors d'/2,(d'-6)/2 is O_a(1) per height (canonical d'=O(H)), giving this condition. On small missing branch, any such macroscopic selected population requires P_core,out>= (1-o(1))F+Z_guard-o(NT), hence macro complementary nonreset gain actually crossing its guards upward. The complementary gain lower follows from P_core,out<=5G_core,out,nonreset. High positive source events have 2w>g and are extended. This is a genuine joint restriction: a sparse-height high nonreset population with negligible extended gain cannot be maintained by a complement consisting almost entirely of resets. It is not an upper bound on general reset gain or general high nonreset gain. If all nonreset core events are selected except low labels, the necessary flux must come from those low nonresets (or missing); if support sparse enough and low gain negligible, selected gain o(NT). Do not add (6) as another independent credit to the signed XXIV ledger. For several guard families on one tag merge them or explicitly account for multiplicity; different per-tag choices are allowed because the final measure is tagged.
