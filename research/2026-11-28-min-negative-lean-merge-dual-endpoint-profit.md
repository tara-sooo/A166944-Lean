## 1. Observed-only capacity and exact dual compression

Write g_A(l)=D*l-c(A,observeAge(A)+l) for 1<=l<=T, and g_A(0)=0 (death at birth, not cost through the observation start). c is any monotone root-history expense with c(A,0)=0. Let P_A(l) contain the l observed pre-tick physical bins, with the window identifier included. All roots of a window have been born by observation start. If an actual prefix policy satisfies cap M in observed bins, the existing exact deadlineClip produces a physically capped policy with exactly the same observed lifetime and no greater history expense. Thus dropping pre-observation cap rows in the net maximization does not change its optimum. Alternatively, discard every prefix with zero observed lifetime; for positive-lifetime roots, pre-observation coalescence implies contact at observation start. Kernel theorems: observedCap_clip_life, observedCap_clip_expense, observedCap_history_upper.

For each observed bin v let F(v) be the set/list of ALL canonical roots at that state, regardless of policy. If F(v) intersects F(w) and time(v)<=time(w) then F(v) subset F(w); this is physical coalescence with common window clock, not position alone. All nonempty distinct sets therefore form a laminar forest. Its leaves are the groups at observation start; any subsequently formed distinct group has at least two children. The number of distinct groups is <=2N-1 if N>0, summed over windows (elementary tree count, not a separately formalized cardinality theorem). Along a chain with unchanged F, replace a dual price tau_v by the same amount at the earliest bin having F(v). Every prefix that used the original price also uses the earlier bin; additional charges can only reduce the required root slack. Total price stays constant. A price on a group of size <=M can be converted to root slack of tau_v for each member, at cost |F(v)|*tau_v<=M*tau_v.

Consequently the dual admits an optimum with prices only at initial groups or earliest bins of newly formed groups of mass >M. The paper proof of exact strong duality and integral optimum is the previous note (circulation with return arcs, no new axiom). Kernel-checked here: fullObsFiber_nested, earliest_fullObsFiber, pushDualPrices_total, pushDualPrices_feasible, heavyDual_feasible, heavyDual_objective, earliestFiber_has_merge, earliestFiber_has_firing. The last theorem says a noninitial earliest group has at least one actual nonidle incoming event. It does not say that event is large.

Define explicit dual feasibility for all root prefixes:
    g_A(l) <= y_A + sum_(v in P_A(l)) tau_v,
with nonnegative y_A,tau_v. Integer-scaled form DualFeasible includes every age k<=observeAge+T, including nonpositive pre-observation profits. List price entries allow repeats. Dual objective F=M sum_v tau_v + sum_A y_A. Kernel dualCharge_sum, dualCharge_cap, dual_weak_bound prove weak duality by double counting. No use of a strong-duality axiom. The compressed maximum-profit circulation can be formed directly: for each distinct full cohort, create one forward edge of cap M to the next changed cohort or retirement sink; each root has its leaf capacity 1; for each root/cohort choose the maximum g_A(l) among prefixes ending just after a tick in that cohort and create its return arc. A root crossing the first tick of a cohort must pay its capacity once; all later repeated-cohort capacities follow from this. The return arc is from that cohort's parent vertex to the root leaf. Forward acyclicity ensures exact recovery of root prefixes. Integral circulations can be optimized by positive residual cycle cancellation; potentials after no positive cycle certify optimality (paper proof, exact independently implemented tests; full flow theorem not Lean formalized).

## 2. Endpoint multiplicity lower bound, without a random-budget loss

For l>=1 define m_A(l)=|F(v_A(l-1))|, where the bin is at the last LIVE observation tick, not at the post-death state. m_A is nondecreasing. Let g_A(l)_+=max(0,g_A(l)). Then the optimal net value obeys the deterministic inequality
    V* >= sum_A max_(1<=l<=T) [g_A(l)_+ min(1,M/m_A(l))].       (E)

Proof 1 (fractional flow and dual). Independently pick a maximizing endpoint l_A for each root, discarding nonpositive gains. Give its prefix weight w_A=min(1,M/m_A(l_A)). At any earlier bin v of this prefix, F(v) subset F(endpoint), so w_A<=M/|F(v)|. At most |F(v)| roots use v. Their total weight <=M, and root weight <=1. This is a feasible fractional prefix packing. The previous exact circulation rounding yields an integral packing of no smaller objective. Alternatively multiply the dual inequalities by w_A and sum: every feasible dual is >= the same RHS. In the Lean proof with common integer denominator H, choose integer numerator w_A<=H and w_A*m_A<=M*H; weightedPrefixLoad<=M*H and endpointWeights_dual_lower prove the last assertion for EVERY feasible dual, without a CRT hypothesis, any strong-duality assumption, or a moment estimate.

Proof 2 (direct, no LP). Choose one uniform random total order of all roots (independently across windows is equivalent for the argument). For each chosen prefix keep it in full iff its root has rank <=M inside the FULL endpoint cohort. The probability is exactly min(1,M/m_A) because relative order in any fixed subset is uniform. Earlier full groups are subsets; hence selected roots at each earlier state are among its first M ranks, so capacity holds simultaneously. The resulting expected net equals RHS of (E). Some order realizes at least the expectation. The core proves endpointRankDeath_observedCap, endpointRankGain_exact, endpointNet_exact, endpointNet_upper, endpointNet_ensemble_upper, twin_above_of_endpoint_ensemble for arbitrary finite lists of orders, without any unproved probability library premise. The exact uniform-order marginal and conditional-expectation derandomization below are paper-level arguments, not yet independently encoded in Lean. In particular no hidden assumption of arithmetic independence is involved.

Constructive derandomization: choose endpoints maximizing (E), then build the global order one root at a time. Given a partial order and an unplaced root whose endpoint group has m members, q already placed, its conditional retention probability is min(1,max(0,M-q)/(m-q)). For placed roots the indicator is known. Compute the sum of g_A times these probabilities. Averaging the conditional sums over every possible next root gives exactly the previous sum (partition of the uniform remaining orders). Choose a maximizer at every step. The last sum is integral and >= initial expectation. This is polynomial in the explicit number of roots and endpoint incidences, with rational arithmetic; the audit uses H=lcm(1,...,N_window) as a common exact BigInt denominator. It does not enumerate all permutations.

For a uniform cutoff K>=M, one can also maximize g_A before m_A exceeds K and color the chosen prefix paths with K colors. Process prefixes by descending observed lifetime. All already processed prefixes intersecting a new one contain its last live state, so they form a clique of size <=K-1 excluding the new root. Assign an unused color. Each color is a capacity-one policy; top M color classes obtain at least M/K of the sum of truncated peaks. The endpoint weighting generally avoids a uniform K and is stronger in flexibility, although the exact top-color policy can outperform its expected value. Both algorithms are independently tested.

## 3. New asymptotic target for the actual MIN arithmetic

    eta <= B0 M twinCount/N + C E*c + o(L^-sigma).
This uniform inequality is still an OPEN analytic premise for the chosen history cost, not a new Lean theorem. Include constant C and the integer rounding in c_A; equivalently use D reward units per tick. Let
    q_A(l)=(g_A(l)/(D*T))_+,
    E_R=(1/N) sum_A max_l q_A(l) min(1,M/m_A(l)).
By (E), the sufficient condition is E_R >= c0 L^-sigma on unbounded R. If so, the cap/expense bound forces >>R/L^2.35 twins on those slabs. Unlike the earlier soft budget condition, only ONE profitable stopping prefix per root is needed; it does not require a positive fraction of all root-time pairs or a separately tiny expense budget, only positive net profit. This is a concrete change of lower-bound target.

In particular choose a,d,e>=0 with a+d+e<sigma. A population of >=c0 N L^-e roots each having SOME prefix with q_A(l)>=c1 L^-a and m_A(l)<=M L^d suffices. Conversely under finite twins and the uniform paper upper estimate, the fraction of roots with those two properties is o(L^-(sigma-a-d)) for a+d<sigma. This necessary restriction on actual prefixes is a target for contradiction. A profit threshold q is best tested at its earliest attainment, since full endpoint multiplicity cannot decrease. Thus the count may be attacked via first profitable passage rather than the occupation of a fixed long interval. No growing-scale population estimate is proved here.

## 4. Arithmetic relocation of small-label merge prices

The merge compression cannot discard small labels. Actual R=600,T=18,M=1, window base 618, u=12, x=618, canonical roots 618 and 624: a p=3 jump merges the two groups. The computed optimal dual assigns price 6 to this group with the finite proxy cost specified below. This refutes the proposition that optimal dual price support can only occur at large-label mergers for the computed dual (it does NOT prove no alternative optimal dual avoids that bin). At R=192000 the compressed dual cost carried by noninitial groups with all moving labels <=5 was 71792, of total state price cost 18128+71792+40448=130368. It is not negligible in this finite example.

To trace such mergers to earlier arithmetic events, fix a positive multiple Q of 6, Q/6<=M, and cells [jQ,(j+1)Q). A native ancestor at bin v with center x has A/Q=x/Q. There are at most Q/6 native canonical roots. For every foreign ancestor there is a genuine first tick entering the final cell, with winner p!=0 and Q%p!=0, by the primorial wall lemma. The core now proves nativeObsFiber_bound and foreignObsRoot_entry exactly for actual roots, including pre-observation history. No uniform distribution assumption. Thus every group of size >M has at least one non-divisor boundary entry among its ancestors; the total number of foreign roots is at least m-Q/6. If Q contains all primes up to z, these entries have p>z.

Paper transport lemma: for a price tau at an observed group G, partition its members into native roots N_G and foreign cohorts E_{G,j} by their first entry physical bin; if the entry is before observation, map to that root's observation-start bin, merging cohorts sharing the same mapped bin. A prefix that uses G must use its own entry bin (or observation start). Move the price to each such bin if its cohort has size >=M, otherwise add tau to individual slack of its members; add tau to every native root slack. These replacements maintain all dual inequalities and cost
    tau (|N_G| + sum_j min(M,|E_{G,j}|)).
Initial-group prices may simply be retained. Relative to original M*tau, the extra cost is the exact displayed amount minus M*tau (can be bounded above; no assumption that it is small). This restricts prices to actual cell-entry bins and initial bins, plus slack. When a large group is dominated by a single entry cohort, inflation can be close to 1; many small entry cohorts can cost m/M. This is a concrete arithmetical price-transport direction, not a proof that inflation is uniformly bounded. Entry cohorts can carry many ancestors; counting only the number of entry events does not bound root multiplicity. For a nonreset merger (postheight 2h != c, c=0 or 6), two different incoming primes p,r both divide the same 2h-c, so pr<=|2h-c|. Reset mergers instead use spatial pins y or y-2. These elementary pin facts are from actual tick_height_pin and source congruences; the audit checks them at group formation. They are useful for studying entry cohort distribution but do not control long small-label ancestry by themselves.
