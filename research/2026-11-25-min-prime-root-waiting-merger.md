# Waiting-state first mergers and distance-adaptive actual-clock charges

2026-11-25 JST. Continuation of 3ec0b49bbcdd00ddcd08bdb931125bd9cf789548 on research/min-prime-root-repeat-energy-2026-11-25. Uses the full original clock-state forest, including waiting states and one terminal snapshot. Its first mergers often precede the first common selected physical vertex. Two distance-adaptive criteria pay actual-clock medium quota charges on either side of a first waiting merger: a truncated-potential local mass test and a chronological partner-rank test. In particular some currently asynchronous charges are paid by future mergers. This is an absolute o(NT) exclusion, not a bound on all large formation costs or on pairs that never merge. No Lean compilation.

## 2. Full waiting-state forest and the first waiting meeting

For an original root M define its integer clock-state path at every j from its initial time to its last actual vertex time, inclusive. Between selected vertices (t_i,C_i) and (t_{i+1},C_{i+1}), the state at t_i<=j<t_{i+1} is (j,C_i). At its terminal time include the single original terminal snapshot; expire the tag immediately thereafter. Do not continue a terminal trajectory or invent an uncompleted wait. The full tag-path thus has one state per completed unit wait and one final snapshot.

For a fixed source cell, at time j and horizon J group all original tags with that state, including terminal tags for this one snapshot. Nonterminal tags in the same state have the same deterministic next clock state. Indeed the least-label earliest eligible tick strictly after j depends only on (j,J). Therefore they either all stay at J for the next unit step or all jump to the same new horizon, with the sole possible split being root-specific expiration. Once two tags share a clock-state vertex, their subsequent common-time states coincide until at least one expires. They cannot leave a shared state and later meet for a second first time. This is the waiting-state counterpart of the selected-state merge forest; it includes root injections and expiry.

For distinct M,M' in the same fixed source cell, define their first waiting meeting as (j_*,J_*) if any common clock-state vertex exists. If they also have a first common selected physical vertex, j_* is no later. A meeting may occur when one tag jumps to a horizon where the other is already waiting, or when a root is injected, or even when one terminal snapshot meets another still-waiting tag. The last case can have no common selected physical vertex within the prefixes. We only use the defined waiting meeting and never infer a continuation beyond expiry.

At a vertex v=(j,J), its incoming branches consist of the stationary group from (j-1,J) if present, jump groups from (j-1,J-p+1) for distinct incoming selected primes p, and singleton newly injected roots. All masses refer to original tags in the fixed cell. Distinct incoming jump labels give distinct predecessor horizons; at most one stationary branch exists. All pairs with endpoints from different branches meet for the first time at v, including pairs involving new roots. Conversely every first waiting meeting is such a pair. Let n_v=sum_i a_i be the original total visiting mass before expiry. The unordered first-pair count is
    B_v=(n_v^2-sum_i a_i^2)/2.
If s is stationary mass, j_tot the sum of jump masses, j_i the individual jump masses, and r0 the number of root injections, the exact disjoint partition is
    B_v = s j_tot + (j_tot^2-sum_i j_i^2)/2
                   + r0(n_v-r0)+r0(r0-1)/2.       (2)
The root class includes all pairs incident to an injected root. No assumption that original root injection cannot meet a waiting tag.

For every retained asynchronous far charge at a shared later horizon E, if the source pair has a waiting first meeting, J_*>E and both actual E selections have completed by j_*. To see this, if J_*<E then both roots must complete E later and the deterministic common continuation forces equal arrival/exit there. If J_*=E, both roots complete E and the scheduled exit is identical. Both contradict the retained far class's different successors. A terminal meeting at or below E is also impossible because both roots complete E. If J_*>E, both already made their E selections (but attached LOW may extend across j_*). Thus future waiting meetings apply to presently asynchronous charges, not just the synchronous suffix. This does not require the two C..E elapsed-time intervals to overlap.

## 3. Truncated convex waiting potential and chronological ranks

Use f_m(n)=n^2 for n<=m and 2mn-m^2 for n>m, where m>=1 is integer, and D_v=f_m(n_v)-sum_i f_m(a_i). Superadditivity gives D_v>=0. At a given vertex with e_v expiring tags the loss is f_m(n_v)-f_m(n_v-e_v)<=2m e_v. Sum over all clock-state vertices and disjoint fixed original source cells: nonexpired groups map intact to one successor, singleton root injections supply f_m(1)=1, and all tags eventually expire once. Thus
    sum_v D_v=sum_v [f_m(n_v)-f_m(n_v-e_v)]-N <=2mN.
At vertices with n_v<=m, D_v=2B_v. Consequently the number of unordered first waiting pairs whose local first-meeting mass is <=m is at most mN. This is valid for every m, and all source cells are disjoint; there is no factor for the number of clock ticks, source cells or stationary vertices. The same identity may be computed by processing only actual event ticks, since untouched stationary states contribute zero increment and no expiry. At an event tick, remove whole arriving predecessor groups from the previous waiting horizons first; then form new groups with any stationary remainder and root injections, count first pairs, and remove terminal tags. The terminal snapshot at the event is necessary for exact expiry and first-pair accounting.

Independent rank test: fix a smaller source M in its original source cell, order all larger source partners M' that ever meet it in the waiting forest by increasing first meeting time j_* and then increasing M' for ties. Let rho(M,M') be the resulting positive integer rank, with no rank for a never-meeting pair. For every m, at most mN unordered pairs have rank<=m. This uses no upper bound on the mass at the meeting. At v the rank of a new pair with smaller source M is
    #larger partners of M first met before j
      + #M'' in other incoming branches at v with M<M''<=M'.
The latter count is the interval count among all v tags minus the same interval count in M's incoming branch. Earlier partners that expired must still be counted in the first term. The rank is defined once from the entire original forest, not recomputed after paid charges or separately for time offsets. It is not an estimate of the final group mass. The waiting-forest algorithm updates the cumulative number of larger partners after processing all queried first pairs at each vertex.

## 4. Distance-adaptive fractional charge exclusion

Set
    m0=max(1,floor(m_g/u^(.25))) = asymp u^(9/2)/ell^4,
    H0=ceil(4H)+2,
    m(x)=max(1,floor(m0 sqrt(H0/(x+1)))), x=0,...,H0-1.
The clock time j of any unit in a focal block and its pair's first waiting meeting time j_* both belong to the focal original time span, of length <=3H; thus x=|j-j_*|<H0. This includes the case that the meeting occurs in the future of the block. Retain all original later-hit aggregate charge densities from (1), after the existing paid tests. For each ordered pair with a waiting meeting, pay its charge density at unit clock j if
    n_*<=m(|j-j_*|) OR rho(M,M')<=m(|j-j_*|),       (3)
where n_* is the original cell-local total mass at that pair's first meeting, and rho uses the smaller source regardless of focal direction. Use mass priority then rank to partition the union. For never-meeting pairs no such test is applied. This is a fractional exclusion at the later E-plus-LOW block, not an assertion that every corresponding edge is paid in full. It works even if the block straddles the merger.

For a fixed distance x, the unordered pairs satisfying either mass or rank test number <=2m(x)N, by section 3. Each pair has at most two clock times at that distance (j_* +/- x, one at x=0), and two focal directions; disjoint later-hit blocks ensure each ordered pair-time is charged at most once with density <=C0/m_g. Therefore the total paid cost is bounded by
    W_wait << (N/m_g) sum_{0<=x<H0} m(x)
           << N m0 H0/m_g
            = O(NT/u^(.25)) = o(NT).                (4)
The sum uses sum_{k<=H0} k^(-1/2)<=2 sqrt(H0). There is no log-factor for dyadic time bands, no quota or interval multiplicity penalty, and no mass bound assumed for the focal/partner active group at the clock time being charged. Each test alone obeys (4). The rank test can pay at first mergers with arbitrarily large n_*. By paying the exact unit-time fractions rather than the entire E block based on its first instant, we incur no extra O(Q) or O(L) charge near the meeting. This is independent of the earlier proxy first-small formation estimates; they can be retained by priority with their original smaller raw caps. The clock forest may also be used to derive hierarchical branch/admission variants, but those are not claimed here.

The new condition can equivalently be expressed as a rank-adaptive two-sided clock window. Ignoring floors for thresholds n_*,rho>=1, a unit at x is paid when
    x+1 <= H0 (m0/min(n_*,rho))^2.
Thus small first mergers (or small rank) have their full charges paid, while even very large first mergers can pay the units sufficiently near j_*. For presently asynchronous pairs, this gives a quantitative restriction on how close their remaining charged units may be to their future waiting first merger. For a synchronous pair the meeting may be far in the past; the same inequality applies. No transfer from current active mass to first-meeting mass is assumed.
