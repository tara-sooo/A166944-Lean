## 1. Setup and exact conditional no-exit product

Let W be an even primorial (W=2 allowed). For integer Z>=W define D_Z=2 product_(3<=p<=Z prime)p and D_W analogously, so W divides D_W and D_W divides D_Z. (The formula also works when W>Z provided W divides D_Z, by truncating the lower alphabet.) Use the finite ghost on odd roots M in (1,D_Z+1], C0=2M-2, t0=M+2, with no individual time cutoffs or accounting stops. Terminate each path at its first executed W-cell exit, or at its natural ghost terminal within the origin cell. Every internal gain is <W and every internal label <=W, so every path terminates this local experiment in finitely many events. The period is aligned to whole W-cells in horizon coordinates. Write Nper=D_Z/2 and nu(W,Z) for the fraction of roots with NO EXIT. This is a finite-alphabet observable, NOT actual twin-prime density.

Condition on an odd residue r modulo D_W, and run the <=W ghost from the corresponding source. Translation by D_W in M preserves its entire relative local trace and cell. If that ghost exits, the <=Z ghost cannot end inside: the first divergence would be an executed label >W, which itself exits because p-1>=W. If the <=W ghost terminates inside, its trace has attained pre-states (C_i,t_i), next internal events at times n_i, and final (C_*,t_*). For each prime W<p<=Z, let F_p(r) be the subset of the p residue classes j mod p of the source M=r+D_W*j for which:
  (a) p has its first strict-next matching clock from some (C_i+2D_W*j,t_i+D_W*j) strictly before n_i+D_W*j; OR
  (b) p divides one of C_*+2D_W*j +/-1.
Ties in (a) lose to the lower label, so strict inequality is essential. The sets depend only on j mod p, since D_W is even. For a fixed horizon sign, the eligibility condition fixes one residue j mod p. Condition (b) excludes exactly two residues (since p is odd and coprime to 2D_W). If no added prime has a forbidden residue then the entire lower path is followed and the final horizon is terminal in the expanded alphabet. Conversely, an earliest preempting added event exits, or an added event at the lower terminal exits. Thus avoiding every F_p is necessary and sufficient for no exit. Uniform j modulo D_Z/D_W has independent uniform prime residues by the CRT. The exact formula is
    nu(W,Z) = (2/D_W) sum_(odd r with lower terminal inside) product_(W<p<=Z)(1-|F_p(r)|/p).
No two-dimensional independent CRT ghost distribution is assumed: this is the original one-dimensional root slice. No assumption on mergers is needed. Each factor is nonnegative, and |F_p(r)|>=2. In particular
    nu(W,Z) <= nu(W,W) product_(W<p<=Z)(1-2/p)
             <= O((log(2W)/log Z)^2).
This bound has no factor W or maximum fibre size. It holds without a no-twin assumption or the height-tail hypothesis.

## 2. Exact tail factorization and local terminal amplification

The root height h=2t-C starts at 6 and remains nonnegative. In fact for ANY eligible prime p at an attained even C with h>=0 and any strict future matching clock n=t+w, the candidate post-height h'=h+2w-(p-1) is nonnegative. It satisfies h'>=3-p, and h' is a multiple of 2p when n is even, or h'==6 mod2p when n is odd. For p>=5 no negative member of the corresponding residue class is >=3-p; p=3 already has lower bound zero. The same argument works whether or not this candidate actually wins the MIN rule.

Before any internal event in the lower trace, g=C-C0<W, e=t-t0<=3g, and h=6+2e-g<=6+5W. The actual lower next wait <=2W. A prime p>10W+14 cannot preempt: a candidate with w<=2W would have h'<=6+5W+4W-(p-1)<0. Hence for EVERY lower-terminal residue r and every p>10W+14, F_p(r) consists EXACTLY of the two final-horizon residue exclusions. It is unnecessary to examine the internal path for such p.

For Z>=Z0>=10W+14, separating the independent factors gives the exact identity
    nu(W,Z) = nu(W,Z0) product_(Z0<p<=Z)(1-2/p).
This is an equality of complete-period proportions, not a sieve approximation. In particular put rho_Z=product_(3<=p<=Z)(1-2/p). Roots which already have no eligible prime at their initial horizon have fraction rho_Z, because 2M-2 is uniform mod each odd prime on the root period. They are all no-exit roots. Define alpha(W)=nu(W,Z0)/rho_Z0, independent of choice of Z0>=10W+14. Then
    nu(W,Z)=alpha(W) rho_Z,  1<=alpha(W)<=1/rho_W=O(log^2(2W)).
This is an exact local amplification factor of the rough-terminal fraction at sufficiently large finite alphabet. If W1 divides W2, the W1-cells refine the W2-cells, and every root terminating within its W1 origin cell also terminates within its W2 origin cell (compare the same full finite trace stopped at exit). Taking a common Z>=10W2+14 proves alpha(W1)<=alpha(W2). Thus alpha is monotone along nested primorial widths. We do not prove divergence or a stronger asymptotic growth law for alpha. The standard Mertens product implies for fixed W, nu(W,Z) is of order 1/log^2 Z, with the exact multiplicative amplification above. These are statements about roughness only at finite cutoff; no conversion to a lower bound for actual twin-prime count is established.

## 3. Representative bound directly on the FULL periodic first-exit forest

The earlier retained-stop proof is not dependent on accounting stops when we restrict to the local first-exit problem. Before their first exit, any two paths at the same physical V(t,C) or executed A(n,p,C) have the same origin W-cell and exactly the same future local experiment. There are no time-cutoff expirations. Thus the full local first-exit/terminal population is non-splitting, including all original roots on the period, not merely retained stopped tags. Stop at the crossing A (with its attained landing available for the height test) or at a natural V terminal. Representatives are the k largest origins at each group as in the preceding note. No resurrection applies and representative prefix incidence at each pre-state and executed event is <=k.

For clarity we prove the necessary uniform per-prime cost bound directly in this periodic model, without using the asymptotic integer sieve or comparison error of the preceding note. All pre-horizons C lie in (0,2D_Z] even. For a fixed odd prime p<=Z, consider those C with p|C-1 or C+1. At such C define r(C) to be the least eligible odd prime (so r<=p). By the CRT, for 1<=u<=p, the number of such C with r(C)>=u equals
    (2D_Z/p) product_(3<=ell<u prime)(1-2/ell).
(The p condition and all smaller-prime exclusions are independent.) The product is O(1/log^2(2u)), adjusting absolute constants at small u. Summation over u<=p yields
    sum_(even C in (0,2D_Z] eligible for p) r(C)
       = O(D_Z/log^2 p),
since sum_(u<=p)1/log^2(2u)=O(p/log^2 p). This is exact CRT counting with a uniform absolute constant, also for p near Z. There are exactly 2D_Z/p eligible even pre-horizons per period. No equidistribution of actual representative paths is needed.

Take the same finite-time clipped potential as in the prior notes: integers Y>=P>=11, 2P<=S_time, 0<=K<=S_time, L=2 product_(3<=p<Y prime)p, H>=max(12,L,2(K+6)), and
    eta=P[Pr_uniform even-C/time phase mod L(D_(Y,2S_time)>K)+K/(S_time+1)]
        +1/log^2 P+sum_(q>=Y prime)(K-q+1)_+/q^2.
Assume W=2 product_(3<=p<z prime)p, z>2H+2, z>=Y, Z>=W, so L divides D_Z. Define a bad representative root to be one attaining H during its representative prefix, or executing a representative jump p>2H+2 before that first height hit. Include the post-state of a final executed representative event for height, even if status is lost on landing. For p>2H+2 at pre-height h<H, nonnegativity gives r>p/8, and the pre-height band and w<=2p allow O(1) matching clocks per (p,C). The exact period cost bound gives O(k D_Z sum_(p>2H+2)1/(p log^2 p))=O(k D_Z/log^2 H) source incidences. Remove them when counting height hits. Remaining prefixes all labels <=2H+2. Each eligible projected key has O(1+H/p)=O(H/p) possible clocks. For labels p>=P the per-prime r-cost bound gives O(k D_Z H/log^2 P) total leakage; the high correction excess is O(k D_Z H sum_(q>=Y)(K-q+1)_+/q^2). For p<P count all possible physical pre-states in each phase pair mod L. There are O(D_Z H/L^2) per pair because H>=L and D_Z>=L. The previously proved finite-time clipped-potential estimate
    [Delta Phi]_+ <=4P 1{D_(Y,2S_time)>K}+2P K/(S_time+1)
then gives O(k D_Z H P[mu+K/(S_time+1)]) total. Every hitting prefix gains at least H-(6+K)>=H/2 in corrected height. Hence the bad representative union has size O(k D_Z eta), with absolute constant. This reproduces the prior bounded-incidence proof on a full period, independently of the no-twin stopping argument.

Every exit packet e is monochrome with m_e roots and its crossing prime >=z>2H+2 by the exact barrier. All min(k,m_e) representatives there belong to the bad union, and the exit packets are disjoint original-root sets. Thus
    c_(W,Z)(k) = (1/Nper) sum_e min(k,m_e) <= C k eta
for some uniform absolute C. This is a theorem for the FULL finite periodic local model, with no no-twin premise. In particular it does not need the previous very-large-exit source sieve or the difference between stopped and full populations. This direct proof is why the proposed anti-concentration target needs correction.

## 4. Obstruction to the preceding proposed route

Write m_(W,Z,>k) for the fraction of all roots belonging to exit packets of size >k (no-exit roots excluded). The remaining fraction is no-exit mass plus mass of packets of size <=k, the latter bounded by c_(W,Z)(k). Sections1 and3 imply the unconditional finite-model inequality
    1-m_(W,Z,>k) <= C k eta + nu(W,W) product_(W<p<=Z)(1-2/p)
                  <= O(k eta + (log(2W)/log Z)^2).
Consequently, along ANY compatible parameter sequence with k eta->0 and log(2W)/log Z->0, the finite model necessarily satisfies m_(W,Z,>k)=1-o(1). This conclusion is independent of actual twins, of the integer source interval X, and of the accounting-stop mass S. If the earlier LOW signed-peak limit is true and supplies eta->0, it supplies exactly this packet concentration rather than a deficit in local packet formation. It is therefore impossible to prove a fixed epsilon anti-concentration bound m_(W,Z,>k)<=1-epsilon SIMULTANEOUSLY with those hypotheses. The previous proposed sufficient condition was a formal implication, but is incompatible with the other desired estimate at its chosen scales; it is not a usable alternative route to the conjecture. This does NOT disprove eta->0 or establish that concentration holds without the eta hypothesis. Neither does it disprove the twin-prime conjecture or invalidate the previously proved representative and large-exit sieve inequalities.

This correction also applies to the preceding actual-full-source approximation: a full local trajectory has no individual cutoff issue before its first exit for W+2B=o(X), because its elapsed time until any crossing <=3W+2B, while K(M)-t0=Omega(X). Thus the same local full-source non-splitting argument works. The direct periodic proof already suffices and avoids any analytic comparison. For actual full no-exit mass, restriction to <=Z cannot create an exit if the full source terminates before one, so the complete-period upper bound plus O(D_Z) end effects gives O(X(log(2W)/log Z)^2+D_Z) at the earlier scales. None of these bounds uses the no-twin hypothesis.

The important distinction for further work is between local packet concentration (compatible with twins and even expected under the LOW estimate) and subsequent transport all the way to the rare accounting stops or distant terminal groups. Return to a mass-weighted post-exit forest estimate or a direct joint stopping deficit, retaining local large packets as inputs instead of trying to exclude their formation. The old final inequality S_small+2 P_phys,far>=N-o(X) on no-twin windows remains the relevant nontrivial reduction; the LOW potential coefficient and global weighted coalescence/retention upper bound remain open. The local product law gives a quantitative diagnostic of terminal amplification, but cannot by itself replace the global estimate. No claim of new progress toward a complete proof from an incompatible pair of conditions.
