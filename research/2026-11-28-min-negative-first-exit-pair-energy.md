## 1. First-exit arithmetic and pair parametrization

Let L=log R and fix 0<theta<1/2. Let F be any subset of canonical roots A in [R,2R] with qmin(A)>=z=R^theta and which are not twin centers. For each such A take its actual first firing (w,p,s), s=+1 for even clock, s=-1 for odd clock. The checked height bound and static calendar yield
    z<=p<=4qmin(A)+7=O(sqrt R), (qmin(A)-7)/2<=w<=2qmin(A).
The latter upper holds because a non-twin has a composite neighbor. Thus p=o(R), and its source neighbor A+s is composite. Let x=A-p+1 be its first destination. The incoming spatial pin gives p|x-(1-s). Every visited site through that first event is of order R.

For two DISTINCT roots of F having the same destination x AND same sign s, the two labels p,q are distinct: otherwise A=x+p-1 fixes the root. Write c=1-s (0 for plus,2 for minus). Since p,q divide x-c,
    x=c+pqt, t a positive integer.
Their fired neighbors and opposite neighbors are exactly
    A_p+s=p(qt+1),       A_p-s=p(qt+1)-2s,
    A_q+s=q(pt+1),       A_q-s=q(pt+1)-2s.
Consequently p,q,pt+1,qt+1 and both opposite neighbors are z-rough. This is independent of actual firing clocks. It counts same-sign coincidence of FIRST DESTINATION POSITIONS even if arrival times differ, a stronger set of pairs than simultaneous first arrivals. There is no implication that these two trajectories are coalesced at their respective first-event ages.

## 2. Uniform double-sieve upper bound for first-exit pairs

For fixed q,t,s, sieve the variable p in the four affine forms
    p, tp+1, (qt+1)p-2s, qt p+q-2s.                       (1)
All four are z-rough. Their six pairwise determinants are nonzero: the first form has distinct roots from the others since constants 1,-2s,q-2s are nonzero; the other three determinants are up to sign (q+2s)t+1, 2st, and q^2 t+q-2s. For q>=5,t>=1 none vanishes. The product of the nonzero coefficients and determinants is R^O(1), uniformly since qt<=O(R), p,q are polynomially bounded. Omit primes dividing this product and primes<=5. Each remaining prime has precisely four distinct excluded classes.

Use the same elementary Selberg upper and omitted-prime product estimate as 8aa47ea section 3, restated here for clarity. For r fixed distinct affine forms, all nonzero coefficients/determinants of size R^O(1), omit the corresponding bad prime product. If the variable interval has length at most X, X>=R^eta for fixed eta>0, count variables in [1,X] whose forms are all z-rough. Take D=floor(min(X^(1/12),z/2)). The good-prime density g(l)=r/l, choose only primes l>2r+1, and use the diagonal Selberg weights of support d<=D, |lambda_d|<=d, denominator G(D)=sum_{squarefree d<=D, good} prod_{l|d}r/(l-r). CRT errors O(D^6) suffice for r<=4: r^omega(lcm(d,e))<=de. For G lower restrict the Euler product to good l<=D^eta' with small fixed eta'; under the product-weight measure the expected log product is O(eta' log D), so at least half of the product mass has d<=D. The full product is >> (log D)^r. The omitted primes dividing a nonzero R^O(1) integer cost at most O((log L)^r): for l<=L^2 use Mertens, for larger l use O(L) prime divisors and reciprocal sum O(1/L). Thus the uniform upper is
    O_{eta,theta,r}(X (log L)^r / L^r).                 (2)
D^6<=X^.5 is negligible. This uses no distribution theorem for primes and no independence assumption for the two sieves below.

For (1), p<=X=(2R+1)/(qt); if X<z there is no candidate. Otherwise (2) with r=4 gives O(X (log L)^4/L^4) possible p. In addition q and qt+1 are z-rough, so for each fixed t the two-form estimate (2) in variable q, on dyadic bands [Y,2Y] with Y>=z/2, bounds the count by O(Y (log L)^2/L^2), uniformly in t. These two forms have determinant 1 and coefficient product t; omitting bad primes as above is valid. There are O(L) bands covering z<=q<=C sqrt R. Consequently
    sum_{z<=q<=C sqrt R; q,qt+1 z-rough} 1/q
        << (log L)^2/L.
(The upper also counts composite q; restricting to primes only decreases it.) Summing the bound for p first, then this reciprocal sum, and finally t<= (2R+1)/z^2, whose harmonic sum is O(L), yields the unconditional same-sign first-exit pair upper
    E_same := sum_{x,s} binom(m_s(x),2)
       <<_theta R (log L)^6/L^4 = o(R/L^2).            (3)
Here m_s(x) counts roots of F with destination x and sign s. In fact the right hand side is O(R L^-4+o(1)); no use of qmin at destination. The p,q ordering or restriction to the winner only lowers the count of admissible tuples. The two-stage sieve in p then q, rather than just the sieve in t, is essential: p q may be comparable to R and t may be bounded, but each prime variable remains polynomially large.

## 3. Consequences for actual trajectory distributions

Let N=|F|, m(x)=m_+(x)+m_-(x). Exactly
    sum_{x,s} m_s(x)^2 = N+2E_same.
Since m(x)^2<=2(m_+(x)^2+m_-(x)^2), we also have
    sum_x m(x)^2 <= 2N+4E_same.
Discard every root having a same-sign first-destination partner: at most 2E_same roots. On the remainder at most one arrival per sign is possible at each x. Hence at least (N-2E_same)/2 distinct first destinations, with the factor 1/2 needed only because of possible opposite-sign arrivals. More sharply the signed site count is at least N-E_same. Simultaneous first arrivals at (x,n) must have the same sign by clock parity, so the physical-state collision pair count is <=E_same and the physical-state count is >=N-E_same. The checked height pin gives an additional exact constraint on any such simultaneous pair:
    pq | h-c_s, c_+=0,c_-=3.
If h<=H and pq>H+3, then h=c_s (reset height). This last observation applies to all actual simultaneous noninitial arrivals, not just the first one, but (3) is specifically for rough FIRST sources; no corresponding all-history pair-energy estimate follows.

For the earlier accepted shifted-prime lower (8aa47ea, theta=.285), at least c R/L^2 canonical roots have A-1 prime and A+1 z-rough (use cutoff 2 R^.285+8). Apart from the twins, every first event is necessarily plus: the minus neighbor's only label is A-1, whose first possible clock is positive and whose wait from birth is of order R; the composite plus neighbor supplies an event within 2 sqrt(2R+1). Thus all their first-exit collisions fall under (3), without the factor 1/2. Either the slab has >=c' R/L^2 twin centers, or >=c' R/L^2 such roots actually fire by 2 sqrt(2R+1), with all but O(R (log L)^6/L^4) roots having mutually distinct first destinations. This holds in every sufficiently large slab, with c' fixed. In the finite-twin hypothesis the second alternative is automatic. It is a new lower on the number of distinct actual destinations, not on long-time survival at a common clock.

Combine with the first-exit neighborhood theorem of 8aa47ea (all rough non-twin roots, both signs): for fixed alpha,beta with 0<alpha<beta<theta and J=floor(L^(2-epsilon)), 0<epsilon<2, at most O(R L^(-2-epsilon)(log L)^4)=o(R/L^2) roots have any center u=x-d, 0<=d<=J even, with qmin(u)>=R^alpha. For the other roots every actual phase before leaving this interval waits <2R^alpha and gains at least two. They cross the distinct cuts x-J in <=2R^alpha(floor(J/2)+1)=o(R^beta) additional ticks after first firing. Thus under the non-twin alternative we have >=c'' R/L^2 distinct first destinations AND distinct subsequently crossed cuts, all crossed by age 2 sqrt(2R+1)+R^beta. This gives genuine trajectories with later motion at the near-square observation scale, but does not bound their ultimate gain from above or prevent their later mergers. In particular a cut-crossing event may cross more than one cut; no injectivity of final states is asserted.

## 4. Unconditional polynomial-label reset supply of density R/L^2, with composite source

We can avoid even the twin alternative at the starting population by a different use of the ordinary linear lower sieve and classical BV. Fix 0<theta1<theta2<1/5, e.g. theta1=.198,theta2=.199. Let B=420. For each prime p in [R^theta1,R^theta2] with p=1 mod B, seek primes v=1 mod B in [(R+1)/p,(2R+1)/p], with every prime factor of pv-2 at least p. Define X=R/p. We have v~X>>p, both p,v are odd, and pv-2 avoids primes dividing B by construction. It is automatically coprime to p. For free odd squarefree d formed from primes <p not dividing B, condition d|pv-2 specifies one reduced class v=2p^(-1) mod d. Together with v=1 mod B this is one reduced class modulo Bd. The sequence of primes v in the fixed class has size ~X/(phi(B)log X) uniformly, by fixed-progression PNT. Equivalently prescribe that main size using the ordinary unprogressed prime interval total divided by phi(B), with the remainder handled in the same BV sum.

Apply the ordinary linear lower sieve at cutoff p and level D=floor(X^(1/2-eta)), with eta>0 sufficiently small that
    (1/2-eta)(1-theta2)/theta2 > 2.
For theta2=.199 take eta=.001. The density g(d)=1/phi(d), one-sided dimension-one product, V(p)>>1/L uniformly, and f(log D/log p) is bounded away from zero since s>2 with a fixed margin. Moduli Bd lie below X^(1/2-eta/2) for large R. The maximum over reduced classes in BV supplies arbitrary log savings, UNIFORMLY in p: the varying residue 2p^(-1) is permitted by ordinary BV at this level; no fixed-residue BFI at higher level is being assumed. The endpoint prime intervals are of order X. Ordinary linear sieve bounded weights and the maximum reduced-class BV error imply for each such p
    # admissible v >>_{theta1,theta2} X/L^2 = R/(p L^2).
All constants uniform as p varies (log X comparable to L). Summing over p=1 mod420 in the broad polynomial range uses fixed-AP PNT and partial summation:
    sum 1/p -> log(theta2/theta1)/phi(420)>0.
Each A=pv-1 is uniquely represented since p<v and v is prime. Hence there are
    >> R/L^2 distinct canonical roots A with
    A+1=pv composite, qmin(A)=p, p in [R^.198,R^.199].  (4)
Note that this density is stronger than selecting a single fixed-width relative band, which would lose one logarithm. No assertion that A-1 is prime; both neighbors satisfy the requisite roughness.

Their first firing is EXACT: v=1 mod4, so the even plus calendar gives w=(p-7)/2, and no earlier label can win because the source is p-rough and the checked height label bound excludes any label >=p at earlier ages. At w, p is eligible and minimal. The destination x=p(v-1) is divisible by420 and in particular by4p. The next three ticks are idle by the checked shortReset_merge_birth (x is 11-rough), so at age (p-1)/2 the actual state is birth(x). This gives a density R/L^2 population of true resets with polynomially growing labels at an explicit exponent near 1/5, unlike the earlier sequential fixed-k construction with much smaller unspecified label exponents.

All sources are non-twin by the forced composite factorization. Apply (3) with theta=.198, s=+1 to this population; after removing o(R/L^2) roots the reset targets are mutually distinct. Apply the earlier neighborhood exclusion with any fixed 0<alpha<beta<.198. Remove another o(R/L^2) roots; then every remaining target and the even centers within displacement J behind it are non-twin and have qmin<R^alpha. Starting from the exact reset birth, the trajectory leaves this neighborhood within o(R^beta) further ticks by the calendar bound. Therefore, for every large R there are >>R/L^2 actual non-twin-source trajectories with distinct canonical reset destinations, first firing p>=R^.198, and mandatory additional gain >L^(2-epsilon) after reset by age R^.199 for large R. The distinct cuts crossed are x-J. Until leaving the neighborhood all observed centers including x are non-twin; the landing center after the crossing is not claimed non-twin. These are unconditional paths even in a world with infinitely many twins. They do not have small gain relative to the time at which the reset occurs: G=p-1 at age (p-1)/2, so ratio is exactly 2 there. This is an actual distribution and postfire theorem, not an improved subcritical gain exponent.

## 5. Re-review of the .295 analytic draft and next genuine obstacle

Reopened primary Pascadi arXiv:2505.00653v2, printed p24 definition/support discussion and (5.3), p28 Theorem 5.4 and Proposition 5.5 (PDF screenshots), and Lichtman arXiv:2109.02851v2 printed pp23-25 (5.15)-(5.21), Lemma5.3, Proposition5.5, Corollary5.6 (PDF screenshots). Appending the prime q to each residual interval-product vector preserves this support at level q_max D_res times arbitrarily small power enlargement; q_max^2 fits the chosen level. Pascadi Proposition5.5 supplies the required factorization for every N in the stated range. This supports the combinatorial transfer step, including arbitrary fixed coefficients in the finite box expansion. However the entire analytic error argument for the appended residual sequence, including all small-N/Type I/large smooth-factor cases and uniformity of switching on the product domain, has not been independently verified. The printed Theorem5.4 applies to its particular full weights and does not, by its wording alone, imply the residual upper with appended q. Keep .295 as a paper proof draft; do not promote it to an accepted theorem solely from this re-review. Further search for the balanced semiprime determinant problem did not produce an applicable theorem at fixed gap 2 with all prime entries balanced; no new claim about its status or about the Ratliff draft.

The new pair-energy result (3) addresses a different barrier: early coalescence of large rough sources does NOT erase their population at the first move. To reach 4.85 through trajectory distribution, a comparable estimate must be extended to genuinely long actual histories, not just first-exit pairs. The current proof relies on the two source roughness conditions and the exact incoming pin; after later low-label events these conditions need not hold, so simply iterating (3) is invalid. A natural next problem is a bound on collisions of bounded-length first-exit suffixes with polynomial label size, or an alternative global cut-flow estimate which controls all later mergers without requiring terminal roughness.
