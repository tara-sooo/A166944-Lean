## 1. Exact local anchors outside every fixed rational strip

Put theta=sqrt(2), alpha=theta/2. For an odd prime p and an odd integer q>=p, choose
    k=floor(alpha q), gcd(k,q)=1,
    H=pq+1, n=2pk, t=n-1.
Then n is even, gcd(n,H-1)=p, and p²<=H+1. At the next tick n the only eligible divisor of the required gcd is p; there is no earlier tick and no smaller tie. Hence the actual exact MIN event from THIS pre-state is p with wait 1, side 0, post-state J=p(q+1), n. This proof does not assume q prime or that the pre-state is reachable from a canonical root. Such an anchor is not a twin-prime stopping horizon (one neighbor is composite). For large q it satisfies H/2<=t<=3H+3 and postdeficit nonnegative.

Write a=(J+2s)/p=q+1, c=(n-2s)/p=2k, with s=0. For every FIXED positive coprime M,N,
    h=Nc-Ma=(N theta-M)q+O_(M,N)(1).                (1)
Since theta is irrational, abs(h) is Theta(q), with constants depending on that rational. For M=1,N=2, h is a positive even integer, and ordinary postdeficit 2n-J=p h EXACTLY. At top scale p in [T/2,T], H in [T²,16T²), q=Theta(T), all fixed rational strips of width polylog X are eventually avoided at ALL anchors (for any fixed finite family, uniformly in that family). The reduced clock fraction can have a large denominator: c/a tends to theta; this implies its reduced denominator tends to infinity uniformly along anchors, not a quantitative lower bound of order T. The projection grows linearly in T and the ordinary deficit is Theta(H).

## 2. Uniform abundance of admissible q

We give a proof, not an empirical prime/gcd independence assumption. The quadratic irrational alpha=1/sqrt(2) is badly approximable: there is c>0 such that ||alpha m||>=c/m for positive integer m. For example, compare the nearest integer n with alpha m using the nonzero integer |m²-2n²|>=1 and factor the difference of squares; n=O(m). (Changing the constant covers small m.)

Consider any interval of q in [c0 T,C0 T] with length Q>=c1 T^(3/4), where c0,c1,C0 positive fixed. Restrict to odd q. For a fixed odd squarefree d, d divides q and floor(alpha q) iff q=d m with m odd and fractional part of alpha m in [0,1/d). Equidistribution of the irrational rotation along odd m gives
    # {q odd in interval: d|q, d|floor(alpha q)}
          =Q/(2d²)+o_d(Q).
The o is uniform in translated intervals of length tending to infinity: the averages of each nonconstant Fourier mode of the rotation are bounded by a geometric-series constant divided by interval length; approximation of interval indicators by trigonometric polynomials gives uniform discrepancy tending to zero. For d fixed the parity and endpoint errors are O(1). Thus for any fixed finite prime cutoff D, inclusion-exclusion gives density (1/2) product_(3<=l<=D prime)(1-1/l²) among all q that avoid these common factors.

Control the large common divisors without assuming uniformity in d. If d divides q and k=floor(alpha q), q=d m, then ||alpha m||<1/d, so by bad approximation d<=m/c and d²<=q/c=O(T). For each such d, the m interval has length O(Q/d+1). Any two fractional parts of alpha m there are separated on the circle by >=c'/(Q/d+1), by bad approximation applied to their difference. Therefore there are O(Q/d²+1) possible m with fractional part in [0,1/d). Summing over all integers d>D up to O(sqrt T), an upper bound on the number of q with a large common prime factor is
    O(Q/D+sqrt T).
Since sqrt T=o(Q), first take D a sufficiently large fixed constant, then T large. The infinite product over odd primes of (1-1/l²) is positive. Thus uniformly every such interval contains Omega(Q) odd q with gcd(q,k)=1. This argument also proves the useful sharper one-sided bound on the bad proportion among odd q:
    #bad <= (Q/2)( sum_(l odd prime) 1/l² + o(1)).
Indeed union bound for finitely many l plus the tail above, then let D grow after T; the errors uniform on this class of intervals. The sum is strictly less than .22, since even the sum over all odd integers >=3 with 9 and 15 removed is pi²/8-1-1/81-1/225<.22. This is an upper bound on bad q, not an asymptotic independence claim. All facts here are elementary irrational-rotation and divisor bounds; no twin prime or simultaneous prime-form lower bound is used.

## 3. Infinite scale blocks and growing support

Take X=16^i, T=sqrt X=4^i, starting at sufficiently large integer i. Divide [X,16X) into S=floor(T^(1/4)) consecutive segments of equal length 15X/S. Choose S distinct odd primes p_j in [T/2,T], for example at evenly spaced indices in their ordered list. Existence for large T follows from the ordinary prime number theorem in a fixed proportional interval (not a twin-prime estimate). In segment j enumerate H=p_j q+1 for odd q with gcd(q,floor(alpha q))=1, trimming 16T at each end. The q interval has length Theta(T/S)=Theta(T^(3/4)), so section 2 supplies Omega(T/S) possible anchors per segment uniformly. Greedily retain them in increasing horizon order separated by at least 16T. Since p_j>=T/2 and q odd, each accepted point discards only O(1) candidates. The trims preserve separation across segments and across adjacent scale blocks. All p_j are eligible because H>=X and q>=p_j. Thus in each block:
    number of retained anchors = Theta(T),
    sum anchor gains p-1 = Theta(X),
    distinct anchor primes = S -> infinity,
    deficit at every anchor = (2theta-1)H+O(T).
Each segment is occupied, and the largest gap between neighboring anchors is O(X/S) including block boundaries (when using the larger scale). The average gap is Theta(T). Within a segment labels repeat; the cofactor q increases, and the number of distinct ordered pairs (label, cofactor) is Theta(T). This is a deliberately broad projection / large complementary gain population, not the short-relay or small-deficit populations in prior sieve theorems.

## 4. Alternating large-label-gap variant

To remove the feature of consecutive equal selected labels, choose S distinct primes p_j in [T/2,5T/8] and S distinct primes r_j in [7T/8,T], one pair per segment; ordinary prime number theorem again suffices. Within the trimmed segment use as many complete bins of width 512T as fit. For a bin beginning at b, take the first acceptable p_j anchor in [b+8T,b+248T] and the first acceptable r_j anchor in [b+264T,b+504T]. Keep BOTH if both exist, otherwise neither. This preserves 16T separation between any neighboring anchors. For consecutive kept bins or segments, the label pattern alternates low/high within these two bands, so each adjacent difference has magnitude >=T/4. Across scale boundaries the same gap holds with the larger T, since the previous scale's maximum is T/4.

There are Theta(T/S) complete bins per segment for large T, because S=T^(1/4). For either fixed track prime p, over the FULL interval of these bins the number of bad odd q is at most (.22+o(1))*512T n_bins/(2p), by section 2. A missing prescribed 240T slot requires that ALL its odd q be bad, at least 240T/(2p)-1 such q. Hence the number of missing slots for that track is at most
    (.22+o(1))*[512T/(240T-2p)] n_bins
      <= (.22+o(1))*(512/238) n_bins.
Both tracks together miss strictly fewer than n_bins, because 2*.22*512/238<1. At least a fixed positive fraction of bins have both anchors. Consequently this variant also has Theta(T) anchors, Theta(X) anchor gain, 2S distinct selected labels, large ordinary deficits, and abs(h)=Theta(T) for each fixed rational projection. Every adjacent selected prime gap is Omega(T). The constant in its macro gain is small (large slot width), but independent of X. It is NOT an all-distinct arbitrary-length word construction: within each segment the same pair repeats, and the complementary gain is macroscopic. This distinction matters for the repeat and rank tolls of XX and earlier.

## 5. Explicit scalar connectors; exactly which conditions are relaxed

Between consecutive anchors let old post-state be (J,n) and next pre-state (H',n'-1). Set E=H'-J, W=n'-1-n. E is even. From separation Delta=H'-H>=16 max scale T, p,p'<=max T, n=theta(H-1)+O(p) with actual error in (-2p,0], and n' analogous, we get
    E=Delta-(p-1), W=theta Delta+O(2 max T+1),
    E<=W<=2E.
For the first connector, start at canonical scalar state H0=X0/16 divisible by 4, t0=H0/2+3 (equivalently M=(H0+2)/2 odd). The inequalities E<=W<=2E also hold for sufficiently large starting X0 by direct substitution, since H'~X0 or within first segment and theta<2. The finite audit uses H0=256 for both variants.

Decompose E into gains of odd prime labels below the chosen growing cap T/8 (rounded down): repeatedly take the largest prime l<=min(floor(T/8), remaining gain+1), subtract l-1. This always finishes, because 3 is allowed, E remains even, and the initial H at a scale boundary is >=X/16, so all connector labels are eligible by their size l²<=H+1. The connectors at a boundary can use the smaller cap instead without changing the proof; in the audited implementation use the new cap, at most the preceding high threshold, which is an even power-of-two multiple and hence not itself an odd prime. Thus interior connector labels stay below T/2, and the boundary convention does not introduce a high label in the preceding block either. Eligibility here means the numerical size cutoff, NOT neighbor divisibility.

If the successive connector gains are g_j=l_j-1 and cumulative gains G_j, set
    w_j=floor(W G_j/E)-floor(W G_(j-1)/E).
Each w_j is an integer in [g_j,2g_j], so 1<=w_j<=2l_j. The connector reaches exactly its required endpoint, and intermediate clock deviates from its linear interpolation by <1. The ordinary deficit increases through every connector event (change 2w_j-g_j), and anchor waits 1 give change 3-p. Nonnegativity of ordinary deficit at anchor pre/post and the canonical starting value ensure it throughout. The upper time envelope t<=3H+3 follows from the interpolation and endpoint envelopes, and every event satisfies the scalar signed change bound -g<=Delta d<=5g and strictly increasing H,t. All labels are actual odd primes, horizon gain is exactly l-1 at each event, no nonprime giant jump. For large H along this stitched path, t/H -> theta uniformly (endpoints approximate theta H to O(sqrt H), interpolation preserves this order after finitely many initial events). In each physical block the anchors have Theta(X) gain; their intervening connector gain is also Theta(X), since each anchor separation costs >=15T-O(1) connector gain and there are Theta(T) anchors. Complementary time/ordinary variation are macroscopic, not a polylog budget. The ordinary deficit has positive linear baseline, no free reset to 6 is introduced after the initial canonical scalar state.

The exact MIN laws are enforced at EVERY designated anchor, including minimum selection and exact clock. At connector events we enforce ONLY the numerical label eligibility, integer gain, time and scalar deficit bounds, not l|H-1+2s, l|n-2s, earliest clock or no crossing of pinned small-label cuts. We cannot apply actual MIN conservation/causal or arithmetic laws to those events just because the scalar relations hold. In particular the stitched model does not satisfy all accumulated theorems. Finite audit below exhibits explicit violations. No construction of a macro actual upward anchor population: every exact anchor wait is 1; positive deficit drift is supplied by relaxed connectors. Normalized waits of the exact anchors are near zero, not broad; the advances between anchor events are large, and the model escapes fixed rational CLOCK/HORIZON strips by (1), not by proving broad distributions of actual MIN waits. The sibling saturation bounds are for actual causal matched mass and do not apply to a relaxed connector as an actual source. No claim that any near-saturated matching is attained.

For a fixed low cap R, the classical cut barrier from IV gives a concrete analytic obstruction to repairing these connectors: a true low-only horizon path cannot cross the next even multiple of 2 times the product of all odd primes <=R (the crossed offset would be even in [0,l-2] while congruent to +-1 modulo l). Equivalently, if 0<=J-H<l-1 with J divisible by l, the necessary H±1 divisibility is impossible for even H,J. Connector gains here grow at least linearly in T, so no FIXED finite prime alphabet suffices to repair all such bridges. The growing caps in the actual tests do not guarantee reachability. This is an exact arithmetic obstacle, rather than an inconsistency in scalar growth.
