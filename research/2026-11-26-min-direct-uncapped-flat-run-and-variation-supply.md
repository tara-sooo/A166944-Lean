# Uncapped fixed-deficit run capacity and variation supply

Continuation of [3214564](https://github.com/tara-sooo/A166944-Lean/blob/3214564a96ec7bab6234d37f253dcb0636b7c229/research/2026-11-26-min-direct-multiband-weighted-deficit-sieve.md). Replace the polynomial multiplier/deficit cap by an explicit divisor multiplicity. Exact flat high-label runs have bounded gain from ordered-pair CRT, without assuming prime cofactors or finite twins. For general near-flat multiband populations the same arithmetic gives a cap-free variation/relay obstruction. A rough-successor sieve is available when the horizon bins can be enlarged and variation is especially small. These results constrain actual persistence of high deficits, but do not show that general high-deficit intervals have small variation or high-label dominance.

## 1. Fixed ordinary deficit: a local persistence theorem

Use actual adaptive MIN event coordinates H,p,g=p-1,w,I,e=1-2I,d,d', with d'=d+2w-g. For p>=5, x=(d'-6I)/2 is a nonnegative multiple of p. On any contiguous physical run with ordinary d'=d=D0>6 at every event, p>=5 divides exactly one of the positive integers D0/2,(D0-6)/2 if it occurs: it cannot divide both because their difference is 3. Thus its side is fixed by D0. A label 3 may occur, but is never included in the high list below. Also 2w=g on every flat event. There is a crude finite bound even for the unrestricted flat run: let P be the product of the DISTINCT primes in {3} union the odd prime divisors of those two integers. Every event label in the flat run divides P. Every multiple of 2P is a horizon barrier for these labels (the elementary half-open pinned barrier property), so the total gain of that run is <2P. This bound alone may be of quadratic order in D0 and is not a useful macroscopic estimate at large deficit.

Now take a contiguous physical flat run with all labels in [T,R], 7<=T<=R, and denote its total gain by G_flat. Let S be its actual distinct label support, s=|S|. Each label has its fixed side as above. Two consecutive labels p,r are distinct: the same-label return identity needs intervening gain E>=p-1 whereas here E=0. For every ordered pair p!=r in S its first pre-horizon satisfies
    H=e(p) mod p, H+p-1=e(r) mod r.
This fixes one residue modulo pr. The number of occurrences of that ordered pair is at most 1+G_flat/(pr), because distinct first pre-horizons lie in an interval of length G_flat. Its ending gain r-1 is at most R. Sum over ordered pairs, paying the first event separately:
    G_flat <= R + sum_(p!=r in S) (r-1)(1+G_flat/(pr))
           <= [1+s(s-1)]R + G_flat (s-1) sum_(p in S)1/p
           <= [1+s(s-1)]R + G_flat s(s-1)/T.         (1)
Consequently if T>=2s^2,
    G_flat <= 2[1+s(s-1)]R.                           (2)
Elapsed ticks in this run equal G_flat/2. Its number of events is at most G_flat/(T-1). This is a bound on an actual flat high-only run, not an upper count of hypothetical starting states. No multiplier cap or long-event factor hierarchy is needed. If all pre-horizons are in [X,lambda X] on a canonical ray, the envelope gives D0=O_lambda(X), and the number of distinct eligible high factors in the two integers is <=2 log(O_lambda(X))/log T. Thus for any fixed a>0, T>=X^a makes s bounded, and such a flat high-only run has O_(lambda,a)(R) gain. At T,R comparable to sqrt X it has bounded event count, independently of how large the multipliers are. For D0<=6 a positive high nonreset is impossible, so the high-only nonreset version is vacuous.

More generally for an arbitrary flat run with selected high nonresets and other labels allowed, let F be the selected gain, U=G_flat-F, S their actual support and s=|S|. On the one selected list retain consecutive pairs with relay E<=B<T-1. Again p!=r, and fixed ordered pair and E give one residue mod pr because sides are determined by D0. Sum E over at most B+1 possibilities, and pay nonclose ending events by their disjoint relay budgets:
    F <= R+(B+1)s(s-1)(R+G_flat/T)+R U/(B+1).         (3)
For F>=rho G_flat, if s^2/T is small and G_flat much larger than s^2 R, one may choose B+1 proportional (with sufficiently small constant depending on rho) to min(T/s^2,G_flat/(s^2 R)); this gives
    U >> (F/R) min(T/s^2,G_flat/(s^2 R)).
In particular a macroscopic flat window with near-square-root selected labels and F>=rho X requires U=Omega(X). This latter statement uses the actual bounded support from the fixed deficit, not a hypothesis that all high labels lie in a single adjusted stratum. Equation (3) permits substantial low-label gain; no universal bound on the length of a high-deficit interval without restrictions on its variation or event population follows.

## 2. Cap-free multiband supply bound

Fix lambda>1, contiguous actual canonical block with pre-horizons in [X,Y],Y=lambda X, cap C=floor sqrt(Y+1), total gain G=O_lambda(X). Choose one arbitrary list of nonreset visits with T<=p<=R<=C, integer T->infinity. There is NO cap on x=hp>0 apart from the canonical envelope x<=K_lambda X, with fixed K_lambda for large X. Let b=max(1,floor(log(K_lambda X)/log T)), so each such positive x has at most b distinct prime divisors >=T. This includes x>=T^2 and multiple high divisors. Let m=1+floor log_2(R/T). Let F be chosen gain,U_out=G-F,V_d total physical ordinary variation.

For consecutive chosen visits use E=H2-H-(p-1),v=d2'-d1',c=x2-x1=v/2-3(J-I). Close means E<=B<T-1, |v|<=A, Q=floor(A/2)+3<=T/2. The distinct-label assertion follows from E<T-1; c=0 is NOW allowed. For each ending label band [L,2L),L=T*2^j, group close edges by the bin of FIRST pre-horizon of length T L, exactly as in 3214564. In a nonempty group let W=range of ending x2 +1, with NO padding to a power of T. For fixed ending x2 and c, both x2 and x2-c are positive <=K_lambda X. There are at most b^2 choices of distinct p|x2-c,r|x2. For fixed E and sides the first pre-horizon lies in one class modulo pr>=T L, hence at most one in the bin. Actual continuation is unique on this selected list. The weighted group count is at most
    O(b^2 L W (A+1)(B+1)).
Here there is no primality sieve in the x coordinate, and no requirement of unique high factor or nonzero displacement. Within each ending band, the group ending-post intervals are disjoint, and W<=4+V_group/2. There are O_lambda(X/(T L)) bins per ending band because T L<=Y+1. Across bands use sum L<=2R; do not assert their intervals disjoint. Thus
    sum_(L,bin) L W = O_lambda(X m/T+R V_d).
As a result,
    F_close << b^2 (A+1)(B+1)(X m/T+R V_d).           (4)
All constants absolute or lambda-dependent; no fixed exponent lower bound on T. Global relay sum <=U_out and global ordinary post difference sum <=V_d, on the ONE selected list, hence
    F <= R + O_lambda(b^2(A+1)(B+1)(X m/T+R V_d))
             + R U_out/(B+1)+R V_d/(A+1).             (5)

For F>=rho X, y=R V_d/X,z=R U_out/X, optimization gives
    b^2(m/T+y)(1+y)(1+z) >>_(lambda,rho) 1.            (6)
To see this, if the product is already above a sufficiently small fixed constant nothing is required. Otherwise m/T>0 implies y,z=O(T/(b^2 m)) with arbitrarily small constant when the product is small enough. Choose A=ceil(K0 y),B=ceil(K0 z) for fixed K0 large relative to rho; by choosing the initial small constant accordingly the cutoffs meet the close constraints for large T. The last terms in (5) are <=2X/K0, R=o(X), and the close term is bounded by a fixed multiple of X times the product. This contradicts F>=rho X if the product is sufficiently small. This argument also covers bounded m,b; it does not require cutoffs to be o(T) in those cases.

For instance, if b^2 m/T=o(1) and y,z both tend to zero fast enough that b^2 y=o(1), a macroscopic high population is impossible. Quantitatively (6) gives U_out+V_d=Omega(X/(R b^2)) in this regime b^2 m/T=o(1): if y+z is smaller than a sufficiently small constant times 1/b^2, the product is too small. If z=O(1), it gives V_d=Omega(X/(R b^2)). If y<=O(m/T), it instead gives U_out=Omega(X T/(R b^2 m)) when b^2 m/T=o(1). For T>=X^a we have b=O_a(1),m=O(log X); these yield an uncapped square-root-size obstruction when R comparable to sqrt X, and a polynomial lower bound on complementary gain in the especially low-variation regime. This complements, rather than improves throughout, the stronger logarithmic bounds with deficit caps. It does not prove that y or z is small in actual macroscopic windows.

## 3. Rough-successor strengthening in wider horizon bins

For a second estimate assume R^2 N<=X with integer 1<=N<=T and A+5B<=(T-1)/2. Every close successor is long: the intervening ordinary increase is <=5E, so d'_2-d_pre2>=-A-5B and 2w2>=g2-A-5B>=g2/2. Hence both H2+-1 avoid every odd prime f with 8f<T-1, by the eligible competitor clock bound. In particular the cofactor q=(H2-e2)/r and the opposite neighbor r q+2e2 avoid them. Prime cofactors themselves are not required.

Group by BOTH starting band [U,2U) and ending band [L,2L), U,L=T*2^j, then the FIRST pre-horizon bin of length U L N. For fixed x2,c, E, sides and a choice of p,r as above, the horizon CRT gives H=H0+pr z on an interval of at most N+1 consecutive integer z. The successor forms are
    q=q0+p z,    r(q0+p z)+2e2,
where q0=(H0+p-1+E-e2)/r is integral. For every odd prime f<T not dividing the slopes (automatic for f<T) their roots are distinct, because their determinant is 2e2*p. Use the earlier dimension-two interval sieve on a padded interval of N+2 parameters, with cutoff floor(N+2)^(1/12) below (T-1)/8 for large T. This gives O(N/log^2(2+N)) candidates per tuple (bounded N is trivial). No singular factor loss is needed since all primes below the cutoff are generic except 2. This is a sieve in H, NOT a prime-line sieve in x, so it remains valid at arbitrarily large multipliers within the canonical envelope.

Per pair of bands, the ordered ending-state intervals remain disjoint across the bins; their ranges W<=4+V_group/2. There are O_lambda(X/(U L N)) bins because U L N<=X. Weight by ending gain <=2L and sum:
    F_close << b^2(A+1)(B+1) (X m/T+N m R V_d)
                                    /log^2(2+N).       (7)
Indeed the baseline sum is O(X sum_(U,L)1/U)=O(X m/T), and the variation contribution is O(N V_d sum_(U,L)L)=O(N m R V_d). Compare (4): (7) can be worse when variation is not very small. For example if R^2 T^epsilon<=X with fixed 0<epsilon<1, take N=floor T^epsilon. When R V_d=O(X/(T N)), the resulting upper bound is
    F_close << b^2 (A+1)(B+1) X m/(T log^2 T).
This gains two logarithms over the cap-free baseline in that regime. If R is near sqrt X, there may not be room to enlarge bins; (4) remains valid without the assumption R^2 N<=X. No bound on the proportion of actual near-flat long-event edges that satisfy these budgets is asserted.

## 4. Global interpretation and remaining problem

The fixed-deficit theorem rules out arbitrarily long uninterrupted runs of large labels maintaining exactly the same high deficit, even with unbounded h, unless the divisor support grows enough. The cap-free product restricts low variation with arbitrary high deficit and mixed labels. But a trajectory with significant ordinary variation, or with substantial other-event gain, can evade these results. In particular the original pin-only low-count alternative does not force F>=rho X for a selected high nonreset list, and there is no established global upper bound on V_d of the size required in (6) or (7). The earlier small/middle crossing counts, resets and large upward nonreset gain remain unresolved. No final contradiction or twin-infinitude proof. A useful next direction is to obtain positive/negative variation budgets localized by *deficit level* that do not assume the entire window is nearly flat; the flat-run theorem alone cannot do that.
