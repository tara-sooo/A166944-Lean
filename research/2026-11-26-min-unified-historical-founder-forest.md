## 2. A historical component forest without ghost trajectories

Process all original true vertices in increasing tick time (ties at distinct vertices are disjoint). Insert each original root as a singleton at its own initial vertex. Whenever incoming active cohorts and any root injection share a vertex, merge their historical components. A historical component is a set of original root tags, including members already stopped. Its mass is its cardinality; retirement never deletes a member from that abstract set. A component with no continuing member never participates in another merge. Only actual active tags traverse physical edges; no stopped tag acquires a further actual vertex or lifetime.

Invariant: the active members of one historical component are at most one physical cohort at a time. Initially immediate. All members of an active cohort that continue take the same deterministic physical successor, and retirement can only remove some; distinct cohorts can merge but never split. Thus at each true vertex distinct actual incoming branches come from DISTINCT historical components; all actual branch members belong to the same component. A singleton injection is its own component. In particular the historical mass of a branch is at least its actual flow. Induction works with terminal-only cohorts and multiple simultaneous incoming branches. The abstract history is a rooted merger forest on disjoint root sets, with strictly increasing historical mass at genuine merges. This construction does not identify two trajectories merely because they meet at different ticks with the same projected horizon.

Call a historical component large if mass >=L. At a vertex let k be the number of incoming large historical components (include actual singleton injections, which are small). A FOUNDER event occurs if k=0 but the union mass is >=L. Its entire union becomes large for the first time. Let F be the number of founders, S their total historical mass. These founder sets are pairwise disjoint even when some of their members were retired before the founder event: once a member is in a large historical component, any later component containing it is still large. Thus
    L F <= S <= N.
At a vertex with k>=1, every small historical incoming component is absorbed into a large one. Let A be the sum of their full historical masses across all such vertices. Every such member crosses the historical threshold once and is not a founder member. Hence
    A+S<=N.
Let J=sum_(vertices with k>=2)(k-1). A founder increases the number of available large historical components by one, a multiple-large merger decreases it by k-1; retirement can make a large component inactive but cannot create a new active component. Thus J<=F (in fact J<=F minus the number of final large historical components if all inactive components are retained in the accounting). Combine:
    A + L J <= A + L F <= A+S <= N.               (1)
In particular #multiple-large historical merger vertices <=J<=N/L, without d or m factors. This is a single conservation inequality for disjoint first-maturity memberships and large-history merger multiplicity; it is not a claim that the active populations are monotone. Historical root membership can span a much longer interval than the lifetime of a currently active tag, so do not use historical masses as actual source denominators or actual lifetime populations.

## 3. Exact remaining birth partition and larger thin cutoff

At the first actual meeting V of any old surviving backward pair, the two actual incoming branches are distinct. At least one has actual mass >=L, and therefore historical mass >=L. Classify the pair using those TWO actual branches' historical component masses immediately before V:
* historical merge: both historical masses >=L;
* historical attachment: one historical mass <L and the other >=L.

This is an exact partition of old surviving nonnegative fractions, not an added transport. The second type can include an actual incoming branch of mass in [m,L), formerly classified as a heavy merge. The new two types are not the same as the old physical m/L types: an actual small branch can have a large history. Preserve all old bad-tag and birth masks.

All historical merge fractions are supported on at most J<=N/L actual true vertices, regardless of the selected prime labels or how many branch pairs occur at the vertex. Aggregate exact remaining historical merge clock B_hist(V) over all such pairs at V, with the original grid average. Pay any vertex with B_hist(V)<=eta H L. Total <=eta H L J<=eta H N=o(NT). Compared with the earlier eta H m aggregate cutoff for two-physically-heavy births, this is larger by L/m->infinity, on the new historical-merge domain. The earlier cutoff remains in force. If desired, use threshold eta H L(k-1) at V with k>=2; the same summed bound holds by (1), and accounts for multiway merges without a d factor. This is a new exact thin-key deletion; no estimate on the sum of remaining large keys. For separate historical branch-pair keys one may use threshold eta H L/d because the number of pairs <=(d/2)J.

Every remaining historical attachment has a UNIQUE small-history member (one of the two actual tags). That tag is crossing the historical threshold at V and can never be a small-history member at another such vertex. This is true even if the actual branch mass is >=m, and does not require the earlier edge-return lemma. Moreover for an old surviving pair the other branch is actually >=L (because the small-history branch has actual size <L), so V is also the small-history member's first actual physical large vertex. The tag could not previously have visited an actual large vertex while its historical mass was still <L. Thus the existing first-large age and final maturity strips apply without change. Let I_hist be the distinct LIVE small-history tags entering such large components (including all actual attachments to historical large components for an upper bound). Then |I_hist|<=A, including cases where other historical members had already retired. The smaller domain with at least one actual-large branch only reduces this count.

For a fixed tag i in I_hist define its attachment vertex V_i and at an original later positive source G, size b, let H_G^hist(V_i) be the number of G members entering V_i via historical-large incoming components. One may restrict this union to actually-large branches because old remaining pairs require at least one branch >=L. Per atom K the total two-orientation clock of all residual attachment pairs with small-history member i is <=2 c_a P H_G^hist(V_i)/b. Sum over i's actual eligible source atoms, zero if no positive source, retaining all earlier strips. The resulting upper envelope satisfies
    B_hist,att <= 2 c_a mean_P P sum_(i in I_hist) sum_(eligible K) H_G^hist(V_i)/b
               <= 2 c_a [sum_(i in I_hist) (F_i-M_i^+-2h)_+ + |I_hist| P_max],  (2)
where the last sum is restricted to actual eligible non-Bad terminal-nonmoving tags with a first physical large vertex and M_i^+=max(J_i,A_i^+) as in 78700a0. Use terminal and final-maturity restrictions only on those original tags satisfying them; no lifetime for retired historical members is added. The unique attachment permits paying any exact residual source fraction with H_G^hist(V_i)<=eta b at total O(eta NT), or an attachment tag with eligible remaining gain <=eta H at the same order. This generalizes the old disjoint actual-small attachment bound to the new historical attachment domain. Do not add the alternative old and new envelopes as though they were disjoint; use a single exact partition or take minima.

Equations (1)-(2) also give a shared founder-budget inequality. If the chosen exact merge-vertex thresholds are eta H L(k-1) and the attachment-small-share rule uses eta, the combined cost is bounded by
    eta H L J + 2 c_a eta (H+P_max) A
    <= (1+2 c_a) eta N(H+P_max).
One can use the smaller maximum of the two coefficients in this estimate, since A+LJ<=N. No constant saving on the untrimmed residual is implied by this identity.

## 4. Historical maturity keys versus physical maturity keys

For an active original tag define its first historical-large vertex as the actual vertex where it first belongs to a component of historical mass >=L. For tags stopped before a founder/attachment, their abstract threshold-crossing event is NOT a visited vertex; such tags cannot occur in later source slots and are excluded from the following classification. At any actual large source, both the historical and actual physical first-large keys exist, with historical time <= physical time. Every surviving birth has distinct historical keys: if both components already large their first-large historical vertices are in different histories; if one small, its first key is the birth vertex and the other's was earlier. Similarly the old physical first-large keys are distinct.

At a later source let n_* be the original subset passing the old source eligibility and final strips. Denote by a_v the counts by physical first-large key, c_w by historical first-large key, and d_vw the joint counts. Then the ordered candidate pair count satisfying both distinct-key conditions is exactly
    n_*² - sum_v a_v² - sum_w c_w² + sum_(v,w) d_vw².
However there is an important simplification: two source tags with the SAME historical first-large key were both alive at that vertex and share its deterministic future through the common source. They cannot have had a physical-large vertex before historical maturity, because historical mass dominates actual mass. Hence they have the SAME physical first-large key. The historical partition refines the physical partition on actual source members; sum_(v,w)d_vw²=sum_w c_w². Thus the combined cut equals the old physical-key cut n_*²-sum_v a_v². The new historical classification DOES NOT improve that particular source-count envelope. Its advantage is the global founder budget (1) and the larger thin-key cutoff, together with unique small-history attachment reindexing. The finite audit confirms joint and physical cut equality; no claim of double savings.

## 5. Age budget and the remaining obstruction

For a founder event with historical union mass q>=L, the origins of its historical members all precede its projected horizon (every actual past membership merge is along a continuing path with nondecreasing horizon). But some of these members may have stopped much earlier: their age at this event is NOT an actual consumed gain. Therefore the old prime-origin lower bound q(q-1)log(2+q) on actual consumed gain cannot be applied to the full founder mass. Apply the age bound of 78700a0 only to live tags at their actual first physical maturity, including the small-history attachment members at V in the surviving domain. This retains the old residence bound R_plus and the per-live-group gain lower bound, but does not turn (1) into a constant-fraction residence deletion. The historic forest is a combinatorial accounting device, not a new arithmetic recurrence. The historical founder count may be small without forcing an upper bound on the remaining attachments' lifetimes.

New exact payments total O(eta NT+NP_max) on top of prior masks. Under finitely many twins the earlier certificate remains
    NT/2-o(NT) <= B_new+(3+o(1))(D_l,old+D_h,old).
The corresponding strict liminf <1/2 suffices, with no-larger exact residual. One can upper-bound B_new by the minimum of the old physical-key residence/cut and the sum of (2) and the old lifetime envelopes over only surviving historical merger keys. The scalar R_plus sufficient condition is unchanged; no strictly improved scalar threshold or final constant margin is established. Main and independent source branches not modified. No Lean compilation.
