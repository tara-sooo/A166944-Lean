## 2. Proportional departure theorem

Take an actual selected p at D, let m be latest canonical point at/before its departure (including virtual points inside that wait), g=D-(2m-2)>=0. Measure current wait w from the actual pre-state, or from the canonical point if inside this wait. Elapsed from canonical to the pre-state <=3g, since every completed event has wait<=3(p'-1), and the first wait may be shortened. Thus pre-deficit <=6+5g. From nonnegative post-deficit,
    2w>=p-1-(6+5g).
In particular if p>=10g+16, then w>=p/4, r(D)>=p/8. Also the post-deficit <=6+5g+4p-(p-1)<=4p+6. This condition is on actual original history; no restriction on how many intervening events or on which labels they use. It includes direct departures with p>=17, and all pre-gains g up to (p-16)/10, not just g<=u^1/8.

Set Y=max(17,ceil(B^1/8)). For any actual event with p>=Y and p>=10g+16, group original tags by labeled physical POST key alpha=(J,t,p), J=D+p-1. Different tags in that group can have different latest canonical m and different pre-times. Each tag visits a given key at most once; all share the actual physical post-state. This changes the previous group identity (type,m). For fixed D,p, the sign s is determined by p|D+2s-1 (odd p cannot divide both). The post clock phase satisfies
    2t-J=6s+2pk, J=2k mod4.
For p>=17 and 0<=delta_post<=4p+6 only O(1) k, at most three, are possible for each D,p. Hence unlike the previous origin-index envelope, NO multiplicity factor proportional to the allowed pre-gain g occurs. It is not necessary that any one canonical origin be unique per key.

Uniform quotient two-form sieve: for fixed p>=Y and sign, D±1=pj,pj±2, j odd; r(D)>=p/8 implies j and pj±2 avoid odd primes up to B^1/64 eventually. Apply the same dimension-two Selberg estimate as e15eb81 in j=2n+1, two distinct forbidden residues at all these odd primes; count O(X/(p u²)+B^3/32)=O(X/(p u²)) pairs D for that p. Thus over all possible physical post keys with these relaxed conditions,
    #keys = O(X/u²) (sum_{Y<=p<=B}1/p=O(1)),
    S_gain = sum_alpha (p(alpha)-1) = O(XB/u³).        (1)
We count actual candidate keys, not source-tag multiplicities. This support bound holds for arbitrarily many origins and across the full proportional pre-gain range.

## 3. Thin physical departures and iterative removal

Let n_alpha be original tag count assigned to a qualifying physical key, m0=floor(u^11/4). All thin keys n_alpha<=m0 together have selected gain <=m0 S_gain=O(XB/u^1/4)=o(XB). Their original full half-wait cost <=1.5 times that gain (including the whole wait when the canonical point was virtual), hence any clipped clock loss is also o(XB). This improves the earlier near-origin gain estimate O(XB/u^1/8) while extending the range. It is a bound on the new physical groups, not on all thin origin groups; the grouping changes which tags are called thin.

For arbitrary reference family, iteratively delete a root if current thin qualifying gain >=eps B, eps=u^-1/8. Charge its currently thin groups, then remove all its memberships. At first charge the active count of that key is <=m0, so its lifetime charge <=m0(p-1). Consequently #deleted<=m0 S_gain/(eps B)=O(X/u^1/8)=o(X). Lost original late slab clock o(XB). At the fixed point total remaining thin qualifying gain <=eps BN=o(XB). Every other qualifying departure has >m0 actual retained tags at a shared physical post-state. No estimate here on the fraction of all gain which qualifies; the other events can have long accumulated gain from the latest canonical point. In particular one cannot infer any bound on unrestricted large ancestor masses from (1). Deleting whole roots is safe for the prior criterion; applying event-only linear masks must respect MED/LOW destination accounting.

## 4. One-large canonical arcs: inverse blocks including arbitrary LOW prefixes

Let y>=2, L=product of primes <=y (include 2). A LOW-only run (labels <=y) has total gain <L: it cannot strictly cross an even multiple bL, since a crossing p-jump from D<bL would have even difference d=bL-D in (0,p-1), with d congruent +1 or -1 mod p, impossible. If it reaches bL it cannot select another LOW there (both neighbors coprime to all LOW primes). Hence every such run ends before or at the next barrier. For a complete canonical arc m->n containing exactly ONE nonLOW selected prime p>=max(Y,10L+16), all other events LOW, write D its pre-horizon and J=D+p-1 its post-horizon. LOW prefix and suffix each have gain <L. From post-state to canonical endpoint the deficit can decrease by at most the intervening gain (any waiting increases it). Therefore post deficit <=6+L, and post phase 6s+2pk (k>=0) forces k=0. The high event enters a reset. By the exact even/odd bridge the next canonical point is the endpoint of the arc. Even if several LOW events followed, the first canonical point must be that bridge endpoint; cutoff cannot interfere with a complete observed arc.

For destination n, odd reset has J=2n-2, p|n; even reset has J=2n-2-4k_b, p|n-1-2k_b, k_b=0,1,2. Each p and case uniquely fixes J,t,D. Thus all one-large incoming canonical arcs at n are grouped into at most
    sum_{d in {0,1,3,5}} omega_{>=P}(n-d)
    = O(log B/log P)
distinct physical high post keys, where P=max(Y,10L+16) or any larger threshold. All origins for one such key lie in D-L<2m-2<=D, an interval of length L/2 in canonical index. In particular each group has <=ceil(L/4)+1 possible odd canonical origins. Total inverse degree O((L+1) log B/log P), with only O(1) distinct physical branches for polynomial P. This extends the previous elementary-return inverse result to arbitrary LOW prefixes, which can contain many distinct canonical origins. These origins need not be consecutive or have bounded original ancestry. Every such high event qualifies for section 2 (g<L), so gain of thin physical branches is handled by (1)-(3). General arcs with more than one nonLOW event or with intermediate labels remain.

## 5. Mixed reverse-word prefix bound independent of exceptional degrees

This additional bound controls actual projected multiplicity from deep LOW histories, with no restriction on canonical resets. Let u=log B, ell=log u, choose y largest cutoff with primorial L<=max(2,(1+ell)^1/4) (eventually y grows), Qr=ceil(sqrt B), K_r=floor(ell/[8 log(20(L+1))]). Consider a tag at pre-horizon C whose entire original selected history STRICTLY BEFORE C contains at most K_r labels >=Qr, at most two labels y<p<Qr, and arbitrarily many LOW labels <=y. Include the root as empty history; current selected label is unrestricted. This includes any number of LOW-only canonical arcs and one-large arcs obeying those high/medium count budgets, but does not require any return at all. Use the full original prefix, not a pruned path.

In reverse, a LOW block ending at a given horizon has at most W=ceil(L/2)+1 possible initial even horizons. The last nonLOW event ending at J has p|J or J+2, predecessor J-p+1. Thus number of possible high labels >=Qr is <=A0=2 floor(log(7X+2)/log Qr)=O(1), and intermediate labels <=A1=2 floor(log(7X+2)/log(max(3,y+1)))=O(u). We do not need to know the predecessor time: enumerate horizon possibilities only, a superset of actual roots. For exactly i high and j intermediate labels in the reversed history, at most binomial(i+j,j) words; for each such word at most W*(W A0)^i*(W A1)^j choices of original horizon (possibly with duplicate candidates). The final LOW block toward root gives the extra W. Each even root horizon determines at most one original odd M. Summing i<=K_r and j<=2 gives the uniform bound
    U_good(C) <= #good tags <=
       W sum_{i<=K_r,j<=2} binomial(i+j,j)(W A0)^i(W A1)^j
       = O(u^(17/8+o(1))).                           (2)
Indeed W A0<=20(L+1) eventually, its K_r power<=u^1/8, and polynomial factors in ell,L and K_r are u^o(1); two intermediate labels contribute u². In particular O(u^9/4) eventually. This count tolerates the worst possible prime-factor degree for each of the two intermediate labels, unlike regular-vertex-only reverse bounds. It does not bound all histories with three or more intermediate labels or more than K_r high events. If no intermediate labels, bound O(u^(1/8+o(1))). For near-B labels >=P0=ceil(100 B log(20(L+1))/ell), eventually P0<B and at most G/(P0-1)<=K_r such events by gain. Hence ALL histories with LOW labels, arbitrary near-B labels >=P0 and at most two labels y<p<Qr satisfy this bound, with no further depth restriction. The number of LOW events can grow much faster than K_r. Labels in [Qr,P0) are allowed under the general explicit count budget, but not automatically covered by gain.

For any natural or fractional masked final sector, write U=U_good+U_other. The exact energy contribution involving at least one good tag weight (including its own diagonal) is
    E-E_other=sum_C r(C)(2 U U_good-U_good²)
             <=2 sup_C U_good R
             = O(XB u^9/4)=o(XB u³).                 (3)
No restriction on other endpoint's mass, current selected p, physical synchronization, or latest canonical arc. Thus both same-arc and cross-arc contributions of these prefixes are paid unconditionally, at both sufficient sector scales. This is an actual pair/weight energy bound, not a claim that removing their linear occupation is harmless. One may leave these weights in the linear clock ledger. Remaining overlap requires both tags to fail the reverse-word prefix condition, i.e. have >=3 intermediate selections or >K_r high selections, possibly different reasons. This complements previous short-depth reverse estimates by distinguishing arithmetically large labels, rather than treating every nonLOW as an equal branching cost. It is not a replacement for earlier stronger results in their own regimes.

## 7. Remaining

The physical-key departure grouping gives an unconditional o(XB) thin gain/clock bound over a much wider pre-gain range; the inverse block theorem controls one-large non-elementary arcs and the mixed reverse-word theorem controls a specific class of actual pair contributions without degree-exception pruning. Need independent control of long renewal excursions and basins with many intermediate selections, or a mechanism that forces a substantial portion of later occupation into the paid prefix classes. Neither general renewal-basin size nor full cross-arc energy has been bounded. No Lean compilation, main unchanged, twin-prime conjecture OPEN.
