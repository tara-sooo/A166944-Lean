# Causal level transport at the recycling scale and same-label stratum loss

Continuation of [cb54101](https://github.com/tara-sooo/A166944-Lean/blob/cb54101e5a4492666783ac87de5c0591d69f1025/research/2026-11-26-min-direct-averaged-upward-return-sieve.md). Move from aggregate clipped-potential balance to causal, non-reused height-by-height matches of physical upward and downward events. The same-label component has an additional arithmetic restriction: apart from a total bounded exceptional cost per source, transport within one label requires loss of exactly one multiplier stratum and a very long source clock. On a comparable-label, narrow-guard regime, the transport can be localized to the actual logarithmic recycling scale, without the polynomial bin-length assumption of 7b426da. These are conditional supply restrictions, not a proof that the required transport is impossible.

## 1. Exact causal matching

Use actual adaptive MIN coordinates H,p,g=p-1,w,I,e=1-2I, ordinary even deficits d,d'=d+2w-g. For p>=5, d'=2ph+6I with h>=0. Work on one physical contiguous canonical block of pre-horizons in [X,Y], Y=lambda X fixed lambda>1, C=floor sqrt(Y+1), total gain G=O_lambda(X). Partition its physical event list by half-open horizon bins of integer width L, assigning whole events by their PRE-horizon. In each bin choose any subset of high fast nonresets p>=T>=9, k=floor sqrt(T), kw<g, with chosen gain F_j. Let E_j be the union of [d',d'+g] for these events, M_j its length. More generally take any finite measurable height guard containing those intervals. We will use only finite unions of intervals. All chosen drops are wholly inside E_j and strictly larger than alpha*g, alpha=1-2/k.

At a level u in E_j consider the chronological changes in the indicator 1_{d>u} at all physical events of the bin (ignore the finite set of endpoints of measure zero). Its transitions alternate upward and downward. Match each downward transition to the immediately preceding upward transition of that level within the bin, if one exists. The first downward transition may be unmatched if the starting deficit lies above u; there can be at most one unmatched downward transition per level. Use this matching for ALL transitions first and retain only the matches whose downward event belongs to the chosen fast population. In particular an intervening unselected downward transition consumes its preceding upward transition; it is not permissible to reuse that upward transition for a later chosen drop. The matching is measurable, piecewise constant on the finitely many intervals between plateau heights and guard endpoints.

Write W(a,b) for the total length of levels matched from actual upward event a to actual chosen downward event b. Every positive weight requires H_a<H_b, H_b-H_a<L, and lies in the intersection of the actual upward and downward segments with E_j. For each source a, sum_b W(a,b)<=length(E_j intersect (d_a,d'_a))<=min(M_j,3p_a+1). For each target b, the matched plus unmatched weight is exactly d_b-d'_b. No source-level unit is used twice. Denoting total matched weight by P_match,j,
    alpha F_j <= P_match,j+M_j.                       (1)
This improves the interpretation of the old telescoping inequality: the charged upward supply actually PRECEDES and pays the matched drop at the same height. It does not depend on net deficit variation across the bin, and it remains valid for bins containing large overshooting events. Distinct bins have disjoint physical events; only their own unmatched costs M_j need be added. No assumption of cancellation between changing potentials is needed.

## 2. Same-label transport is almost entirely a one-stratum loss

Consider a matched pair with the SAME label p>=T and source post-deficit a=2ph+6I, target post-deficit b=2pk+6J. Write u_up=d'_source-d_source>0 and u_down=d_target-d'_target>0. The clock bounds give u_up<=3p+1, u_down<=p-3 (since w>=1). Both are even. The matched levels lie within (a-u_up,a) intersect (b,b+u_down). Since p>=9, h-k<0 implies b>a, so cannot have positive overlap. If h-k>=3 then a-b>=6p-6>4p-2>=u_up+u_down, also impossible. Thus h-k=0,1,2 are the only possibilities.

* If h=k, all overlap, regardless of side, lies in [a-6,a]. For ONE fixed source the total matched weight to any number of same-stratum same-label targets is <=6, by the level non-reuse.
* If h-k=2, then a-b=4p+6(I-J). Positive overlap requires I=0,J=1, since u_up+u_down<=4p-2. In that case b=a-4p+6 is fixed, and all such overlap lies in [a-3p-1,a-3p+3], of length 4. Also u_up>3p-3, hence u_up>=3p-1 by parity, and source wait w>=2p-1. Total matched weight to all two-stratum same-label targets of ONE source is <=4. This case can only use a nearly maximal source clock.
* For h-k=1, a-b>=2p-6. Positive overlap implies u_up> a-b-u_down>=p-3, so u_up>=p-1, w_source>=p-1. Thus the same-label one-stratum transfer requires a source ordinary increase at least its own horizon gain. It can have arbitrarily large h, but the target's adjusted deficit must be exactly 2p smaller.

In particular a weak upward source with u_up<=p-3 can transport at most 6 to same-label targets in total, not just per target. More generally the entire zero- or two-stratum same-label exception costs at most 10 PER SOURCE, independent of guard width or number of matching targets. The number of high upward sources is at most G/(T-1). Summing over any disjoint bins, the total exceptional same-label matched flux is <=10G/(T-1)=o(X). Consequently if selected fast gain is >=rho X and sum_j M_j=o(X), the total matched flux is >=(rho-o(1))X, and all but o(X) of its same-label part consists of one-stratum loss. The entire transport has the alternative:
    cross-label matched flux + one-stratum same-label matched flux
          >= alpha sum_j F_j - sum_j M_j - 10G/(T-1).
In particular one of these two types is macroscopic. This conclusion does not assume low-label scarcity, a narrow label band, finite twins, or small total variation. It concerns actual causal matches, not hypothetical eligible labels.

There is a further exact return-clock relation in the one-stratum case. Write q_i=(H_i-e_i)/p, m=(q_2-q_1)/2>=1, and l=((n_2-2J)-(n_1-2I))/p>=1, where n_i are the firing ticks. The known same-label identity D_2-D_1=2p(l-m), D=d'-6I, now reads l=m-1. Hence m>=2 and m-1=J-I mod2. The two matched events need not be consecutive occurrences of p. This is not the same pair as the two upward events in the preceding averaged-return sieve. In addition if p>2(Y+1)^(1/3)+1, the strong source w>=p-1 ensures all factors of H+-1 are > (Y+1)^(1/3) (eligible small rival clocks would fire strictly earlier). The selected cofactor is then prime: q>=p and a composite q would give at least three factors of H-e above the cube threshold, impossible. The downward target need not have prime cofactor. No twin pair is deduced from this relation.

## 3. Upward supply on logarithmically short bins

A useful refinement of the previous time-height sieve is a bound that retains its actual short interval length. For a fixed high label p in a bin of arbitrary integer width L, the selected upward events have q in at most N=ceil(L/p)+2 consecutive integers for each side. At p>=T->infinity both q and pq+2e avoid the small odd primes below (p-1)/4. Use z=floor(min(p,N)^(1/12)), then z^6<=sqrt(N). By the previous elementary two-root upper sieve, with bounded values of min(p,N) handled by the trivial count,
    # upward occurrences of p << N/log^2(2+min(p,N)).
This is uniform in the translated bin, no condition L>=C*T^epsilon. It is weaker than the earlier logarithm in T when N is short, as it must be. In particular for p in [T,K_1 T], K_1 fixed, and L=ceil(T(log T)^2), we have N=Theta((log T)^2) and the total possible positive flux of this label in the bin is
    O_(K_1)( L/(loglog T)^2 ).                       (2)
Weighting the occurrence count by <=3p+1 proves this even for arbitrary height guards and causal matches, because each source's matched weight is at most its own upward increment. No prime-cofactor hypothesis. The scale in (2) is not log^2 T in the denominator; the bin has only a polylogarithmic number of cofactor candidates.

For large upward labels use a short-bin tail estimate independent of the global cap C. For any B>=T, the number n of actual p>B source events in a bin satisfies n<=1+L/(B-1). Indeed the first-to-last pre-horizon span contains the whole gain of each except the last, all at least B-1. Therefore their total matched flux in the bin is
    <= M_j (1+L/(B-1)).                              (3)
The +1 pays even a large final jump which overshoots the bin; no assumption L>=C is necessary. Similarly the selected fast gain F_j when their labels <=R0 satisfies F_j<=L+R0, by summing their gains up to the last chosen post-horizon. The WHOLE physical bin gain may be larger; do not use G_j<=O(L) here when C>>L. Low-label matched flux is <=5 G_low,j, where G_low,j is actual total gain of ALL p<T events in this bin. Equations (1)-(3) therefore give a short-bin causal supply inequality for arbitrary cutoffs B>=T:
    alpha F_j <= M_j + 5 G_low,j
       + M_j(1+L/(B-1))
       + K sum_(p in S_j) (3p+1) N_p/log^2(2+min(p,N_p)),
where S_j is the actual distinct middle source labels T<=p<=B with a nonzero matched weight and N_p=ceil(L/p)+2. The middle sum is only over actual source labels, not all eligible primes.

## 4. Macroscopic causal transport at the return scale

Take T->infinity, T<=C, fixed K0>=1, L=ceil(T(log T)^2), and suppose across a sequence of canonical macroscopic windows:
    F=sum F_j>=rho X,
    chosen fast labels in [T,K0 T],
    M_j<=K0 T in each bin,
    G_low=sum G_low,j=o(X).
These are comparable-band and narrow-local-guard hypotheses, stronger than the preceding general time-height transport regime. They do NOT require small total variation or small global guard support. The bin count is O_lambda(X/L), since T<=C and L=o(X); even if many horizons are skipped the upper bound holds. Hence sum_j M_j=O(X/(log T)^2)=o(X). The same proof works if bins are split at boundaries of larger parent bins of length >=L, with O(X/L) nonempty short pieces, each width <=L, and a common guard in a parent of width <=K0 T. This allows applying it to previously chosen guards; no assertion of independence between bin selections is made.

Set B=max(T,1+ceil(K0 T/delta)) with fixed small delta>0. By (3) total large-label matched flux <=delta L * #bins + sum M_j = O_lambda(delta X)+o(X). Take delta sufficiently small depending on rho,lambda. Unmatched flux is o(X) by (1), and low-label matched flux is o(X) by the low gain hypothesis. Thus at least rho X/2 for sufficiently large X (or any smaller fixed fraction by reducing delta) is actual matched transport from upward sources p in [T,B]=[T,O_(rho,lambda,K0)(T)] to chosen fast drops. These sources precede their matched targets by <L in horizon units. Both labels are comparable to T up to fixed constants, and all matched levels are actual traversed levels of both events. No distant-height or delayed positive source can be used for this flux.

For the distribution across short bins, the middle matched flux is bounded above by the total selected actual drop <=F_j<=L+K0 T<=2L. Since #bins<=c_lambda X/L, there are Omega(X/L) bins with middle matched flux >=c L for a fixed c>0: otherwise their summed weight could not reach rho X/2 (choose c=rho/(4c_lambda), then the small bins cost <=rho X/4). In each of these bins, (2) forces
    |S_j|=Omega((loglog T)^2).
This is local diversity on the horizon scale T(log T)^2, not merely the long-bin local diversity of the previous note. The same-label arithmetic implies that, apart from o(X) over all bins, this middle transport either uses distinct labels or a one-stratum same-label target. In the latter case the strong source has w>=p-1 and the exact clock relation above. If one class accounts for a fixed positive fraction of the total, it too occurs in Omega(X/L) bins, since matched flux per bin <=2L. No claim that the same class dominates in every busy bin.

The preceding cb54101 theorem had repeated UPWARD pairs separated by Theta(p(log T)^2) under near-minimal weighted support. When those source labels are comparable to T, their separation scale is the same as L here. The present causal theorem places actual recovery transport at that scale under its own fast-gain, local-width and low-gain hypotheses. It does NOT assert that every one of those same-label upward return intervals contains such a transport, nor that the hypotheses of either theorem imply the other. In particular the one-stratum relation does not hold for arbitrary pairs of upward visits. We have not forced fast dominance, narrow local guards, or small low-label gain on an infinite ray. General small/middle crossing supply, macro upward supply, and a direct unbounded twin witness remain open. The next target is to constrain cross-label transport using the two distinct endpoint divisibility conditions, or rule out substantial one-stratum same-label transport by the intervening MIN clocks; these are separate possible branches, not an established contradiction.
