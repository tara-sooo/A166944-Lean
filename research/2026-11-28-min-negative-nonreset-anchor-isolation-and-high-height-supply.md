## 1. Exact space-time pins and a uniform near-anchor bound

Let L=log R, T=ceil(8 sqrt(2R+1)), fixed C>0, consider arbitrary actual canonical histories in the slab through age <=CT. A physical firing key is represented by (x,n,p,s) at its destination x and posttick absolute clock n, incoming prime p and parity sign s. Write
    c_s=1-s, b_+=0, b_-=3, h=x/2+n=m p+b_s.
The checked incoming spatial/height pins give p|x-c_s, m>=0 for p>=11. The eligible clock is n=0 mod2p for plus, n=p+2 mod2p for minus. Heights are <=CT+3 and labels <=2CT+7. For fixed B>0 let Y=T/L^B. A top anchor is such a key with p>=Y. It is called slow if it has at least one actual full preceding wait >=U=R^alpha, alpha fixed in (0,.5). For the asymptotic below Y>>U. We count distinct keys, not their already-coalesced root multiplicities.

Consider two distinct anchors with destination/clock differences d=x'-x, e=n'-n satisfying |d|<=D, |e|<=H. Assume H+D/2+3<Y and D+2<Y. Their heights obey
    |mp-m'q| <= H+D/2+3.                                (1)
In particular one cannot have m=0 and the other m'>0: the nonzero multiple of q is >=Y. If p=q then (1) implies m=m'. The spatial pins give d=c_t-c_s modp and the distance margin forces d=c_t-c_s. If signs agree this forces x=x',n=n',p=q, identical key. If signs differ then e=b_t-b_s-d/2 is +/-2, contradicting opposite clock parity. Therefore distinct nearby keys necessarily have p!=q, even if their signs differ. This uses actual clock parity, not only the two congruences modulo p.

For both nonreset coefficients m,m'>0, 1<=m,m'<=O_C(L^B). For fixed p,m,m', (1) restricts the integer q to an interval containing O(H+D+1) candidates (we can ignore primality here). For fixed p,q,d, signs, the spatial CRT x=c_s modp, x+d=c_t modq prescribes one class modulo pq. The destination slab is of length O(R), so O(1+R/(pq))=O_C(L^(2B)) candidates for x. For each m the height determines n uniquely, and m' determines n'. Thus counting over at most O(T) p's, two signs for each, m,m', and O(D+1) offsets, gives
    E_nonreset(D,H) <<_(B,C) T (D+1)(H+D+1) L^(4B).     (2)
No primality asymptotic or roughness required for this count. It applies even when pq~R and there is no useful long CRT parameter for a sieve. In particular take
    D=H=W=floor(R^(1/4)/L^(2B+3)).
For large R the margins above hold, and (2) gives E_nonreset(W,W)<<R/L^6. The estimate includes keys at equal positions or clocks and moving anchors of both signs. It counts pairs rather than possible actual root arrivals.

## 2. Remove top slow reset anchors, then isolate short connectors

For a slow top anchor with m=0, its clock is n=b_s-x/2, determined by x. Its source a=x+p-1 has qmin(a)>=U/2 by the actual static calendar. Write a+s=pv; v and pv-2s are U/2-rough and v>1. For fixed p the variable v has range length O(R/p) polynomially large. The elementary two-form upper sieve of earlier notes, with determinant +/-2 and only large p omitted, yields O(R/(p L²)) candidates, uniformly in p. Summing over Y<=p<=2CT+7 uses Chebyshev/dyadic reciprocal bound O_B(log L/L), giving
    K_reset << R log L/L³.                             (3)
This is a count of ALL top slow reset keys, not just the subset participating in simultaneous slow-slow collisions. A fast top reset key need not satisfy this estimate.

Delete all top slow keys with m=0 and both endpoints of every pair in (2) at D=H=W. The bad top slow key count is
    K_bad << R log L/L³ + R/L^6 = o(R/L²).              (4)
For each remaining key define its connector by following its actual deterministic suffix from (x,n), retaining states until H further ticks or before the first move that would make the displacement from x exceed D. Do not retain the crossing landing state. If two distinct retained anchor connectors shared a physical state, their anchor destinations would differ by at most D (both are to the right of the common site) and their clocks by at most H; this contradicts deletion of all nearby pairs. Hence these entire connector state sets are mutually disjoint. There is no bound on the number/order of short events inside a connector. If the suffix is continued through H ticks, either it retains H+1 states, or the omitted exit event has total gain from anchor >D. The pre-exit event source belongs to the connector. In particular for any collection of J distinct retained anchors, either at least J/2 connectors supply >=H+1 distinct states each, or the aggregate gain on the other >=J/2 connectors INCLUDING the crossing edge is >J D/2; the firing keys are disjoint because the pre-tick states are disjoint. This is a conditional occupation-or-gain alternative based on the number J of occupied anchors, not an asserted lower bound for J or for the number of connectors surviving H ticks.

Apply to the original observation windows, with fixed C large enough to include pre-observation, observation and H extra ticks if required. A physical anchor can belong to only O_C(1) windows because any original A is in [x,x+2CT+6] with windows of width comparable to T. Under cap M, each bad anchor can cost at most M roots if all roots passing its posttick bin are retired there (if pre-tick clipping is needed, retire only roots associated with this key). Each such retirement costs <=T future observed ticks. Thus for M~L^2.1,N~R the normalized lifetime loss from eliminating bad top slow anchors is
    O(M K_bad/R)=O(L^(-.9) log L)=o(L^(-.25)).          (5)
For two still-live representatives with DIFFERENT recent top slow anchors, and each still within its retained connector, no common-clock merger is possible. Multiple roots already merged at one anchor are not separated by this construction. A path with no recent top slow anchor, a fast anchor, or a path outside either connector envelope can still merge with a good connector. A connector can end after just one large jump, so (2)-(5) do not establish a long elapsed-time lower for all top anchors. They do extend the previous direct-contact estimate to arbitrary short-phase intervening histories, and identify an explicit space-time scale W~R^.25/L^7 for B=2. No global near-square density bound is implied.

## 3. Construct high-height nonreset arrivals with both visited centers non-twin

The preceding result for top anchors does not provide a population of such anchors. We now obtain an unconditional smaller-scale supply with nonzero postheight and a long second stationary phase. This is a new construction, not a claim to reach the top band Y~sqrt(R)/L².

Fix theta1=.198, theta2=.199. Choose small fixed kappa>0 below .00005, to be reduced as needed for the small-sieve fundamental lemma below. Let H0 tend to infinity with R and satisfy
    L <= H0 <= R^kappa/(2 L^10).
Put K=ceil(H0 L^10), so K<=R^kappa for sufficiently large R. The notation H0 is separate from H in section 1. Choose primes p in [R^theta1,R^theta2], p=1 mod420, and auxiliary primes r in [R^kappa,R^(2kappa)] with r not dividing (p-1)(p-3). Seek primes v in [(R+1)/p,(2R+1)/p] satisfying
    v=7 mod12, v=1 mod5 and mod7,
    v=1-p^(-1) mod r,
    P^-(pv-2)>=4p,
    P^-(p(v-1)-1), P^-(p(v-1)+1)>=K.                  (6)
The fixed v class modulo B0=420 is reduced. Because r does not divide p-1, the pinned v residue modulo r is reduced; because r does not divide p-3, the source opposite neighbor pv-2 is nonzero modulo r. The auxiliary pin forces x+1 divisible by r, where x=p(v-1), and x+1>r. Also p<v (indeed v>4p) for large R. The fixed residues ensure A=pv-1 is divisible by6, and both x±1 and pv-2 avoid 2,3,5,7. In particular A+1=pv and x+1 are composite, and both centers are non-twin.

The exact canonical plus calendar from birth(A) with v=3 mod4 gives first eligible wait of p at
    w0=(3p-7)/2.
At any age <=w0 the checked height bound gives every possible winner <=2w0+7=3p. The other prime on A+1 is v>4p, and every prime on A-1=pv-2 is >=4p, so none can fire first. Thus the first actual event is p, with destination x=p(v-1), clock n0=p-x/2 and height h0=p (NOT a reset height). Since x=2 mod4 and p is odd, n0 is even, indeed 0 mod2p. This also follows from the exact calendar identity. We next establish abundance of (6), then exclude early firings at x; roughness by itself would not imply a long idle phase from height p.

## 4. Two-range lower sieve on v primes with a forced composite destination

Fix p, write X=R/p, and denote its v interval by I. The modulus for the fixed and auxiliary classes is B_r=B0*r. We use ordinary BV for v primes, with arbitrary reduced residues, not a large-modulus fixed-residue theorem. The prior split sieve in a95c55d sections 3-5 gives the needed lower with the following parameters and error accounting.

Let small prime group be free primes 11<=ell<K and large group K<=ell<4p, excluding the fixed primes, p and r. For each free small ell, forbid the zeros of all three forms
    pv-2, p(v-1)-1, p(v-1)+1.
Among reduced v classes there are nu(ell)<=3 (some roots can coincide or be zero); ell>=11 ensures nu(ell)<ell-1. Thus g1=nu(ell)/(ell-1) has a uniform one-sided dimension-three product bound by Mertens and summable O(1/ell²) corrections. Its product V1>>1/(log K)^3, uniformly. For the large group only forbid pv-2; each free ell has one reduced bad class v=2/p mod ell, g2=1/(ell-1). The omitted primes p,r are harmless because the source form is nonzero there by construction. This group has uniform one-sided dimension one and
    V2>>log K/L
by Mertens, also when K is only polylogarithmic. Both estimates are lower estimates on local products, not asserted equality. The auxiliary r is >=K by construction (the strict small cutoff excludes it). The forced pin does not obstruct either target form at primes <K.

Use small sieve level D1=R^b1, b1=.0005, large level D2=R^b2, b2=.3985. Uniformly in p, log D2/log(4p)>2 by a fixed margin for large R since b2>2 theta2=.398. The standard ordinary linear lower/upper functions have f(s2)>0 and F(s2) bounded on this fixed s2 range. First fix a small approximation error eta>0 depending on that positive margin, then choose kappa sufficiently small that log D1/log K >= b1/kappa is sufficiently large for the ordinary fixed dimension-three combinatorial fundamental lemma with relative main-sum error eta. There is no growing-dimensional theorem needed.

Let U1,L1 be pointwise upper and lower small-sieve weights with |lambda_d|<=1, supported <=D1; U1>=I_small>=L1, so the sequences weighted by U1 and U1-L1 are nonnegative. In the local product model their main totals for fixed p,r are X_{p,r}^+ and X_{p,r}^diff, satisfying
    X_{p,r}^+ >= (1-eta) X_{p,r}^*, 
    0<=X_{p,r}^diff<=2 eta X_{p,r}^*,
    X_{p,r}^* = pi(I) V1 / phi(B_r).
Apply the large linear lower sieve to the U1 weighted sequence and the large upper sieve to the U1-L1 weighted sequence, with the r-specific prime omissions and local products. Since I_small>=U1-(U1-L1), their difference bounds the actual joint count from below. The resulting main difference is >=c V2 X_{p,r}^* for fixed c>0 when eta sufficiently small, allowing the ordinary sieve o(1) terms. Uniformity in all these bounded-dimension local products follows from the same one-sided product bound; the large group can be truncated, as in the earlier vector sieve. This is not applying a dimension-three lower sieve at cutoff 4p. Keeping the two nonnegative sequences separate is essential.

For both large-sieve remainders, after expanding the small weights, the count in each CRT class modulo m=B0*r*d1*d2 is pi(I)/phi(m) plus at most E_X(m), the sum of maximum reduced-class prime-count errors at the two endpoints of I. There are nu(d1)<=3^omega(d1) classes from the small group; d2 contributes one. Sum absolute remainders over r (not assume a pointwise AP asymptotic for growing r). The multiplicity of m is <=tau(m)^C0 for a fixed C0: r is a prime divisor of m, after choosing it the disjoint prime ranges determine d1,d2, and the small class count is bounded by that divisor power. Weight magnitudes <=1 or <=2 for the difference. The total modulus is <=B0 R^(2kappa+b1+b2) < X^(.5-eps0) for some fixed eps0>0 uniformly in p, since X>=R^.801 and 2kappa+.399<.4005. The weighted classical BV bound sum tau(m)^C0 E_X(m)<<_A X/L^A follows from ordinary BV with max over reduced classes and arbitrary log savings, Cauchy-Schwarz, elementary E_X(m)<<X tau(m)/m, and fixed divisor-power harmonic moments (as in a95c55d section4). This holds uniformly for every fixed p in the range; all modulus residues may depend on p. Use the fixed p sequence and then sum p; no factorability requirement beyond ordinary BV.

For each p, the total over r of 1/phi(B_r) is bounded below by a positive constant for all large R: by PNT the reciprocal prime sum over [R^kappa,R^(2kappa)] tends to log 2; deleting divisors of (p-1)(p-3) costs O_kappa(R^-kappa). Hence summing the positive main terms and errors gives at least
    >> X/[L² (log K)²] = R/[p L² (log K)²]
pairs (r,v) satisfying (6) for this p. The p reciprocal sum in the fixed AP over its broad polynomial range tends to log(theta2/theta1)/phi(420)>0. Thus total triples (p,r,v)>>R/[L²(log K)²]. Each root A uniquely determines p and v because A+1=pv, p<v both prime; possible r's are distinct prime factors >=R^kappa of x+1<=2R+1, so at most O_kappa(1). We conclude that (6) holds for at least
    >> R/[L²(log K)²]                                  (7)
DISTINCT canonical roots in every sufficiently large slab. Constants may depend on fixed kappa and the sieve margins, not on H0 in the stated range. The number of roots is unconditional, not a consequence of assuming no twins.

## 5. Exclude fast interruptions at high height, and separate the actual second phases

At (x,n0) from (6), height is p, so a rough target could still have a quick firing. Suppose its first subsequent firing is at age u with 1<=u<=H0 relative to that arrival. Until then gain is zero. Its label q>=K, q!=p because p|x and p cannot divide x±1. The nonnegative-height identity gives q<=2(p+u)+1<=3p for large R. The checked height pin for this firing gives
    q | 2p+2u+1-2b, b=0 or3,                           (8)
and the integer is positive, of order p. For each fixed p,u,b there are O(L/log K) possible prime divisors q>=K. Their spatial eligibility x+s=0 modq prescribes one class for v modulo q (q!=p). Even ignoring prime and other roughness restrictions, there are O(R/(pq)) integers v in I in that class, since pq<=3R^(.398)<<R. Summing p reciprocal O(1), u and both signs gives at most
    O(R H0 L/(K log K)) = O(R/L^9)
exceptional roots (in fact a log K saving as well), uniformly. We count distinct roots, with no additional multiplicity of auxiliary r. Since log K<=kappa L, this is o(the lower in (7)). Remove these exceptions. Every surviving trajectory has EXACTLY ONE actual event through age w0+H0; its two visited centers are both non-twin; its height at postarrival elapsed u<=H0 is p+u. In particular it has two long stationary intervals separated by a polynomially large firing, and does not reset to height 0 or3. The total gain p-1 is positive; it is NOT a low-gain ratio theorem for the first interval, since W/G~1.5.

We also obtain a separate space-time lower. For the first anchors in this family (plus sign,m=1), let D0=H0. If two distinct such anchors are at distance <=D0 and time difference <=H0, then |p-q|<=H0+D0/2 and p!=q by the same-label argument. Since H0=o(R^.198), q~p, and for each p there are O(H0+D0+1) possible integer q. Fixed offset d prescribes x=0 modp, x+d=0 modq, so O(R/p²) possible x (the interval has polynomial CRT room). Thus the count of nearby pairs is
    O(R(D0+1)(H0+D0+1) sum_{p>=R^.198}1/p²)
       << R^(1-.198) (H0+1)^2
       = o(R/L^4)                                       (9)
because H0<=R^kappa, 2kappa<.198. This bound even sums over all integer p. Delete both roots of such pairs. At least >>R/[L²(log K)²] actual high-height anchors remain, and their full H0-tick stationary connectors are pairwise disjoint PHYSICAL states, even if the same spatial center could occur at very different clocks. They yield at least
    >> R H0/[L²(log K)²]
distinct physical-state incidences on those second phases. All roots have actual first-event age w0~p, and those second phases end by O(R^.199) original age, not a common original age or a common global absolute clock. Since H0 is explicitly chosen in advance, this is a common relative elapsed-time lower AFTER a nonreset first event, rather than a stopping-time upper. For example:
- H0=floor(L^a) for any fixed a>=1 (a=1 use ceil L), K~L^(a+10): >>R/[L²(log L)²] roots with that second idle time.
- H0=floor(R^delta) for any fixed 0<delta<kappa: >>R/L^4 roots with polynomial second idle time, and >>(R^(1+delta)/L^4) distinct high-height physical states.
- H0=floor(exp(L^eta)), fixed 0<eta<1: >>R/L^(2+2eta) roots; second idle subexponential in L and physical-state count accordingly.
No explicit effective kappa is claimed; it is chosen once depending on the small fixed sieve error, and each example uses a compatible H0. Both source and destination are composite on their plus neighbor by independent factor pins. The target has a future event within 2r ticks from the current clock by its r calendar, but r need not be the winner. This lower does not use the unreviewed .295 draft.

The asymptotic main result of sections1-2 does not require such source constructions; section5 is a separate application of its space-time arithmetic at m=1 and smaller p, coupled with a lower sieve that produces genuine connectors. It does not furnish a positive population of top anchors p>=T/L². Nor can the resulting disjoint connectors be concatenated freely without controlling crossings with unanchored paths.
