# Signed band cost and active local section slicing

2026-11-28 JST, Issue #10. Base a33ad0bd7f39cf03bb7af6884120009c76ea1e57. Continuation toward M85 and twin infinitude. New results: a uniform integrated top-wait capacity for DISTINCT first-height sinks, a signed additive hard-budget score with no record-count restriction, and a conditional multi-section localization of the genuinely dynamic M85 branch to much smaller prime alphabets. The latter includes a new application of the progress-weighted record argument to arbitrary local section starts. No density lower at H=.4, M85, unconditional twin proof, new Lean compile or independent specialist review.

Notation L=log R, canonical births A in [R,2R], 6|A, h=3, coordinates h=x/2+n. First-H means the first POST-tick height H; all moving ticks have nonpositive height change, and upward increments are at most one. Gain G=A-x, age H-3+G/2. Throughout R^theta0<=H<=C sqrt R, fixed theta0>0,C, and hard budget B=4H, unless explicitly stated otherwise. All positions lie in [R-4H,2R]. Distinct selected final sinks have physically disjoint histories before first H, because a shared state has the same deterministic future first H. Select one actual witness per sink; for existential bad-witness assertions select a violating witness. This disjointness is not a root-multiplicity bound.

## 1. Integrated full waits of a polynomial band of primes

Let Y>=R^alpha for a fixed alpha>0, Y<=2H+1, and call events p>=Y top. More generally restrict to any subset P of the prime labels in [Y,2H+1]. Write W_P for sum of FULL waits from previous moving arrival or birth, over the completed selected events, not partial terminal idle. Each complete wait w<=2 qmin(source), and w<=2p. The number of top events on a budgeted witness is at most 4H/(Y-1). Let U=R^(alpha/2). Their waits below U have total at most O(H R^(-alpha/2)) per witness.

Every event with w>=U has both source neighbors U/2-rough. For fixed p and sign s, write the fired neighbor p v, opposite neighbor p v-2s, v>1, v in a containing interval of length O(R/p). Both v and pv-2s are U/2-rough. The inherited uniform two-form Selberg upper gives O(R/(p L²)) choices: choose a fixed sufficiently small polynomial cutoff below U/2, below p, and small relative to R/p; outside 2 and p the two roots are distinct. For fixed source and p,s the physical clock has O(H/p+1)=O(H/p) possibilities in the strip. This also follows from the landing height pin h=b_s mod p, b_+=0,b_-=3. Thus the possible slow keys with this p have count O(R H/(p² L²)); weighting each by 2p bounds their full waits. Across selected witnesses for distinct sinks no physical key is shared. It follows, uniformly for any such P and any distinct sink selection S,
    sum_(x in S) W_P(x)
       << (R H/L²) sum_(p in P) 1/p
          + |S| H R^(-alpha/2).                          (1)
This is a capacity for actual total waits, not merely the number of slow events or a bound on all roots. In particular, for P all top labels,
    sum_(x in S) W_top(x)/H
       << R/L³ [1+log^+(H/Y)] + |S| R^(-alpha/2),         (2)
using the elementary reciprocal-prime sum over polynomial-sized dyadic bands. The second term can always be absorbed in O(R/L³) since |S|=O(R).

For any fixed eta>0 the number of distinct budgeted first-H sinks admitting ANY witness with W_top>eta H is consequently
    O_eta(R/L³ [1+log^+(H/Y)]).                           (3)
For Y=H R^-epsilon (round down, fixed epsilon small with a fixed polynomial lower), this is O_eta((epsilon+1/L)R/L²), with constants uniform as epsilon decreases within a fixed exponent margin. Thus at any supplied positive density c R/L² we can choose epsilon>0 sufficiently small in terms of eta,c and retain most of that density with EVERY witness having W_top<=eta H. In contrast previous global top pruning used a threshold H/sqrt L and negligible slow-key COUNT; (1)-(3) use weighted waits and apply at a polynomially smaller cutoff even when individual top events are numerous. For Y=H exp(-L^gamma), 0<gamma<1, the loss is o(R/L²) at every fixed eta. There is no bound here on the sum of gains of fast top events, which can be large.

## 2. Signed additive cost with the earlier exact potential slack

Use the inherited mod2310 finite-state certificate from c0e45b0, as stated in the earlier mod2310/single-cap note: every contiguous block of completed waits from any even state and clock obeys
    W <= 17G/8 + 395/12 - (1/8) sum (p-281)_+ .
The finite-state certificate and infinite-tail argument are inherited, not independently recertified in Lean here. It applies also to a complete event block starting at an arbitrary section state with its next positive wait measured from that state.

Write G_low and G_top for the gains at p<Y and p>=Y, respectively, S_low=sum_(p<Y)(p-281)_+, m=#top events. The low events form at most m+1 consecutive blocks. Define the integer signed cost
    C_Y=13 G_low - S_low - 4 G_top
       = sum_(p<Y) [13(p-1)-(p-281)_+]
         - sum_(p>=Y) 4(p-1).                            (4)
The full height ledger gives
    H-3 <= C_Y/8 + W_top + I + (395/12)(m+1),            (5)
where I is the terminal unfinished idle, I<2 qmin(x). The top jumps have NEGATIVE cost because their gain reduces height; their full wait is retained separately in (5). The certificate's slack is included for all low labels. No actual jump is removed or modified.

At H=.4, suppose a density-scale dynamic M85 family exists, i.e. c R/L² distinct budgeted sinks with qmin<4H. Remove sinks with qmin>=Q=H R^-delta but <4H, choosing a sufficiently small fixed delta>0: the prior two-form rough interval upper is O((delta+1/L)R/L²), so the deletion can be made an arbitrarily small fixed fraction. Choose epsilon>0 small relative to the remaining density and eta>0 by (3). On every remaining witness at Y=H R^-epsilon, W_top<=eta H and I<2Q=o(H). Since m=O(H/Y)=o(H), (5) gives
    13G_low-S_low-4G_top >= 8(1-eta)H-o(H),
    G_low >= (8/13)(1-eta)H + (4/13)G_top
                      + S_low/13-o(H).                   (6)
In particular G_top<=(44+8eta)H/17+o(H) from total budget 4H. This extends the previous high-band ledger to a polynomially smaller label cutoff, includes the certificate's extra slack, and holds for every budgeted witness in a density-scale subset (conditional on the original lower). It does not bound all individual active paths or supply their existence.

## 3. A record-free hard-budget scalar score and twin sufficient condition

For each actual first-H center x let C_min(x) be the minimum (4) over all actual witnesses with hard gain budget 4H, infinity for no witness. Define
    V_Y(x)=min(1, max(0, [H-3-C_min(x)/8]/H)).
No gain or record discount is used within the budget. The nonnegative score is at most1, and a zero-gain stationary birth supplies at least 1-3/H. Choose one attaining minimum witness for each finite score; minima exist by finiteness. By (5),
    V_Y(x) <= [W_top+I+(395/12)(m+1)]/H.                 (7)
The block error summed over O(R) centers is O(R/Y+R/H)=o(R/L³), and (2) applies to the selected attaining witnesses.

We give a separate terminal-idle estimate instead of a fixed terminal cutoff. Let S be any set of even centers of scale R with qmin(x)<=Umax, where Umax=4H or sqrt(2R+1). For each real t in [R^-theta0/2,1], qmin>=tH/2 requires a least factor p of one neighbor in [tH/2,Umax]. In this range the factor is polynomial, p=O(sqrt R), and both its cofactor and the opposite neighbor are tH/2-rough. The same uniform two-form upper and reciprocal-prime sum give
    # {x in S:qmin(x)>=tH/2}
       << R/L³ [1+log^+(Umax/H)+log(2/t)].
If the interval is empty the upper still holds. For smaller t use O(R) centers. By the layer-cake identity and integral_0^1 log(1/t)dt=1,
    sum_(x in S) min(1,2 qmin(x)/H)
       << R/L³ [1+log^+(Umax/H)].                        (8)
All constants uniform in t in the specified range because the sifting threshold has a fixed positive polynomial lower. The endpoint idle I<=H and I<2qmin, so (8) bounds the sum of I/H. This is not a distribution estimate on the history itself.

At H=.4 use Umax=4H for nonstationary centers qmin<4H. At H=floor(4 sqrt R/L^B0), fixed B0>=0, any non-twin center has qmin<=sqrt(2R+1), so use that Umax. Combining (2),(7),(8),
    sum_(qmin<4H) V_Y(x) << R/L³ [1+log^+(H/Y)],          (9)
    sum_(non-twin x) V_Y(x)
       <<_B0 R/L³ [1+log L+log^+(H/Y)].                  (10)
For B0=0 the log L term can be dropped. The score of every twin birth in [R,2R] is >=1-3/H; all other twin endpoints outside this slab number O(H). Thus (10) also gives a leading-order equivalence between total score and actual twin count to this error precision (extra errors O(R/H+H) negligible). At H=.4 the analogous static count is the qmin>=4H canonical births. These statements demonstrate the scope and arithmetic obstacle of the criterion.

A NEW sufficient lower for twins is a total signed-cost score omega(R[1+log L+log^+(H/Y)]/L³) on unbounded near-square slabs with the stated hard budget. In particular Y=H exp(-L^gamma), 0<gamma<1, permits an Omega(R/L²) lower. More generally, suppose c R/L² distinct actual first-H centers at near-square H have some witness with C_Y<=8(1-eta)H, fixed c,eta>0. Such centers have score at least eta/2 for large R. Even for Y=H R^-epsilon, choosing a sufficiently small fixed epsilon in terms of c,eta and the uniform upper constant in (10) makes the non-twin capacity strictly smaller than this lower. Hence such a density lower, for that fixed exponent gap, would imply infinitely many twins. This differs from the previous subpolynomial postevent-record criterion: there is NO cap on the record count. It permits large total gain provided the signed cost is small. For example the purely algebraic split G_low=H, G_top=2H, G_total=3H gives C_Y<=5H<8H, with no claim such paths exist. No lower on this signed-cost score is established.

## 4. Exact Pareto transport with signed costs, and a multi-stage alternative

On the physical tick DAG or the event-to-event DAG, attach to each actual witness the pair (A,C), with C the accumulated additive cost (4) and A its birth. At a common physical state label1 dominates label2 if A1<=A2 and C1<=C2. For a fixed common future, future costs are identical even when negative, and the first label never has larger gain A1-x. Hence pruning dominated labels is exact for every hard-budget signed-cost threshold and for the minimum cost at every first-H sink. The DAG is finite and acyclic (moves decrease x, idle increases h), so negative edge costs create no cycles. The running postevent maximum from the previous record Pareto method is not needed. At intermediate first-height sections, keep the nondominated pairs and continue the exact deterministic suffix with the SAME final budget. The minimum signed-cost objective need not be optimized by the minimum ancestor alone; tradeoffs must be retained. A complete true forward lower is not implied by the recurrence.

## 5. Conditional active section slicing: smaller alphabets at many common heights

This part needs only the rough interval sieve and the earlier block inequality for its lower gain bound. Let H=floor(R^.4), and suppose N>=c R/L² distinct nonstationary first-H sinks with qmin<4H and budget4H exist. Choose one actual witness per sink. For ANY height h in [H/2,H], the first-h states of these selected witnesses are distinct; if two shared a physical state there, they would have the same final H sink. No witness can visit a center with qmin>=4H before H, because no eligible p<=2H+1 exists at that center and the rest of its path to H would be stationary, contrary to the nonstationary terminal. Fix Q=floor(H R^-delta), 0<delta small fixed, with Q polynomial. For any fixed section h, the number of selected witnesses whose first-h center has qmin>Q is at most O(R/L³[1+log(4H/Q)])=O((delta+1/L)R/L²), uniformly in h and in the choice of witnesses. This uses DISTINCT first-h states, not the count of visits at varying clocks.

Set D=floor(H R^(-delta/2)), so Q=o(D), D=o(H), and use consecutive integer sections h_i=floor(H/2)+iD, i=0,...,J, J=floor((H-floor(H/2))/D)~H/(2D). On each actual witness divide the prefix from first h_0 to first h_J into its exact consecutive first-height segments. If g_i is the displacement in segment i then sum g_i<=4H; hence for fixed M>=1 the number of segments g_i>M D is <=4H/(M D). Their duration is exactly D+g_i/2. Choose M sufficiently large absolute, then delta sufficiently small relative to c and the uniform sieve constant. Summing over witness-section pairs shows at least, say, 3/4 of the N J pairs have BOTH endpoint qmin<=Q and gain g_i<=M D. Indeed the fraction of excessive gain is <=(8+o(1))/M, and the two endpoint exceptional counts cost at most 2C(delta+1/L)/c. The choices M,delta can be made with strict slack. The preceding count holds for any chosen representatives. At least half of the common section intervals consequently have >=N/2 such actual connectors, by counting pairs.

Each such connector is physically disjoint from every other connector in the same interval, including intermediate clocks. Every actual completed label satisfies p<=g_i+1<=M D+1, since its own gain is p-1. Therefore the entire segment is identical to its continuation under a trial rule retaining only p<=M D+1 (including idle ticks and exact first-height stopping); omitting larger labels does not affect a winner already within the retained alphabet. This is an exact common-graph assertion for the selected connectors, no assumption that every trial connector is actual. It starts at occupied first-h states, not new canonical births. Conversely the block inequality applied from the initial section clock, followed by terminal idle I<2Q, gives
    13 g_i - sum_(segment)(p-281)_+ >= 8D-16Q-790/3,
    g_i >= (8/13-o(1))D.                                (11)
Thus a genuinely active M85 lower entails a density-scale collection of actual local transfers of height increment D with gain Theta(D), common small alphabet <=M D+1, duration Theta(D), and roughness Q=o(D) at both ends, at a positive fraction of the J polynomially many common sections. They do not rely on long terminal idle of length comparable to the section spacing. No unconditional construction of these occupied local transfers is claimed. A local transfer at arbitrary starting states alone would not supply canonical reachability.

## 6. Reuse the progress-weighted depth bound locally at arbitrary starts

The proof of the preceding note a33ad0bd §§1-2 works on each connector interval with target h_i+D, starting at height h_i, and initial deficit d0=D, rather than a canonical birth at height3. Indeed first-target stopping still gives p<=2d+1 for each completed moving event, previous deficit<=6d, and strict new postevent record relative to the section start implies w>(p-1)/2. The first wait is measured from the section start, so the same full-next-wait bound applies. Source roughness and both landing pins remain exact. The log-progress ledger up to b=2Q has ell=log(D/(2Q))=(delta/2)L+O(1). Every good connector of section 5 must cross b before its final idle to h_i+D, since I<2Q. The major-record key capacity uses landing-deficit bands [d_band,2d_band) of width d_band, so the large absolute starting height does not add a factor H/D. The number of possible heights with given p is O(d_band/p), and p<=4d_band+1; arithmetic upper uses only source positions of scale R. Thus for m0=R^sigma with sufficiently small fixed sigma>0 (below the polynomial margins and depending on c), at each section the number of DISTINCT final-H sinks admitting any good connector whose local major progress is >=ell/4 is
    O(R/L³ [1+log m0]) = O((sigma+1/L)R/L²).             (12)
This uses selected violating witnesses and uniqueness from any common physical connector key to final H, not independence between sections. Constants uniform in i. Delete these bad witness-section pairs in aggregate, with sigma small enough relative to c. With the previous 3/4 good fraction and strict slack, retain at least 2/3 of all witness-section pairs with exact small-alphabet continuation, gain Theta(D), and
    # strict LOCAL postevent records >= (3/8) m0 ell
                                      >> R^sigma log R.
They arise from minor p<d_band/m0<=D R^-sigma. These local records are not asserted to be new global postevent maxima. At least a positive fraction of the selected witnesses therefore have Omega(J) such sections, and their total completed event count is >=c' J R^sigma L >> R^(delta/2+sigma) L. A stronger every-witness formulation follows by selecting violating witnesses for a set of sinks whose every-witness good-section count fails: summing the uniform rough and major exceptions over J sections and using the per-witness excessive-gain bound makes the number of sinks admitting <J/2 good sections an arbitrarily small fixed fraction of N, by choosing delta,sigma sufficiently small and M sufficiently large. This argument is conditional on the supplied density; it does not imply an unconditional lower on total events or give a global record count of this size. It gives a concrete multi-stage necessary structure beyond a single final-depth condition.
