## 1. The whole source mass, without a packet mass cap

Use canonical MIN on all odd sources M in [X,2X), with C0=2M-2, t0=M+2, individual K(M)=floor((7M-1)/4), B=ceil(sqrt(7X)), X divisible by 4. We can first analyze the original FULL paths up to their first departure from their origin W-cell, regardless of whether they would later be retained in the accounting-stop forest. A retained path that executes its first departure is an unchanged prefix of one of these full paths. A proposed unexecuted prime-stop event is not a departure. Let W be an even primorial (2 is also permitted), W<=B, cells (cW,(c+1)W]. All original origins of a first-exit packet are in the SAME origin cell.

Before a first exit, let g=C-C0 and e=t-t0 at the attained pre-state of the crossing event. By strict growth and the right-closed origin cell,
    0<=g<W, e<=3g, h=2t-C=6+2e-g<=6+5W.
The elapsed bound follows from w<=2p<=3(p-1) at every executed event. Every preceding internal event has gain p-1<W, hence p<=W. No bound on the physical group mass is needed. Now suppose the actual first-exit label p>10W+14. Nonnegative post-height yields
    2w>=p-1-h>p/2,
    r(C)>=w/2>p/8,
    w>2W.
If ANY label <=W were eligible at C it would fire within 2W, contradiction. Thus the pre-state is terminal for the deterministic MIN ghost using all odd primes <=W. All earlier events agree exactly with that ghost, since they have labels <=W and the full least-clock/tie rule chooses the same event when larger labels are omitted. In particular the truncated ghost has not left the origin cell, and terminates there. This conclusion concerns the actual first exit, not an arbitrary later large jump.

Let D_W=2 product_(3<=q<=W prime)q. It is a period in the original root variable M for this <=W ghost stopped at its first exit or terminal: M->M+D_W shifts t by D_W and C by 2D_W, preserving every possible clock, horizon eligibility and the position within a W-cell (W divides D_W). The relative attained state and event labels up to termination/exit are determined by a single residue M mod D_W. The deterministic finite ghost either exits or terminates since internal horizons strictly increase by >=2 and have range <W. For each residue r whose ghost terminates inside the cell, let g_r be its terminal gain. Every actual large first exit p>10W+14 in this phase must be selected from
    C=2M-2+g_r.
In particular this terminal location is fixed by the original source residue, not an arbitrary phase of a large merged physical group. Different roots in one packet are counted individually in this relation. This is the new way to avoid the multiplicative W (or maximum packet mass) loss in counting the sources taking a very large first exit.

## 2. Uniform root-slice upper-bound sieve

Let Z>10W+14, with Z>=W^200 for convenience (one can weaken this exponent), Z<=B. Use D_W<=B^(1/4), for example by taking W<=log^(1/4)B, since log D_W=O(W). All constants below are absolute for these conditions and sufficiently large X; no asymptotic is claimed for the small numerical test windows.

Fix a terminal residue r mod D_W, a prime p>Z and p<=B, and a sign sigma in {-1,+1}. A necessary condition for the actual first exit p is
    p | 2M-2+g_r+sigma.
Because p>W, gcd(2D_W,p)=1. Therefore this fixes precisely one congruence class M=M0+D_W p j within the residue r (possibly with no j in the original interval). For an odd residue r all such M are odd. After dividing the eligible neighbor by p, define
    f1(j)=(2M-2+g_r+sigma)/p=m0+2D_W j,
    f2(j)=2M-2+g_r-sigma=p f1(j)-2sigma.
At the selected pre-state r(C)>p/8, so both neighbors have no odd prime divisor <=p/8. In particular for every prime ell with W<ell<=u=p^(1/100), both f1 and f2 are nonzero mod ell. For large W/p under the stipulated scales, u<p/8, ell!=p, and ell does not divide 2D_W. Each form excludes one residue modulo ell, and they are distinct since f2-p f1=-2sigma. The residue count for squarefree d composed of these sieve primes is exactly 2^omega(d), with remainder O(2^omega(d)) in a consecutive j interval. The uniform dimension-two upper-bound sieve used in the earlier growing-cutoff note gives
    # admissible j
       <= O((X/(D_W p)+1) product_(W<ell<=u)(1-2/ell)
                  + u log(2u)).
Here one uses the same sieve-level convention and remainder bound as the earlier note. Equivalently one may take the sieve prime range to p^(1/200) and level p^(1/100), with the corresponding harmless increase of the fixed power lower restriction on Z/W; any fixed such choices yield the displayed logarithmic bound below. The dimension-two sieve has uniform local factors on these primes, and Mertens product comparison gives
    product_(W<ell<=u)(1-2/ell) = O((log(2W)/log p)^2).
If the chosen sieve prime range is empty, use the same bound with its enlarged absolute constant (we ultimately take log Z/log W->infinity). The interval length X/(D_W p) is >=c B^(3/4) because p<=B and X asymp B^2. Thus the sieve remainder u log(2u) is uniformly negligible versus even X/(D_W p log^2 p), and +1 can be absorbed. Alternatively sum the remainders over at most D_W phases and B prime candidates: O(D_W B^(1+1/100) log B)=o(X). Hence
    # admissible M for fixed r,p,sigma
       <= O(X/(D_W p) * log^2(2W)/log^2 p).
This is an upper bound for sources, not distinct landing states. It allows arbitrary prior mergers. It is the exact same two-form determinant structure as the preceding reset-start sieve, now with a root residue selected by the local cell-exit problem.

Sum over at most D_W residues r and two signs, then over primes p>Z through B. By the elementary prime-count upper bound and partial summation,
    sum_(p>Z prime) 1/(p log^2 p)=O(1/log^2 Z).
Thus the TOTAL original source mass E_>(W,Z) whose first executed W-cell departure has label >Z satisfies
    E_>(W,Z) = O(X (log(2W)/log Z)^2).
The bound is also valid for the retained stopped subset. Unlike the previous count of rare destination cells times W or capped representative count, there is NO multiplicative maximum packet mass or k factor. The main term can further be multiplied by the fraction of r mod D_W that are odd terminal-inside-cell phases (with a factor two depending on normalization); an analogous restriction to a subset of such phases preserves the same proof. This refinement is not used in the following conclusion.

For example choose W->infinity so slowly that W<= (log log B)^(1/4), and take Z=floor((log B)^(1/2)). Then D_W<=B^(1/4), Z>W^200 eventually, W<Z<B, and log(2W)/log Z->0. Hence E_>=o(X) unconditionally. One may also arrange W=2 product_(3<=p<z prime)p with z>2H+2, H->infinity and Y growing sufficiently slowly to have L<=H, 2(K+6)<=H if the earlier eta hypothesis is available; choose H and all these parameters by slow diagonal selection instead of using the previous larger illustrative H=(log B)^(1/4). In particular W grows slowly enough for the much larger D_W period, and the previous representative cap remains independent of W. For fixed W the same tail conclusion holds with any Z->infinity subject to the conditions. This does not establish a small mass at exits with p between z and Z. Nor does it apply to arbitrary later large jumps made by an already departed packet.

## 3. Exact common-part comparison with a finite periodic root model

Use a finite cutoff Z>=W in addition to the full B model. From each ORIGINAL source, run the MIN ghost with all labels <=Z, stopping at its first executed departure from its original W-cell or at a natural LOW terminal. If applying an individual time cutoff, use K(M) in both processes. No accounting stops are imposed on this auxiliary full-source model. All internal events have p<=W<=Z regardless of whether the process is full or finite. If the full process has a first exit p<=Z, the two records up to and including that exit agree: at every selected event the full minimum is already in the restricted alphabet. If the full process has no first exit within its time cutoff, the two records (including absence of exit) agree: a first different event would necessarily be a larger selected label >Z in the full process, which cannot remain inside the cell. This proof also covers a natural terminal with no full eligible label; the smaller alphabet has none. A restricted event within the cutoff could not precede a full minimum outside the cutoff. Hence every source with different first-exit outcome (including clock or horizon) belongs to E_>(W,Z). The reverse inclusion need not be needed, but a full exit >Z cannot be the same label as a finite exit.

Define the full and restricted exit packet partitions by actual common first-exit key (origin cell, pre-horizon C, event time n, prime p); exclude no-exit roots from both. This grouping is valid without using a non-splitting assumption on paths AFTER exit. Remove the at most E_> differing sources from both partitions. Their remaining exit packet sizes, at every matching key, are IDENTICAL. Write C_full(k), C_Z(k) for sum min(k,m_e) over their respective packets, and M_full,>k, M_Z,>k for the source mass in packets of size >k. Insertion/deletion of a single source increases/decreases sum min(k,m_e) by at most 1, and changes large-packet mass by at most k+1 (the maximal jump is from k to k+1). Both partitions extend the same common partition by at most E_> sources. Therefore
    |C_full(k)-C_Z(k)| <= E_>,
    |M_full,>k-M_Z,>k| <= (k+1) E_>.
These are absolute differences, not independence approximations. Both functionals are monotone under insertion; this is why one can use E_> instead of 2E_>. For the previous retained accounting-stop population, every retained first-exit packet is a subset of its full-source packet at the same key. In particular mass in retained packets larger than k is at most M_full,>k. The finite periodic distribution is thus a useful one-sided majorant for that mass, at an error bounded by (k+1)E_>. For growing k, make k(log W/log Z)^2->0 if the mass comparison is needed. It is NOT enough merely to have E_>=o(X) with unrestricted growing k.

When W+Z=o(X), no source-specific cutoff can affect the auxiliary restricted first-exit/terminal calculation: elapsed time to the last internal event <=3W, and to a possible exit <=3W+2Z, while K(M)-t0=Omega(X). Its entire outcome is determined by the residue of M modulo
    D_Z=2 product_(3<=q<=Z prime)q.
M->M+D_Z translates C by 2D_Z and t by D_Z. W divides D_Z; we may partition the infinite source line into full periods of D_Z in M aligned to complete W-cells in origin horizon. For example the odd M in (1,D_Z+1] have initial C0 in (0,2D_Z] and fill whole cells. In such a period all packet sizes are unchanged by translation. With Z=floor(sqrt(log B)), log D_Z=O(Z), D_Z=B^o(1)=o(X). The two partial periods at the ends of [X,2X) contain O(D_Z) sources; deleting these affects capped mass and total large-packet mass by O(D_Z) and O((k+1)D_Z), respectively. Thus, if c_{W,Z}(k) denotes the capped packet sum on a whole period divided by D_Z/2 (the number of odd sources), and m_{W,Z,>k} analogously denotes the mass fraction of large packets, then
    C_full(k)/N = c_{W,Z}(k)+O(E_>/X+D_Z/X),
    M_full,>k/N = m_{W,Z,>k}+O((k+1)(E_>+D_Z)/X).
This finite periodic formulation is a theorem about counts of root-slice trajectories. It does not equate the source slice with uniform independent two-dimensional CRT phase pairs from the LOW height problem. The periods can be enormous and are not exhaustively enumerated in this turn.

## 4. Link to the previous capped representative result; precise remaining quantity

Under a no-twin window, S=N-o(X) for the existing accounting stops. The previous representative theorem for the W-cells with z>2H+2, z>=Y gives C_exit(k)<=O(k X eta) for the retained first exits. The retained resident terminal mass is o(X) because W=B^o(1), W is a multiple of the accounting L, and J_p<=N/B, J_c=O(log^2Y). Thus if eta->0 along a diverging sequence and k->infinity with k eta->0, almost all original mass lies in retained first-exit packets larger than k. Every such packet is contained in a full packet larger than k. Choose parameters sufficiently slowly so that k(E_>+D_Z)/X=o(1), e.g. by the above scales and slow k. It follows that a necessary condition on such no-twin windows is
    m_{W,Z,>k} = 1-o(1).
Also C_full(k) differs from retained C_exit(k) by at most N-S plus resident retained tags with no retained exit but a possible subsequent full exit; both are o(X), since retained residents are o(X). Therefore c_{W,Z}(k)=o(1) on these same conditional windows. The mass and cap formulations are consistent: they assert concentration into increasingly large exit packets, not that this concentration has been ruled out.

A sufficient alternative to the earlier weighted packet merger bound is an upper bound m_{W,Z,>k}<=1-epsilon along a suitable diverging parameter sequence (with fixed epsilon>0, eta and comparison errors tending to zero as specified). This uses only packet FORMATION before first exit and avoids analyzing any subsequent long-range mergers. No such uniform upper bound is proved. In particular the direct root-slice sieve only removes p>Z, leaving the entire middle band z<=p<=Z. The finite periodic mass distribution may itself develop larger fibres as W grows; a finite computation at one W cannot establish the required limit. Nor does the tail sieve prove eta->0. Thus there are two unresolved analytic tasks, now with a concrete alternative target involving a finite periodic one-dimensional source slice rather than all later weighted mergers.
