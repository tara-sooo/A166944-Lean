## 1. Exact full-coverage earliest-arrival policy and the refund identity

Use birth reward D>0 per live tick, common retirement per window, and the actual phase gap cost of 59432b9: at a completed phase at center x and firing clock f, cost is D(f-a_min) if qmin(x)<K and zero otherwise. Here a_min is the earliest arrival among FULL canonical paths of this window into that very phase. Idle cost is zero. All ticks including outgoing last live transition count; a firing on arrival clock equal to retirement is charged, but arrival is not live. Define Q_act,M^* as the prefix optimum of this objective. A phase is defined by center and next firing clock; an arrival at that very clock belongs to the next phase. No assumption about possible arrivals after a root's death is made in computing its actual costs.

There is a particularly simple cap-one policy, different from the minimum reflected debt policy:
- At each newly occupied physical phase retain one of its earliest arriving roots, by fixed deterministic tie breaking.
- Keep it through the phase's last live tick. Reject later arrivals and births at the occupied phase. If two firings and/or births arrive at the same vacant phase at the same clock, keep just one.
- Process all outgoing ticks, including their costs, before selecting survivors at the next clock. Rejecting an arriving root does not erase its preceding completed phase.

This defines actual prefix deaths, with no resurrection. Induct on absolute clock from first birth: every full-path occupied bin has exactly one live representative, since the predecessor of each full arrival has a representative following the identical deterministic tick; newborns are offered independently. In a stationary phase no representative moves before its own firing, hence the kept root's arrival is exactly a_min. The cap-one policy covers every occupied tick from that earliest arrival until the phase's end. This also gives a short direct proof of the full-coverage property for birth-to-retirement (the core already proves related full-deadline clipping for observed bins).

Let U_K be the number of all occupied physical bins with qmin(x)>=K from first birth to retirement in their own windows (count each bin once, irrespective of root multiplicity). Let Z_K be the occupied ticks of the nonrough phases cut off by retirement *strictly before* their next firing. For the above policy d_e, every completed nonrough phase pays exactly its FULL wait, since its representative is earliest. Every completed or terminal rough phase has zero cost. Death upon merging at a new phase adds no unpaid idle there. Thus summing phase by phase, and even separately for each selected root, gives
    Q_act(d_e)/D = U_K + Z_K.                              (1)
In particular no propagation of reflected credit or debt is needed to obtain the lower U_K. This is not a claim that the earliest representative minimizes reflected debt, or that d_e optimizes Q_act.

For any capped-one policy, its nonrough completed waits are at most the charged gap; a final incomplete nonrough phase has length <=2qmin(x)<2K by the calendar bound. All its rough time is bounded by U_K since all live ticks are full-path ticks and cap one. Hence
    U_K + Z_K <= Q_act,1^*/D <= U_K + 2 K N.               (2)
The static-gap objective lacks the exact cancellation (1) if the earliest actual arrival is later than the previous eligible calendar clock. This is why the actual gap version matters. The hybrid cost has the earlier upper bound, but no identity (1) is claimed for it. There is no claim of a corresponding identity at M>1: later arrivals can be overcharged by earliest-arrival gap.

For T~sqrt(R)/L^B, L=log R, and K=T/L^c with c>3, the normalized difference between Q_act,1^*/(DNT) and U_K/(NT) is O(L^-c), negligible at the target log L/L^3. Thus for the pure actual-gap objective it is sufficient AND necessary at that scale to show a large lower on occupied rough bins. The long-phase release recursion is still useful for hybrid costs and exact finite optimization but cannot by itself amplify small-scale birth credit into a substantial pure actual-gap objective without producing occupied rough bins. This identifies a structural limitation of the proposed amplification strategy, not a failure of the prior scalar optimizer.

## 2. Quantitative density target versus infinitude

Write B_R=#twins centered at even x in [R,2R]. For all large R these centers are canonical multiples of 6. A twin canonical birth stays idle for its entire birth-to-retirement horizon of <=1.5T=o(R): the checked height label bound p<=3T+7 excludes both neighboring prime labels of order R. Hence U_K>=T B_R for the thresholds here. Every visited site lies in I=[R-3T-6,2R]; any site can occur in O(1) windows by the gain bound and W=6 floor(T/6). Its total full occupied time, counting windows, is <=C T. The independent two-form semiprime upper of 0e9b47b/ c0e45b0 therefore gives
    T B_R <= U_K <= C T (B_R + O(R log L/L^3) + O(T)).
The O(T) counts centers in the lower boundary extension, negligible here. Thus for the original pure actual-gap route at T~sqrt(R)/L^B, the condition
    Q_act,1^*/(DNT) = omega(log L/L^3) along unbounded R
is equivalent to B_R/N=omega(log L/L^3) along unbounded R (or unbounded limsup of the respective ratio). For omega use a subsequence where the ratio tends to infinity. This is stronger in quantitative content than merely finding one twin in each of an unbounded sequence of slabs; it is not an equivalence to the conjecture of infinitude alone. The same observation applies to old uniform-upper objectives where twin births give a full positive lower. It explains why time amplification via record counts has a genuine density bottleneck. It does not prove that a density proof is impossible.

## 3. Remove the log L factor by a weighted physical occupation field

Take S=ceil(sqrt(2R+1)), T~S (or any T=o(R) with T<=C S and W~T), and fixed sigma>0. At each physical bin from birth to retirement use nonnegative weight
    w_sigma(x)=min(1,(qmin(x)/S)^sigma).
This is a DIFFERENT objective: no phase charge, no record debt. The exact cap-one optimum W_sigma is the sum of weights over the full occupied bins, attained by the earliest-arrival policy above. The following independent weighted frontier estimate applies at all these horizons, not just near-square cutoff K.

Fix 0<alpha<1/2. Non-twin x in I has q=qmin(x)<=S, and one composite neighbor qr, r>=q, with both r and qr+-2 having no prime factors <q. The least factor belongs to a composite neighbor because a prime neighbor is of order R. This includes q^2. For a fixed q>=R^alpha, write X=(2R+1)/q. Sift variable r<=X by the two distinct forbidden residue classes of r and qr+-2 for odd primes <=min(X^(1/12),q/2); these are distinct since the sieve primes are strictly below q. This cutoff has logarithm >>_alpha L. The elementary Selberg upper from 0e9b47b gives O_alpha(R/(q L^2)) centers for each sign and q. In any dyadic band [y,2y] with y>=R^alpha/2, intersected with q>=R^alpha, sum over primes by Chebyshev:
    # non-twin centers with qmin in that band <= O_alpha(R/L^3).
Unlike the former rough-frontier sum, weight each dyadic band of q below S by 2^(-j sigma) times a fixed constant. The geometric series converges. For q<R^alpha the contribution to the weighted site count is <=O(R (R^alpha/S)^sigma)=o(R/L^3). Hence
    sum_(non-twin x in I) w_sigma(x) = O_(alpha,sigma)(R/L^3). (3)
This proof uses no lower sieve, no assertion that semiprime factors near sqrt are equidistributed, and no prime-pair asymptotic. Rounding S changes constants only.

All twin centers have weight 1 for large R. As before each site has total bin duration across windows <=C T, and each twin birth contributes >=T. Thus for T~S:
    B_R/N <= W_sigma/(NT) <= C B_R/N + O_sigma(L^-3).      (4)
We have removed the log L loss by downweighting small factors instead of accumulating the whole rough band. A sufficient lower is omega(L^-3) for this objective, but it is again equivalent to B_R/N=omega(L^-3), not a weaker result about mere infinitude. (4) is valid with T~sqrt R/L^B too; no reason to claim changing horizon alone yields the lower.

In fact a static initial-wait field gives the same threshold. Write w0(A)=first wait from birth. By height p<=2w0+7, w0>=(qmin-7)/2; by calendar w0<=2qmin. Truncate at T~S and count first occupied phase through its firing tick (no cost in weighted field). Distinct initial centers produce disjoint bins, so
    W_sigma >= sum_A w_sigma(A) min(T,w0(A)).
The right hand side is itself between T B_R and T B_R+O(RT/L^3), by (3). For this omega-threshold the large dynamic weighted objective and a static birth statistic are equivalent. For unweighted first idle time, the same upper follows from min(T,w0)<=C T min(1,qmin/S) and (3) with sigma=1. Its lower is T B_R up to harmless integer endpoint conventions. Hence there is no proven amplification in this regime of weighted frontier estimates; the missing lower amounts to a quantitative twin-density lower.

## 4. Upgrade the actual initial-idle and reset-family lower to an explicit time exponent

The older fixed-small-power fundamental lemma used level sqrt R and did not quantify delta. Use the published dimension-two Diamond–Halberstam–Richert (DHR) lower sieve at a much higher level instead. In a progression-class interval of X integers, the two affine neighbor forms have two prohibited classes at each free odd prime, and exact CRT remainders |r_d|<=2^omega(d) for squarefree d. In a fixed progression modulo a base modulus B, omit primes dividing B; the dimension-two product hypothesis
    prod_(v<=p<w, free)(1-2/p)^(-1) <= (log w/log v)^2 (1+C/log v)
holds uniformly by Mertens and the convergent product of O(1/p^2) factors, with C enlarged for small v. Omitting primes only decreases the upper product. Also the product V(z) over free primes >=5 is >>1/log^2 z uniformly (omitting primes increases V). For canonical roots A=6j without additional CRT conditions use X~R/6, with primes 2,3 omitted. For an admissible fixed residue class modulo B use X=R/B+O(1); the two residues are distinct when p does not divide 2B, and prior local checks guarantee no excluded pinned class.

DHR lower sieve (Diamond-Halberstam, Theorem 9.1, as restated in Franze–Kao eq.(20)) yields
    count >= X V(z) [ f_2(log y/log z) - O((loglog y)^2/(log y)^(1/6)) ]
                - 2 sum_(d<y,d|P(z)) 4^omega(d)|r_d|.
The dimension-two DHR positivity threshold is below 4.267 (reported 4.266 in Franze Table 1, 4.2665 upper in Ford Table 1). Take y=floor(X/(log X)^12). Since 8^omega(d)<=tau_8(d) on squarefree d and sum_(d<=y)tau_8(d)<<y(log(2y))^7 by product counting, remainder is O(X/(log X)^5). For z=R^theta times any fixed constant, B=R^o(1), and fixed 0<theta<1/4.267, log y/log z tends to 1/theta>4.267. Thus the lower bracket is bounded away from zero and every admissible class contains >>_theta (R/B)/L^2 survivors uniformly. This is the same exact local CRT sequence as before; no Bombieri-Vinogradov input needed for this final two-form step. Constants uniform for B=R^o(1) in the concrete families below (once log B/L sufficiently small depending on theta). We use theta=.234 as a concrete safe exponent, not an optimal threshold of sieve theory.

(a) In the full slab, at least c_theta R/L^2 canonical roots have qmin>2H+7 where H=floor(R^theta). They are idle throughout H ticks by checked height bound. At ANY longer common horizon T (including T~S or earlier T~sqrt R/L^B), kill those roots at age H and all others at birth. This is a simultaneous cap-one policy with no charges in the calendar/actual-gap/hybrid birth objectives. Consequently
    Q_act,1^* >= c_theta D R^(1+theta)/L^2
and the same lower for the other birth-credit objectives, in particular exponent 1.234. For the weighted field at T~S, the same initial phases give
    W_sigma >> R^(1+theta + sigma(theta-1/2))/L^2.
Both lower bounds remain far short of the near-square target.

(b) Uniform local supply. In ANY interval of canonical j of length X->infinity within the large slab, choose H=floor(X^theta), z=2H+8, y=X/log^12 X, and repeat the exact CRT argument. At least c_theta X/log^2 X roots have idle time H, uniformly in location. In a full spatial window W~T~S, X~T/6, this supplies >>T/log^2 T distinct quiet births at age >>T^theta ~ R^(theta/2). For global H=R^theta, uniform supply only follows on interval lengths >=R^(4.267 theta+epsilon) (and <=R), not on all square-root windows. This is a local lower input for any multiscale construction, but no lower on their later survival.

(c) Apply the improved final sieve to the actual nonnested reset fans from 67d4716. There the modulus is B=12 prod_(5<=q<=2P) q with admissible class density rho>=exp(-O(r log P)), and prime labels in [P,2P), selected via the earlier BV/rough-prime lemma. For clarity, B here denotes CRT modulus, not the fixed log power in the horizon. The local compatibility and shortReset_merge_birth hypotheses are unchanged; B=R^o(1). The final sieve now gives >>rho R/L^2 centers with qmin>z=R^theta, for EVERY fixed theta<1/4.267, rather than an unspecified sufficiently small delta. Remove edge centers within O(P) of window boundaries at total cost O((R/T+1)P); negligible in both prior regimes since T=floor((z-8)/3) polynomial and rho=R^-o(1). All r+1 canonical births merge exactly at birth(x), and remain stationary through the common observation of length T; the checked height label bound excludes a firing at x for the entire window. Thus the prior growing wide-fan multiplicities are now attained at T~R^.234/3:
  * P=floor(L^1/2), r=floor(P^epsilon/4): >=R^(1-o(1)) centers with >=c L^(epsilon/2) actual roots; gain of each noninitial direct parent <=2P.
  * r=floor(log L/(loglog L)^2), P=ceil(r^(2/epsilon)): >=R L^(-2-o(1)) centers with >=(log L)^(1-o(1)) actual roots, each noninitial direct parent has nonzero gain <=2P=(log L)^O(1)=L^o(1).
The fan-root sets are disjoint at each center within its window because different centers are stationary at the common observation start and a root has a unique trajectory; different windows have disjoint root sets. In (b)-density regime this yields at least R L^(-2-o(1)) NONZERO-GAIN actual canonical roots with total gain <=(log L)^O(1) at their own elapsed age T, after one genuine firing. (At their own age T they have already merged, since P=o(T), and have not fired again by the longer window retirement.) This is a stronger growing-horizon construction on actual nonstationary paths, not merely a family of arbitrary formal residue environments. It does not reach sqrt R; the factor R^(1/2-.234) remains. The same final sieve upgrade applies to the earlier nested subset fan with B=R^o(1), rho>=L^-o(1), and span L^o(1), without altering its multiplicity ceiling.

## 5. What the results imply for the next proof route

The current pure actual-gap credit-amplification route has been reduced to full occupied rough bins by (2); for the omega density target, (4) and section 2 identify a quantitative twin-density requirement. This is valuable structurally but does not settle the conjecture. The reset construction is now effective at an explicit substantially larger polynomial horizon; standard dimension-two lower sieve is not available at the near-square cutoff. Merely sharpening the error factor or adjusting cap/debt cannot bridge that polynomial gap. The next research should concentrate on the arithmetic of actual trajectories past their R^.234-scale idle phases, in particular the previously derived direct square-root single-root gain witness with exact margin (c0e45b0 section 2), rather than treat successive upper-bound refinements as proof progress. No claim of level 4.85, no new Lean compile, no novelty claim.
