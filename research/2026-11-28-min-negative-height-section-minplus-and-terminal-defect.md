## 2. Last-defect, sink-local residual certificate

Let the trial graph have the same vertices, source b(v)>=0 and first-H sink set as true graph, but a different deterministic outgoing edge on some set B_bad; missing edges and edges leaving the rectangle are allowed. Each graph is acyclic with outdegree<=1, and weights in [0,1] agree on common edges. In topological order define the clipped trial and true flows F,C by min(1,b+weighted incoming flow). Form a third graph by deleting every noncommon trial edge and retaining only common edges; G denotes its clipped flow. Thus G<=F and G<=C pointwise by induction. This is a subgraph of the TRUE graph, not a claim that trial paths are true when they contain a changed edge.

For a trial vertex u with a changed outgoing edge, let T(u) be its trial forward path, and define u to be LAST-BAD for a sink s if T(u) reaches s and contains no further changed edge after u. Let d(u,s) be the product of trial edge weights from u to s. Put
    D_s=sum_{u last-bad for s} F(u) d(u,s).
Then
    C(s) >= G(s) >= [F(s)-D_s]_+ ,
    sum_s C(s) >= sum_s [F(s)-D_s]_+.                    (1)
Proof: at a vertex v, monotonicity and 1-Lipschitz clipping imply
    F(v)-G(v) <= sum_common u->v w(F(u)-G(u))
                    + sum_changed trial u->v w F(u).
Iterate this nonnegative inequality along the COMMON subgraph. A source of error at changed edge u->v can reach a sink only along a common continuation, exactly the last-bad condition; no error is charged through another changed edge. Since outdegree<=1, each term reaches at most one sink. Clip each sink's loss to its own F(s) by nonnegativity of G. This proves (1). It improves both the raw all-defect bound and the version weighted by trial downstream discount but counting every changed edge, including repeated changes on the same forward path. No probabilistic assumptions. The exact common subgraph flow G can be even stronger than (1); (1) is a sufficient lower certificate useful only if its RHS can be estimated without solving the full true graph. Reinserting the true edges can only increase C relative to G, but no estimate of that extra increase is asserted.

## 4. Exact composition in min-plus, not in clipped discounted mass

Let 3<=h0<h1 and fixed B>=0. Every root reaching h1 for the first time has a unique first h0 hit earlier, since upward height increments <=1. At its h0 state, substitute the smallest ancestor a_h0^B(x0), which exists because its own gain to h0 is <=B. The substituted root has the exact same suffix, and never has larger total gain (at any future x, gain=A-x). It has not reached h1 earlier, because h1 cannot precede first h0. It follows that computing the first h1 hit from each h0 state using the minimum ancestor and discarding budget exits, then taking minima over resulting equal states, gives EXACTLY a_h1^B, not just a lower bound. This is an exact min-plus transport of ancestor coordinates with infinity for unreachability, or equivalently a min-plus displacement recursion with edge cost p-1 or0. No tracking of all histories or multiplicities needed. Iterate through any finite sequence of increasing heights with the SAME total budget B. The proof tolerates descent below earlier height sections on a continuation. It does not assert that raw ancestor multiplicities can be compressed in this way.

For distinct budgets B_j=4 h_j increasing with the stage height, run the same minimum transport using only the prior retained states, and prune when the new budget is exceeded. This is a lower subset of the exact a_hj^(4hj); it may omit a root that exceeded the earlier smaller budget but would meet a later one. Do not assert exactness with changing budgets. At each stage, if N_j distinct previous states are transported, let e_j be number of budget failures, m_j be number of successful inputs minus distinct outputs. Then
    N_(j+1)=N_j-e_j-m_j.                               (2)
This is exact even if two input states coalesce far before the next section, or one arrives at a state on another's trajectory. The minimum ancestor continues and is sufficient for all future budget tests.

Warning: do NOT assume an exact composition law for the clipped discounted flow. Abstract counterexample in a deterministic DAG: two roots each of mass1; root1 first hits section v, then reaches u below the section at edge weight1/2; root2 reaches u with weight1 without previous section hit. Then both follow u->w (section) at weight1/2. True cap at u leaves1 so flow at w is1/2. If stop at the first section and restart from its values v=1,w=1/2, flow at w is1/4+1/2=3/4. The latter overestimates due to delaying the earlier clip. Min-plus avoids this because the smallest ancestor is a genuine witness and gain differences remain ordered. The example is an abstract weighted graph, not a claimed MIN instance.

## 7. Why a fixed small-label trial graph cannot by itself break the roughness barrier

Suppose a TRUE canonical path reaching first H uses only primes<=z. The earlier spatial wall theorem gives G<Q_z=2 product_{3<=p<=z}p; a downward path using these labels cannot cross any multiple of Q_z. For z=o(log H) (in particular fixed z), Q_z=H^o(1) by Chebyshev. Completed waits W<=17G/8+395/12 (c0e45b0 finite-state certified inequality, not newly Lean checked). The final idle I=T-W with T=H-3+G/2 obeys

Thus qmin(x)>I/2 >=(1-o(1))H/2. This applies to all common-graph reachable sinks from the cutoff-z pilot. In particular for H comparable sqrt R/L^B0, assuming finitely many twins, the existing non-twin rough frontier upper O(R log L/L³) bounds their count, regardless of residual estimate. Their clipped mass is at most that count. So no fixed cutoff common-graph certificate can alone prove a density R/L² at near-square H under finite twins; such a lower would already establish a strong rough-pair theorem (and hence infinitely many twins). At H=R^.4 this is not a contradiction: the rough upper at polynomial exponent .4 is only O(R/L²). But the proposed strategy, if relying solely on the common graph at small z, has not circumvented the arithmetic problem.

More generally for ANY first-H sink with a canonical ancestor of gain G<=H/4, the displayed ledger gives I>=19H/32-O(1), qmin>=19H/64-O(1). Thus the large low-gain-minimum fraction in the finite audit is a warning that the first-height target may still be dominated by rough centers. For qmin<H/16, I<H/8 gives G>=7H/13-O(1) for EVERY canonical ancestor, not merely the chosen minimum. Such a first-hit state is intrinsically active; it cannot be supplied by a long static initial quiet phase.

Another record issue: in the high family first event p at wait (3p-7)/2, h1=p, but the immediately preceding pre-tick height is 3+w-1=(3p-3)/2. The positive increments of size O(loglog R) in 58cabbc do not recover the previous maximum for large R. Thus endlessly repeating a fixed small number of those local increments is not a polynomial-scale first-height improvement.

## 8. Terminal alignment after the last top event: a new history-free theorem

For any H of fixed polynomial size in [R^theta0,O(sqrt R)], fixed theta0>0, Y=H/L², let K,D>=1 with K+D=o(H). Consider distinct first-H sinks of canonical histories with gain<=4H, qmin(x)<K, having at least one top event p>=Y, and total subsequent gain from its LAST such event to first H at most D. No restriction on count or order of intervening lower-label events, no slow-wait assumption for the top event. Let (y,h_a) be the poststate of that last top arrival. Then h_a<H (first hit has not happened, moving cannot increase h). All subsequent complete waits obey W_suf<=17D/8+395/12, by the universal block inequality. Final idle <2K. Hence
    H-(13/8)D-395/12-2K <= h_a < H.
The height pin p|h_a-b_s and spatial pin y=c_s modp hold. For each integer h_a in this short band and each sign, the positive integer h_a-b_s=O(H) has O_theta0(1) prime factors p>=Y; for each such p, y in the O(R) slab has O(R/p) possible values. Absolute clock n=h_a-y/2 is determined. Therefore the number of candidate distinct anchors is
    O_theta0(R(D+K+1)/Y).                               (3)
Each physical anchor leads to at most one first-H sink by deterministic continuation, so (3) bounds the number of distinct sinks themselves (not root multiplicities). There is NO additional factor for the number of positions displaced by the short suffix or the number of intervening events. This is a new use of the height pins on a moving-to-first-record endpoint rather than pair contacts.

Taking K=D=floor(H/L^6) gives O(R/L^4)=o(R/L²). For H=R^.4 it says a density-scale intrinsically nonrough first-H lower at qmin<K cannot mainly consist of short connectors following top arrivals. Except for O(R/L^4) sinks, any witness with qmin<K must either have NO top label or must traverse >D gain after the last top label. At H comparable sqrt R/L^B0, the existing rough frontier bound additionally removes all qmin>=K non-twin sinks at cost O(R log L/L³); under finite twins this applies to the whole high-section population for large R. Thus a density-scale near-square first-height theorem, under no twins, would require paths with no top event or substantial subtop gain after their last top event. Occupying top local anchors alone, even canonically, and controlling a short connector is insufficient. No claim that the remaining cases are impossible. The theorem uses the prior verified paper block inequality; it is not a new Lean theorem.
