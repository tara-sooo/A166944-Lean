## 3. Buffered last-entry levels force earlier actual shared gain

The previous level strip (D,2D) touches the lower edge of I=[2D,4D). An ascent origin can therefore occur arbitrarily close in gain to a hit at the bottom of I. Use instead the buffered strip
    lambda in (D/2,D), width D/2,
with the SAME disjoint hit band I=[2D,4D). For an arbitrary nonnegative integrated hit weight, use a new uniform allocation across this buffered level strip (or any bounded density having the correct integrated mass). This is a separate mathematically valid exact partition of the hit ledger, not preservation of the old layer origin labels. Alternatively begin the entire residence argument with these buffered levels from the outset. The exact nonnegative last-entry formula still holds with normalizer 2/D. The birth integrated capacity (1) is unchanged. For ascent use
    A'_j(a,b)=length((D_j/2,D_j) intersect (d_a,d_b)).
The buffered strips are also consecutive and disjoint over j, so their sum on each edge is <=positive delta d and (2) is unchanged. In particular no logarithmic overlap, and no new asymptotic factor from the normalizer: one may view the pointwise donor budget as 3 L_H (D_j/2), integrated over width D_j/2. Both separate joint flow models use the same old capacities and separately valid new budgets; never add their payments together.

Let x be an actual band-I shared natural high hit with deficit d_x>=2D. If its buffered origin is a last upward donor a->b, then d_a<lambda<D. Along the actual common physical segment from a to x, gain G=C_x-C_a and elapsed time W=t_x-t_a satisfy
    d_x-d_a=2W-G>=D,
    W >= (G+D)/2,
    G>=D/5,                                         (4)
using W<=3G on every completed original segment. Also there are at least ceil(D/(5B)) completed edges from a to x and sum of the actual least-factor r on those edges is >=(G+D)/4, since W<=2 sum r. These bounds are independent of whether the donor is itself natural high, or its incidence is retained, and hold for the actual weighted layer on every shared pair. If the origin is birth v and d_v<=D, exactly the same inequalities hold from v to x (the birth must be above lambda to serve as origin). Consequently any buffered high hit whose actual common physical prefix before the hit has gain <D/5 MUST have been born with deficit >D. This last assertion includes either possible origin: a shared ascent would contradict (4), and a low-deficit birth would too. Thus a true high-deficit birth is the only short-common-gain exception. This supplies a quantitative split for the arithmetic birth sector without assuming uniform layer density or relative absorption of a masked donor.

For future work, the segment from a or low-deficit v to x is a real shared complete segment; if its traversing cohort is large, the complete representative cover of a1c35db applies, including intermediate completed edges. Equation (4) is a lower bound on gain/clock and on cumulative r, not a claim that the intermediate r-sum consists of natural high events. It does not itself upper-bound the amount of actual pair mass using a long segment.

## 4. Intermediate selected-label gain coercivity in buffered segments

For any z>=3 let L_z=2 product_(odd primes p<=z) p. A sequence of consecutive actual selected labels p<=z cannot cross the next multiple of L_z on the even gain axis: p|C-1 or p|C+1 at the prehorizon, the positive even distance to the next multiple is at least p-1, and a multiple of L_z admits no such outgoing selected label. Thus the total gain of a maximal small-label run is <L_z. This argument does NOT need a deficit upper bound (the bounded-deficit result of 9ce7d27 was a way to ensure small selected labels).

For any actual completed segment of gain G write G_>z=sum of gains p-1 with p>z, and k=#such steps. It has at most k+1 intervening small-label runs. Since p>z gives p-1>=z,
    G <= G_>z+(k+1)L_z
      <= (1+L_z/z)G_>z+L_z.
Applied to (4), this gives
    G_>z >= (D/5-L_z)/(1+L_z/z).                      (5)
When D>5L_z the segment contains a genuinely larger selected label before the high hit. Choose z=max(3,floor(log u/32)) eventually, and use the elementary Chebyshev upper bound on the prime theta sum (<=4z for large z). Then L_z=O(u^1/8), while D>=D_0~B/sqrt u, so every buffered ascent or low-deficit birth segment has G_>z=Omega(D/u^1/8). The estimate counts actual complete shared gains, not occurrence of hypothetical labels or independently sampled primes. It holds for arbitrarily long source histories and includes intermediate reset/nonreset events. It is intentionally a coarse lower bound. It does not imply any one selected label is near B, that the larger-label gain is natural high, or that its actual source multiplicity is small. A remaining high Hall obstruction in the buffered model must use these long real shared prefixes, or genuine high-deficit births. This is the new dynamical constraint to combine with arithmetic support and inherited cohort bounds.
