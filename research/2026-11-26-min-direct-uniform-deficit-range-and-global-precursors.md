# Uniform deficit-range sieve and global positive-variation precursors

Continuation of [6f7af67](https://github.com/tara-sooo/A166944-Lean/blob/6f7af67a2016b11ce933bc5bcde6ddf286629a58/research/2026-11-26-min-direct-deficit-coordinate-sieve-and-polynomial-multipliers.md). Extend its one-dimensional deficit-coordinate estimate to an arbitrary polynomial lower label threshold and the entire canonical deficit envelope, with a logarithm depending on the actual maximum deficit and range. Then use the canonical prefix, rather than treating each macroscopic window independently: a near-square-root population with small normalized relay and variation budgets needs a linear baseline deficit, and a previous window within bounded multiplicative distance must have supplied macroscopic positive variation. Connect that supply to the earlier small/middle crossing count or actual large upward nonreset alternative. A separate accumulation argument gives an unconditional (on a hypothetical infinite canonical ray) infinitely-often logarithmic improvement over the square-root relay/variation scale. These are paper deductions, not a twin-infinitude contradiction or Lean kernel verification.

## 1. Definitions and uniform counting of close transitions

Fix a canonical root (H_root,t_root) with ordinary deficit 6. An event of label p has pre-horizon H, gain g=p-1, wait w, side I, e=1-2I, ordinary post-deficit d'=d+2w-g, and adjusted D=d'-6I. For p>=5, nonreset means x=D/2=hp is a positive integer. On a physical block with pre-horizons in [X,Y], Y=lambda X with fixed lambda>1, the canonical envelope is
    0<=d'<=6+5(H+p-1-H_root).
It follows that x=O_lambda(X). The cap on labels is C=floor sqrt(Y+1).

Take any fixed a in (0,1/2], and an integer threshold T with X^a<=T<=C. Choose ANY subset of actual nonresets p>=T in physical order, with no restriction on positive multipliers h, cofactor primality, crossing status or clock length. (Constant multiples of sqrt X also satisfy this for any fixed a<1/2.) Let F be total chosen gain, U_out=G-F total complementary physical gain, V_d=sum physical |d'-d|, Hcap=max chosen x, R_x=max x-min x and W=max(T,R_x+1). If no selected visits, the conclusions about macroscopic F are vacuous. Otherwise T<=W<=Hcap<=O_lambda(X).

For consecutive selected visits with labels p,r, let E=H2-H-(p-1) be the intervening physical gain, v=d2'-d1', and c=x2-x1=v/2-3(J-I), where I,J are the respective sides. Close means E<=B, |v|<=A, with nonnegative integer cutoffs B<T-1 and Q=floor(A/2)+3<=T/2. Same-label return requires E>=p-1 by the earlier odd-cofactor spacing identity, so p!=r on every close edge. No need to exclude c=0. At any x<=Hcap the number of distinct prime divisors >=T is O_(lambda,a)(1), by the product of those distinct divisors. This includes the high-deficit case x>=T^2 where the uniqueness used in the previous note fails.

Fix signed c, relay E, both sides, x, and high prime divisors p|x and r|x+c with p!=r. The necessary horizon residues are H=e mod p, H+p-1+E=f mod r. They specify one residue class mod pr. Since pr<=Y+1, the number of H in [X,Y] is O_lambda(X/(pr)). Each H corresponds to at most one actual starting event in the monotone ray and its chosen successor is unique. Charge the ending gain r-1: the weighted candidate count is O_lambda(X/p), independently of r. For a fixed x,p there are O_(lambda,a)(1) possibilities for r, because x+c must be another selected positive deficit coordinate <=Hcap. Thus an upper count weighted by 1/p over x and its prime factors suffices. This removes the requirement that labels be comparable or at square-root scale.

Write
    Dlog=log(2+WT/Hcap).
For each dyadic label band U<=p<2U, U=T*2^j, we claim the number of incidences (x,p), with x in the chosen range and <=Hcap, is
    <<_(lambda,a) W/Dlog.                               (1)
If Hcap>=WT, Dlog=O(1), and the bound follows directly from bounded divisor multiplicity and W integer positions. Otherwise first take an interval [L,L+W-1] containing the chosen x with L=min x>=W. Writing x=hp, each h lies in [L/(2U),2L/U] and is <=Hcap/U. For fixed h the candidate primes p are contained in an interval padded to N=ceil(W/h)+1 integers; W/h>=WU/Hcap>=WT/Hcap>1. The elementary one-dimensional upper sieve gives O_a(N/log(2N)) uniformly on translated intervals when the primes sought are >=T. For completeness choose its sieve cutoff floor N^min(1/12,a/2): N=O_lambda(X), so the cutoff is below T for large X; its Selberg error is <=N^(1/2) and its denominator has order log(2N), with constant depending on fixed a. Small N is handled trivially. Thus the contribution for this h is O_a((W/h)/Dlog). The harmonic sum over the bounded-ratio h interval is O(1), including a lower endpoint below 1. This proves (1) away from zero.

If L<W, all chosen x are in [T,min(2W,Hcap)]. Cover by dyadic deficit shells [S,2S), S=T*2^i, padding the last shell to full width S if necessary. Total shell widths are O(W). For fixed label band U, the multiplier h is in [S/(2U),2S/U]; within a shell the parameter interval has length at most ceil(S/h)+1, with S/h>=U/2>=T/2. Its logarithm is comparable to or larger than Dlog, since W<=Hcap and WT/Hcap<=T. The same harmonic summation on each shell gives O_a(S/Dlog); summing shells proves (1). No logarithmic loss from the number of shells.

Combining (1) with the weighted horizon CRT count, each label band contributes O(X W/(U Dlog)) for fixed c,E,I,J. The sum over label bands is geometric: sum_j 1/U<=2/T. Summing both signs of c (including zero), E and the four side choices proves the uniform estimate
    F_close <<_(lambda,a)
        X (W/T)(A+1)(B+1)/log(2+WT/Hcap).                (2)
This is a bound on actual ending-event gain of close consecutive selected transitions, not an independence or reachability assertion. It includes all multipliers, zero/nonzero displacements and zero/nonzero cofactor minors. The bound may be trivial when Hcap is comparable to or greater than WT, but the logarithm diverges in the subquadratic regime Hcap=o(T^2) with W>=T. For T comparable to sqrt X and Hcap=O(X^beta) at fixed beta<1, it reproduces the one-dimensional polynomial range estimate from the preceding note. Unlike that earlier theorem it is uniform even as beta approaches one; it is not a two-dimensional sieve estimate in that regime.

## 2. Full selected-population product bound and baseline consequence

The first selected event costs at most C. For consecutive selected edges the relay intervals are disjoint, sum E<=U_out; post-to-post intervals are disjoint, sum |v|<=V_d. Hence
    F <= C + O(X(W/T)(A+1)(B+1)/Dlog)
                  + C U_out/(B+1)+C V_d/(A+1).          (3)
Set z=C U_out/X, y=C V_d/X, u=R_x/T. Then W/T<=1+u+1/T, and R_x<=V_d/2+3. If F>=rho X for fixed rho>0, the same cutoff optimization as before gives
    Dlog <<_(lambda,a,rho) (1+z)(1+y)(1+u).              (4)
Indeed let P=(1+z)(1+y)(1+u). If P>=Dlog there is nothing to prove. Otherwise A=ceil(K y), B=ceil(K z) for sufficiently large fixed K depending on rho satisfy the cutoff conditions: Dlog<=log(2+T)=O(log X), so A,B=O_rho(log X). The last two terms are <=2X/K, C=o(X), and the close term is O(X P/Dlog). Rearrangement proves (4). In particular for some constant K1 depending only on the fixed parameters,
    Hcap >= WT exp(-K1 P).                              (5)
Every physical plateau in the block, including the initial one, has ordinary deficit at least 2Hcap-V_d, by the definition of total variation and d'=2x+6I. This connects the local supply upper bound to the absolute baseline.

For square-root thresholds T=ceil(eta sqrt X), fixed eta>0 with T<=C, F>=rho X and normalized budgets U_out,V_d<=K0 sqrt X imply z,y,u=O_(lambda,eta,K0)(1). Thus (5) strengthens the prior X^(1-o(1)) statement for bounded budgets to Hcap>=c0 X and, for sufficiently large X,
    d_start >= delta X,   all physical d>=delta X         (6)
for a constant delta>0 depending on lambda,eta,rho,K0 (take delta<=1). No fixed multiplier cap or restriction on the sign of d changes is needed. More generally (5) yields a lower scale with an explicit exponential dependence on the product of the three budgets; the constant is not numerically optimized. This is a conditional baseline necessity, not a lower bound on the deficit of every arbitrary MIN state.

## 3. Cumulative positive-variation precursor and crossing alternatives

Assume an infinite canonical ray from a fixed root (in particular it would exist above the last twin center under finite twins). Fix X0 sufficiently large and geometric levels X_i=16^i X0, i>=0. Use the physical block from the first plateau with H>=X_i to the first with H>=16 X_i. Consecutive blocks partition the ray from the first such plateau, and their gains satisfy G_i=15X_i+O(sqrt X_i). Let C_i=floor sqrt(16X_i+1). Call a sufficiently large block good for fixed eta,rho,K0 if there is a chosen nonreset population p>=ceil(eta sqrt X_i) with F_i>=rho X_i, and U_out,i,V_i<=K0 sqrt X_i. The populations can be chosen independently in different blocks; U_out,i includes ALL other physical events, including resets. This condition is deliberately stronger than macroscopic dominance.

Every good block i satisfies (6), with a common delta>0. Choose an integer L0>=1 such that 5*16^(-L0)<=delta/4. At the first plateau crossing X_(i-L0), the fixed-root envelope gives d<=5X_(i-L0)+O(sqrt X_(i-L0))+6<=delta X_i/2 eventually. At the first plateau crossing X_i, d>=delta X_i. If P_+,k denotes the sum of positive ordinary deficit changes in block k, exact telescoping along this one ray yields
    sum_(i-L0<=k<i) P_+,k >= delta X_i/2.                (7)
Since sum_(i-L0<=k<i) X_k < X_i/15, some such k satisfies
    P_+,k >= delta X_k.                                 (8)
In fact a larger constant than delta is possible, but is unnecessary. This proves a macroscopic precursor, at a scale within a fixed multiplicative factor of the good block. It cannot be paid by a large inherited deficit at an arbitrarily remote scale, because of the canonical envelope. All constants are independent of i.

Connect this to the previous [crossing-clock hierarchy](https://github.com/tara-sooo/A166944-Lean/blob/6f7af67a2016b11ce933bc5bcde6ddf286629a58/research/2026-11-26-min-direct-crossing-clock-and-factor-hierarchy.md). In the precursor block k use its balanced primorial cut family, cube threshold T3, H_T set of actual surviving-cut crossers with p>=T3, U gain of ALL events outside that set, and M_<T3 normalized actual crossing mass at labels<T3. Earlier coverage gives
    U <= M_<T3 + 2(ell-1)G/(T3-1)+O(E_X)
      = M_<T3 + o(X_k).
Here ell=X_k^(2/11+o(1)), T3 grows at cube-root scale, and E_X=o(X_k). Let eta0=delta/100 and F_up total gain of events in H_T whose ordinary change d'-d>eta0(p-1). Every outside event contributes positive variation at most 5g, every other inside event at most eta0*g, and the high label upper envelope is d'-d<= (3+4/(T3-1))*g. Therefore
    P_+,k <= eta0 G_k +5U+(3+4/(T3-1))*F_up
            <= eta0 G_k+5M_<T3+(3+o(1))*F_up+o(X_k).     (9)
Using (8), eta0 G_k=(.15delta+o(1))X_k, so either M_<T3>=delta X_k/20 or F_up>=delta X_k/16 for sufficiently large k (if both fail, the right side is <delta X_k). In the first case, the earlier upper mass inequality implies either small crossing count Omega(X_k/kappa) or middle crossing count Omega(X_k/T3) at the stated cutoffs. In the second case every counted upward large event is long (2w>g), hence has prime cofactor by the cube certificate, and it is a NONRESET: a reset post-deficit is at most6, whereas its positive movement exceeds eta0*g>6 and pre-deficit is nonnegative. Thus the precursor can be classified into the same three scale families, but the high branch is now a macroscopic actual large UPWARD nonreset population rather than just a general long or B/reset population. This implication uses earlier analytic crossing estimates; no new Fourier smoothing exponent is asserted.

There is an immediate global obstruction to a persistent good regime. Block k in (8) cannot itself be good, since V_k>=P_+,k>=delta X_k>K0 sqrt X_k for large k. Every sufficiently large good block has at least one non-good predecessor among the previous L0 blocks. Hence runs of consecutive good blocks have length at most L0, and non-good blocks have lower asymptotic density at least 1/(L0+1) in the logarithmic block index. This is not a positive density assertion in the horizon variable and not a contradiction to infinite MIN by itself. It rules out maintaining the bounded-budget regime at all large scales. Neither the original small/middle alternative nor large upward gain has been universally excluded.

## 4. Infinitely-often logarithmic lower bound via accumulation of the earlier two-dimensional estimate

A different global consequence does not need the bounded-good-block baseline theorem. On the same fixed infinite ray and geometric blocks, choose arbitrary nonreset populations p>=ceil(eta sqrt X_i) in each block (including empty populations), with fixed eta>0. Put U_i=G_i-F_i and V_i=total ordinary variation. Then for some constant c_eta>0, on infinitely many i,
    U_i+V_i >= c_eta sqrt X_i (log X_i)^(2/3).            (10)
Proof: suppose the reverse strict bound holds eventually with a sufficiently small constant epsilon. The complementary gain is o(X_i), so F_i>=X_i eventually. Accumulated ordinary variation bounds every deficit at every state in block i:
    d <= O_fixed_prefix(1) + sum_(j<=i) V_j
      <= O_fixed_prefix(1)+(4/3)*epsilon sqrt X_i (log X_i)^(2/3).
The geometric factor follows from sqrt X_j=4^(j-i)sqrt X_i and monotonicity of log X_j. Thus the entire selected population satisfies x<=X_i^(5/8) eventually. This is within the earlier two-dimensional deficit-coordinate sieve range beta<3/4. Apply its range-sensitive product inequality from [6f7af67](https://github.com/tara-sooo/A166944-Lean/blob/6f7af67a2016b11ce933bc5bcde6ddf286629a58/research/2026-11-26-min-direct-deficit-coordinate-sieve-and-polynomial-multipliers.md), at fixed beta=5/8, K=1, rho=1, lambda=16 and eta. In its notation,
    (1+C_i U_i/X_i)(1+C_i V_i/X_i)(1+C_i R_x/X_i)
          >= k_eta log^2 X_i
for a positive constant k_eta independent of i and epsilon. But C_i<=5 sqrt X_i for large i, R_x<=V_i/2+3, and the assumed bound makes this product <=K2 epsilon^3 log^2 X_i eventually for an absolute K2. Choose epsilon so that K2 epsilon^3<k_eta. Contradiction. Thus (10) holds, on any fixed infinite canonical ray for the chosen near-square-root nonreset populations. The finite prefix may have arbitrarily large deficit/variation; its contribution vanishes compared with the eventual polynomial scale. The conclusion allows U_i to be large because the chosen population is not dominant; it does not force such a near-square-root population to exist. In particular it does not eliminate the full large-crosser branch, whose labels may be as small as cube-root scale or may be resets.

The new logarithmic bound is stronger than the local O(sqrt X) obstruction when requiring all sufficiently large blocks to have small budgets, but still far below a macroscopic gain/variation bound. The main remaining task is a supply upper bound or twin witness for the small/middle crossings and the macroscopic upward large-event alternative in (9), or for configurations without any good blocks. The local nonzero-minor, high-baseline regime has been connected to a finite-distance precursor on the same ray, rather than excluded. No global contradiction is obtained from these results alone.
