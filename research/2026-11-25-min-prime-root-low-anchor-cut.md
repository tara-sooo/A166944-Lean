# LOW-compressed first-contact intersections, gain-weighted source-anchor budgets, and local mask cut envelopes

2026-11-25 JST. Continuation of d58653cc47c1896e131927471bfb5add203c98b3 on the user-selected prime-root branch. The preceding birth-tail mincut has many projected keys with small incoming labels. Here trace each first projected contact through its immediately preceding LOW-only block to the last non-LOW selection (or its own initial prime root). Count pairs of anchor keys independently of the number of possible contact horizons. More importantly, add actual SOURCE-ANCHOR block budgets charged to the original selected gain, even if the group at the anchor is enormous. The exact joint cut theorem extends to these additional keys. Local LOW visit masks reconstruct the first shared local horizon and both incoming labels, giving an explicit prefix-filtered upper envelope for each cutset. This is a new localized sufficient target, not a bound on all deep ancestry. No Lean compilation or twin-prime proof.

## 1. Original paths and LOW compression

Keep dyadic X, B=ceil(sqrt(7X)), u=log B, ell=1+log u, N=Theta(X/u) original prime roots M in [X,2X), T=floor(X/u^.25), H=T+B, F=T²Bu. Original gain-stopped MIN paths include initial and terminal vertices. Total gain G_M<H, full waits <=2r<=3(p-1). Use the existing frozen medium ledger and the natural high set I (p>=Q, r>p/sqrt(log p)), unchanged by this new accounting partition. Each unordered pair has remaining actual medium weight w_med (both directions) and/or retained ordered high weight w_high (both directions). Both are nonnegative. The whole high pair weight <=3H; for medium the earlier directed endpoint density and disjoint focal blocks give O(H/m_g), m_g=u^(19/4)/ell^4, with a uniform constant in the asymptotic ledger. Finite diagnostic allocations use their own caps.

Choose an independent LOW threshold y, with L=2 product of odd primes <=y, growing slowly, e.g. the maximal primorial allowed by L<=ell^.25 eventually. The partition need not coincide with the earlier medium LOW rule. Every completed selection with p<=y occurs at even pre-horizon C with p|C-1 or p|C+1. At a multiple of L no such prime is eligible. Moreover a LOW selection cannot jump strictly across the next multiple K of L: writing C=K-h with 0<h<p-1, eligibility would require h=1 or h=p-1 modulo p, and h is even. There is no even h strictly between 0 and p-1 satisfying this. Thus C+p-1<=K whenever a LOW step could reach K. A maximal consecutive LOW block cannot cross a barrier; its gain is <L, including the possible endpoint at K. Its actual total wait is at most three times its gain.

For each original vertex v=(t,C) define its anchor a(v)=(t_a,D) as the poststate of the last completed p>y selection, or its own initial state if no such selection has occurred. Write q(v)=p or 0 respectively. Then
    0<=C-D<L, even, 0<=t-t_a<=3(C-D).
A positive anchor label q divides D or D+2. Each root's vertices are partitioned into disjoint anchor blocks: one initial block, then a new block at each completed non-LOW event, even if the final block consists only of a terminal snapshot. The anchor block contains its anchor and the subsequent original vertices reached using only LOW events, but not the poststate of the next non-LOW event. The partition is frozen from full actual histories.

At a pair's first common projected horizon C*, take its two anchors (D,p),(E,q). They have |D-E|<L. They cannot be the same compressed key: if their common anchor horizon is <C*, this was an earlier shared horizon; if it equals C* and their common positive label is the same, the pre-horizon is shared earlier; and a zero anchor at the same horizon specifies the same initial root. This is the existing rare-contact compression applied to the whole-pair allocation, not a claim that an anchor specifies its ancestor mass or physical time.

## 2. Compressed first-contact key quotas for ALL non-LOW labels

Use the preceding global quota bases
    A_med=NT/(X u^.25), A_high=F/(X u^.25).
Write A for either. Set Y=max(5,ceil(B^.125)), h1(p)=1 if p>=Y else ell; h2(p,q)=1 if both >=Y else ell². As before, sum 1/(p h1(p))=O(1), and sum over distinct primes 1/(pq h2(p,q))=O(1), from elementary prime reciprocal estimates. All prime sums have p<=B.

Each pair receives ONE new compressed key determined at C*. If one anchor is initial, use a root key indexed by that source M (smaller if both initial), with quota A X/N. There are at most N such keys, including root-to-LOW contacts not occurring at the root's own initial horizon.

Otherwise for distinct p,q>y order anchors by increasing D, breaking ties by label. Key is the ordered pair of anchors, WITHOUT C*. For fixed labels and an even offset E-D of absolute size <L, CRT gives O(X/(pq)) possible anchor pairs in the horizon range [2X-2,7X], after at most four side choices. The count includes the additive one since pq<=B²=O(X). Sum offsets: O(X L/(pq)). Give each key quota A/(L h2(p,q)). The full quota sum is O(X A). This gains a factor L in the number of anchor pairs over a count that additionally records the independent contact offsets at both anchors; no extra multiplicity for first-contact horizons is needed, since each original pair is assigned just once to its compressed key. This extends the earlier close-large-anchor arithmetic to arbitrary labels and fractional pair costs.

For p=q>y, anchors have distinct D,E, and their nonzero difference is 0 or ±2 modulo p. There are O(1+L/p) possible differences and O(X/p) horizons per difference. Hence the support is O((X/p)(1+L/p)), and quota A/[(1+L/p) h1(p)] again sums to O(X A). For p>L+2, necessarily |D-E|=2, opposite divisibility sides. Their actual posttime difference is at least p-2 by the two parity clock progressions. Since their LOW waits are each in [0,3L), the arrival time difference at C* is >=p-2-3L. No synchronous first contact for p>3L+2 (with p>L+2); this does not preclude a later physical merger.

All three compressed quotas together are O(X A), without counting possible C* separately. They apply to the whole actual remaining pair weights, including never-physically-meeting pairs. The additional payment is O(NT/u^.25) medium or O(F/u^.25) high.

## 3. Gain-weighted per-source anchor capacity

A different family works directly with actual source-anchor blocks, not relaxed arithmetic anchor keys. For original root M and one of its blocks a define its weight
    g(M,a)=q-1 if the anchor follows a non-LOW selection q,
           B if initial.
Each noninitial anchor corresponds injectively to its own completed non-LOW selection. Therefore
    sum_(M,a) g(M,a) <= sum_M G_M + NB <= N(H+B).       (1)
This is a deterministic bound on original membership with multiplicity over source tags, not on the number of distinct projected anchors. In particular a heavy anchor with 1000 original sources has 1000 individually charged source-blocks, not just one.

Give each such key (M,D) quota
    b(M,D)=A X/(NH) * g(M,a).                         (2)
The total capacity across all actual source-anchor blocks is <=AX(1+B/H)=O(XA). Each original pair can allocate to EITHER of its two source-anchor keys at the first projected contact, as well as to the compressed key, projected key and optional physical key of d58653cc. These are additional fixed capacities independent of the eventual charged E, actual residual weights, or prior payment choices. No later contacts create new capacities for the same pair. The original source-anchor blocks of one source cannot repeat D.

In particular if all pair weights assigned to a given source-anchor endpoint sum to <=its quota, they are paid in full regardless of the other side's ancestry. A whole-high-pair criterion is that at most floor(b(M,D)/(3H)) distinct partners are assigned to it, since every pair's full weight <=3H. For a large q comparable to B this count is of order u^(7/4) in the asymptotic high ledger: b/(3H) is of order q B u^1.5/H. There is no useful uniform positive integer cap for all small q. Fractional capacities remain valid even below one. This is a sufficient partner-count criterion, not a bound on the actual number of first contacts. The finite implementation uses capacities themselves, not that coarse worst-case partner-count threshold.

For any subset of blocks one can use its actual summed gain in (1) rather than a worst-case support count. A useful consequence of the joint cut formula is that every unpaid pair is incident to TWO source-anchor keys in the maximizing cut; a large projected or physical group alone need not be an obstruction. The new necessary cut conditions on actual source membership go beyond counts of arithmetic candidate states. The entire family of additional capacities still costs o(NT) and o(F), respectively.

## 4. Extended cut theorem

Use the exact allocation network of d58653cc: each pair has one projected key, optional physical key, one compressed projected key and two source-anchor keys. At most five keys per pair, and no duplicates across key families. Aggregate actual pairs with the same eligible key set. For metric w, let K be all actual queried keys with capacities b_k. The optimal residual is exactly
    U=max_{S subset K} [W(S)-sum_(k in S) b_k],
    W(S)=sum_(pairs e with ALL eligible keys in S) w_e.
The proof is unchanged, since an intersection node's outgoing edges to every eligible key have capacity greater than the total input. A larger set of eligible keys cannot increase the optimal total residual, since the previous flow remains feasible. Each payment is charged to one capacity; no double subtraction. Medium and high flows are separate. All old and new families together have O(XA) capacity. The earlier relative error terms remain relative on the final retained incidence energy, not absolute estimates for energy of linear-deleted tags.

## 5. Local LOW masks give an explicit cut envelope in terms of actual ancestor sets

At each projected anchor alpha=(D,p) let its full original membership be the roots whose actual block begins there. For a tag M in the block let V_alpha(M) be the subset of even offsets in [0,L) visited by the block; equivalently a bit mask indexed by half-offset. This is a compact description of the original LOW portion only, not a guessed trajectory or a mask on the residual graph.

For a pair of distinct anchors alpha=(D,p), beta=(E,q), translate the masks to the common base min(D,E). If their intersection is empty, those two blocks cannot contain the pair's first projected contact. Otherwise let C0 be the least common local horizon. A necessary condition for the pair's actual first contact to belong to this anchor pair is C*=C0. If D<E, the later-anchor source cannot have visited D earlier, because then D would already be common. If E<D exchange the roles. If D=E both blocks start at the common horizon. These are necessary filters, not sufficient global first-contact conditions: the paths may have shared an earlier horizon outside both LOW blocks.

At C0 the original incoming labels are fully determined by the masks. If C0 equals that source's anchor D, its incoming label is p (possibly 0). Otherwise the previous visited bit in its mask gives the preceding horizon C1 and the actual incoming prime equals C0-C1+1. Both positive labels must differ for a global first contact. If one label is zero, the projected key is the corresponding root key. Thus the old projected key is reconstructed from these local masks; one need not record it as an independent combinatorial choice.

For an arbitrary subset S of the extended keys, take each compressed anchor pair and each pair of actual source-block members satisfying:
  (a) different sources, nonempty translated mask intersection, and earlier-anchor exclusion;
  (b) reconstructed incoming labels distinct (root sentinel included);
  (c) the reconstructed projected key, compressed key, and BOTH actual source-anchor keys belong to S.
For medium additionally require same fixed original source cell, as required by the existing medium charge ledger. Ignore the optional physical-key restriction, all earlier pair masks, and any earlier common horizon not detected by (a). This can only enlarge the candidate set. For root compressed keys, enumerate actual anchor pairs represented by that key, not just one arbitrary pair of anchors. Distinct anchor pairs are counted once per unordered pair of projected keys; overcount of the same original pair in different candidate products is harmless. Let P_med(S) count the resulting medium candidates with multiplicity. For high let R_M(C0) be its full natural I tail from C0 inclusive, defined on that actual vertex. Then
    W_med(S) <= O(H/m_g) P_med(S),
    W_high(S) <= sum_candidates 2 min(R_M(C0), R_M'(C0)).
The medium constant comes from the inherited actual endpoint charge bound and disjoint focal attached blocks, independently in both directions; this is not a finite diagnostic constant. The high bound follows by strictly increasing original horizons and the full natural tail bound, even if times differ or they recontact. Every actual internal pair appears among these candidates, proved by its first projected contact and its fixed original LOW blocks. The sum remains valid for arbitrary earlier accounting holes.

This formula allows further arithmetic/mass work directly on full source sets at non-LOW anchors. It can also be evaluated by grouping the source-block members by actual masks and by their required key membership in S. For D<E first filter the later group by not visiting D, separately for each anchor pair. For fixed mask pair, C0 and both labels are determined (root anchor horizons determine their roots when q=0). Partition additionally by source cell for medium. The count is a sum of products of the resulting group sizes, excluding identical sources if present. The high sum of minima can equivalently be evaluated via a one-dimensional tail layer integral of these filtered group-size products (or sorted tail lists), with diagonal exclusions for common source tags. This is an exact upper envelope on the candidate products, not a bound on all the group sizes. The finite audit enumerates products explicitly and does not implement the aggregated layer version.

The sufficient remaining targets after all earlier reductions are still U_med=o(NT), U_high=o(F) on the same unbounded dyadic sequence. A stronger, potentially useful condition is to bound the maximum excess using the explicit upper envelopes above. A large upper envelope alone is not a lower bound on U; in particular the new formula does not replace actual cut weights by equal candidate weights.

## 7. Next target

The new cut constraints require actual source-block keys on both sides in addition to the arithmetic contact keys; the source-block weights are tied to real selected gain. The remaining high cut includes many specific original ancestor members of non-LOW anchors. Investigate bounds on their actual membership products and natural tail distributions, possibly exploiting several consecutive non-LOW anchors and the source-cell localization. The local LOW masks alone do not exclude earlier common horizons outside the blocks or bound deep ancestry. A useful next theorem would control the full tail products on cutsets using actual earlier ancestry rather than merely adding new small quota families. Joint medium and high little-o estimates and twin-prime infinitude remain OPEN. Main and the independent short-clock branch unchanged.
