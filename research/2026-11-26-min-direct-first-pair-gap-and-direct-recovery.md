# First-pair label separation and direct causal recovery at all label scales

Continuation of [568cfa4](https://github.com/tara-sooo/A166944-Lean/blob/568cfa484c91708e7e9196731f7a8566b9deda52/research/2026-11-26-min-direct-bounded-word-crt-and-repeat-supply.md). The preceding full-word CRT sieve required a long progression in the horizon. Here two complementary counts avoid that restriction: sieve the two labels themselves for near-equal first pairs, or sieve the initial cofactor of a fixed source when the first successor has an extended clock. In the latter case a sparse source support and sufficiently small aggregate low relay force direct causal recovery already at the first high successor. These deductions hold even when T is comparable to sqrt X. They do not exclude arbitrary all-distinct words.

## 1. First-pair setup

Fix lambda>1 and an actual contiguous canonical block with pre-horizons in [X,Y], Y=lambda X, G=O_lambda(X), C=floor sqrt(Y+1). Let T->infinity, T<=C. Consider a collection of physical words from 1b10c9f or any actual words with high-list length 2..J (fixed J), every high label in [T,KT] (fixed K), and intervening events of label <T. Assume the initial high event s is upward. The next high event t has label r, source label p, source sign e, target sign f. Set E=total strictly intervening low gain; delta=H_t-H_s=p-1+E. We count first pairs, with weights at most K_w T per word, K_w fixed. A fixed actual first pair can occur in at most J words of this form (the later high-prefix length determines the word). For disjoint words there is no such multiplicity. No condition on the final target of the original word is needed in sections 2-3.

## 2. Sieve the first two labels, not the horizon progression

Suppose p!=r. Fix E and the two signs. The conditions H_s=e mod p, H_s+delta=f mod r give one residue modulo pr. Over [X,Y] the number of possible H_s is <=1+(Y-X)/(pr)=O_lambda,K(X/T^2), since T^2<=Y+1. This estimate does NOT require X/(pr) to grow. Each H_s fixes at most one physical starting event and one next high event on this trajectory. For integer A>=1, consider 0<|p-r|<=A. For each positive difference a<=min(A,(K-1)T), sieve the smaller prime in a translated interval padded to length ceil(KT)+2: the two forms are u,u+a, and the elementary shifted two-root estimate of global-sieve gives O_K(T J(a)/log^2 T) pairs, with J(a)=prod_(odd prime l|a) l/(l-2). Indeed the sieve cutoff floor(N^(1/12)) is below T. The earlier mean sum_(a<=A)J(a)<=e A yields O_K(T A/log^2 T) ordered prime pairs, including both orientations. This is an upper bound even when a has an inadmissible parity.

Consequently the total word weight for p!=r, |p-r|<=A, E<=B, B>=0 integer, satisfies
    F_gap(A,B) << X A(B+1)/log^2 T.                    (1)
Proof: multiply the prime-pair count by the CRT count, four signs, B+1 possibilities for E, at most J word choices, and weight K_w T. Constants fixed. No source roughness is used; the bound also holds for non-upward sources. It is uniform for all T<=C, including square-root-scale labels, with no long CRT progression requirement. It counts all-distinct words as a special case, and words whose first pair differs even if there is a later repeat.

Let a disjoint collection have total weight F>=rho X, rho>0 fixed, and distinct first pair in every word. Define the weighted means
    a_bar = sum W |p-r|/F,    b_bar = sum W E/F.
Take A=ceil(4 a_bar), B=ceil(4 b_bar); by Markov, at least F/2 weight meets both cutoffs. Since a_bar>=1, (1) gives
    (1+a_bar)(1+b_bar) >>_(fixed parameters,rho) log^2 T. (2)
Also b_bar<=K_w T G_low/F, where G_low is the gain of ALL physical p<T events on the block (disjointness of the words). Thus G_low=O(X/T) forces a_bar=Omega(log^2 T), whereas bounded a_bar forces G_low=Omega(X log^2 T/T). These are actual tradeoffs between label separation and relay, not claims that typical normalized labels differ by a fixed fraction of T. The preceding bounded-word construction has distinct first pair for sufficiently large T because its total low relay is o(T): a same-p return needs pre-horizon difference >=2p-2, hence E>=p-1. This count handles the first pair of that population without requiring it to be all-distinct.

## 3. Two-plateau roughness at the first successor

Now retain upward sources p in [T,KT] and first high successor r in [T,KT] with wait w_t>=T^alpha for a fixed 0<alpha<1. The source wait w_s>(p-1)/2. A competitor prime dividing either neighbor of a plateau fires within 2l ticks if eligible. Hence both pairs of neighbors H_s+-1,H_t+-1 avoid all odd primes l<min(w_s,w_t)/2, for large T (the cutoff is below T and eligibility is automatic). For fixed p,e and E, write H_s=pq+e, delta=p-1+E. The four forms in q are
    q, pq+2e, pq+e+delta-1, pq+e+delta+1.
The first q is rough because H_s-e=pq. For l>4,l!=p, the four roots are 0,-2e/p,-(e+delta-1)/p,-(e+delta+1)/p; a collision occurs if and only if l divides
    D(delta)=delta(delta-2)(delta+2).
Indeed the within-plateau root differences are +/-2/p and the four cross differences correspond to delta,delta+2,delta-2,delta. For T large delta>=T-1>2, so D is nonzero. Define J_4(D)=prod_(l|D,l>4 prime,l!=p)(1-4/l)^-1. For fixed p in the band and integer B with (log T)^a<=B+1<=T^d for fixed a,d>0,
    sum_(E=0)^B J_4(D(p-1+E)) <<_(a,d,K) B+1.         (3)
Here is the adaptation of the averaged upward-return sieve cb54101. Set V=B+1, y=V^eta for sufficiently small fixed eta. D=O(T^O(1)); factors above log T have bounded product, and those in (y,log T] have bounded product by Mertens, since log y>=eta a loglog T. In the small-prime part expand the factors with squarefree weights W(d)=prod_(l|d)4/(l-4). Normalized inclusion probabilities are 4/l, so weighted mean log d <=O(log y)<=log V/2. Markov bounds the full small product by twice its expansion restricted to d<=V. Each prime l>4 has at most 3 roots of D(p-1+E) as a polynomial in E, so CRT bounds the number in the interval by 2V*3^omega(d)/d. Summing yields <=4V prod_(l>4)(1+12/(l(l-4)))=O(V). This proves (3); no bounded-V uniform mean is claimed.

The q interval is padded to N=ceil(lambda X/p)+2, with N>>T since p<=KT and T^2<=Y+1. Apply the four-dimensional sieve of repeated-cofactor-cluster-sieve with cutoff z=floor(min(T^alpha,N)^(1/12)). Then z<T, z<min(w_s,w_t)/2 eventually, log z>>_alpha log T, z^6<=sqrt N. The count at fixed p,e,E is O(N J_4(D)/log^4 T), with the sieve error absorbed since N>>T. Both endpoints' roughness is used, but no assumption that the successor cofactor is prime or that its label is fixed. We do not introduce a CRT progression in r. Thus this estimate works near sqrt X, where the earlier full-word CRT sieve does not.

Let S count distinct source primes in the word collection. Weight <=K_w T, sum E by (3), and account for the bounded prefix multiplicity. For (log T)^a<=B+1<=T^d,
    F_extended(B) << X S (B+1)/log^4 T.                (4)
This bound applies to all words whose first high successor has w_t>=T^alpha and whose FIRST low relay is <=B. The assumption of sparse source support is needed for the application below, not for (4). A short first successor does not satisfy the four-root condition and is not counted by (4).

## 4. Force direct recovery rather than a longer word

Suppose the words are event-disjoint, total weight F>=rho X, and all sources have ordinary rise u_s=d'_s-d_s>=eta(p-1), fixed eta>0. Assume
    S=O(log^2 T),     G_low=o(X log^2 T/T).
These are additional global support and low-gain hypotheses, not implied by the previous local weighted-support lower bound or merely G_low=o(X). Let g_T=T G_low/X, and choose
    B=ceil(max((log T)^(1/2),sqrt(g_T log^2 T))).
Then B+1=o(log^2 T), B+1>=(log T)^(1/2), B=o(T), g_T/(B+1)=o(1). The weight discarded for E>B is <=K_w T G_low/(B+1)=o(X). The extended successor weight with E<=B is o(X) by (4). Thus all but o(X) of F has first high successor wait <T^alpha and first relay <=B. Such a first pair has distinct labels, since B<p-1 for large T. Its target ordinary drop v_t=r-1-2w_t is >=(1-o(1))r.

There is an actual causal matching of large weight between these FIRST two high events, even if the original word ended much later. Indeed put R_low=sum_(s<i<t)|d'_i-d_i|<=5E, since |2w_i-g_i|<=5g_i for odd p_i>=3. Then |d_t-d'_s|<=R_low. The overlap of the source upward segment (d_s,d'_s) with the target downward segment (d'_t,d_t) is at least min(u_s,v_t)-R_low: the latter segment is a downward interval of length v_t starting at d_t, within R_low of d'_s. For causal matching remove levels on which an intervening downward crossing already consumed the source. Their total measure is <=R_low, by summing intervening negative variations. Hence the full height-axis causal weight between s and t satisfies
    W_direct(s,t)>=max(0,min(u_s,v_t)-2 R_low)
                  >=max(0,min(u_s,v_t)-10 E).         (5)
The matching here may be outside the original selected guard; it is the actual source-target overlap and does not claim to preserve the same individual matched levels of the longer word. For the retained words u_s>=eta(T-1), v_t>=T-1-2T^alpha, E=o(T), so W_direct>=c_eta T for large T with fixed c_eta>0. There are Omega(X/T) retained words because original weight <=K_w T; their first-pair subwords remain disjoint. Therefore there is a disjoint collection of Omega(X/T) direct high up-to-down pairs, with total causal weight Omega(X), negligible low relay and target wait <T^alpha. This reduces the bounded-word problem to just two major events in the stated near-minimal GLOBAL source-support, small-relay regime, at any scale T<=C including T~sqrt X. No universal upper bound on these fast direct pairs is proved. If the support or relay conditions fail, the corresponding branches remain. In particular (1)-(2) give a necessary label gap/relay tradeoff for these distinct pairs, not a contradiction.

## 5. Limitations and diagnostics

This work deliberately counts first pairs rather than every word CRT modulus, to handle near-square-root labels. It does not eliminate all-distinct words generally, nor does the four-root sieve apply to a fast successor. General small/middle crossing and macro upward supply are unresolved, as is the forcing of the earlier narrow guard regime. Next target the actual phase constraints for the direct fast high successor (especially when E is very small), or remove the sparse-support condition. No twin-infinitude contradiction or Lean kernel verification.
