# Asymmetric medium-prime guard and a duration-only quiet-gap criterion

Original elementary probability deductions, exact pathwise checks in finite shared-CRT samples. No Lean compilation. Neither the clipped-height tail criterion nor the independent physical far-coalescence upper bound is proved.

## 1. Separate two revelation filtrations

Keep the common CRT probability space, relative time and horizon, and least-matching-prime rule of the previous ghost notes. Let U be a positive integer time horizon. Choose two cutoffs 11<=P<z, with P^2>=4U, and integer 1<=v<z-2, v<=U. We call labels >=z high and labels in [P,z) medium. A fresh high event is a first selection of some q>=z. We will prove a guard against ALL labels >=P just after such fresh high events, including both medium and high labels.

For the proof of this guard use the filtration revealing only coordinates of p<P at time zero, common parity, and the selected tick labels including 0 through each tick. Coordinates of an unselected p>=P are NOT revealed. The exact conditional-history law then gives the conditional probability of selecting an unused p at tick s<=U+v as <=1/(p^2-s+1)<=2/p^2. In particular the conditional fresh-high hazard is O(1/(z log z)), and the conditional hazard for any unused p>=P is O(1/(P log P)). These inequalities hold irrespective of prior repeats. The preceding single-prime deletion bound also gives, for p>=P, Pr(p selected by U)<=U/p^2.

Later, for the terminal support calculation and the quiet-gap tests, use a DIFFERENT filtration, revealing all p<z at time zero. This makes the counterfactual LOW suffix after each candidate q history-measurable. The medium hazard estimate need not hold after that larger revelation. The probability bounds are proved under the appropriate filtration separately, then combined on the common original probability space by a union bound. We never use the unused-medium hazard conditional on revealed medium coordinates.

## 2. An asymmetric guard against medium and high events

For an already selected p>=P, its two possible clock classes mod2p are known in the first filtration. Irrespective of horizon eligibility, the number of integer starts s in [1,U] for which one of its clocks occurs in (s,s+v] is O(v(1+U/p)). Count only starts after the first selection of p. Pathwise this number is at most O(v(1+U/p)) times the indicator that p has been selected by U. The corresponding indicator at s is known before tick s.

Multiply by the fresh HIGH hazard O(1/(z log z)), and sum over ticks and p>=P. The expectation of the number of fresh high events followed within v ticks by a possible clock of a previously selected p>=P is bounded by
    O(v/(z log z) * sum_(p>=P prime) (1+U/p) U/p^2)
      = O(v U/(z P log z log P)
           + v U^2/(z P^2 log z log P)).
The prime sums are bounded by partial summation of pi(x)=O(x/log x). This counts possible clocks whether or not their horizon residues match. The new q itself has no possible clock in (s,s+v] because its two clock classes have minimum separation q-2>=z-2>v.

After any fresh high event at a history stopping time s<=U, the conditional probability of selecting a label >=P that is still unused at its selection, within the next v ticks, is O(v/(P log P)). Apply the unused-label hazard successively up to the first such selection; other selected labels may occur in between. The expected number of fresh high selections by U is O(U/(z log z)). Summing over their stopping times bounds the probability of any such unused-label interference by
    O(v U/(z P log z log P)).
We can add O(v/(P log P)) if we also want this guard from time zero, but it is unnecessary for the delayed-initial-state theorem below.

Consequently, outside an event of probability
    E_guard = O(v U/(z P log z log P)
               + v U^2/(z P^2 log z log P)),
NO label >=P is selected in the next v ticks after ANY actual fresh high event through U. This includes repeated medium labels, repeated high labels, newly selected medium labels and newly selected high labels. It is an unconditional all-depth statement for the whole time window, not a probability conditional on a given fresh event with all medium coordinates known. The same estimate works in arbitrary finite upper alphabets. It is asymmetric because the arrival hazard uses z and the possible subsequent event labels use the smaller P.

## 3. Remove both the medium-count and terminal-spread restrictions

For an actual fresh high event at time s, consider the LOW-only ghost with labels <z from its post-state. On the good guard event its event record through s+v agrees with the full process, since no high >=z event is selected there. The LOW-only record also agrees with the <P ghost record through s+v, since no medium label is selected. Frozen tails are included in these comparisons. In particular, IF the <z suffix terminates within v, all of its events use p<P, so its total even gain d is <=v(P-1).

This implication is one way. Termination of the <P continuation within v does NOT imply termination of the <z continuation: a medium label might be eligible at its terminal horizon and have its next clock later than v. We retain the lifetime condition for <z itself.

Switch to the filtration revealing p<z, in order to count terminal reactivation at an actual fresh high selection. Before tick s, for each unused q>=z consider its hypothetical <z suffix from the post-state (s,g+q-1). Restrict to those which terminate within v and select no p>=P. Regardless of the number or order of events, its terminal offset belongs to a set of at most
    M = 1+floor(v(P-1)/2)
distinct even integers. This is a deterministic bound for the full counterfactual RESTRICTED support, finite or infinite, not a bound on the unrestricted support. The guard just proved ensures that every actual fresh event with <z suffix lifetime <=v lies in this restricted subset except on the single global E_guard exception.

Apply the terminal congruence calculation of the previous note in the second filtration. For each previously selected high p>=z, each possible d, and each sign, the fresh q must lie in one residue class mod p to reactivate p at the LOW terminal. Sum q^-2 over that class by the integer progression bound O(1/z^2). For reactivation of q itself, q divides one of d-1,d+1,d-3; they are nonzero odd integers of size <=v(P-1)+3<z^2 under these parameters, so there are at most O(1) prime candidates >=z per d. Conditional selection probability is <=2/q^2. The total bound over all ticks and histories with a restricted suffix is therefore
    E_term = O((1+vP) U/z^2
              + (1+vP) U^2/(z^3 log z)).
Use E[number of distinct previously selected high labels at s]=O(s/(z log z)). This is a direct application of the earlier terminal-support proof with a different restricted subset; no separate support-concentration event is required.

Combining E_guard and E_term, with probability at least 1-O(E_guard+E_term) EVERY fresh high event through U whose LOW-only (<z) suffix terminates within v is followed by that actual suffix, with no medium/high selection in its interior and no previously selected high (including its own q) eligible at its terminal. Since no label <z is eligible at this terminal, it is a quiet-gap opportunity for the conditional-history law.

The proof does not require any bound on the number of events p<P on the suffix. The preceding clock-ticket subset-sum result required a restriction on the medium-event count (with P potentially logarithmic), or a separate bound on the full terminal offset spread. The new result trades a power-size P and a shorter time cap for unconditional absence of medium selections in short suffixes. It does not claim a larger time cap than every earlier conditional result, and does not estimate the probability that a given <z suffix is short.

## 4. Explicit admissible scales

Let z=ceil(U^theta), P=ceil(U^alpha), v=floor(U^rho). Choose fixed 6/7<theta<1, alpha=2theta/3, and
    0<rho<7theta/3-2.
Then alpha>1/2, alpha<theta, v<z-2 eventually and P^2>=4U. The dominant guard power is rho+2-theta-2alpha, and the dominant terminal power is rho+alpha+2-3theta. Both equal rho+2-7theta/3<0. The other powers are smaller: in the guard compare 1-theta-alpha with 2-theta-2alpha, and in the terminal compare 1-2theta with 2-3theta, with theta,alpha<1. Terms without vP also vanish. Thus E_guard+E_term=o(1). For example theta=.96, alpha=.64, rho=.22 give both dominant powers -.02. As theta approaches 1 from below, admissible rho can approach 1/3 from below. These are convenient sufficient choices, not optimized lower bounds on required suffix duration.

## 5. Delay the initial opportunity: a duration-only reduction

Return to Y->infinity, T=floor(Y sqrt(log Y)), w=2Y, U=T+w, and the above admissible cutoffs z,P,v defined using U. Eventually z<Y, v=o(Y), and log z/log(2w)->theta. Let t0=floor(T/2). We need not require the INITIAL <z continuation to finish in the short time v used after fresh high events.

Define E0 to be the event that the initial <z ghost has lifetime >t0 AND the full process has no fresh high selection by t0. Before the first high event the full process agrees with the initial <z ghost. On the complement of E0, either the first high event happens by t0 (it is fresh), or the initial <z ghost terminates by t0 without any prior high event. In the latter case it yields a quiet-gap opportunity, because all previously selected primes are <z and now ineligible. In the former case, if that first fresh high event has a suffix lifetime <=v, the good guard and terminal events imply a quiet-gap opportunity by t0+v. This also covers the case when the initial ghost terminates earlier and the first high event occurs later.

Let E_long mean that SOME actual fresh high event through T has a <z suffix lifetime >v. On the complement of E0, E_long and the two unconditional exceptions, start quiet-gap tests at the first opportunity no later than t0+v. At any such terminal, with the second filtration, all known labels are ineligible. Unused p>=z have at most s excluded pairs by the exact history law. The earlier Mertens product estimate gives a conditional probability >=c_theta>0 of no event for the next w ticks. A failed test ends at its first event within w, which must be a fresh high. Its suffix finishes in <=v and yields another opportunity. These are stopping-time tests; their probabilities are conditioned ONLY on the revealed history, not on absence of the exceptional events. On sigma_Y>T, no test starting by T can succeed. Hence at least m=floor((T-t0-v)/(w+v)) tests start by T and fail, for sufficiently large Y. Here m->infinity. Induction of the conditional failure probabilities gives
    Pr(sigma_Y>T)
      <= Pr(E0)+Pr(E_long)+O(E_guard+E_term)+(1-c_theta)^m
       = Pr(E0)+Pr(E_long)+o(1).
In particular Pr(E0)<=Pr(tau_z>floor(T/2)). The sharper E0 itself can be written exactly by conditioning on the lower trace through t0 and using the earlier forbidden-pair insertion product: multiply the indicator of tau_z>t0 by the conditional probability of no high insertion through t0, then average. This is a duration-only criterion with no medium-count, full support-spread, or short initial suffix condition. If both residuals tend to zero along a diverging sequence, the finite-time cutoff coupling and clipped-height theorem delete small physical cell terminal classes. The remaining physical far-coalescence upper estimate is still independently required. Neither duration residual is bounded sufficiently here.

## 6. Optional weaker occupation criterion, allowing long fresh suffixes

The union over ALL long fresh suffixes in section5 is sufficient but not necessary. Here is a second useful exact reduction that can tolerate a few long suffixes. It requires a separate repeat-occupation estimate; we do not assert repeats are already negligible. Keep the two unconditional good events from sections2-3, with the same parameters.

Partition the full path's elapsed time interval [0,T] into an initial interval up to its first high event and the intervals beginning at each high event and ending at the next high event, truncated at T. Boundary time points are counted with zero length. Call a high-start interval GOOD if its high event is fresh and its <z suffix has lifetime <=v. Other high-start intervals are BAD (repeat high, or fresh with long suffix). Let B_T be the sum of lengths of BAD high-start intervals, and let I_T be the length of the initial interval. This is a random occupation-time statistic of the full path, including any gaps; no independence is assumed.

On the good guard and terminal events, each GOOD interval whose low terminal is reached by T has an actual quiet-gap opportunity within v of its start. On sigma_Y>T each such interval has length <=v+w: after the LOW terminal the next event must occur within w, is high and fresh, and therefore ends the interval (unless T truncates it sooner). A GOOD interval truncated before its LOW terminal has length <=v, and there is at most one such last interval. Thus if I_T+B_T<=T/2, the number of GOOD intervals of positive length is at least T/(2(v+w)), up to harmless rounding. Apart from at most the last interval, their terminal opportunities occur by T and all tests fail on sigma_Y>T. Test actual opportunities in chronological order, ending a test at its first event or at w quiet ticks. Each of these GOOD-interval terminals can be tested: an earlier test has either succeeded (inconsistent with sigma_Y>T) or ended by an event no later than that terminal. If it is itself a test time, count only once. Each tested opportunity is a history stopping time for the second filtration and has conditional success probability >=c_theta. Consequently, with m2=max(0,floor(T/(2(v+w)))-1),
    Pr(sigma_Y>T)
        <= Pr(I_T+B_T>T/2)+O(E_guard+E_term)+(1-c_theta)^m2.
The right-hand occupation term can be studied instead of Pr(E0)+Pr(E_long); it is not claimed small.

For a fresh high start at s with counterfactual <z lifetime tau_q, on sigma_Y>T the length of its high-start interval is at most min(T-s,tau_q+w). Indeed until the next high event the <z trajectory is followed exactly. If a high event interrupts it, the interval ends earlier; if it reaches its LOW terminal first, either T truncates the interval or the next event must occur within w since no long gap starts by T. This argument does not require the short-suffix guard. Thus the expected BAD fresh-interval occupation on the no-long-gap event is bounded by the following predictable weighted sum:
    E[ 1{sigma_Y>T} sum_(BAD fresh starts) interval length]
       <= O(sum_(s<=T) E sum_(unused q>=z)
                 q^-2 min(T-s,tau_q+w) 1{tau_q>v}).
The counterfactual tau_q is determined by the second filtration. Apply its conditional selection bound before tick s and drop the no-long-gap indicator after the pathwise upper bound. This weighted criterion can ignore extremely unlikely candidates even if their counterfactual lifetime is enormous. A finite high cutoff V=ceil(U) changes paths through U with probability O(1/log U); for probability estimates one may work in that finite model and pay this once. No numerical expectation bound on the displayed weighted sum is obtained here. Repeat BAD-interval occupation and initial occupation also need control. This occupation formulation is an alternative sufficient reduction, not a proof that either earlier residual vanishes.
