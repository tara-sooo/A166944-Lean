## 2. NEW polynomial-width boundary-cell lemma

Fix real D>=1 with 2D<=H-a. For each source M, mark its two relative-window slices [s+a,s+a+D) and [s+H-D,s+H). Let V_i(C) be actual occupation weight of these slices in the original wait at C (scaled by the same incidence multiplier if masked). This can split one wait into marked and unmarked portions. The sum of marked linear costs over every horizon, for one root, is <=D, because total time of slices <=2D. Thus R_V=sum_C r(C) sum_i V_i(C)<=ND.

At fixed C put r=r(C). Partition arrival times t into half-open clock cells I_j=[j(r+D),(j+1)(r+D)) for integer j. Let U_j(C) be full actual occupation mass of tags arriving in this cell, V_j(C) their marked mass. Every root with its first slice meeting a wait in I_j has start time s+a in [j(r+D)-D,(j+1)(r+D)+2r]. Analogously a last-slice contribution implies cutoff s+H in [j(r+D),(j+1)(r+D)+2r+D]. Each such interval length is 3r+2D; each contains at most (3r+2D)/2+2 distinct original odd-source starts or stops. Each marked piece for that endpoint has weight <=min(1,D/(2r)). For r>=3 and D>=1, the product for each endpoint is O(D), uniformly in r/D; e.g. the two combined <=6D. This uses distinct ORIGINAL sources, not counts of canonical origins or raw physical state arrivals. Therefore
    V_j(C)<=6D,
    E_V,cell=sum_C r(C) sum_j V_j(C)^2 <=6D R_V<=6ND². (1)
Also E_cell=sum_C r sum_j U_j²<=E by nonnegativity.

Pay the actual ordered energy products with at least one marked endpoint and with arrival cells identical or adjacent. This includes diagonal contributions, all synchronous pairs and every asynchronous pair with |t_i-t_j|<=r+D (and possibly additional near pairs depending on the grid boundary). No assumption of same selected label, first contact, same canonical arc or relative source distance. The exact paid ledger is sum over ordered cell pairs |j-k|<=1 of
    r[U_j U_k-(U_j-V_j)(U_k-V_k)].
It is <=2 sum_C r sum_{|j-k|<=1} V_j U_k. Each cell has at most three partners; Cauchy on directed cell incidences gives
    E_paid <=6 sqrt(E_V,cell E_cell)
            <=eta E + 9 E_V,cell/eta
            <=eta E + O(XD²/eta),                    (2)
for any eta>0. This is an actual portion of the original energy (not counting all same-cell energy as paid). Far arrival-cell products with marked pieces are not covered.

Take eta=u^-1/4 and, in HIGH, D=B^1/2 u, in MED, D=B^13/16 u, eventually both satisfy 2D<=H-a. Then
    E_paid,H <=eta E_H+O(XB u^9/4),
    E_paid,M <=eta E_M+O(XB^(13/8) u^9/4).
Consequently these classes can be absorbed at the original sufficient scales. One can truncate D to min((H-a)/2,D) at finite B. Unlike the previous source-distance tail budget D_j=B m/[d_j(j+1)^2], this permits a polynomial-width source-relative cutoff slice even for source pairs separated by order B. It only pays pairs with at least one endpoint's OWN relative end slice, and for async only near-clock partners, not arbitrary return-defined tails of both endpoints. The two theorems cover complementary classes; take their union with actual overlap subtraction or priority. In particular all synchronous different-arc products in waits whose pre-time is within D of the earlier source's cutoff are paid by (2), whenever the earlier source has positive weight (its whole occupied wait portion is in its own end slice). This applies without an observed next canonical return. For a long shared nonrenewing episode the surviving sync contribution has both unmarked pieces supported in the interior of each source's evaluation window. There is no bound here on that interior mass. The earlier endpoint slices also cover synchronous same-arc and close asynchronous terms.

## 3. NEW low-deficit occupation strip

Use delta(x,C)=2x-C during the actual waiting interval at C. This is not just the post-event deficit at arrival. For p>=17 and a cap A>=1 and 0<rho<=1/8, define d_p=min(A,rho p). Mark the actual wait portion with x in [C/2,C/2+d_p), equivalently 0<=delta(x,C)<2d_p. Let its weight be L_i(C). For labels p<17 no piece is marked. Since post-state C+p-1<=2v, v>=C/2+(p-1)/2. For a marked wait, t<C/2+d_p and
    v-(C/2+d_p)>=p/4, r(C)>=p/8.
Thus there is a deterministic following subinterval of length p/4 before the selected event. This holds for reset as well as nonreset events, regardless of canonical origins or previous history.

If the original cutoff b_M=s+H>=C/2+d_p+p/4, and marked occupation is positive, the lower slab endpoint is before the strip end. The following length-p/4 interval lies wholly in the selected wait and evaluation window. The marked weight <=4rho times the actual following interval weight, using the same multiplier for masks. Summing these strong pieces gives L_strong(C)<=4rho U(C).

Otherwise cutoff b_M<C/2+d_p+p/4<=v. Since the strip piece is positive, b_M>C/2. Using d_p<=p/8<=r and p/4<=2r, all exceptional cutoffs lie in the ONE interval (C/2,C/2+3r), independent of possible selected p at C. At most 3r/2+2 distinct original tags, each exceptional piece <=A/(2r); hence L_exc(C)<=3A. Moreover these exceptional tags are in their actual cutoff-crossing wait, since b_M<v and their piece before b_M is positive. Each original tag has at most one such wait over its whole prefix, and exceptional marked length<=A. Therefore
    R_exc<=NA/2,
    E_exc=sum r L_exc²<=3A R_exc=O(XA²).
Let L=L_strong+L_exc. Pay full actual projected energy incident to marked low-deficit pieces, without clock-nearness restriction:
    E_low=sum r(2U L-L²)
         <=8rho E+2 sqrt(E E_exc)
         <=(8rho+eta)E+O(XA²/eta).                   (3)
Choose rho=min(1/8,u^-1/4), eta=u^-1/4, A=B^1/2 u for HIGH and B^13/16 u for MED. Then (3) is o(1)E plus an additive O(XB u^9/4) or O(XB^(13/8)u^9/4) respectively. This generalizes the earlier pre-virtual boundary absorption, which was restricted to a constant-width strip. For HIGH every p>T eventually p>=17, so after paying (3), remaining pair products can be formed entirely from time pieces with delta(x,C)>=2 min(A,rho p). For selected p near B the permitted strip width reaches order sqrt(B) log B eventually. For p near T=B^1/4 the width is order p/u^1/4. In MED, selected labels <17 remain unmarked (and eventually absent since z grows). Do not infer that pre-state deficit is large; a wait arriving at small deficit can have unmarked later occupation. There is no restriction on actual raw projected ancestry. This theorem works for far-async as well as sync, unlike (2). If both estimates are used, the total paid union is <=sum of bounds, and the actual remaining energy must use the union, not subtract overlapping pieces twice.

## 4. Nonreset excursions: structural gain constraint

This lemma concerns the actual post-event reset lines delta=6s, s=t mod2, rather than every virtual canonical boundary. For any selected p>=P>=7, if post-state is not reset then phase delta'=6s+2pk with k>=1 (nonnegative deficit excludes k<0 for p>=7). Thus delta'>=2p. A nonreset p>=P whose pre-state deficit<=P has
    2w=delta'-delta+p-1>=3p-P-1>=2p-1,
so integer w>=p and r>=p/2. Call this a low-deficit nonreset launch. If such an event has a previous selected q>=P after the last actual reset (take latest such prior event), the preceding high post-state had deficit>=2q, and all intermediate selected labels <P. Let g be total intervening gain, excluding both high events. Each event can decrease deficit by at most its gain, because elapsed time is nonnegative. Hence
    g>=2q-P>=P.                                      (4)
The intervals charged to distinct repeated launches of one root are disjoint: they are between consecutive high events, with no intervening reset. Summing over original tags gives
    P * #repeat launches <= sum_charged g <= G_<P,
    sum_repeat (p-1) <= (B/P) G_<P,
where G_<P is total original selected gain at labels <P (can include reset events too). This applies even to arbitrary long actual nonreset histories, and does not require distinct physical states across tags. It is a linear relation, not a small-gain estimate on G_<P, nor a bound on future ancestor masses. Any canonical-free shared interval has no actual resets except possibly within its last seven ticks (by exact even-reset bridge), so (4) also applies to its interior high launches with both high events inside the reset-free part. No claim that repeated launches are impossible. Finite audit found none even with lowered P; this is an empirical observation, not proof that the gain bound is always vacuous.

## 6. Remaining

Polynomial-width boundary cell absorption is substantially stronger for cutoff-local synchronous overlaps than a per-root-pair distance cap; the low-deficit strip separates the actual interior high occupation from small-clock-deficit pieces. The large nonrenewing shared-suffix problem is now localized to interior occupation and, for HIGH, large instantaneous deficit pieces. Repeated low-deficit launches after high nonreset events require smaller-label gain; numerical absence merits investigation but is not an invariant proved here. Need actual interior mass bound or a recurrence theorem on nonreset trajectories; far asynchronous boundary products and older residual classes also remain. Main unchanged, twin-prime conjecture OPEN, no Lean compilation.
