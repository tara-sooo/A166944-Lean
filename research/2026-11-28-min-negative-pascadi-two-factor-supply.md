## 2. Exact squarefree-mask lemma (including endpoint restrictions)

Let a fixed finite set of 1-bounded coefficient products alpha_n beta_m represent distinct integers mn in [R,2R] (at most one representation each; a fixed bounded multiplicity also suffices). For an odd positive modulus d define
  Delta(d)=sum alpha_n beta_m [1_(mn=2 mod d) - 1_((mn,d)=1)/phi(d)].
We have |Delta(d)| << R log(2R)/d for d<=D<R, by trivial integer progression count and d/phi(d)<<log(2d). The latter follows also from sum_{e<=d}1/phi(e)<<log(2d). Let lambda be an order-one well-factorable component of level D, in particular it admits a convolution lambda=lambda1*lambda2 with 1-bounded factors supported on d_i<=D_i, D1 D2=D. Suppose the distribution theorem bounds the bilinear remainder with ANY two such independently 1-bounded coefficient sequences by O_A(R/L^A) for every A>0, with this particular choice of D_i, and with the residue a=2 coprime to the moduli. No triple well-factorability assumption.

Let S_z be the set of squarefree integers with all prime factors odd and <z. Restrict each factor independently to S_z. Then the desired sum over d|P(z) of lambda(d)Delta(d) is exactly
  sum_e mu(e) sum_{d1,d2 in S_z, e|d1,e|d2} lambda1(d1)lambda2(d2)Delta(d1d2).
Indeed for squarefree d_i the product belongs to S_z iff gcd(d1,d2)=1. The prime-support restriction is multiplicatively separable; the shared-prime correction is the Mobius identity sum_{e|gcd}mu(e). No assertion that the masked lambda remains well-factorable. No endpoint restriction on d1*d2 beyond D is needed since supports ensure it. Arbitrary independent interval restrictions on factors or prime-support sets are harmless.

Take E=L^K with K fixed. For e<=E, apply the distribution theorem to the independently restricted coefficient sequences (the residue remains 2, not a/e²; the original variables and their dyadic ranges are unchanged). Decompose each into O(L) dyadic intervals if required. The theorem is uniform in coefficients. Sum O(L^(K+2)) estimates with A chosen larger by that fixed amount. For e>E, use
  |tail| << R L sum_{e>E} sum_{d1<=D1:e|d1}1/d1 sum_{d2<=D2:e|d2}1/d2
         << R L³/E.
Thus for any desired A0 choose K>A0+4, then A>A0+K+4. We get O(R/L^A0) on the restricted sum. Exact identity handles squares even when two factors share a SMALL prime, no special disjoint prime boxes or assertions about repeated-prime weights required. Also no need to inspect altered linear-sieve weights from Pascadi section5. For Wu Lemma2.5 one can directly apply its unrestricted well-factorable component estimate, or apply this same argument after splitting where a suitable arbitrary two-factor estimate is available. In fact for Wu at .3 there is an even simpler option: use the ordinary order-one well-factorable weights constructed for the sifting set (as standard lower sieve), or use the Pascadi extension below at the new boxes to establish the .3 exponent independently. Do NOT claim Wu Lemma2.5 bounds an arbitrary fixed pair of restricted factors at the same D: it requires well-factorability for every split. Section3 proves the required fixed-split estimate using Pascadi, so the new theorem is independent of this potential issue in the old proof.

## 3. Pascadi's two-factor slice of Proposition 4.4

Reopened Alexandru Pascadi, On the exponents of distribution of primes and smooth numbers, arXiv:2505.00653v2 (29 Jun 2025), https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.00653. Read printed pp20–23, Def4.3, Prop4.4, (4.15), Prop4.5 and its dyadic proof; inspected page images. Prop4.4 handles independent 1-bounded modulus coefficients in three dyadic ranges under MN~R and
  Q1<=N/R^eps,
  N² Q2 Q3²<=R^(1-15eps),
  N² Q2^5 Q3²<=R^(2-40eps).
alpha_n must be 1-bounded, SW for all coprime progressions including removal of (n,d)>1, and supported on least factor >=R^(1/(log log R)^3); beta_m only 1-bounded. Residue 2 fixed. Take the third sequence to be the unit at 1, with the constant-size dyadic interval containing 1. The proposition's lower bound Q_i >>1 permits positive constant-sized ranges; Prop4.5 proof itself allows Q_i>=1 and decomposes into dyadic ranges including units. Thus this is the two-factor specialization, not Theorem1.3(i) applied to ordinary weights. All dyadic blocks with smaller Q1,Q2 remain in the inequalities, and constant factors are absorbed in fixed power margins. If a convention n~N uses strict lower endpoints, take the unit interval with lower endpoint 1/2; this is still asymptotic size comparable to 1. This is an explicit boundary interpretation to be checked in independent review; no need for nonunit dummy padding.

Fix 0<delta<.001. Let nu=log_R N in (3/8-delta,3/8), D=R^(5/8-delta), D1=N R^(-delta/10), D2=D/D1, eps=delta/100. With Q1<=D1,Q2<=D2,Q3 bounded, the exponent bounds are
  log_R D1 = nu-delta/10,
  2nu+log_R D2 = nu+5/8-9delta/10 <=1-.9delta,
  2nu+5log_R D2 =25/8-3nu-4.5delta <=2-1.5delta.
Compare 15eps=.15delta and 40eps=.4delta. Fixed slack absorbs endpoints and dyadic constants uniformly. In this fixed N window the ordinary TWO-factor splitting of each Iwaniec lower component suffices. No use of full programmable/triply-well-factorable weight conditions, and no transfer of modified upper-bound weights to lower bound. Combine with mask lemma §2. This specifically closes the sieve-weight restriction issue for products with bounded representation multiplicity. It does not automatically treat products with an appended variable prime modulus, arbitrary prime sequence restrictions in the short variable, or every choice of N.

## 4. New density-scale canonical supply below 5/16

  5/16-3delta < log_R P < 5/16-2delta,
  3/8-delta < log_R N < 3/8.
Set V=R/(PN), M=R/N. Take primes p in (P,1.1P], u in (N,1.1N], v in (1.1V,1.2V], with p=1 mod6; no restriction on u except primality. p<v<u with power slack: v exponent 1-nu-log_R P >5/16+2delta, and <5/16+4delta<3/8-delta. The product mn=pvu lies in (1.1R,1.452R]; alpha_u unrestricted prime indicator and beta_m indicator of m=pv in (M,2M]. Unique factorization plus disjoint size ranges gives at most one representation; per box prime count B_box~R/L³ uniformly by PNT (fixed AP for p). SW for alpha_u: for q<=L^C subtract endpoint SW estimates for unrestricted primes, remove primes dividing auxiliary d or q at cost O(omega(d)+omega(q)); for q>L^C use trivial N/q+1 and N/phi(q), choose C sufficiently large from phi(q)>>sqrt(q). Absorb removal of primes dividing d into tau(d)^O(1); rough-support threshold holds since u has fixed polynomial size. All hypotheses of Prop4.4 are satisfied.

Sift m u-2 with odd prime cutoff z=4R^(5/16-2delta), D=R^(5/8-delta). The dimension-one local model is g(d)=1/phi(d) on squarefree odd d. To replace the distribution theorem's coprime main term by B_box/phi(d), account for every factor p,v,u of mn. For a prime ell|mn,
  sum_{d<=D,ell|d,d squarefree}1/phi(d)

Each order-one well-factorable component itself is 1-bounded (split D*1); fixed many components. Thus coprime correction O(B_box L R^(-5/16+3delta))=o(R/L^4), whether or not a prime factor lies below z. The remainder is o(R/L^4), main >>R/L^4. At least this many sifted products per box. ## 6. Scope of ceiling and remaining direction

With exponent variables nu,a,b,c (a,b,c for Q1,Q2,Q3) and no eps slack, (4.15) implies a<=nu, 2nu+b+2c<=1, 2nu+5b+2c<=2. Multiply the latter by 3 and 1, add: 8nu+8b+8c<=5. Therefore a+b+c<=5/8. The current slice attains the limiting point (nu,a,b,c)=(3/8,3/8,1/4,0). Combining ordinary lower sieve s>2 with this distribution mechanism cannot reach sigma>=5/16. This is only a ceiling of this sufficient system and simple lower sieve, not a general impossibility or a ceiling on switching/double sieve. The new 5/16 is a useful checkpoint for Astra; next higher exponent will require a substantive switching/subtraction method, stronger distribution, or different dynamic transport.
