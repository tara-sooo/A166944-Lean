## 5. Enlarge absolute thin-payment thresholds and sufficient tests

First apply all old sector and earlier paid destination key masks on the identical frozen measures. Their combined payment O(NT/ell²) by pointwise domination is o(tau NT). Next use graded h(r)=log²(2+r)/(1+log log(2+r))²; at fixed eligible p, sum_C h(r)=O(X/p) by earlier two-form support and layer integration. Destination p>y0. Pay the entire new sector charge when m_eff,s'=Dbar_s'/(2 C_s')<=
    H tau² h(r)/(C_s' u ell).
Its cost per sector <=O(XH tau²/(u ell) sum_p 1/p)=O(tau² NT). For p>=sqrt P_min the reciprocal sum O(1) allows replacing ell by1; for rough r>=(p-1)/log(2+p) replace h(r) by log²(2+p) using the earlier rough support. Union only a fixed number of masks. After multiplication by 1/tau these payments cost O(tau NT)=o(NT). At fixed p,r the new ABSOLUTE charge thresholds are larger by tau² ell² than the old thresholds of ba359c9; this is independent of the additional capacity gain in the effective mass. All final measures D_s,rem' are pointwise <=old respective residuals because the old masks were applied first. W_massive' uses same old physical cutoff and only further deletes original waits, so <=old massive clock.

Under finitely many twins the total paid actual wait is o(NT) and original deficit identity gives
    NT/2-o(NT) <= W_massive' + [(3+o(1))/tau](D_l,rem'+D_h,rem').
Thus for tau=1/log ell the sufficient condition is
    liminf_(dyadic X) [W_massive'+3 log(ell)(D_l,rem'+D_h,rem')]/(NT)<1/2. (2)
For any family tau>=1/log ell, tau->0 replace 3 log ell by 3/tau. On a subsequence with strict margin the normalized objective is bounded, so the asymptotic coefficient error is harmless. This is logically implied by the preceding 8 ell criterion on the same ensemble, not just a reparametrization. No strict separation on realized ensembles proved.

There is a useful RATE-FREE corollary for the *preceding* ba359c9 residual measures. Write d_old(X)=(D_l,rem^old+D_h,rem^old)/(NT). Suppose on the same unbounded dyadic subsequence d_old->0 and liminf W_massive^old/(NT)<1/2. Restrict further to the constant-margin subsequence for the massive bound. Choose
    tau(X)=max(1/log ell, sqrt(d_old(X)))
there; it tends to zero and meets the parameter regime. One can set tau=1/log ell outside the subsequence. This is an existence proof of a sufficient adaptive family, not a claim that d_old actually vanishes. By pointwise comparison, (D_l,rem'+D_h,rem')/(tau NT)<=d_old/tau<=sqrt(d_old)->0 (zero if d_old=0). Then (1) and the payments contradict finitely many twins. Therefore it is sufficient to prove ONLY d_old=o(1) on a subsequence with the strict massive bound, with NO specified logarithmic rate. The old sufficient subroute required d_old=o(1/ell). The choice tau may be defined everywhere by clamping sqrt(d_old) below a vanishing upper envelope if such an envelope is established, or just used on the chosen subsequence. Do not conclude that a fixed positive tau pays o(NT), or that D_old=o(NT) alone is enough without the massive bound. No final upper contraction, no Lean verification.
