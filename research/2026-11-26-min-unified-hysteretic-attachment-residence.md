## 2. Edge-return retirement lemma

For arbitrary integers 1<m<L, mark an actual small edge e if some tag crosses e as the FIRST small edge after visiting some large vertex V. The witnessed continuation from V through e has no earlier small edge. For each distinct marked edge choose one such witness vertex V_e and use its FULL original cohort of >=L tags. Every tag of that cohort either traverses e or stops before crossing it, by determinism along the actually witnessed continuation. At most m-1 traverse e. Hence >=L-m+1 distinct tags of the cohort retire before e (including at its predecessor or at V_e). Denote their set Q_e.

The sets Q_e are disjoint over different marked edges. If a tag lay in Q_e and Q_f, it would have visited both witness vertices before its own terminal; order those vertices by true tick time, say V_e first. Its actual trajectory follows the witnessed path toward e up to termination, hence V_f lies on this path before e. No small edge lies from V_f up to e exclusive, so the witnessed first small edge after V_f is e, forcing f=e. This also covers identical witness vertices. No stopped path is extended. Each actual tagged first-small return is counted once at its edge, even if multiple earlier large witnesses exist. Thus
    #marked edges <= N/(L-m+1),
    #tagged return incidences <= N(m-1)/(L-m+1).
In particular the number of distinct original tags Bad_edge ever making a return is bounded by the latter expression. This is the true-edge analogue of 685c795's projected-light arrival lemma. It additionally detects retirement-induced shrinkage of an incoming branch even if the next true vertex becomes large again immediately. A merely large projected horizon does not suffice for the witness.

Pay ALL surviving exact original source role involvements of Bad_edge in BOTH transports, before and after the offending edge. Their total clock <=2 c_a (H+P_max)|Bad_edge|=O(NT m/L)=o(NT). Counts of edges, vertices, original source and branch masses stay frozen. This may overlap old Bad; pay sequentially. Every remaining tag after its first large vertex never traverses a small continuation edge.

## 3. Two thresholds for actual first meetings

Apply the previous global both-small pair payment with threshold L replacing m: by truncated quadratic f_(dL), the total ordered pairs whose first meeting has BOTH incoming branches <L is <=2dLN. This uses the sum of the small branch squares exactly as in 03de6f7, not an upper count of all pairs at those vertices. Whole backward lifetime per ordered pair <=c_a(H+P_max)/b_min, so exact cost O(NT dL/b_min)=o(NT). Keep earlier both-small and heavy-thin masks.

Now any remaining backward birth has at least one incoming branch >=L. Partition into:
* large attachment: other branch <m;
* heavy merge: other branch >=m.

Heavy merge vertices lie in the previous two-m-heavy support <=2N/m. Keep prior aggregate cutoff and all old individual pair cutoffs. For a large attachment at V, a non-Bad_edge tag from the smaller branch could NOT have previously visited a large vertex: it just crossed a small continuation edge, or it is a root injection with no past. Since V itself has population >=L, its first large vertex is precisely V. As subsequent small edges are forbidden, every original non-Bad_edge tag can have AT MOST ONE such attachment membership across its entire trajectory. The old O(2N) aggregate attachment incidence estimate is replaced in this narrowed domain by a disjoint set of at most N tags. This is not a claim about unfiltered attachments at threshold m or about actual first meetings before first large. Full heavy side can still receive many attachments; the uniqueness concerns the small side.

Let A_i^+ be the horizon of the first large true vertex of tag i, with key V_i^+ including true tick, if it exists. Any member entering V on a branch >=L has visited a large vertex STRICTLY before V. Thus every remaining backward pair has different V_i^+ keys: if two tags already visited the same earlier first-large vertex then their first meeting cannot be V; if one first becomes large at V then the large incoming branch member's first-large vertex was earlier. This applies also to heavy merges when the other incoming branch is in [m,L), regardless of that member's previous history. Do not identify different true vertices merely by projected horizon.

## 4. Disjoint attachment residence and refined all-birth envelope

Let Bad be union of previous bad-tag set and Bad_edge. Every surviving backward source participant has first large vertex by its actual massive source. Define J_i global final weighted-moving expiry posthorizon, F_i actual terminal, M_i^+=max(J_i,A_i^+), for non-Bad terminal-nonmoving tags with a first large vertex. The previous final-tail strip remains. Additionally pay the initial strip K<A_i^++h at cost O(N(h+P_max)) by the original either-role per-tag cap. Then every surviving backward atom satisfies
    M_i^+ + h <= K <= F_i - h
for both members. Set R_plus=sum_i (F_i-M_i^+-2h)_+. Since A_i^+>=A_i^phys when both exist, R_plus<=R_joint of 03de6f7, with the smaller domain too. New all-backward scalar upper bound
    B_new <= c_a (R_plus+NP_max).
No assertion of a positive constant difference.

For attachment tags i in disjoint set I_att, let V_i^+ be their attachment and use actual full original heavy incoming union at that vertex, now restricted to branches >=L. At a source atom K of original b-slot G on i's path, let H_G^L(V) count full G members entering V on these >=L branches. Any surviving large attachment pair involving i as its unique small member has its partner among them. Original ordered-pair cap therefore gives the exact total upper bound
    B_att <= 2 c_a mean_P P sum_(i in I_att) sum_(eligible K of i)
                      H_G^L(V_i^+)/b
          <= 2 c_a [sum_(i in I_att) (F_i-M_i^+-2h)_+ + |I_att|P_max].
The sum is over actual eligible original source atoms, zero if no positive source. Old source masks and allowed pair keys may be retained. Previous attachment bound had 2N membership incidences; now each attachment tag supplies only its one final lifespan, with no reset multiplicity. The total backward row bound also applies. Pay all exact attachment fractions at sources where H_G^L(V_i^+)<=eta b: total O(eta NT), using unique i. This can strengthen the previous test on the sum of >=m branches; no inference about individual heavy branches. A further small-lifetime payment for i whose eligible final length is <=eta H costs O(eta NT+NP_max). None of these masks changes original source weights.

At a positive source G let n_* be its full subset passing new bad-tag, maturity and terminal strips. Partition it by first-large key V_i^+ with sizes a_v. Every surviving backward pair lies between distinct keys. Thus a tighter joint capacity valid for ALL birth types is
    B_atom <= c_a P [n_*² - sum_v a_v²]/b.
This includes both-heavy and attachments with the original common denominator. In particular it is <=c_a P n_*. For attachment only let a count subset of n_* that are in I_att. Every attachment pair uses at least one such member, so one can also use c_a P(2 a n_* - a²)/b, or its minimum with the distinct-key cap and the actual heavy-union ratio above. Do not add these alternative envelopes. This yields a normalized aggregate of all allowed source cuts, not a claim that the relaxed first-large family partition characterizes all eligible births. The sum over grids is <=c_a(R_plus+NP_max).

## 5. Prime-origin age bound for every fresh first-large cohort

For EACH actual true vertex V let F_V^+ denote the set of original tags whose first large vertex is V; include all such tags even if older tags also visit V. These fresh sets are pairwise disjoint. They include all-new seeds, new tags joining old large branches, and new tags crossing the threshold together with older participants. For q_V=|F_V^+|, all q_V roots have distinct prime origins C0=2M-2<=C_V. In an interval of horizon length x, the number of prime roots is <=C(1+x/log(2+x)), uniformly for 0<=x<=H, with absolute C: use the prior one-form upper sieve in an interval of length x/2+O(1) with cutoff x^(1/40) and absorbed O(x^.2) error for large x; small x use an elementary bound. Original roots M>=X exceed all sieve primes. This is an upper bound only, no prime lower bound or uniform asymptotic needed. For q sufficiently large choose x=c q log(2+q) with absolute c small enough that at most q/2 roots have age C_V-C0<x; x<=O(H) for any actual feasible q by the same bound at H (alternatively if x>H it would contradict feasibility). At least q/2 ages are >=x. For 2<=q bounded, elementary distinct odd prime spacing C0 gives sum of ages >=2 q(q-1), absorbing constants. For q=1 use zero lower bound. Therefore
    sum_(i in F_V^+) (A_i^+-C0_i) >= c0 q_V(q_V-1) log(2+q_V).
Unlike the seed-only result in 03de6f7, this holds even when a very large older group is present. It also applies to ANY subset of a fresh cohort, notably the non-Bad surviving attachment tags at V or the eligible joint-tail tags. Summing over fresh cohorts counts each tag exactly once. For a subset S_V of q fresh tags, the total residence <=sum_i (H-2h-(A_i^+-C0_i))_+. By the layer formula this equals integral_0^(H-2h) # {i in S_V: A_i^+-C0_i<x} dx, hence <= integral_0^(H-2h) min(q,C(1+x/log(2+x))) dx. Another bound uses (H-2h-a)_+ <= (H-2h)(1-a/H) for 0<=a<=H, giving
    residence(S_V) <= (H-2h)[q - (c0/H) q(q-1) log(2+q)].
These upper bounds can be used on R_plus or the attachment residence, with the corresponding actual fresh group sizes and the old final-maturity restriction (which only reduces residence). They need not produce order NT savings: fresh attachment cohorts can be singletons, and the minimum large threshold itself is o(H/u). The age inequality does NOT assign the age of an old partner to a new tag. A new tag may enter near its own root while its old partners have already consumed large gain. For pair-level use the actual common remaining lifetime <= (H-max(C_V-C0_i,C_V-C0_j))_+, from both gain budgets. This can further restrict the actual heavy-union source ratio. No global constant margin derived from age alone.
